Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park Beach Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park holds the southern tip of Key Biscayne, about a mile of Atlantic beach wrapped around the Cape Florida Lighthouse, built in 1825 and described by Florida State Parks as a beacon with a long freedom history. This is the wild end of the island: a state park beach with nature trails, bike paths, fishing, and picnic pavilions rather than hotels. Florida State Parks lists entry at $8 per vehicle for two to eight people, $4 for a single occupant vehicle or motorcycle, and $2 for pedestrians and bicyclists, checked October 30, 2026. The park opens at 8 a.m. and closes at sunset.

Where is the park and how do you reach it?

Drive the length of Key Biscayne. From mainland Miami you cross the Rickenbacker Causeway, pass Crandon Park and the Village of Key Biscayne on Crandon Boulevard, and keep going until the road ends at the park entrance. Florida State Parks lists the address as 1200 Crandon Boulevard, Key Biscayne, FL 33149. The park covers the southern third of the island, with the Atlantic beach on its east side and Biscayne Bay, No Name Harbor, and the mangrove trails on its west side, so a single visit naturally splits into an ocean half and a bay half. Because entry is per vehicle and the park is popular on weekends, gates can back up late in the morning; arriving near the 8 a.m. opening gives you the lighthouse, the beach, and the trails at their quietest.

The lighthouse and the beach

The Cape Florida Lighthouse is the reason many people come. Florida State Parks dates it to 1825 and tells its layered story on the park page: a guide for shipping through the Florida Channel, and a gathering point tied to Seminole history and to Black Seminoles and fugitive slaves who took passage from this cape to the Bahamas. Guided tours through the lighthouse and the keeper cottage run on the park schedule; the park page is the place to confirm tour days and times before you build a morning around a climb, because schedules and access change with weather and conditions. The beach itself runs for about a mile along the Atlantic side, backed by sea grapes and park greenery instead of buildings. It is a state park beach rather than a guarded city beach, so plan your swim accordingly: go in with a companion and stay aware of currents.

Beyond the sand: trails, bikes, and the bay side

Bill Baggs rewards visitors who move. Paved paths loop the park and suit bikes, which you can bring or rent inside the park in season; walking trails cut through mangrove and coastal hammock on the bay side. No Name Harbor, the small bay inlet inside the park, is the boating and sunset side, with fishing along its seawall and views back toward the Miami skyline. Picnic pavilions and grills are spread through the park, some reservable through the park, which makes this a strong cookout beach as long as you follow the posted park rules and pack out what you bring in. Food options inside the park have included cafe style service near the beach and the harbor; hours and operators change, so check the Florida State Parks page for this park before counting on a specific restaurant being open.

Fees, hours, and planning your visit

The money facts are simple and come from Florida State Parks. Admission is $8 per vehicle carrying two to eight people, $4 for a single occupant vehicle or motorcycle, and $2 per pedestrian, bicyclist, or extra passenger, checked October 30, 2026. The park is open from 8 a.m. to sunset, every day of the year. Add the Rickenbacker Causeway toll to your drive budget. Inside the gate, parking is included in admission, and the lots sit behind the beach accesses, so your walk to the sand is short. A good shape for the day is lighthouse and trails first, beach through the middle, and No Name Harbor for the late light. The facts that matter

Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, 1200 Crandon Boulevard, Key Biscayne, FL 33149, at the southern tip of Key Biscayne (Florida State Parks). Open 8 a.m. to sunset, 365 days a year. Admission listed by Florida State Parks and checked October 30, 2026: $8 per vehicle (two to eight people), $4 single occupant vehicle or motorcycle, $2 pedestrians and bicyclists. The Cape Florida Lighthouse was built in 1825. The Atlantic beach runs about a mile. This is a state park beach, not a lifeguard staffed city beach.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to enter Bill Baggs?
Florida State Parks lists $8 per vehicle for two to eight people, $4 for a single occupant vehicle or motorcycle, and $2 for pedestrians and bicyclists, checked October 30, 2026. The causeway toll on the drive out is separate.

Can I tour the lighthouse?
Guided tours of the lighthouse and keeper cottage are part of the park program, but days and times are set by the park and can change with conditions. Confirm the current schedule on the Florida State Parks page for this park before you go.

Are there lifeguards at Bill Baggs beach?
No, not in the city beach sense. This is a state park beach without the staffed Ocean Rescue or county lifeguard system used in Miami Beach and Sunny Isles, so swim with a companion and extra care.

How is Bill Baggs different from Crandon Park?
Crandon, up the boulevard, is the Miami-Dade County park with big picnic grounds, sports facilities, and a family amusement center. Bill Baggs is the state park: quieter, more natural, and built around the 1825 lighthouse and the bay side harbor.
