Crandon Park Beach Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Crandon Park is a Miami-Dade County park on the northern half of Key Biscayne, reached from mainland Miami across the Rickenbacker Causeway. Its beach runs for about two miles along the Atlantic side of a park of more than 800 acres, with picnic grounds, a nature center, a marina, golf, and tennis sharing the same land. The water here is usually calmer than on the Miami Beach barrier island, which is why families pick it. Parking is the main cost of the day: the county lists $7 plus tax Monday to Thursday and $10 plus tax Friday to Sunday, checked October 30, 2026.

Where is Crandon Park and how do you get there?

Key Biscayne is an island village southeast of downtown Miami. You drive out on the Rickenbacker Causeway, which carries a toll, cross Virginia Key, and continue onto Crandon Boulevard. Crandon Park occupies the northern part of the island; the Village of Key Biscayne sits in the middle, and Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park holds the southern tip, so one causeway drive strings three different beach days together. The county lists park addresses along Crandon Boulevard, with the main beach park office at 6747 Crandon Boulevard and the park office phone at 305-361-5421. The county lists park hours as 7 a.m. to sunset, with the main park office open seven days a week from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Weekends fill early in good weather, and the lots are the pinch point, so a morning arrival makes the day.

What is the beach like?

This is a park beach, not a resort beach. Behind the sand there are lawns, palms, picnic shelters, and parking lots instead of hotels, and the crowd is mostly local families and groups who have come set up for the whole day. An offshore sandbar takes much of the swell, so the water is often shallow and gentle near shore, although the county notes conditions change with weather and the sandbar shifts. Lifeguard stands line the busiest central stretch of the beach; the county does not give this page a single fixed lifeguard timetable to print, so treat the stands as your guide on the day, swim near one, and ask a guard about conditions when you arrive. The ends of the two mile beach are quieter, with the nature center and the Bear Cut preserve trails at the north end giving the day a walkable second half.

What are the rules and facilities?

Two county rules are worth knowing before you pack. Glass containers are not permitted in the park, and pets are prohibited, with the exception of service animals, per the Miami-Dade County park page. Plan drinks in cans or plastic and leave dogs at home. The facility list is what makes Crandon different from a simple strand: picnic shelters, restrooms and concessions near the beach, a family amusement center, the Marjory Stoneman Douglas Biscayne Nature Center at the north end, plus the Crandon Park marina, golf course, and tennis center elsewhere in the park. Some of those carry their own fees and hours, which the county handles through its ParkLink system, so a beach day here can grow into a kayak outing or a nature walk without anyone getting back in the car.

How should I plan a Crandon Park day?

Arrive in the morning, pay the parking fee at the lot, and set up near a lifeguard stand with a picnic as your anchor; most groups cook or carry food in, within the park rules, rather than relying on concessions alone. Give the middle of the day to shade, the nature center, or the preserve trails, and swim again late when the light drops and the lots thin out. Budget for two money items beyond food: the causeway toll on the way out and the county parking fee, $7 plus tax on weekdays and $10 plus tax Friday to Sunday as listed by the county and checked October 30, 2026. The facts that matter

Crandon Park is a Miami-Dade County park at 6747 Crandon Boulevard, Key Biscayne, on the northern part of the island, reached by the Rickenbacker Causeway. County listed parking: $7 plus tax Monday to Thursday, $10 plus tax Friday to Sunday (checked October 30, 2026). County listed park hours: 7 a.m. to sunset; main park office 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. seven days a week, phone 305-361-5421. County rules printed on the park page: glass containers are not permitted, and pets are prohibited except service animals. The park covers more than 800 acres with about two miles of Atlantic beach.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Crandon Park cost?
The county lists parking at $7 plus tax Monday to Thursday and $10 plus tax Friday to Sunday, checked October 30, 2026. The Rickenbacker Causeway toll is separate. Activities such as golf, tennis, and rentals have their own fees.

Can I bring my dog to Crandon Park?
No. Miami-Dade County lists pets as prohibited in Crandon Park, with the exception of service animals. Glass containers are also not permitted, so pack accordingly.

Is Crandon Park good for small children?
It is one of the family staples of Miami-Dade because the offshore sandbar usually keeps the near shore water shallow and calmer than the open Miami Beach surf. Conditions still change daily, so swim near a lifeguard stand and check with the guards on arrival.

How is Crandon different from Bill Baggs?
Crandon is the county park in the north of Key Biscayne, with big lots, picnic grounds, and sports facilities. Bill Baggs is the state park at the southern tip, built around the Cape Florida Lighthouse. They sit on the same boulevard and pair well in one trip.
