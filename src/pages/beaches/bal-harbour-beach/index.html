Bal Harbour Beach Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Bal Harbour Beach is the public Atlantic beach of the Village of Bal Harbour, a small village at the northern tip of the Miami Beach barrier island, between Surfside at 96th Street to the south and Haulover Inlet to the north. The beach itself is public and free, about a mile of carefully kept sand fronting luxury resorts and Bal Harbour Shops a short walk inland. What makes it different is the access pattern: public entry is limited, the prettiest path in the village is residents only, parking is scarce, and the Village does not publish a lifeguard schedule for this beach, so plan a careful, self reliant beach day.

Where is Bal Harbour and where do you get onto the beach?

Bal Harbour is its own village, distinct from Surfside to its south and from Bay Harbor Islands across the 96th Street bridge to its west; the similar names confuse visitors every season. The village runs along Collins Avenue from 96th Street north to Haulover Inlet. For a day visitor, the practical public entry points are at the south end around 96th Street, where you cross from Surfside, and at the north end by the inlet, where a walkway follows the channel in from the parking under the Haulover bridge. Do not count on the 102nd Street Beach Access Path. The Village of Bal Harbour describes that path on its official site as a residents entry point: a 25 foot wide, paver lined path with benches, shade, and a body and foot shower, with entrances secured by locked gates opened by the Bal Harbour All Access Card, which requires proof of residency. It is a good example of how this village works: beautiful, orderly, and organized first for the people who live here and the guests of its hotels.

What is the beach like?

Quiet is the product. The sand is wide and groomed, the towers behind it are set back, and there is no boardwalk scene, no music spilling from cafes, and no chair rental row aimed at day visitors; the serviced sand in front of the resorts is set up for hotel guests. A paved beach path runs the length of the village behind the sand, linking the 96th Street end to the jetty walk at Haulover Inlet, and it is one of the best short walks on this coast: south toward Surfside and the Miami Beach Beachwalk, north to watch boats work the inlet. The jetty at the inlet is the natural end point, a place to watch the channel traffic and the Sunny Isles skyline across the water. Swimming needs a note of honesty: the Village does not publish a lifeguard schedule for Bal Harbour Beach, and this guide found no village lifeguard program to report, so treat this as an unguarded swim. Stay close to shore, swim with a companion, and if a guarded swim matters to your group, Haulover Beach Park just across the inlet and Sunny Isles Beach just beyond it run staffed Ocean Rescue and county lifeguard services.

Parking and practical planning

Parking is the constraint that shapes a Bal Harbour beach day. There is no big public beach lot in the village. Day visitors generally use the metered parking under the Haulover bridge at the north end, or park in Surfside and walk in at 96th Street, and both options are limited and priced by their respective cities, so check current rates and time limits before you rely on a space. Many visitors avoid the problem entirely by staying in a Bal Harbour or Surfside hotel, arriving on the village and city shuttles, or folding the beach into a Bal Harbour Shops day: shops and lunch inland, then a walk on the path and an hour on the sand. Bring what you need with you, including water and shade, because public facilities on this beach are minimal compared with the county and state park beaches. The Village Hall address is 655 96th Street, and the village site, balharbourfl.gov, is the source to check for current access, shuttle, and parking information before you set out.

The facts that matter

Bal Harbour Beach is the public beach of the Village of Bal Harbour, Collins Avenue from 96th Street north to Haulover Inlet, about a mile of sand. The 102nd Street Beach Access Path is residents only: the Village describes a 25 foot wide paver path with a shower, gated and opened by the Bal Harbour All Access Card, which requires proof of residency. Public day access is at the 96th Street south end and by Haulover Inlet at the north end. The Village does not publish a lifeguard schedule for this beach. Village Hall: 655 96th Street, Bal Harbour, FL 33154.

Frequently asked questions

Is Bal Harbour Beach public or private?
The beach is public and free. What is restricted is access infrastructure: the 102nd Street path is gated for residents with the All Access Card, and the chair setups on the sand belong to the hotels for their guests.

Are there lifeguards at Bal Harbour Beach?
The Village of Bal Harbour does not publish a lifeguard schedule for this beach, and visitors should not count on a guarded swim here. For staffed lifeguards, use Haulover Beach Park across the inlet or Sunny Isles Beach to the north.

Where do I park for Bal Harbour Beach?
Options are limited: metered parking under the Haulover bridge at the north end, or parking in Surfside and walking in at 96th Street. Rates and limits change; check the village and Surfside information before you go.

Is Bal Harbour the same as Bay Harbor Islands?
No. Bal Harbour, with a u, is the oceanfront village with the beach and the Shops. Bay Harbor Islands is a separate town in Biscayne Bay to the west, connected by the 96th Street bridge.
