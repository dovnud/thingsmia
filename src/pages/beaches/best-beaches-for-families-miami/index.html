Best Miami Beaches for Families

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. For most families, Crandon Park Beach on Key Biscayne is the first pick in Miami: Miami-Dade County describes a two mile beach with calm water, because its offshore sandbar protects swimmers from a crashing surf, plus concessions, picnic areas, cabanas, and a beachside carousel. Matheson Hammock is the calmest wading option, with a man made atoll pool on Biscayne Bay. South Beach suits older children, Bill Baggs suits a nature day with one trade off, and Haulover suits families who accept open ocean surf. The ranking is ours; the facts are from the parks and the city.

How we ranked these family beaches

Four criteria, in this order. Water first: calm water matters more to a family with young children than scenery does. Facilities second: concessions, picnic space, and rentals decide whether a day lasts two hours or six. Shade and a plan B third, because a park survives midday heat better than bare sand. Parking last: a per vehicle fee is a fair trade for a short walk with children and bags. No beach here removes the need for supervision. Read our beach safety guide and treat every flag as part of the plan.

1. Crandon Park Beach, the family first pick

Crandon earns first place on the county's own description. Miami-Dade County describes a two mile beach celebrated for pristine sand, calm water, a winding promenade, concession stands, picnic areas with tables and grills, cabanas, and a beachside carousel, and it states that Crandon's offshore sandbar protects swimmers from a crashing surf. That is the family formula: easier water, food in the park, and room to spread out. It is a county park on Key Biscayne, reached by the Rickenbacker Causeway, with entry and parking as the county posts. See our Crandon Park Beach guide.

2. Matheson Hammock Park, for the youngest swimmers

Matheson Hammock, the county park in Coral Gables at 9610 Old Cutler Road, is built around a man made atoll pool, naturally flushed by the tidal action of Biscayne Bay. The City of Coral Gables describes a 630 acre park with that pool, a tranquil beach that is a haven for families, a marina, a snack bar and restaurant in a historic coral rock building, picnic pavilions, and nature trails. For toddlers, a bay side pool setting beats any open Atlantic beach on a breezy day. The trade off: this is wading and paddling more than an ocean swim, best as a planned park day.

3. South Beach, for older children and a city day

South Beach suits older children, when the day mixes swimming with the city. It is a City of Miami Beach beach with Ocean Rescue lifeguards, and the city advises swimming in a guarded area near a lifeguard stand. Across Ocean Drive are Lummus Park, the Art Deco streets, and the widest food choice on this list. The limits: the city prohibits alcohol and smoking on its beaches, the beach is closed overnight under city code, and the strip gets louder later. Families do best with a morning pattern. See our South Beach guide.

4. Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, the nature day, with a warning

Bill Baggs, at the southern end of Key Biscayne, is the greenest family day here: Florida State Parks describes over a mile of Atlantic beachfront for sunbathing, swimming, and picnics, plus the Cape Florida Lighthouse, built in 1825 and reconstructed in 1846, with guided tours twice daily, Thursday through Monday. As a picnic and lighthouse day, it is excellent. The warning is the state's own: there are no lifeguards here, so swimming is at your own risk, and in winter visitors should watch for Portuguese man o' war warnings at the ranger station. Choose it for the park, and supervise the water closely. See our Bill Baggs guide.

5. Haulover Beach Park, the big park day

Haulover is a facilities pick, not a calm water pick. The county describes open ocean surf here, and lists food concessions, a kite store, rentals, picnic areas with tables and grills, a marina, and a dog beach area across a 1.4 mile park. For a large group with confident swimmers, that works well. County signage marks a clothing optional section in the northern part, so families who prefer the main beach should set up in the central and southern parts, and young children belong at the water's edge on a rough day. See our Haulover Beach guide.

The facts that matter

Crandon Park: two mile county beach, calm water, sandbar protects swimmers from a crashing surf, concessions, picnic areas, cabanas, and a carousel (Miami-Dade County). Matheson Hammock: 630 acre park, atoll pool flushed by bay tides, picnic pavilions, snack bar and restaurant (City of Coral Gables). South Beach: Ocean Rescue lifeguards, no alcohol and no smoking, closed 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. Bill Baggs: over a mile of beachfront, no lifeguards (Florida State Parks). Haulover: open ocean surf, signed clothing optional section in the north.

Frequently asked questions

Which Miami beach is best for toddlers?
Matheson Hammock for wading in its atoll pool setting, and Crandon Park for a real beach with calm, sandbar protected water. Both beat an open surf beach for the youngest children.

Which family beach has lifeguards?
South Beach is guarded by the City of Miami Beach Ocean Rescue division, and the city's advice is to swim near a lifeguard stand. Bill Baggs, by contrast, has no lifeguards, per Florida State Parks, so do not choose it expecting a guarded swim.

Do these parks charge to enter?
County and state park beaches here work on per vehicle fees the parks post and can change. Check the park page for the current amount.

What should we pack?
Shade, water, snacks as a backup to the concessions, and a change of clothes. Check the flags on arrival and ask a lifeguard where to swim.
