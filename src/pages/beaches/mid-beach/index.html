Mid-Beach Miami Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Mid-Beach is the middle stretch of the city of Miami Beach, generally described as running from the low 20s streets, around 23rd Street, up to about 63rd Street along Collins Avenue. It is the resort row: big oceanfront hotels, a wide beach, and a calmer pace than South Beach to the south. The beach here is a City of Miami Beach beach, so the city rules apply in full: no alcohol, no smoking, an overnight closure, and Ocean Rescue lifeguards on duty.

Where exactly is Mid-Beach?

Mid-Beach is a neighborhood of Miami Beach, not a separate city. The city organizes services by area and has a Mid Beach coordinator, but it does not publish one codified boundary line, so treat street numbers as a practical guide. The working description is the stretch from about 23rd or 24th Street up to about 63rd Street. Collins Avenue is its spine. On the ocean side stand the hotels and towers; on the west side, Indian Creek and the bay side residential streets sit a block or two inland. South of the low 20s you are in South Beach. North of 63rd Street the island turns into North Beach, quieter and more residential again.

Two landmarks help you place yourself. The Fontainebleau, at 4401 Collins Avenue, sits near the middle of the strip and is its best known resort address. Further south, the blocks from about 32nd to 36th Streets form the Faena District, a cluster of hotels and arts spaces that gives the southern end of Mid-Beach its own identity. The Julia Tuttle Causeway lands at 41st Street, which makes Mid-Beach the part of the island many drivers reach first from the airport side of the mainland.

What is the beach itself like?

The sand in Mid-Beach is wide, and the crowd is spread thinner than it is on Ocean Drive. Much of the daytime life is organized by the hotels: guests walk from the pool decks to chair and umbrella setups on the sand, while day visitors use the public access points at the street ends. Behind the beach runs the oceanfront path, part boardwalk and part paved Beachwalk, which is the natural way to move up and down this stretch on foot or by bike. It is a good sunrise coast, at its calmest before the hotel decks fully set up for the day.

What are the beach rules and lifeguard hours?

The rules are the City of Miami Beach rules, the same ones that govern South Beach. Alcohol is prohibited on the city beaches, and smoking is prohibited too. The city code closes the public beach overnight, from 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. Ocean Rescue, the city lifeguard service headquartered at 1001 Ocean Drive, staffs towers along Mid-Beach as it does along the rest of the city shoreline. The city lists lifeguard duty hours as 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. during Daylight Saving Time, roughly March to October, and 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. during Standard Time, roughly November to February. Individual tower opening and closing times on a given day can differ slightly, and the city posts the day figures, flags, and advisories on its current beach conditions page, so check it before you swim. The Ocean Rescue hotline is (305) 673-7714. As everywhere in Miami Beach, the advice is simple: swim near a staffed lifeguard tower.

How should I plan a Mid-Beach day?

If you are staying on the strip, plan around your hotel beach setup and use the path for range: walk south toward the Faena blocks for lunch, or north for a quieter stretch of sand. Day visitors should pick one access point and settle, because parking in this corridor is mostly city garages, hotel valets, and metered spaces that change by block, so check the city parking information for current rates rather than counting on a fixed price. The getting around Miami guide covers the causeways and trolleys that make a car optional here. Evenings in Mid-Beach are dinner driven rather than club driven, which is why many travelers who found South Beach too loud base themselves here and visit the south end instead of sleeping in it. Frequently asked questions

Is Mid-Beach the same as Miami Beach?
Mid-Beach is one neighborhood inside the city of Miami Beach, the barrier island city across the bay from downtown Miami. South Beach is the neighborhood to its south and North Beach the one to its north.

Is Mid-Beach quieter than South Beach?
Generally, yes. The beach is wide, the crowd is mostly hotel guests, and the nightlife is thinner. The trade is distance: Ocean Drive and Lincoln Road are a ride south, not outside your door.

Can I drink alcohol on the beach in Mid-Beach?
No. The City of Miami Beach prohibits alcohol and smoking on its beaches, and Mid-Beach is a city beach. Drinks belong in the hotel bars and restaurants behind the sand.

Where do I check conditions before swimming?
The City of Miami Beach posts daily beach conditions, including tower hours, flags, and advisories, and Ocean Rescue runs a hotline at (305) 673-7714.
