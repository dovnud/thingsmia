Best Miami Beaches for Swimming

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. For a relaxed swim, start with Crandon Park on Key Biscayne, where Miami-Dade County describes calm water protected by an offshore sandbar. For a guarded city swim, Miami Beach is the pick, because its Ocean Rescue lifeguards staff stands along the beach and the city advises swimming in a guarded area near a stand. Matheson Hammock offers the calmest wading in a bay side atoll pool. Haulover is a surf swim for stronger swimmers. Bill Baggs is last on purpose: its beach is open to swimming, but Florida State Parks states there are no lifeguards there. Check the flags first, every time.

How we ranked these swimming beaches

Two tests decide the order, and scenery is not one of them. Cover first: a guarded beach outranks an unguarded one, because City of Miami Beach and National Weather Service guidance agree, swim near lifeguards and, if in doubt, do not go out. Water character second, as the park itself describes it: calm, sandbar protected water ranks above open ocean surf for most swimmers. This ranking can flip on the day: a red or double red flag at the top pick makes the correct choice no swimming at all. Read the beach safety guide alongside this page.

1. Crandon Park Beach, the calm water swim

Crandon takes first place on water character. Miami-Dade County describes a two mile beach celebrated for pristine sand and calm water, and states that its offshore sandbar protects swimmers from a crashing surf. For distance without fighting breakers, or for building confidence in open water, that protected feel is the best starting point in Miami. The park adds concessions, picnic areas, cabanas, and a promenade. It is a county park on Key Biscayne with per vehicle entry as the county posts. See our Crandon Park Beach guide, and still check the flags on arrival: calm is a tendency, not a promise.

2. Miami Beach, the guarded city swim

Miami Beach, from South Beach through Mid-Beach, is the best guarded swim here. The city says lifeguard stands are positioned throughout its seven mile stretch and staffed by trained professionals who post flag warnings for conditions such as strong currents and marine life. Ocean Rescue guidance is direct: always swim in a guarded area, near a lifeguard stand, because a lifeguard with an elevated view can see rip currents a swimmer cannot. That cover is why the city beach ranks second despite livelier surf than Crandon. City rules: no alcohol and no smoking on its beaches. See our South Beach guide and Mid-Beach guide.

3. Matheson Hammock, the calmest wading

Matheson Hammock is not an ocean swim, and that is its value. The county park in Coral Gables centres on a man made atoll pool, naturally flushed by the tidal action of Biscayne Bay, which the City of Coral Gables describes as a tranquil beach and a haven for families who enjoy its warm, safe waters. Read that as wading and gentle swimming for less confident swimmers and young children with close supervision, without surf or a rip current conversation. The limit is real: this is a bay side pool in a 630 acre park, not the Atlantic. If your test is the calmest water to get into, this is a strong third.

4. Haulover Beach Park, the surf swim

Haulover ranks fourth because its strength is the opposite of Crandon's. The county sells this 1.4 mile park on open ocean surf, white sand, and space, with concessions, rentals, and picnic areas. For a strong swimmer who wants surf and room, Haulover is the honest pick. The same surf is why it sits below the calmer picks, and why weaker swimmers should stay close to shore and to any staffed stand. Our Haulover Beach guide covers its beach areas, including the signed clothing optional section in the northern part.

5. Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, beautiful and unguarded

Bill Baggs closes the list as a deliberate caution. Florida State Parks describes a mile and a quarter of Atlantic beach open to swimming, by the Cape Florida Lighthouse. It also states there are no lifeguards, so swim at your own risk, and that in winter visitors should watch for Portuguese man o' war warnings at the ranger station. A confident swimmer who checks those warnings and swims with a buddy can enjoy this water. A reader looking for the safest swim should stop at picks one to three. The prettiest swim is not the best swim if nobody is watching the water.

The facts that matter

Crandon Park: calm water, sandbar protects swimmers from a crashing surf, two mile beach (Miami-Dade County). Miami Beach: lifeguard stands throughout a seven mile stretch, staffed by professionals who post flag warnings (City of Miami Beach). Matheson Hammock: atoll pool flushed by bay tides, tranquil with warm, safe waters (Coral Gables). Haulover: open ocean surf, 1.4 mile park (Miami-Dade County). Bill Baggs: a mile and a quarter open to swimming, no lifeguards, swim at your own risk (Florida State Parks).

Frequently asked questions

Where is the calmest water for swimming in Miami?
Crandon Park for an ocean beach, on the county's description of calm, sandbar protected water, and Matheson Hammock for the calmest wading of all, in its bay side atoll pool.

Which swimming beach has lifeguards?
Miami Beach, where the city says stands are positioned throughout its beaches and staffed by trained professionals. Bill Baggs has no lifeguards, per Florida State Parks, and says so in its own swimming description.

Can I swim at Bill Baggs Cape Florida?
Yes, the state park says its Atlantic beach is open to swimming. It also says there are no lifeguards and to swim at your own risk, so it suits confident swimmers who check the ranger station warnings first.

What if the flags are red?
Do not treat the ranking as permission. Red means high hazard and double red means the water is closed. Use the day for the park or a pool instead.
