Little Havana Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Little Havana is the neighborhood along Calle Ocho, which is SW 8th Street, west of downtown Miami. The visitor core is the stretch roughly between SW 12th and SW 17th Avenues: Domino Park, live music at Ball & Chain, cigar shops, cafecito windows, and Azucar ice cream, with the landmark Versailles restaurant a few blocks further west on the same street. Walk it in a morning, eat lunch there, and you have seen the essential version.

Where is Little Havana, and which part do visitors walk?

Calle Ocho is officially SW 8th Street, and VISIT FLORIDA calls it the epicenter of Miami's Little Havana. The full corridor runs west for many blocks, but the part a first time visitor should walk is the dense core between about SW 12th Avenue and SW 17th Avenue, where the restaurants, cigar shops, murals, and the Calle Ocho Walk of Fame are concentrated. The Greater Miami Convention and Visitors Bureau places the Walk of Fame between 12th and 17th Avenues, and the giant painted roosters along the street are part of the same stretch.

What should you see on Calle Ocho?

Domino Park (Máximo Gómez Park)

The park's official name is Máximo Gómez Park; everyone calls it Domino Park. It sits at 1444 SW 8th Street, on the corner of 15th Avenue, and its tables fill with domino players through the day. Watch a few games from the edge of the park, take photos respectfully, and you are seeing a real daily ritual, not a performance staged for visitors.

Ball & Chain

At 1513 SW 8th Street, Ball & Chain is the neighborhood's best known live music bar, open daily from 11 a.m. with no cover charge, per the venue's own site. It is the easiest place on the street to hear Cuban music without planning around a concert schedule.

Azucar Ice Cream Company

Azucar, at 1503 SW 8th Street, makes ice cream in Cuban flavors a few steps from Domino Park. It is the standard sweet stop on a Calle Ocho walk.

Where do you eat and drink?

Versailles Restaurant, at 3555 SW 8th Street, is the neighborhood's landmark Cuban restaurant, a short ride west of the walking core on the same street. Order the Cuban sandwich, croquetas, or a full plate of ropa vieja, and finish with a cafecito. El Pub Restaurant, at 1548 SW 8th Street in the middle of the core, is a long running, more everyday Cuban restaurant and a good sit down alternative. Between meals, the street runs on ventanitas, the walk up coffee windows where you order a cafecito or a colada to share, standing at the counter. That small ritual is the neighborhood in miniature.

What is the cigar and cafecito culture about?

Cigar shops along Calle Ocho keep rollers working in or near their front windows, rolling by hand while you watch. You do not need to smoke to enjoy it; the craft is the point, and the shops are happy to explain what makes a cigar well made. Cafecito is the other half of the culture: strong, sweet Cuban espresso, ordered small and often, and shared in little plastic cups when someone orders a colada for the group. Say yes when one is offered. It is a social drink, not a caffeine errand.

When is Viernes Culturales?

Viernes Culturales, or Cultural Fridays, is a free evening festival of art, music, and street culture on Calle Ocho, run by the nonprofit Viernes Culturales / Cultural Fridays, Inc. It is held on a Friday each month, and the exact Friday has varied between editions, so check the current month's listing on the Greater Miami Convention and Visitors Bureau events calendar before you plan around it. Admission is free; food, drinks, and art are sold along the street.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need to speak Spanish in Little Havana?
No. Shopkeepers and restaurants on Calle Ocho deal with visitors all day and English is fine. A few words of Spanish are appreciated and usually start a better conversation.

Is the Tower Theater open?
Not reliably right now. The historic theater at 1508 SW 8th Street has been closed during a change of operator, and a reopening under Miami Dade College management has been reported for late 2026. Check its status before planning an evening around it.

How do you get to Little Havana?
It is immediately west of downtown and Brickell. Metrobus routes run along SW 8th Street, the free City of Miami trolley network serves the area, and rideshares from downtown are short. Driving is easy too, but parking on the core blocks can take patience at lunch time.
