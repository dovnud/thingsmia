Coral Gables Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Coral Gables is the city George Merrick dreamed up in the 1920s and largely built: a planned, Mediterranean Revival suburb of coral stone, grand entrances, plazas and banyan canopy, southwest of downtown. Visitors come for three landmarks, the Biltmore Hotel, the Venetian Pool and Miracle Mile, and for the calm: this is Miami at its most beautiful and least beachy, a half day or a full slow day in the city's garden suburb.

Merrick's city and how to read it

Founded in 1925 and planned street by street, the Gables was designed in a single architectural language: Mediterranean Revival, with coral rock walls, tiled roofs, and themed entrances and plazas named for the countries that inspired them. The strict codes that preserve it are why the city still looks like its postcards. Driving or walking the residential streets south of Coral Way is the core experience; the banyan canopy roads around the Biltmore are the postcard version. Our guides section places the Gables in the wider first timer's map.

The Biltmore and Venetian Pool

The Biltmore Hotel, opened in 1926, is the Gables' cathedral: a tower modeled on Seville's Giralda, a storied pool, and a Sunday brunch that locals treat as an institution. Tours and afternoon tea run for non guests at times; check the hotel's own listings, and see our Biltmore guide for the staying version. Ten minutes away, the Venetian Pool is the city's other icon: a public swimming pool built in 1924 from a coral rock quarry, spring fed, with waterfalls and grottos, unlike any municipal pool in America. It fills on summer weekends and sometimes closes at capacity, so go early and check the city's page for the day's status.

Miracle Mile and the eating streets

Miracle Mile, the Coral Way strip through downtown Gables, is the city's main street: bridal shops by tradition, and increasingly the restaurants that matter, with Giralda Plaza, the pedestrian block just north, carrying the evening crowd under its canopy. The Gables has quietly become one of Miami's strongest dining neighborhoods, strong enough that our Coral Gables restaurants guide treats it as its own scene. The Colonnade and the Merrick Park shops cover the staying and shopping bases; the Hotel Colonnade guide is the Mile's own hotel.

Fairchild Garden and the southern edge

At the city's southern edge, Fairchild Tropical Botanic Garden spreads 83 acres of tropical collections, a butterfly conservatory and tram tours at 10901 Old Cutler Road; it is the Gables' full day option and one of the great American botanical gardens. Pair it with Matheson Hammock Park's bayfront and marina on the way back north. Getting to the Gables from downtown or the beach is a 20 to 30 minute drive or a Metrorail ride to Douglas Road or University station plus a short hop; the city's own trolley circulates free within the Gables.

The themed villages and the free trolley

Merrick's planners hid a game across the Gables: a set of themed international villages built in the 1920s, French, Dutch, Italian, Chinese and more, whole blocks designed as if a corner of another country had been set down among the banyans. Finding them is the neighborhood's best free walk. Moving between the sights, the city's own trolley service runs its routes free of charge, which makes the Gables one of the easiest Miami neighborhoods to tour without a car. Combine the villages with Miracle Mile's lunch counters and you have the Gables at its intended speed: slow, shaded and slightly theatrical.

The facts that matter

Anchor facts: Coral Gables was founded in 1925 and planned by George Merrick in Mediterranean Revival style. The Biltmore Hotel opened in 1926. The Venetian Pool was built in 1924 from a coral rock quarry and is spring fed; it can close at capacity. Fairchild Tropical Botanic Garden covers 83 acres at 10901 Old Cutler Road. The Coral Gables trolley circulates free in the city.

Frequently asked questions

How much time does Coral Gables need?
A half day for the Biltmore area, Miracle Mile and a meal. A full day if Fairchild Garden or the Venetian Pool is on the list.

Can I swim at the Venetian Pool?
Yes, it is a public pool with admission, spring fed and drained and refilled on its own schedule. Check the city's page for hours and capacity closures before you go.

Is Coral Gables good for dinner?
It is one of Miami's strongest dining neighborhoods right now, centered on Miracle Mile and Giralda Plaza.

How do I get there without a car?
Metrorail to Douglas Road or University station, then the free Coral Gables trolley or a short rideshare.
