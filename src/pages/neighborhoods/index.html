Miami Neighborhoods Guide

Miami is not one place; it is a string of distinct neighborhoods separated by water, causeways, and very different ideas about a good day. This hub is the map: what each visitor neighborhood feels like, who it suits, and where to read the full guide before you book a hotel in the wrong one.

The neighborhoods, one by one

South Beach and the Art Deco District

Ocean Drive, the historic district, and the beach: the Miami most people picture, with the practical layer underneath.

Doral Visitor Guide

CityPlace, the Blue Monster golf resort, Downtown Doral and the Doral Yard: what a visitor actually does in Doral, and when it is the right base.

Wynwood Visitor Guide

Murals, galleries, breweries, and warehouses turned creative spaces: how to do Wynwood in a day or a night.

Miami Design District Visitor Guide

Luxury flagships, public art, and destination restaurants in Miami's design quarter.

Brickell Visitor Guide

Miami's financial district turned residential: skyscraper hotels, rooftop bars, and life along the Metromover.

Downtown Miami Visitor Guide

Bayfront Park, the museums on the bay, Kaseya Center, and the working heart of the city.

Little Havana Visitor Guide

Calle Ocho's cigar rollers, cafecito windows, and Domino Park: the food walk that explains Miami.

Coral Gables Visitor Guide

The planned city of plazas, banyan trees, and grand old hotels: Miami's elegant side, worth at least a half day.

Coconut Grove Visitor Guide

Miami's oldest neighborhood: bayside parks, marinas, and a village pace under the tree canopy.

Little Haiti Visitor Guide

Haitian culture, the Little Haiti Cultural Complex, and the food and music along NE 2nd Avenue.

Key Biscayne Visitor Guide

The island village over the causeway: two parks, a historic lighthouse, and beaches far calmer than South Beach.

Bal Harbour and Surfside Visitor Guide

The quiet, polished north end of the barrier island: luxury shops, calm sand, and small town ease.

North Beach, Miami Beach Visitor Guide

63rd Street to 87th Terrace: the Bandshell, Oceanside Park, the quieter stay, and the trolley transfer that links it south.

Aventura Visitor Guide

The mall is the anchor: 300 plus stores at 19501 Biscayne Boulevard, a Brightline station next door, Tidal Cove day entry from $55, Oleta River State Park, and Sunny Isles sand ten minutes east.

Frequently asked questions

Is South Beach a separate city from Miami Beach?
No. South Beach is the southern end of the city of Miami Beach, roughly the streets below 23rd Street. Miami itself is a different city, across Biscayne Bay on the mainland. The mix up is the single most common Miami geography mistake, and it changes where you should book.

Which neighborhood should I stay in on a first trip?
South Beach if the beach and the Art Deco scene are the trip. Brickell or downtown if you want a city base with easy transit and bay views. Coral Gables or Coconut Grove if you want quiet streets and do not mind driving or rideshares to the beach. Each neighborhood guide above gives the honest trade offs.

Where exactly is Calle Ocho?
Calle Ocho is SW 8th Street, the main street of Little Havana, on the mainland west of downtown Miami. It is not in Miami Beach, despite how often visitors look for it there.

Is Wynwood easy to reach from the beach?
Wynwood sits just northwest of downtown Miami on the mainland. From Miami Beach it is a drive across the causeway; most visitors go by car or rideshare. The Wynwood guide covers arrival and parking in detail.
