Bal Harbour and Surfside Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. At Miami Beach's northern end, Bal Harbour and Surfside run a quieter, richer register than South Beach: Bal Harbour Shops, the open air luxury mall that invented the genre, a string of five star resorts, Surfside's small town main street, and wide, calm beaches linked by a beachfront path, with Haulover Park's kite flying lawns and famous beach just north. Come for a polished beach day, serious shopping, or a resort stay where the scene is the absence of one.

Bal Harbour Shops: the mall that is a garden

Bal Harbour Shops, opened in 1965 by developer Stanley Whitman, is the template for luxury open air retail in America: two levels of flagship stores around landscaped courtyards with koi ponds and palm canopy, anchored by Neiman Marcus and Saks Fifth Avenue. It is a working place of business for the fashion industry during the season and a remarkably pleasant place to browse the rest of the year. Dress is smarter than beach casual in the courtyards, the restaurants book out at lunch in season, and valet is the default parking move. Our Miami shopping guide places it in the city's full retail map.

Surfside: the small town between the resorts

Surfside, the incorporated town immediately south of Bal Harbour, is the counterweight: a quiet residential grid, a community center with a pool and water slides that residents guard happily, and Harding Avenue's small main street with kosher restaurants and bakeries serving the town's large Orthodox Jewish community. Visitors staying here get the same sand as Bal Harbour with a neighborhood's calm and a local's price on breakfast. The beach path runs the length of both towns, making the morning walk from Surfside to the Bal Harbour jetty one of the best in Miami.

The beaches and Haulover Park

The sand here is wide and the crowds thin compared with South Beach; the resorts run their chair concessions and the public access points are clearly marked along Collins Avenue. At the north end, Haulover Beach Park sits between the ocean and the inlet: famous for the clothing optional section at its northern beach, signposted so nobody arrives there by accident, and just as notable for its kite flying field, fishing jetty and the food truck gatherings on the bay side. Families use the southern stretches; the park publishes its own rules and fees, so check before a first visit.

Staying at the north end

The resort row here, the St. Regis and Ritz-Carlton Bal Harbour among them, sells space, service and quiet at the top of Miami's price range, and Surfside adds boutique options a step down. It suits travelers who want Miami Beach's sand without South Beach's tempo, and it pairs with the Design District and Wynwood, 20 minutes south, for culture days. Our Ritz-Carlton Bal Harbour and St. Regis Bal Harbour guides cover the two flagships; the hotels hub compares the area against the rest of the beach.

The beach path and the Collins strip

The two towns share one continuous oceanfront, and the paved beach path that runs the coast threads them together: walk it at sunrise from the Bal Harbour jetty, where the inlet frames the boats heading out, south through Surfside's residential beach to where the path continues toward Miami Beach proper. Collins Avenue is the landward twin, a strip of resort towers with the small Surfside downtown, Harding Avenue, set a block inland with its kosher bakeries, cafes and the community center. Staying up here buys a particular rhythm: resort polish at one end of the day, small town quiet at the other.

The facts that matter

Anchor facts: Bal Harbour Shops opened in 1965 and is anchored by Neiman Marcus and Saks Fifth Avenue in an open air, landscaped format. Surfside is an incorporated town directly south of the Village of Bal Harbour, both at the northern end of the Miami Beach barrier island. Haulover Beach Park, just north, includes a signposted clothing optional beach section at its north end. The beachfront path links Surfside and Bal Harbour along the sand.

Frequently asked questions

Is Bal Harbour worth visiting if I am not shopping?
Yes for the beach day and the people watching; the Shops' courtyards are a sight in themselves, and Surfside's calm is the real draw.

How far is Bal Harbour from South Beach?
About 25 to 35 minutes by car up Collins Avenue in normal traffic. The A1A drive is scenic and slow in season.

Is Haulover's clothing optional beach the whole beach?
No, it is the signposted northern section only. The rest of Haulover is a conventional family beach park.

Where do I park?
Bal Harbour Shops runs paid parking and valet. Surfside has municipal lots near the community center and beach access points.
