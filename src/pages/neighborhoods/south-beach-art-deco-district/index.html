South Beach and the Art Deco District

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. South Beach is the bottom slice of Miami Beach, and its Art Deco Historic District is the densest concentration of the architecture that made Miami famous: pastel hotels from the 1930s and 40s along Ocean Drive, Collins Avenue and Washington Avenue, protected since the district's listing on the National Register in 1979. Walk Ocean Drive in the morning with the buildings lit by early sun, swim from Lummus Park's beach, and save Lincoln Road for the evening. Everything a first timer wants from the district fits inside about twenty walkable blocks.

The district itself

The historic district holds more than 800 Art Deco and related buildings by the count usually cited from the preservation community, the largest such concentration anywhere, saved largely through the Miami Design Preservation League's campaign after the district's 1979 listing. The style here is Tropical Deco: streamline curves, porthole windows, racing stripes, neon, and eyebrows over the windows to shade the glass. The Carlyle, the Cardozo, the Colony and the Breakwater on Ocean Drive are the postcard row; the quieter residential streets west of Collins hold the apartment buildings that make the district a neighborhood rather than a set. The Miami Design Preservation League runs the official walking tours from its Art Deco Welcome Center at 1001 Ocean Drive, and our Art Deco walking tour guide compares going guided or solo.

Ocean Drive and Lummus Park

Ocean Drive between 5th and 15th Streets is the famous strip: cafe terraces under the hotel awnings on one side, Lummus Park and the beach on the other. Mornings are the local secret, when the light hits the facades, the cafes serve breakfast rather than spectacle, and the park fills with volleyball and skaters instead of crowds. The Versace Mansion at 1116 Ocean Drive, formally the Casa Casuarina, is viewed from the sidewalk by nearly everyone who walks the drive; it operates as a hotel and restaurant for those booking ahead. By late afternoon the drive shifts into its louder register, which is either the point or the cue to move inland, depending on your taste.

Lincoln Road and the inland streets

Lincoln Road, the pedestrian mall running east from Washington Avenue to the bay, is the district's living room: shops, galleries, cafe tables down the center, and the colony of cats and herons that own the side streets. Espanola Way, two blocks south, is the Mediterranean Revival counterpoint, built in the 1920s as an artists' village and now a lantern lit restaurant row. Collins and Washington Avenues carry the working hotels and the better value stays; our South Beach hotel guide and the Art Deco hotels list sort the staying options by street.

Staying, eating and the beach itself

The beach at South Beach is wide, white and patrolled, with the famous pastel lifeguard towers and rental concessions for chairs and umbrellas; the water is the turquoise of the postcards on calm days. Swim near lifeguards, and read our South Beach beach guide for access points and rules. Eating on Ocean Drive itself is priced for the view; one street back, the same kitchens cost less. The district's restaurants and the wider city's best tables are in our restaurants section. Nights split between the cocktail bars inland and the big rooms; the nightlife section covers both.

Practical South Beach

The Miami Beach trolley loops the island free, the DecoBike style rental stands have given way to Citi Bike docks, and rideshares surge on weekend nights. Parking is the scarce resource: the city garages on 7th, 13th and 17th Streets are the sane options, priced on city meters that change by season, and spring break brings its own rules and curfews, covered in our seasonal guides. The district rewards walking above all: nearly everything in this guide is inside a 20 minute stroll.

The facts that matter

Anchor facts: the Art Deco Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1979; the preservation community's commonly cited count is more than 800 preserved buildings. The Art Deco Welcome Center is at 1001 Ocean Drive. The Casa Casuarina (the Versace Mansion) is at 1116 Ocean Drive. Ocean Drive's postcard stretch runs 5th to 15th Street, facing Lummus Park and the beach.

Frequently asked questions

How much of South Beach can I see in one day?
The whole district core: Ocean Drive in the morning, the beach midday, Lincoln Road and Espanola Way by evening. It is compact by design.

Are the Art Deco hotels bookable?
Many are working boutique hotels, including icons on Ocean Drive itself. Our Art Deco hotels guide separates the real stays from the photo ops.

Is South Beach good for families?
By day, yes: the beach, the park and the northern residential streets are family territory. The late night Ocean Drive scene is the adult part, and it is easy to route around.

When is Art Deco Weekend?
The MDPL's festival is held in January on Ocean Drive. Dates are set per year; check the league's site when planning a January trip.
