North Beach, Miami Beach: the quieter stay, planned properly

Last reviewed: November 7, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. North Beach is the stretch of Miami Beach north of 63rd Street to the city limit at 87th Terrace, and it's the stay for people who want the same ocean with a thinner crowd. Its anchors are public, not commercial: the Miami Beach Bandshell at 7275 Collins Avenue, a 1961 venue on the National Register that runs 140 plus public concerts, festivals and events a year; North Beach Oceanside Park, running from 78th to 87th Streets with a dog beach and eleven outdoor fitness stations; and Altos Del Mar Park between 76th and 77th Streets. The hotel stock is small and mostly modest, the nightlife is a bus ride rather than a doorstep, and the free trolley system links you south on a transfer. If that trade sounds good, this page plans it. If it sounds dull, South Beach is honest about what it is and our South Beach hotel list is the other door.

The Bandshell: the neighborhood's living room

Start with the building that gives North Beach its calendar. The Miami Beach Bandshell, 7275 Collins Avenue, sits in Bandshell Park between 72nd and 73rd Streets. It was designed by Norman Giller, opened in the early 1960s as the last surviving bandshell of its kind in Miami Beach, and joined the National Register of Historic Places in 2022. The Rhythm Foundation has run it for the city since 2015, under a contract extended with options through 2028, and the venue's own count is more than 70,000 people at over 140 public events in a year: concerts, festivals, and community nights, many of them cheap or free at the gate. Its official site is miamibeachbandshell.com, and the calendar there is the single best reason to time a North Beach stay. A long weekend with a Bandshell night in it is a different trip from the same weekend without one. The venue also lists its practical side plainly: the free Collins Express trolley stops serve it, and municipal lots sit at 72nd Street, at 75th Street and Collins, and at the Deauville lot on 67th Street.

The two parks that carry the beach days

North Beach Oceanside Park is the big one, spanning 78th to 87th Streets along Collins, open sunrise to sunset, with beach access, restrooms at the pavilions, a playground for ages 5 to 12, a bark park and bark beach for dogs, and eleven outdoor fitness stations on the interior path between 82nd and 85th Streets. Its pavilions at 8051, 8301 and 8491 Collins rent for gatherings at $164 plus tax and a $99 janitorial fee for residents, $405 plus tax and the same fee for non-residents, booked through the city's parks office. City sources give the park's street address two ways, 8500 Collins in the resident guide and 8328 Collins in place listings; navigate by the street span, 78th to 87th, which every source agrees on. Altos Del Mar Park, about 2.5 acres on Collins between 76th and 77th Streets with the Beachwalk on its east side, is the smaller, shadier stop: sunrise to sunset, playgrounds for ages 2 to 5 and 5 to 12, outdoor fitness gear, two volleyball courts and a metered lot. Between the two parks and the sand in front of them, a North Beach day needs no ticket and no plan more complex than a towel.

Getting around: the loop, then the Express

The free Miami Beach trolley is the neighborhood's transport spine, and it works in two pieces. The North Beach Loop circulates inside the neighborhood, about 65th to 88th Streets plus Normandy Isle. The reach south comes from a transfer to the Collins Express, the limited stop run from 88th Street down to Washington Avenue and Lincoln Road, about every 15 minutes, on the system's 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. daily schedule with about a 20 minute average wait on the loops. Guides that promise the North Beach Loop alone will take you to South Beach are skipping the transfer. It's one change at a shared Collins Avenue stop and it costs nothing. Where to stay, and the empty lot you should know about

North Beach hotel supply is thin and mostly smaller properties, a fact this page states as the neighborhood's shape rather than a flaw to hide. The right way to shop it is the Miami Beach listings on Expedia filtered to the 63rd to 87th Street span, checking each property's own booking path, because the stock turns over and a named list would age badly. What you get for the thin supply is the trade at the top of this page: lower rates than the South Beach core on most dates, a residential street grid, and sand you don't share with a convention. What you give up is choice at the top end. For that, Bal Harbour and Surfside sit ten minutes north with the luxury row, and Mid-Beach's resort row starts right at 63rd Street going south.

One site on Collins needs a warning, because its name still floats through old hotel lists. The Deauville at 6701 Collins Avenue, the 1950s hotel at 67th Street, closed in 2017, was demolished in 2022, and the site has stood empty since. Approvals for a redevelopment with a partly rebuilt historic hotel and a new residential tower cleared the City Commission in June 2025, with design review still ahead and no room count settled in the approved reporting. It is not a hotel you can book, at any price, on any site that should know better. If a listing tries to sell you a stay there, close the tab.

North Beach against its neighbors, in one pass

Against South Beach: North Beach wins on quiet, price and park space, and loses on restaurants, nightlife and hotel choice. Against Bal Harbour: North Beach wins on price and on having a public events calendar at the Bandshell, and loses on polish and shopping. Against Mid-Beach: it's the same sand with fewer resorts and more neighborhood. The July 4th celebrations make the contrast concrete: the city's North Beach show is built around this end of the island, at Altos Del Mar Park, while the South Beach show sits at Lummus Park, and our July 4th guide takes the city's 2027 release as its source when it lands. Pick North Beach when the beach and a concert are the trip. Pick south when the trip is the scene.

Frequently asked questions

Is North Beach safe and lively enough for a first Miami trip?
It's a residential neighborhood with a real events calendar, and plenty of first trips base here happily. If your list is Ocean Drive, the Art Deco district and late nights, base south and visit North Beach for a beach day instead.

How long is the trolley ride to Lincoln Road?
Plan on the loop to a Collins Express stop, the express south, and the walk at the end. It's free and simple, and it's a ride you take with time in hand, not a timed connection.

Can I walk the beach from North Beach to South Beach?
Yes, on the sand and the Beachwalk, and it's about 4 miles from 80th Street to the South Beach core. Walk it in the morning. Ride back.
