Coconut Grove Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Coconut Grove is where Miami started: settled in the 1800s by Bahamian and New England pioneers, shaded by banyans and peacock patrolled side streets, wrapped around Dinner Key Marina's sailboat forest. The Grove runs at village pace inside a big city: brunch on the main streets, boats at the marina, peacocks in Peacock Park, and Vizcaya's Italian gardens at its northern edge. It is the neighborhood Miamians name when they say the city used to be gentler, and enough of it survives to see why.

The village core

The Grove's commercial heart is compact: Grand Avenue, Main Highway and McFarlane Road around the rebuilt CocoWalk, which returned in a new open air form with shops, restaurants and a cinema after its long renovation. Peacock Park, on the bay at the foot of McFarlane, is the village green, named for the Grove's first hotel family and still patrolled by the peacocks that are the neighborhood's unofficial mascots. The streets behind the core are the real Grove: cottages from the Bahamian settler era, limestone walls, and canopy so thick the temperature drops walking into it.

Dinner Key and the water

Dinner Key Marina, the city's big municipal marina, fills Dinner Key with masts; the historic Pan Am seaplane terminal at its heart is now Miami City Hall, one of the great repurposings in Florida. The bayfront parks run south from here through Kennedy Park, where the locals play volleyball and the food trucks gather, toward the Barnacle, below. Sailing is the Grove's sport: the bay off Dinner Key hosts regattas through the winter season, and the sailing clubs and charters here are the city's working fleet. Boat days on this water are in our Miami boat tours guide.

The Barnacle and Vizcaya: the two historic houses

The Barnacle Historic State Park preserves Ralph Munroe's 1891 bayfront home, the oldest house in Miami-Dade still on its original site, in a hardwood hammock that shows what this coast looked like before the city. At the Grove's northern edge, Vizcaya Museum and Gardens, James Deering's 1916 winter estate, is the grand version: an Italianate villa and formal gardens on the bay, and the single finest historic house visit in Miami. Both reward a slow morning; Vizcaya gets its own planning in our attractions section.

Staying, eating and getting there

The Grove's hotels sit on the bay and the main streets, quieter and greener than downtown or the beach, and popular with boat show and regatta crowds; the hotels hub covers the options. Eating runs from old Grove institutions to the new CocoWalk crop, strongest at brunch and golden hour. Metrorail stops at Coconut Grove station on US 1, a short walk or trolley hop from the core; the free Grove trolley circulates the neighborhood. By car it is 15 minutes south of downtown in normal traffic, and parking garages sit behind CocoWalk.

Peacock Park and the sailing life

The Grove's front porch is Peacock Park, the bayfront green where the village gathers, named for the family whose hotel stood here when this was a pioneer settlement with a post office before Miami existed. Offshore, the sailing clubs and the city marina keep the bay busy with dinghies and training fleets, and the view from the park across to the anchored boats is the Grove's postcard. The bohemian layer is thinner than it was, the artists and sailors priced steadily north and south, but the park at golden hour still explains why everyone who came here wanted to stay.

The facts that matter

Anchor facts: Coconut Grove is Miami's oldest continuously settled neighborhood, with Bahamian settlers central to its founding. The Barnacle, built 1891 by Ralph Munroe, is the oldest house in the county on its original site. Miami City Hall occupies the former Pan Am seaplane terminal at Dinner Key. Vizcaya, James Deering's estate, dates to 1916 and stands at the Grove's northern edge. Peacock Park is named for the Peacock family, early Grove hoteliers.

Frequently asked questions

What is Coconut Grove known for?
Age and shade: Miami's founding neighborhood, its sailing marina, its historic houses and its village pace.

Is the Grove a good base?
For travelers who want green and quiet over beach and scene, yes. It is 15 minutes from downtown and half an hour from the beach.

What should I not miss?
The Barnacle in the morning, Peacock Park and the marina at golden hour, and Vizcaya if the trip allows one big house visit.

Are the peacocks real?
Yes. Free roaming peacocks have been a Grove fixture for generations. Admire, photograph, and give them the road.
