Key Biscayne Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Key Biscayne is Miami's island escape, ten minutes from Brickell across the Rickenbacker Causeway and a world away in pace: two big beach parks, a lighthouse from 1825, a village of locals, and the best skyline views in the county from the causeway itself. Come for a beach day at Crandon Park or Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, climb the lighthouse, and eat in the village. One correction to old guides: the Miami Seaquarium on the way over is closed permanently as of October 2025, so do not plan around it.

Getting there: the causeway is part of the visit

The Rickenbacker Causeway, tolled, runs from Brickell across Virginia Key to Key Biscayne, and the bridge span gives the postcard skyline view that half of Miami's car commercials use. Cyclists own the causeway on weekend mornings; drivers should expect them. Virginia Key, the island in the middle, holds beaches and trails of its own, including the mountain bike trails and the historic Virginia Key Beach Park, the beach that served Miami's Black community in the segregation era and now operates as a historic park. Parking at the big parks is by the carload and fills on holiday weekends; arrive before mid morning then.

Crandon Park: the family beach

Crandon Park, on the island's north half, is the classic Miami family beach: a long, palm backed sand beach with shallow, calm water, picnic pavilions, a nature center, and the old zoo grounds' gardens at its southern end. It is the beach Miami families actually use, which makes it the right recommendation for travelers with small children and a cooler. Our Crandon Park guide covers access points, facilities and the nature side in detail.

Bill Baggs and the Cape Florida Lighthouse

At the island's southern tip, Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park holds the wilder beach and the lighthouse: built in 1825, rebuilt after a Seminole attack in the 1840s, and the oldest standing structure in Miami-Dade County. The park runs lighthouse tours on set days, the beach is consistently ranked among America's best by the annual Dr. Beach list, and the two casual restaurants inside the park serve the beach crowd. Rentals of bikes and beach gear run from the concessions. Check the state park's own page for tour days and hours before you build the day around the climb.

The village and the practical notes

The Village of Key Biscayne itself is residential and low key: a small commercial strip with cafes and the restaurants locals use, including the famous Rusty Pelican back on the causeway's mainland side for skyline view dining. Hobie Beach, on the causeway's south side at Virginia Key, is the windsurfing and paddleboard beach, with rentals and the skyline as backdrop. There are no big hotels on the island besides the Ritz-Carlton; most visitors day trip, which is the right model. Bring cash or a card for the toll and park entries, and remember the sun here is the full Florida article even in winter.

The wild side: mangroves, turtles and quiet water

For an island fifteen minutes from downtown, Key Biscayne keeps a genuinely wild edge. The mangrove fringes shelter herons and nursery fish, the bay side shallows are manatee water in the cooler months, and in nesting season, roughly spring through fall, sections of beach are roped for sea turtle nests and the island's lights dim for the hatchlings. None of it is a zoo; it is a working coastal ecosystem with a village attached. Early morning is the viewing hour, before the heat and the beach crowds, and the causeway toll is the admission price for the quietest near city escape Miami has.

The facts that matter

Status checked October 30, 2026: the Miami Seaquarium, on Virginia Key along this causeway, closed permanently in October 2025; do not plan around it. The Cape Florida Lighthouse dates to 1825 (rebuilt 1846) and is the oldest standing structure in Miami-Dade County. Crandon Park and Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park are the island's two beach parks; both charge per vehicle entry and can fill on holiday weekends.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get to Key Biscayne without a car?
Buses run from Brickell station across the causeway, and rideshares are easy. Cycling the causeway is the local favorite if you are equipped for it.

Which beach is better, Crandon or Bill Baggs?
Crandon for families, facilities and calm shallow water. Bill Baggs for the wilder beach and the lighthouse. Both in one day is entirely doable.

Is the lighthouse open to climb?
The park runs tours on set days and hours that change; check the Bill Baggs state park page before you go and arrive early on tour days.

What happened to the Seaquarium?
It closed permanently in October 2025 after the county terminated its lease. Older guides and map listings may still show it; it is not reopening as an aquarium.
