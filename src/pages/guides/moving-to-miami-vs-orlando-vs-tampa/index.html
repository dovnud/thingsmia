Moving to Miami vs Orlando vs Tampa: the honest comparison

Last reviewed: November 5, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. All three cities are drawing movers. Redfin's Q2 2026 report, the latest it has published, counts Orlando third in the country for people searching to move in (net inflow 5,742), Miami fourth (5,239) and Tampa seventh (4,260), with New York the top origin for all three. The cities split on price and on water. Miami costs the most and gives you the ocean. Tampa costs the least to buy into and gives you the bay. Orlando sits inland, priced between them, and trades the coast for the theme park economy. Pick by the trade-off you can live with, then verify it on a scouting trip.

Rent: one series, one month

Every rent figure in this cluster comes from Zumper's rent research pages, October 2026, so the three cities are measured the same way. A one-bedroom runs $2,500 a month in Miami, $1,400 in Orlando and $1,350 in Tampa. Two-bedrooms run $2,900, $1,700 and $1,800. Read that gap plainly: the same one-bedroom budget that rents a mid-range Miami apartment rents something larger or newer in the other two, or banks the difference of roughly $1,100 a month. That's $13,000 a year. It's the single biggest line in this comparison.

Buying: one source, one month

Median single-family sale prices for September 2026, all from Florida Realtors' monthly release: Miami metro (Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach) $659,000, Orlando metro $441,860, Tampa metro $400,000. Townhouse and condo medians run $330,000, $285,000 and $253,500 in the same order. The Miami metro figure covers three counties, so a Miami-Dade-only search will feel pricier still in the coastal neighborhoods most movers target. The order never flips: Miami is the expensive door in Florida's big three, by a wide margin, on both renting and buying.

Insurance: the gradient is real, and smaller than the rumors

Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation publishes the average homeowners premium actually charged, including wind cover. In its July 2026 stability report (policies in force March 31, 2026), Miami-Dade averages $5,975 a year, against $3,610 in Orange County (Orlando) and $3,525 in Hillsborough County (Tampa). Miami-Dade runs about 1.7 times the other two. You'll see claims online that coastal counties pay double or more; the regulator's own county figures say 1.7 times for these three, and this page prints the regulator's numbers. Budget the real figure. It's high enough without the exaggeration.

Pick by the life, not the spreadsheet

Beaches and open water. Miami first: real ocean beaches, a year round swim season, and boating as a daily option. Tampa second: the bay, the Gulf beaches a short drive west, and calmer water. Orlando has lakes and a theme park coastline of a different kind; the ocean is a day trip.

Families and theme parks. Orlando, and it isn't close. The parks are the local industry, the suburbs were built around family life, and the rents above leave room in the budget for both.

Remote work value. Tampa. The lowest buy-in of the three, a real downtown and waterfront, and an airport (TPA) that punches above its size. Movers working for coastal salaries keep the spread.

Culture, dining and an international airport. Miami. MIA's route map across Latin America and Europe is the deepest in the state, the dining scene is the deepest too, and Spanish is a daily working language. Orlando's MCO is the bigger theme park gateway; both dwarf most US airports, but they serve different worlds.

Every one of these frames has a counterweight. Miami's insurance and rent eat the no-state-income-tax saving faster than movers expect; Orlando's inland summers are the hottest of the three; Tampa's growth has pushed its prices up from the bargain they were five years ago. Frequently asked questions

Which city is cheapest overall?
Tampa on buying, roughly tied with Orlando on renting, per the figures above. Miami costs more on every housing line measured here.

Is Miami insurance really double Orlando's?
No. The regulator's county averages put Miami-Dade at about 1.7 times Orange County. High, and worth budgeting, but not double.

Which city are most movers choosing?
By Redfin's searcher counts for Q2 2026, Orlando edges Miami, with Tampa third of the three. All three sit in the national top ten.
