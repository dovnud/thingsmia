MIA airport lounges with day access: what a long layover actually buys

Last reviewed: November 9, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. At Miami International, some lounge doors sell a day pass and some never will. The Admirals Clubs in Concourse D sell a one day pass for $79, or 7,900 AAdvantage miles, if you're flying American or a oneworld airline that day and the club has room. The Centurion Lounge sells no pass at any price; an eligible American Express card is the only way in. Delta's own terms sell no general public day pass for the Sky Club either, whatever the airport's lounge page implies. And one lounge in Concourse J stays open around the clock, which settles the overnight question for most layovers. This page goes concourse by concourse, with prices only where the operator publishes them.

First, the layout rule that decides everything

Every public lounge at MIA sits past security, and MIA's concourses are not all connected on the airside. A lounge you can't reach is a lounge you don't have. The airport's lounge directory lists nothing at all for Concourses A, B, C, or G. Your realistic lounge is the one in your departure concourse, on a same day boarding pass that satisfies that lounge's own rules. Read your concourse below, not the whole list.

Concourse D: American's terminal

D holds the airport's deepest lounge bench. The Admirals Club at D15 (third floor, above the gate) runs 5:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m., and the Admirals Club at D30 runs 6:00 a.m. to 8:30 p.m. Both sell the $79 one day pass under the same terms: same day travel on American or a oneworld partner, bought online or at the club, always subject to capacity. The Flagship Lounge across from D30, open 5:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m., is the premium room; the airport directory lists a single visit Flagship Pass sold by capacity, and American's own pages published no price for it in this check, so no price is printed here. The Centurion Lounge near D12, open 5:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m., works on the card rule: Platinum, Business Platinum, Corporate Platinum, and Centurion card members enter with a same day boarding pass, within 3 hours of departure, or 5 hours on a layover. Guests cost $50, or $30 for children ages 2 to 17, unless your card has earned guest access. Amex's own note for MIA matters if you're connecting across terminals: the lounge sits on the D Skytrain, and arriving from another terminal means re-entering security with a connecting or onward boarding pass.

Concourses E, F, and H

In E, the Admirals Club is listed as temporarily closed, the British Airways Lounge in the E Satellite (1:00 p.m. to 10:30 p.m.) admits BA premium cabin passengers and oneworld Sapphire and Emerald members on transatlantic flights with no day pass sold, and the Turkish Airlines Lounge (5:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.) is listed by the airport as selling a one day pass to any passenger with a same day boarding pass, with Priority Pass accepted. No day pass price for the Turkish lounges could be confirmed on the operator's pages in this check, so none is printed. Concourse F's Club America is listed as temporarily closed; don't plan around it. In H, the Delta Sky Club (4:15 a.m. to 9:30 p.m.) needs the correction at the top of this page in full: the airport directory says a single visit pass is available, but Delta's current access terms list no such product for the general public. Access runs through Sky Club membership, premium cabins and status, and eligible American Express cards, whose holders get a set number of visits a year and then pay $50 per extra visit, with guests at $50. The Turkish Airlines Lounge in the H and J connector area runs 4:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. on the same day pass and Priority Pass pattern as its E sibling.

Concourse J and the overnight answer

J holds the two lounges that matter most to a long international layover. The VIP Lounge run by LATAM sells access at its reception desk only: from $70 for adults and $45 for children age 2 to under 12, taxes included, for a maximum stay of 3 hours, subject to capacity, on LATAM's own page. Older guides quote $60 and $25. Those figures are stale; LATAM's page is the source and it says $70 and $45. The lounge is under renovation for about 11 months from October 6, 2026 and stays open through the work. Its published hours conflict between the airport directory and the airline's own page, so check the day you fly rather than trusting either print. The Avianca and TAP Portugal Lounge, across from the J gates, is listed by the airport as selling one day passes subject to capacity with Priority Pass accepted, price not published on the operator pages checked, and it is the only lounge at MIA listed open 24 hours. Every other room on this page closes overnight. On a true overnight layover, that leaves you three real options: the J lounge if your ticket puts you in J, the terminal itself, or a bed. The luggage storage guide covers the bag side of leaving the airport, and the airport hotel block below covers the bed.
