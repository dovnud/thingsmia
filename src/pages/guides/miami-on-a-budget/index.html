Miami on a Budget Guide

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami gets affordable when you build the trip on its free layers: the Metromover and the Miami Beach trolley cost nothing, the beaches are public, Wynwood's street murals are an outdoor gallery you view from the sidewalk, and transit fares cap at $5.65 a day. Spend your money on one or two paid highlights, compare hotels by the total price including resort fees, and let the free city carry the rest of the trip.

Where is Miami genuinely free?

Start with the beach itself: Miami's beaches are public, so the sand and the water cost nothing; you only pay if you rent chairs and umbrellas. The Metromover loops downtown and Brickell free of charge, and the City of Miami Beach trolley runs the island free from 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. Wynwood's wider streets are covered in murals you can wander for hours at no cost; note that the Wynwood Walls complex itself is a ticketed attraction, so budget for it separately if it is on your list. The Art Deco District in Miami Beach is likewise an outdoor museum: the architecture is on public streets, and a self guided walk costs nothing but time.

How do you keep transport costs down?

Ride transit and let fare capping work. Metrorail and Metrobus cost $2.25 a ride, and when you pay with the same EASY Card or contactless card, your charges cap at $5.65 for a day of unlimited rides (Miami-Dade Transit, checked October 26, 2026). The airport run is the emblematic save: the free MIA Mover plus a $2.25 Metrorail ride replaces a cross town taxi fare. Save rideshare for late nights and spread out sights, and skip the rental car entirely if your trip stays in the core, because parking is where a cheap trip goes to die. Our Getting Around Miami guide has the full fare table.

How do you keep hotel costs down?

Compare totals, never headline rates. Many Miami hotels add a mandatory daily resort or destination fee on top of the room price, and the honest comparison is rate plus fees plus taxes. Since May 12, 2025, the FTC's Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees has required advertised lodging prices to show that total prominently, which makes the comparison easier than it used to be; our Resort Fees Explained guide shows exactly where to look. Season helps more than any coupon: the same room costs least in the late summer and fall weeks covered in the Best Time to Visit guide, and mainland areas like downtown, Brickell, and the airport corridor usually undercut beachfront addresses.

Where will Miami still cost you?

Honesty section: beach chair and umbrella rentals, beach area parking, boat tours, and nightlife are priced for a resort city, and no guide can budget those away. The budget move is selection, not denial: pick one paid boat day or one big night out, build the rest of the trip on the free layers, and eat like a local in neighborhoods like Little Havana, where the famous food is also some of the city's best value.

The facts that matter

Free in Miami, verified: the Metromover (Miami-Dade Transit), the City of Miami Beach trolley, 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. daily (City of Miami Beach), and the MIA Mover at the airport. Transit value, checked October 26, 2026: Metrorail and Metrobus $2.25 a ride, daily fare capping $5.65 with the same payment card, 7-Day Pass $29.25 (Miami-Dade Transit). Lodging prices advertised since May 12, 2025 must show the total price including mandatory fees (FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees).

Frequently asked questions

What is a realistic cheap day in Miami?
A beach morning with your own towel, a free trolley ride, a Metromover loop downtown, and a neighborhood lunch can cost little more than your food and a capped transit day. Add one paid attraction and the day is still modest by resort city standards.

Is South Beach doable on a budget?
Yes, if you sleep slightly north or on the mainland, use the free trolley, and treat the beach itself as the entertainment. South Beach prices bite hardest in restaurants, rentals, and nightlife, all of which are choices, not entry fees.

Which months are cheapest?
Generally the late summer and early fall hurricane season weeks, when demand drops with the heat and storm risk rising. Book refundable rates in those months, as the hurricane season planning guide recommends.
