Accessible Miami Travel Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami Beach runs one of the most useful accessibility programmes in American beach tourism: city provided beach wheelchairs that roll on sand, first come first served with a photo ID, at Ocean Rescue Headquarters and South Pointe Park, plus beach access mats at entrances from South Pointe to 79th Street. Around that centerpiece, this guide gathers the accessible transit, venue, and rental facts a mobility impaired visitor actually needs.

The beach itself: wheelchairs, mats, and Adaptive Beach Days

The City of Miami Beach's Special Abilities and Inclusion programme offers beach wheelchairs to access the sand for people who are mobility impaired. The terms are simple: first come, first served, with a driver's license or photo ID required, from two locations, Ocean Rescue Headquarters at 1001 Ocean Drive and South Pointe Park at 1 Washington Avenue, with the programme's information line at 305-673-7000 extension 7714. These chairs are the difference between watching the beach and being on it; they are built for sand in a way a standard wheelchair is not.

The city also maintains beach access mats at entrances running from South Pointe Park through 79th Street, including 10th, 12th, Lincoln Road, 17th, 21st, 44th, and 64th Streets, which firm up the crossing from the boardwalk to the sand. And the city's calendar includes Adaptive Beach Days, a pop up programme run with the Sabrina Cohen Foundation providing full service ocean access with trained staff, volunteers, access decks, and beach wheelchairs, running April through November 2026 on the third Sunday of the month; if your trip can bend to one of those Sundays, it is the best supported ocean swim in the city.

Getting around: county transit and the free Metromover

Miami-Dade Transit builds accessibility into the whole system: accommodations for people with disabilities across Metrobus, Metrorail, and Metromover include elevators, platform tactile tiles, braille and raised signage, wheelchair turnstiles, and public address announcements, and the county runs STS, its door to door ADA paratransit service, for eligible riders. The Metromover, downtown and Brickell's free loop, removes the fare step entirely and is the easiest urban transit in the city for a wheelchair user spending a downtown day.

In Miami Beach, the free trolley network covers the island, and the convention center publishes its own accessibility setup: accessible parking on the rooftop of its garage, sidewalk ramps, accessible restrooms and elevators, and a ramp at the Northeast (Washington Avenue) entrance closest to the Grand Ballroom. If your visit is built around an event there, our convention center guide has the venue geography.

Attractions and equipment

Zoo Miami's own guest guidance sets the pattern for the big attractions: service animals are permitted (emotional support animals are not service animals), and wheelchairs and ECVs, electric convenience vehicles, are available to rent for the day, with the zoo recommending reserving ahead, especially on weekends and holidays. That reserve ahead advice generalizes: Miami's major attractions publish accessibility pages, and a five minute read of the specific venue's page before your visit beats discovering its system at the gate.

For equipment across the whole trip, powered scooter rental in Miami runs on delivery: scooters arrive charged at your hotel, home rental, or cruise ship, which is why our mobility scooter rental guide is the companion read. Remember the sand rule from that guide: rental scooters do not go on the beach, which is exactly the gap the city's beach wheelchairs exist to fill.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get on the sand in a wheelchair in Miami Beach?
Use the city's beach wheelchairs: first come, first served with photo ID, at Ocean Rescue Headquarters (1001 Ocean Drive) and South Pointe Park (1 Washington Ave). Beach access mats at many entrances firm up the route to the sand.

Are the beach wheelchairs free?
The city presents the programme as a city provided service; its programme page lists locations and the ID requirement without a stated charge. Confirm current terms on the city's programme page or at 305-673-7000 x7714 before your visit.

Is Miami public transit accessible?
Yes. Miami-Dade provides accessibility accommodations across Metrobus, Metrorail, and Metromover, including elevators, tactile tiles, and wheelchair turnstiles, plus STS door to door ADA paratransit. The Metromover is free for everyone.

Can I rent a wheelchair at Zoo Miami?
Yes, the zoo rents wheelchairs and ECVs for the day and recommends reserving ahead, especially on weekends and holidays. Service animals are permitted.
