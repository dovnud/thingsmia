Rent a boat, book a captained charter, or take a boat tour: the Miami verdict

Last reviewed: November 8, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Three questions settle it. Who drives? How many people? What is the day actually for? If you want to drive and you were born on or after January 1, 1988, you need Florida's boating safety ID card before any rental dock can legally hand you the keys, and that one rule sends most visitors to a captained charter instead. If your group is seven or more, an uninspected charter boat cannot take you all, and the choice narrows to a bigger inspected vessel or a tour. If nobody wants responsibility for the boat at all, the tour wins and it is not close. The sections below work each branch with the rules attached.

The credential rule that decides branch one

Florida requires anyone born on or after January 1, 1988 who operates a vessel of 10 horsepower or more to complete an approved boating safety course and carry the Florida Boating Safety Education ID Card, per the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission. The card does not expire, and FWC is plain that it is not a boating license. A NASBLA-approved course from another state, a US territory, or Canada also satisfies the rule, and visitors without one can use FWC's temporary certificate examination path. The step-by-step for that exam, including the 90 day window in the current statute, lives in our Florida boater card guide. Sort the credential before you shop for boats. A rental booked for Saturday cannot be fixed at the dock on Saturday morning.

Rent it yourself: the duty, and the risk, are yours

A bareboat rental means you are the operator in law and in fact. Florida rental companies, called liveries, must give pre-rental and pre-ride instruction to everyone who rents the vessel and to every potential operator, and there is a list of situations where a livery is flatly prohibited from renting at all: no livery permit, more passengers than the capacity plate allows, required safety equipment missing, an unseaworthy vessel, no written rental agreement, instruction not given, or a renter under 18 on a powered vessel. Read that list as your own checklist at pickup. If the dock skips the instruction or the equipment looks thin, walk away, because the citation and the liability on the water will have your name on them, not the dock's.

One definition changed recently and it draws the cleanest line in this whole comparison. Since July 1, 2025, a Florida livery by definition does not provide a US Coast Guard licensed captain. So if the offer includes a captain, it is not a rental, whatever the listing calls it. It is a charter, with charter rules. That distinction decides who is responsible when something goes wrong, which is exactly when the label stops being marketing.

Captained charter: you buy the captain's license and local knowledge

On a captained charter the licensing, the navigation, and the legal duty sit with the captain, and your group just picks the day: sandbar, skyline cruise, fishing, or a slow bay loop. The size rule is the one to learn. A captain running an uninspected passenger vessel, the familiar six-pack arrangement, can carry six or fewer passengers for hire under the Coast Guard's OUPV endorsement. Seven friends cannot legally squeeze onto a six-pack boat, and a captain who waves that off is telling you something about the operation. Groups of seven to twelve need a larger inspected vessel, and groups beyond that are tour boat territory. Our fishing charter comparison works the same split for anglers, and the yacht charter page covers the crewed end of the market.

Boat tour: the per person product with no homework

A scheduled tour sells you a seat, a route, and a departure time. No credential, no fuel stop, no docking in a crosswind while your friends watch. The trade is control: the boat goes where the route goes, for as long as the schedule says. For a first Miami day, a visitor group with mixed ages, or anyone whose real goal is the skyline and the bay rather than the helm, the tour is the honest answer, and the per person price is usually the lowest of the three for small groups. Miami's tour shelf is deep, from Millionaire's Row cruises to sunset sailings, and our Miami boat tours page sorts it. Money and weather: read the inclusions, not the headline price

The three models bundle costs differently, and no honest page can print one going rate for any of them. Rentals separate the boat from fuel and deposits. Charters may bundle the captain and fuel while leaving gratuity and catering apart. Tours fold nearly everything into the seat price. The check is the same on every listing: what is included, what is charged at the dock, what the weather cancellation terms say, and who decides when a day is called off. Lightning and small craft conditions cancel Miami boating days every summer, so the cancellation line matters as much as the price line. Whichever branch you pick, pack the same kit: a dry bag for phones, reef-safe sunscreen, and a hat that stays on at speed.

The verdict, by traveler

You hold the card and want the helm: rent, and run the livery checklist at pickup. A group of two to six celebrating something: captained charter, and let the captain work. Seven or more: an inspected charter vessel or a tour, never an overloaded six-pack. First day in Miami, kids in the group, nobody volunteering to drive: the tour, booked for the morning in summer, when the water is calmest and the storms have not built yet. Nobody in the group can legally drive and the date is this weekend: charter or tour today, and the boater card course before the next trip.

The facts that matter

Boating facts checked November 8, 2026: the FWC credential rule covers operators born on or after January 1, 1988 on vessels of 10 horsepower or more; the ID card does not expire; NASBLA-approved out-of-state courses count. Liveries must instruct every renter and potential operator and are prohibited from renting in the listed safety cases (FWC, Rule 68D-34.002). Since July 1, 2025 a Florida livery does not provide a USCG-licensed captain by definition (FWC). Uninspected passenger vessels under the OUPV endorsement carry six or fewer passengers for hire (US Coast Guard National Maritime Center).

Frequently asked questions

Can the rental company just add a captain if we ask?
Then it is operating as a charter, not a rental, under Florida's current definitions. Book it as a charter, with the captain's credential and the passenger cap that come with that product.

Does the boater card rule apply to a jet ski?
Yes, a personal watercraft is a vessel well over 10 horsepower. Our jet ski tours page covers the guided route, which is how most visitors ride without holding the card.

What if weather cancels our day?
Each model answers that in its own terms. Read the cancellation line before paying, and in summer pick morning departures, which lose the fewest days to afternoon storms.
