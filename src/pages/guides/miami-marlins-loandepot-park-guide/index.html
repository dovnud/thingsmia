Marlins Game Guide: loanDepot Park

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. loanDepot park sits at 501 Marlins Way in Little Havana, on the site of the old Miami Orange Bowl, about two miles west of downtown. It is the most weather proof sports venue in Florida: a retractable roof that closes in about 15 minutes and glass outfield walls that frame the downtown skyline. For visitors it is also the easiest big game to combine with a neighborhood, because Calle Ocho is right outside.

The building: small, roofed, and built for Miami weather

Opened on March 5, 2012, as Marlins Park and renamed loanDepot park in 2021, the ballpark holds 36,742 for baseball, 37,442 with standing room, which makes it one of the smaller parks in the majors and one of the easiest to navigate. The 8,300 ton retractable roof exists for exactly the Miami problem set: summer heat, humidity, and late afternoon storms. It opens or closes in roughly 15 minutes, and the outfield wall's retractable glass panels keep the downtown view in play when the weather allows.

The practical effect for visitors is certainty. A Marlins game is the one outdoor plan in a Miami summer itinerary that rain almost never cancels, which makes it the perfect anchor for the day a tropical forecast scrambles your beach plans. Tickets and seats

Baseball's long season makes the Marlins the best value ticket in Miami sports. Weeknight games against non marquee opponents are routinely the cheapest seats in the building's short history, the upper levels give a full field view at the lowest prices, and the park's small footprint means few truly bad seats. The bag policy allows more than the arenas do: non clear bags are permitted up to 16 by 16 by 8 inches, with small clutches under 6 by 8 inches and medical and infant bags excepted from the limit.

Buy through the team's official channels at mlb.com/marlins, and if you are choosing one game on a trip, a Friday or weekend game adds the neighborhood energy on Calle Ocho before and after, while a weekday afternoon game is the quiet connoisseur's pick.

Parking and getting there

The ballpark wraps itself in its own parking: four garages on the north and south ends and six surface lots on the east and west sides, with attendants running free flow parking on arrival. Prepaid parking is sold through the team; otherwise you register your plate and pay through PayByPhone after you park. Secondary reporting puts typical parking prices in the $15 to $30 range depending on the game, and gates generally open about 90 minutes before first pitch; treat both as patterns to confirm on the team's game day page rather than fixed numbers.

Little Havana is also one of the better neighborhoods to arrive in by rideshare, and the pregame move locals know is to eat on Calle Ocho first, then walk to the park. Versailles and the ventanita culture of the neighborhood are a short distance away, and our Little Havana food tour guide maps the food side of the same streets.

The game day shape

Arrive hungry and walk the park once before your inning one hot dog decision. The food program leans into Miami flavors more than most ballparks, the local craft beer and Cuban sandwich options are the point, and the building's art and the skyline view through the outfield glass are part of the ticket. Families should know the park runs a roof closed, air conditioned comfort for most summer games, which makes it the rare Florida stadium where a July game with kids is a relaxing plan rather than an endurance test.

Frequently asked questions

Where is loanDepot park?
At 501 Marlins Way in Little Havana, about two miles west of downtown Miami, on the site of the old Miami Orange Bowl.

What happens if it rains during a Marlins game?
The retractable roof closes in about 15 minutes, so games are rarely rained out. It is the most weather safe ticket in Miami sports.

How much is parking at the Marlins game?
The park has four garages and six lots. Prepaid parking is sold by the team, or you register your plate and pay with PayByPhone. Prices vary by game; secondary reporting puts typical games in the $15 to $30 range.

What can I bring into the ballpark?
Bags up to 16 by 16 by 8 inches are allowed, larger than most Miami venues permit, with small clutches and medical and infant bags excepted.
