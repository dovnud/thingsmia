Chicago to Miami weekend: the winter escape and its summer version

Last reviewed: November 9, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Chicago to Miami is about three hours in the air, and you've got two distinct trips on the route. The winter version is an escape: nonstop flights, an event weekend as the anchor, and the Everglades at their driest. The summer version is a value run: the same flights, lower hotel rates under the city's summer programs, and days planned around afternoon storms. Pick your version below, then pick your airport. This page compares time and logistics only. It prints no fares, because fares move daily and a printed fare is a wrong fare by Friday.

The flights, checked November 9, 2026

The nonstop pairs you can book today, from the airlines' own route pages: Southwest flies Midway to MIA once daily with an average flight time of 3 hours, and Midway to Fort Lauderdale two or three times daily at about 2 hours 53 minutes. JetBlue started O'Hare to Fort Lauderdale nonstop on July 9, 2026, twice daily. American sells O'Hare to MIA and O'Hare to Fort Lauderdale through the winter as well; check whether your date prices as a nonstop before you pay, because American's route pages don't promise it on every date. Two names you won't find on this list: Spirit, whose Chicago to Florida flying ended with the airline's shutdown, and Frontier on O'Hare to MIA, which launched in February 2026 and then came and went in secondary reporting. If a search result shows either as a sure thing for January, verify it on the airline's own site before you build a weekend on it.

Three hours changes the math. A Friday flight out of Chicago puts you on Miami sand the same afternoon, and a Sunday evening return still gives you two full beach days. That's the whole appeal.

Which arrival airport: MIA or FLL

MIA sits about 9 miles west of Miami Beach, the airport authority's own figure; the road run to South Beach is about 11 miles. From the terminal, the free MIA Mover takes you to the Miami Intermodal Center, where the Metrorail Orange Line runs downtown in about 25 minutes and Tri-Rail heads north. Fort Lauderdale's airport is the low cost gateway about 30 miles north of South Beach. Plan on about an hour to South Beach in light traffic and well over that at rush hour. There's no Brightline station at either airport; Brightline's stations are downtown at MiamiCentral and in downtown Fort Lauderdale, so a rail leg means a transfer at one end or the other. The full rail comparison is in the Brightline, Tri-Rail or drive guide. Rule of thumb: if you're staying in Miami Beach or downtown, MIA is the simpler arrival. If the fare gap is wide or you're splitting the trip with Fort Lauderdale, FLL earns its drive.

The winter version: January and February

Winter is dry season in the Everglades, December through April on the National Park Service's own season pages, which makes a Chicago January trip the one time of year the airboat day runs cool, bright, and low on mosquitoes. Build the weekend on an anchor. The Life Time Miami Marathon runs Sunday, January 31, 2027, finishing at Bayfront Park; the Marathon visitor guide and the where to stay guide cover that weekend. The Discover Boating Miami International Boat Show runs February 10 to 14, 2027 across four venues; start with the Boat Show stay guide. On a non event weekend, the anchor is simpler: one Everglades morning, one beach day, one good dinner. Book the airboat slot before you fly, because dry season boats fill.

The summer version: June through September

Summer flips the deal structure. Miami Hotel Months runs June 1 to September 30 and Miami Spa Months runs July 1 to August 31, both Greater Miami Convention and Visitors Bureau programs with those standing windows; the 2027 hotel and spa lists publish in spring 2027, so book the rate structure now and check the participant list before you commit to a specific property. Days run on the storm clock: beach and boat in the morning, spa or museum in the afternoon, dinner late. You'll trade January's dry air for empty tables and the lowest hotel rates of the year. For a lot of Chicagoans, that's the right trade.

The return flight is the one to protect

Outbound timing is flexible in a way the return is not. A January or February Sunday return out of South Florida, on a Marathon or Boat Show weekend, from an airport running its peak winter schedule, is where this trip breaks. Build the return first: a midday departure beats an early one after a full Miami weekend, and the airport math from the top of this page runs in reverse. At MIA, the free MIA Mover and the roughly 25 minute Metrorail ride make a downtown or Brickell last night workable. At FLL, the longer road transfer to Miami Beach argues for a later flight than you think you need. Land back in Chicago with the trip intact. That's the whole game.
