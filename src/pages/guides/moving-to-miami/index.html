Moving to Miami: start here

Last reviewed: November 4, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami-Dade County holds about 2.8 million people on the Census Bureau's July 2025 estimate, and 54.5% of them were born outside the United States. The moving picture has changed shape: Florida's net migration from other states cooled to 22,517 people in the year to July 2025, down from 310,892 in 2022, while arrivals from abroad carried the growth. People still come. They just come from farther away, and the ones who plan win. This hub runs a Miami move in the order it actually happens: one deadline to protect, the money math, the scouting trip, the neighborhood, the checklist, and the first month.

The one date to plan around: December 31, 2026

On November 3, 2026, Florida voters approved Amendment 3, which raises the homestead exemption against non-school property taxes. The schedule splits on a single date. Hold Florida permanent residence through December 31, 2026 and the exemption starts at up to $150,000 in 2027 and up to $250,000 in 2028. Become a resident on or after January 1, 2027 and it starts at up to $50,000. On a worked City of Miami example, the gap is worth about $9,400 across four tax years. Renting counts, as long as the residency is genuine: you don't need a closing by New Year's Eve, you need to actually live here. The full rule, the math, and what counts as residency are in our Amendment 3 guide. If your move can land either side of that line, land it early.

Stage two: the scouting trip

Come for a week if you can swing it. Sleep in the neighborhood you're weighing, not across town from it. Do the morning commute at the hour you'd really do it. Grocery shop. Sit in the traffic you would sit in. A scouting week costs a few hundred dollars in hotel nights and can save a year of the wrong lease.

Stage four: the first month

Florida's deadlines start the day your residency does. Vehicles get titled and registered within 10 days, with Florida insurance in place first. Your driver license follows within 30 days. If you bought a home, the homestead exemption needs you living in it on January 1 and form DR-501 filed by March 1. The checklist page carries the fees and the full list.

Frequently asked questions

When's the best time to move to Miami?
This winter, if you can. Being a Florida permanent resident by December 31, 2026 puts you on the larger Amendment 3 exemption schedule, worth about $9,400 over four years on the worked example. Beyond the tax date, winter arrivals dodge the worst of the summer heat and land outside hurricane season's peak weeks.

Should I rent first or buy first?
Renting first protects the December residency date without a rushed purchase, and the Amendment 3 text covers residents who will establish a homestead later. A year of renting also teaches you the neighborhoods with your own commute, school run, and grocery store, which no listing can.

Do I need a car in Miami?
Most households do. Metrorail links the airport, downtown, and the southern suburbs, and the Metromover loops downtown and Brickell for free, but the city is spread out and plenty of daily life sits off the rail lines. Price a car, and its insurance, into the move.

How long should a scouting trip be?
A week, if the budget allows it, based in the neighborhood you'd actually choose. Shorter trips still help; a long weekend in the right area beats a week based somewhere you'd never live.

What's the first thing to do after I arrive?
Start the clocks consciously: Florida insurance on the car, then title and registration inside 10 days, license inside 30 days, and your homestead dates on the calendar. The checklist page sequences all of it.
