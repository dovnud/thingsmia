What it costs to move to Miami: movers, containers and a rental truck

Last reviewed: November 6, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. A household move from New York to Miami runs about 1,280 miles, and you have three real ways to cover it: hire a full service mover, load a portable container, or rent a truck and drive it yourself. PODS publishes the only route priced example in this comparison: $2,587 to $4,569 for a 2 to 3 bedroom home, New York City to Orlando, including transport and one month of storage, checked on pods.com in November 2026. Orlando is the nearest route PODS publishes, so treat it as a proxy for Miami, not a Miami quote. United Van Lines, in its own long distance cost guide, says most rough estimates for a 2 to 3 bedroom home run over $8,000 at the low end and over $10,000 at the high end. Those two figures describe different products measured different ways. This page keeps them separate and labeled, because blending them would produce a number nobody actually charges.

Method one: a full service mover

The mover loads, drives and unloads. You pack, or you pay extra and they pack too. United Van Lines' guide figure above is a national long distance guide number, not a route price, and United publishes no fixed route list. Use it as the top of the market frame: a full service move for a family home is a five figure decision at the high end, and any quote far below the guide range deserves a harder look at the company behind it, not a faster signature.

What drives the quote is weight, distance and timing. Winter is the busy season for Florida bound moves, and a quote is only real when it's written, signed and tied to a survey of your actual goods. The federal rules in the protection section below exist because the gap between a phone estimate and a delivery day demand is where movers have hurt people for years. Get the estimate in writing. Then check the company.

Method two: a portable container

The company drops a container, you load it on your schedule, they drive it and store it if your Miami home isn't ready. PODS sells three sizes: an 8 foot container for up to about 500 square feet of goods, a 12 foot for about 500 to 800 square feet, and a 16 foot for up to about 1,200 square feet. One rule catches people out: the 12 foot container is for local moves and storage only. It does not travel long distance, so a cross country plan is built on the 8 foot and 16 foot sizes.

The PODS route example, from the company's own long distance moving page, checked November 2026: New York City to Orlando, 2 to 3 bedroom household, $2,587 to $4,569 for the container route, including transport and one month of storage and excluding insurance and taxes. The same table puts a full service mover on that route at $2,746 to $6,333 and a rental truck at $1,436 to $2,838 before fuel. Loading labor is on you with a container. Budget a day of hired help at each end if your furniture is heavy, and price that help locally rather than guessing a rate here.

Boxes, tape and packing supplies on Amazon are the one part of a container move you fully control. Order them before the container lands, not after.

Method three: rent the truck and drive

The rental truck is the cheapest line in the PODS table and the most expensive in your time. The math has four parts: the truck rental itself, fuel for about 1,280 miles, tolls on the way down, and loading help at one or both ends. Add a hotel night. At 1,280 miles this is a two day drive for almost everyone, and a loaded truck at highway speed is not the day to test your limits. The saving is real. So is the work. Price it honestly, with your own back in the ledger.

The Miami side charges nobody quotes

Three charges wait at the Florida end, and none of them appear in a mover's headline price. First, building access: many Miami condo and apartment buildings require an elevator reservation and a loading dock booking for move in day, and some charge a move in fee set by the association. The amount is the building's, so get it in writing from the association before the truck is loaded, not from the curb. Second, storage: if the home isn't ready, a container sits in storage at a monthly rate and a full service mover charges for storage in transit. The PODS example includes one month, which tells you how common the gap is. Third, timing: if your lease starts mid month and the truck comes early, a few nights of hotel or a short stay can cost less than a rushed delivery window. The federal protection checks, before you sign

Interstate movers and brokers must register with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration and carry a USDOT number and proper insurance. Run any company through the Protect Your Move search tool at fmcsa.dot.gov before you pay a deposit. By law the mover must give you the booklet Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move and the Ready to Move brochure, and must give you a written, signed estimate. There are no oral estimates in this industry, whatever a salesperson says on the phone.

One delivery day rule matters most. At delivery, you cannot be required to pay more than 100 percent of a binding estimate, or 110 percent of a non binding estimate, with the balance billed 30 days after delivery. A driver demanding the full balance above those lines before unloading is breaking the rule the booklet describes. Complaints and registration checks run through the FMCSA Complaint Center, formerly the National Consumer Complaint Database, at 1-888-368-7238. Delivery dates themselves live in your bill of lading: the mover must honor the agreed pickup and delivery dates in that document or give proper notice. Read those dates before you sign, because a range in the bill of lading is the promise you actually bought.

The local leg has its own regulator. A move inside Florida is an intrastate move under chapter 507 of the Florida Statutes, and the mover must register with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. Registration runs $300 a year, $600 per two year registration, and an unregistered mover can face a cease and desist order and fines up to $5,000. If a Miami local mover handles your final leg, check that registration too.

The facts that matter

Moving cost facts checked November 6, 2026: New York to Miami is about 1,280 miles by road. PODS publishes New York City to Orlando at $2,587 to $4,569 for a 2 to 3 bedroom household on the container route, including transport and one month of storage, excluding insurance and taxes, with full service at $2,746 to $6,333 and a rental truck at $1,436 to $2,838 before fuel on the same route table (pods.com, August 2026 figures). United Van Lines' cost guide puts most 2 to 3 bedroom long distance estimates over $8,000 low and over $10,000 high as national guide figures (unitedvanlines.com). PODS container sizes are 8, 12 and 16 foot; the 12 foot is local moves and storage only. Interstate movers must register with FMCSA, provide the rights booklet and a written signed estimate, and cannot demand more than 100 percent of a binding estimate or 110 percent of a non binding estimate at delivery (fmcsa.dot.gov). Florida intrastate movers register with FDACS under chapter 507, Florida Statutes.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there no exact Miami price on this page?
Because no mover publishes one. Route prices come from a survey of your goods. The published figures here are labeled by company and route so you can see which kind of number you're holding.

Which method suits a one bedroom?
Usually the container or the truck. A one bedroom fits the smaller container sizes, and the full service premium is hardest to justify at low weight. Run the truck math with fuel before you decide.

What if my Miami home isn't ready when the truck is?
Storage is the standard answer, priced by the month with containers and by the shipment with full service movers. The sight unseen renting routine covers the gap housing side of the same problem.

What happens to my car?
That's a separate decision with its own math. Our car shipping guide compares driving it, shipping it and selling it first.
