Art Basel Traffic and Parking Guide

Last reviewed: October 28, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. If you drive to Art Basel Miami Beach, use the Miami Beach Convention Center's own garage first: 800 spaces on the 4th and 5th levels of the center at 1901 Convention Center Drive, a $20 flat fee, open 6 a.m. to 1 a.m. When it fills, the city garages around 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue are the fallback, at about $2 an hour up to a $20 daily maximum at most of them. Better still, park once anywhere on the island and use the free Miami Beach trolley, because Art Week traffic on the causeways and around the convention center is the worst of the Miami year.

Where exactly is the fair, and what does that do to traffic?

The Miami Beach Convention Center stands at 1901 Convention Center Drive, in the blocks between 17th and 19th Streets, Washington Avenue, and Meridian Avenue. That is also the Lincoln Road district, the busiest pedestrian area on the island, so during Art Basel the neighborhood absorbs fair crowds, Design Miami next door on Convention Center Drive, Collins Park programming across Washington Avenue, and ordinary December beach traffic at the same time. The public days for 2026 are Friday December 4 to Sunday December 6, and the pattern every year is the same: mornings are manageable, the streets lock up from late morning, and the evening opening circuit slows the whole district again.

What are the parking options, in the order we would use them?

1. The convention center garage. The MBCC's own garage holds 800 spaces on its 4th and 5th level decks, charges a $20 flat fee, and is open from 6 a.m. to 1 a.m., seven days a week. Enter from 1901 Convention Center Drive on the west side or 2000 Washington Avenue on the east side. Electric vehicle charging sits on the 5th floor and the accessible spaces are on the rooftop deck. Arrive before the fair opens on public days; by midday this garage is the first to fill. Valet is offered at the main entrance off Convention Center Drive, at a $35 standard event fee when checked.

2. The 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue city garages. The convention center's own directions point drivers to the municipal garages ringing the district: the 17th Street garages at 581 17th Street, 640 17th Street, and 1735 Meridian Avenue, the Pennsylvania Avenue garage at 1661 Pennsylvania Avenue, the Fillmore North garage at 1662 Meridian Avenue, and the 23rd Street garage at 1909 Meridian Avenue, all within a short walk of a fair entrance. City garage pricing at most South Beach garages runs about $2 for the first hour, rising by the hour to a $20 maximum per 24 hours, and the city switches some garages to event flat rates on the biggest days, so read the rate board at the entrance rather than trusting any number, including ours. Rates on this page were checked October 28, 2026.

3. The 13th Street garage and street meters. The garage at 1550 Collins Avenue works if you are pairing the fair with the beach. On street meters are the last resort: they are the most expensive layer per hour in the entertainment district, time limited, and the city pays close attention during event weeks. Pay through the ParkMobile or PayByPhone apps, and double check the plate you enter.

4. Your hotel. If you are staying on the island, the honest answer is to leave the car with the hotel. Hotel valet in Miami Beach is expensive enough on a normal week, and during Art Week the car earns you nothing but causeway time. Walk, trolley, or rideshare from a beach hotel instead.

How do the causeways behave during Art Week?

Three causeways feed the convention center district: the MacArthur Causeway (I-395) from downtown, the Julia Tuttle Causeway (I-195) from midtown and the airport side, and the Venetian Causeway, the low, local link between the Venetian Islands and the beach. The MacArthur carries the heaviest load and backs up first, especially eastbound in the morning and westbound after the evening events. The Venetian is the local pressure valve but it is a two lane drawbridge road; one bridge opening or one stalled car and it stops. If you are coming from the mainland for one fair day, cross early, park on the island once, and stay on foot.

What are the no driving options?

The Miami Beach trolley is free, runs from 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. daily at roughly 20 minute intervals, and loops the island's neighborhoods, including the convention center and Lincoln Road area; it is the single best Art Week transport deal in the city. Metrobus crosses the causeways all day, including the Route 150 Miami Beach Airport Express between Miami Airport Station and Miami Beach at the standard $2.25 fare, which makes a mainland park and ride pattern workable. Rideshare is plentiful but surge pricing during fair peaks is severe, and pickup zones around the convention center crawl; walking five minutes from the hall before ordering a car saves both money and time. Art Basel and the satellite fairs publish their own shuttle and visitor transport arrangements in the run up to the fair each year; check the visitor pages at artbasel.com in late November before you rely on any shuttle route.

Frequently asked questions

How much is parking at the convention center?
The MBCC garage charges a $20 flat fee, checked October 28, 2026, and is open 6 a.m. to 1 a.m. Nearby city garages charge hourly rates up to about a $20 daily maximum, with event flat rates possible on peak days.

When does the convention center garage fill up?
On public fair days it is the closest and cheapest structured option, so plan as if it fills by late morning. Arriving before the doors open is the reliable strategy; the 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue garages are the fallback.

Can I take transit from the airport area?
Yes. Metrobus Route 150, the Miami Beach Airport Express, runs between Miami Airport Station and Miami Beach, and our getting around Miami guide covers the fares and the rest of the transit picture.

Is rideshare a good idea during the fair?
Workable, with patience: expect surge pricing and slow pickups right outside the hall. Walk a few blocks away from the convention center first, then order.
