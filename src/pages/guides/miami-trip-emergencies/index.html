Miami Trip Emergencies Hub

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Trips go sideways in predictable ways: someone gets sick, a phone dies, a rental car locks its keys inside, a storm moves a flight. This hub is the index for those moments. Each guide below covers one problem properly: what to do first, who to call, what it should cost in effort if not in money, and which scams and dead ends to skip. Save this page before you travel, the way you save your hotel address.

If it is an emergency, it is 911

If anyone is seriously ill or injured, in danger, or you witness a crime in progress, call 911 before you read anything else on this page. Everything else here is for the problems that feel urgent but are not dangerous: those have orderly fixes, and the guides below give you the order.

Storm problems

If the problem is weather, start with the hurricane season planning guide for how storms are forecast and announced, and the flight and cruise disruption guide for rebooking, refunds, and hotel nights when a storm moves your travel. For storm facts during the event, the only sources that matter are the National Hurricane Center and local officials.

Lost something?

For items lost at the airport, the airport's lost and found is linked from the official MIA homepage; contact it before you assume the item is gone. For a lost phone, the Phone Died in Miami guide covers tracking, locking, and replacing it. For anything left in a rideshare or taxi, contact the operator through the app or receipt first; speed matters more than procedure.

Keep planning

Prevention beats repair: the Miami Packing List covers the document copies and chargers that prevent half of these problems, and the MIA Airport Guide covers the arrival hour where most trips wobble first.
