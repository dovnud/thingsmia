Bachelorette Party in Miami: The Planning Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. A Miami bachelorette works when the big pieces are booked like logistics and the fun is left loose: a boat day on Biscayne Bay, a beach plan that respects the city's rules, lodging that is actually legal for a group, and the free trolley doing the driving. This guide covers those bones honestly, including the two Miami Beach rules that end parties early every weekend: no alcohol on the public beach, and no legal house rentals in single family neighborhoods.

The boat day is the anchor event

Every Miami bachelorette format orbits the same centerpiece: a few hours on the water. Miami Beach Marina, at 300 Alton Road, lists charters, boat tours, and rentals among its on site offerings, with 400 slips for vessels up to 250 feet, and group catamaran charters depart daily from the marina on vessels listed for groups of up to 49 and up to 42 guests, sized for exactly this kind of party. Book the boat first, before restaurants and matching outfits, because weekend charter inventory is the scarcest thing in the plan and everything else schedules around the tide and the departure time.

Ask the operator the alcohol question directly: policies differ by boat, with some operators running bring your own formats and others running bars, and the same question applies to swimming stops, music, and the route, sandbar versus skyline cruise. Get the answers in the booking confirmation, not on the dock.

Beach rules that shape the beach day

City of Miami Beach law is unambiguous and the city publishes it for visitors: consuming alcohol in public is illegal, and the prohibited list for the beach includes coolers, tents and tables, glass containers, loud music, and cigarettes, with smoking on the city's beaches and parks banned under a city ordinance carrying fines of $100 to $500. The beach day that works inside those rules is a hotel beach club day, a rented cabana setup at your hotel's beach concession, or a simple towels and umbrellas morning with the drinks waiting at the beachfront cafe afterward.

This is the section most bachelorette guides skip and most Miami bachelorettes need. The city's enforcement is real, the fines are real, and the alternative, your hotel's pool deck and beach club, is usually the better party anyway. Lodging: the house rental trap

The classic bachelorette plan, one big house for the whole group, runs into Miami Beach law: the city prohibits short term rentals in all single family homes and in many apartment buildings, which makes a whole group of hotels and condo hotels in permitted zones the safe default, and our group trips guide explains the rules in detail. A block of rooms in one South Beach hotel gives the group a pool, a beach concession, housekeeping, and no legal exposure, and it puts the free trolley outside the door.

Moving the group is the easy part: the free Miami Beach trolley's South Beach loops run 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. daily about every 20 minutes and cover the island's party geography, rideshares multiply well for groups spread across two or three cars, and nobody in the group should be the designated driver of a rented van on Collins Avenue at midnight. In an emergency, the city's own instruction is the universal one: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Can we drink on the beach in Miami Beach?
No. Alcohol in public is illegal in Miami Beach, and coolers, glass, tents, and loud music are prohibited on the beach. Drink at your hotel's beach club or a beachfront cafe instead.

What is the best group activity for a Miami bachelorette?
A boat day on Biscayne Bay. Group catamarans depart Miami Beach Marina at 300 Alton Road on vessels listed for up to 49 guests. Book it first; weekend inventory goes early.

Can we rent a house for the group in Miami Beach?
Not legally in a single family home: the city prohibits short term rentals in all single family homes and many apartment buildings. Hotels and condo hotels in permitted zones are the safe default.

How do we get around as a group?
The free Miami Beach trolley covers South Beach from 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. daily. For late nights, split the group across rideshares and let nobody drive.
