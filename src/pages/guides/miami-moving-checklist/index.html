Miami moving checklist: 12 weeks out to the first week in

Last reviewed: November 4, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Work backward from one date: the day you become a Florida permanent resident. This winter that date carries money. Be a resident by December 31, 2026 and Amendment 3 puts you on the larger homestead exemption schedule, worth about $9,400 over four years on the worked example in our Amendment 3 guide. Everything below hangs off that date: the mail, the insurance, the utilities, and the two deadlines Florida enforces in your first month, vehicles inside 10 days and a driver license inside 30.

12 weeks out: fix the dates

Pick the residency date first, then the moving date around it. Residency is a fact, not a form: Florida treats you as a resident once you take a job here, enroll your children in public school, register to vote, file for homestead, or live here more than six consecutive months. For a winter move, your lease or closing date, your arrival, and your working life should all land on the same side of December 31.

If schools are part of the move, the magnet calendar runs on its own clock. Miami-Dade County Public Schools takes magnet applications from October 1 to January 15 each year through the Parent Portal at dadeschools.net, with results on March 15. Enrollment at your assigned neighborhood school runs year-round, so a mid-year move still works; the magnet choices are what close in January.

Book the move itself now too. Winter is Miami's high season for arrivals, and the good moving dates go first. Get three quotes in writing, with the same inventory list sent to each company, so you're comparing the same job.

6 weeks out: mail, insurance, and the packing pile

Change of address. File it with USPS directly, at usps.com or in person. Online filing carries a $1.25 identity verification fee; in person, with form PS Form 3575, it's free. You can file up to 90 days before the move or up to 30 days after, and forwarding on a permanent move runs 12 months. USPS warns that look-alike third-party sites charge $40 or more for the same filing. Type the address yourself; don't follow an ad to get there.

Insurance quotes. Two of them. If you're buying, homeowners quotes belong in hand before closing, because Florida premiums vary by thousands between carriers for the same house; the statewide average is $3,815 by the regulator's April 2026 figures and far higher on some aggregator math. If you're renting, a renters policy is cheap and covers the truck days too. For the car, get the Florida quote now, because Florida insurance has to be in place before the state will title and register your vehicle, and Florida's minimums are $10,000 of personal injury protection and $10,000 of property damage liability.

Packing supplies. Order once, generously. A moving box kit, packing tape with a dispenser, and furniture pads on Amazon cover most apartments. Boxes you run out of on the last night cost triple at a drugstore.

2 weeks out: accounts and utilities

Set the start dates for electricity with FPL and, inside Miami-Dade, water and sewer with the county's Water and Sewer Department. Both take service requests ahead of the move date, and a dark first night is a self-inflicted problem. Book internet installation for the same week and put the appointment in writing.

Build the documents folder, physical and scanned: lease or closing papers, photo IDs, vehicle title and current registration, insurance declarations, school records and immunizations if children are moving, and pet records if a pet is. The folder rides in your car, not in the truck. Half the errands in your first week ask for something in it.

Moving week

Check the calendar against hurricane season, June 1 to November 30. A move landing inside the season needs a storm shelf from day one: water, a flashlight per person, batteries, and a charged power bank, bought with the first grocery run rather than during a storm watch. Our hurricane season guide was written for visitors, and its booking and preparation logic is the same logic a new resident needs in year one.

Photograph the empty rooms at both ends, old place and new, before anything crosses the threshold. Deposits and damage claims are won with timestamps, not memories.

The first week: Florida's deadlines

Within 10 days: title and register your vehicles. Florida gives new residents 10 days from establishing residency to title and register a vehicle brought from another state. The order matters: Florida insurance first, then the tax collector's office. Budget the one-time fees: a $225 initial registration fee, about $85.25 for the title, and $28 for the plate, roughly $340 before the annual registration itself, which is figured by vehicle weight.

Within 30 days: your driver license. A Florida Class E license costs $48. Bring your current license, proof of identity and Social Security number, and two proofs of your Florida residential address; the tax collector offices that issue licenses list the full document set, so check it before you queue.

On the calendar: homestead. If you bought, the exemption needs you owning and living in the home on January 1 and form DR-501 filed with the Miami-Dade Property Appraiser by March 1. Put both dates on the calendar the week you move in. If you rented, there's nothing to file yet, and your December residency is what holds your place in the Amendment 3 line.

Then the unglamorous hour: register to vote at the new address if you vote in Florida, update the address with your bank and your employer, and walk the block. Knowing which pharmacy, grocery, and ventanita is yours settles a place faster than any paperwork.

The facts that matter

Deadlines and fees checked November 4, 2026 against Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles guidance and Miami-Dade sources: new residents title and register vehicles within 10 days of establishing residency and obtain a Florida driver license within 30 days. Florida insurance must be in place first; minimum coverage is $10,000 personal injury protection and $10,000 property damage liability. One-time vehicle fees: $225 initial registration fee, title about $85.25, plate $28; Class E license $48. USPS change of address: $1.25 verification fee online, free in person with PS Form 3575, file up to 90 days ahead, forwarding lasts 12 months. Frequently asked questions

How much cash do I need for the vehicle step?
About $340 in one-time state fees: the $225 initial registration fee, about $85.25 for the title, and $28 for the plate. The annual registration itself is added on top and depends on the vehicle's weight.

Can I register the car first and insure it after?
No. Florida wants Florida insurance in force before it titles and registers the vehicle. Get the quote at the six-week mark so the policy can start on your residency date.

When do the 10 and 30 day clocks start?
From establishing residency, which Florida reads from your actions: starting a job, enrolling kids in school, registering to vote, filing for homestead, or living here six months. Plan as if the clock starts the day you arrive to stay.

Why does the checklist keep pointing at December 31, 2026?
Amendment 3. Permanent residents as of that date start at a homestead exemption of up to $150,000 in 2027 and up to $250,000 in 2028; residents from January 1, 2027 start at up to $50,000. On the worked example in our Amendment 3 guide, the gap is about $9,400 across four tax years.

What should never go in the moving truck?
The documents folder: IDs, lease or closing papers, vehicle title, insurance papers, school and medical records. If the truck is late or lost, your first week still works.
