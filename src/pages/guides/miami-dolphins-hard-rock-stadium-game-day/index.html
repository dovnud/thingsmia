Dolphins Game Day Guide: Hard Rock Stadium

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Hard Rock Stadium sits in Miami Gardens, at 347 Don Shula Drive, roughly 15 miles north of downtown Miami and a world away from South Beach traffic logic. About 65,000 fans funnel into a stadium surrounded by parking lots rather than a city neighborhood, so game day is a driving and tailgating event first and a transit event second. Get the bag policy and the parking pattern right and the rest of the day is easy.

The bag policy, because it decides what you carry

The stadium runs a strict clear bag policy for Dolphins games. Bags must be clear plastic and no larger than 12 by 6 by 12 inches, or a small bag or fanny pack no larger than 4.5 by 6.5 inches. Diaper bags are permitted but are searched. One factory sealed plastic water bottle of 20 ounces or less is allowed per person, which in Miami heat is worth using. If you arrive with the wrong bag, Guest Experience Bag Check locations near Gates 3, 5, 8, and 14 will hold oversize bags for a card only fee that runs $12 to $20 depending on size, and bag check closes an hour after the gates close, so do not leave a bag there and drift to a long postgame dinner.

Everything else about entry is phone based and cashless in practice: have your ticket on your phone before you reach the gate plaza, because stadium wifi under a full crowd is not where you want to be downloading a wallet pass. Fans who need help on the day can text FAN with the issue and location to 69050, the stadium's published fan conduct line.

Tailgating: one space, behind your car, grills yes, kegs no

Tailgating at Hard Rock Stadium is a real scene, and it has rules that shape it. Each vehicle gets one parking space, and your tailgate must fit in the marked 8 by 10 foot space directly behind your vehicle: no spilling into the driving lanes or the next space. Gas and charcoal grills are permitted; open fires are not, and kegs are prohibited outright. Commercial catering is banned, which keeps the lots home made in character.

For Dolphins games, the parking choreography differs by pass color. Orange pass holders park freely in their lot for the first hour after lots open, after which directive parking begins, with attendants filling rows in order; yellow lots are directive from opening. The practical meaning: if you have a group arriving in several cars and want to tailgate together, arrive together, in the same window, because once directive parking starts you go where the attendant points. College games and concerts run directive parking in all lots from opening.

Getting there from the beach and downtown

There is no rail station at the stadium. Metrorail does not reach Miami Gardens, so the realistic options are driving, rideshare, or a park and ride shuttle where one is offered for your event. From Miami Beach, plan the drive as the better part of an hour in game day traffic and leave earlier than feels necessary; the stadium sits just off Florida's Turnpike and I-95 at the north end of the county, and the last two miles are the slowest.

Driving yourself means buying parking in advance where your ticket seller offers it and following the lot color on your pass, because the lots are not interchangeable once the directive system starts. Rideshare works but the postgame surge and the walk to the pickup zones make it the slowest exit of the day; the classic local move is to walk to a nearby restaurant or bar after the game, let the first wave clear, and order the car an hour later.

Where to stay for a game

If the game is the point of the trip, stay north: Aventura, Sunny Isles Beach, and Hallandale Beach put you within a short drive of the stadium with full beach access for the rest of the weekend, and Miami Gardens itself has a small set of stadium adjacent hotels that sell out first. Staying in South Beach turns one game into two long drives and is the right choice only if the beach days matter more than the football. Inside the bowl

Hard Rock Stadium was rebuilt around a canopy roof that shades most seats while leaving the field open, which changes the Miami math: a 1 p.m. September kickoff is still hot, but a shaded seat is a different experience from the old open bowl. Sun position matters most on the east side early in the day. Food and drink are stadium priced and the lines at halftime are the day's worst queue, so eat before kickoff or during the first quarter. The stadium's own site publishes the current game day guide for each event, and checking it the week of your game is worth five minutes, because gate and lot times are set per event.

Frequently asked questions

What is the bag policy at Hard Rock Stadium?
Clear plastic bags up to 12 by 6 by 12 inches, or a small bag up to 4.5 by 6.5 inches. Anything bigger can be checked near Gates 3, 5, 8, and 14 for $12 to $20 by card.

Can you tailgate at Dolphins games?
Yes, in the marked 8 by 10 foot space directly behind your vehicle. Gas and charcoal grills are allowed; open fires and kegs are not.

How do I get to Hard Rock Stadium without a car?
There is no rail link. Use rideshare, a taxi, or an event shuttle where offered, and expect the postgame exit to be slow. Staying in Aventura or Sunny Isles shortens the trip considerably.

How big is Hard Rock Stadium?
About 65,000 seats for football. It also hosts the Miami Hurricanes, the Miami Open tennis tournament, and the Miami Grand Prix campus around it.
