Cost of living: New York City to Miami, line by line

Last reviewed: November 4, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Two lines do almost all of the saving. Rent: the median one-bedroom asking rent in October 2026 was $4,610 in New York City and $2,500 in Miami, a gap of about $25,320 a year. Income tax: New York State and City together take about $8,301 on a $100,000 salary; Florida takes $0. Add those two and a renter keeping the same salary is ahead about $33,600 a year before anything else moves. Some lines barely move at all, and one line, homeowners insurance, can hand a chunk of it back if you buy. Here's every line with its source, so you can rebuild the math on your own numbers.

Rent: the biggest line

Zumper's October 2026 national rent report puts the median one-bedroom asking rent at $4,610 in New York City and $2,500 in Miami, which ranked sixth among US cities. That's $2,110 a month, $25,320 a year, for the median listing in each city. Asking rents are not signed leases, and the medians describe different housing stock, so treat the gap as the shape of the market rather than a promise about a specific apartment.

A second source lands in the same place. Numbeo's city-center one-bedroom figures, updated November 1, 2026 from listings its users report, put Miami at $2,873.53 and New York at $4,500.41. Different method, same story: a four figure monthly gap. Before you sign anything, come and look at the actual buildings. A week of scouting in the neighborhoods you're weighing beats a month of listings.

Income tax: the line that goes to zero

Florida has no state or local income tax. New York has both, and they stack. The figures below are computed from the official 2026 New York State and City tax tables for a single filer, after the $8,000 state standard deduction, on salary alone:

Your own number depends on filing status, deductions, and how much of the year you lived in each state, so run it against the tables before you count it. One condition matters more than the arithmetic: the saving exists if the job and the salary actually move with you. A remote role your employer reclassifies, or a New York source income rule that still reaches you, changes the answer. Clear that with your employer and a tax professional first. Then count it.

If you buy: property tax, and the insurance warning

Miami property tax is not cheap; it's the exemption and the rate structure that do the work. Take a home assessed at $650,000 inside the City of Miami at the 2025 adopted total rate of 19.9878 mills. The bill before exemptions is about $12,992. The current homestead exemption, $25,000 against all levies plus $25,000 against non-school levies, trims about $837, leaving about $12,155. From 2027, Amendment 3 grows the non-school exemption sharply, and your move date sets how fast: that timing is worth about $9,400 across four years on the worked example in our Amendment 3 guide.

Two warnings belong in this section. First, a purchase resets the Save Our Homes cap, so your first bill is figured on the sale value, not the seller's old assessed value; sellers' tax bills in listing photos are history, not your future. Second, homeowners insurance. Florida's insurance regulator puts the statewide average premium at $3,815 a year including wind coverage, on April 2026 data. Insurify's 2025 figure for Florida is $8,292. Those are different methods producing very different numbers, and this page prints both rather than blending them. Either way, insurance is the line most likely to eat your tax saving, and quotes belong at the start of a house hunt, not the end.

The smaller lines

Sales tax. Miami-Dade charges 7% total, the 6% state rate plus a 1% county surtax. New York City charges 8.875%. On $20,000 a year of taxable spending, that's about $375 back in your pocket. Real, and small.

Getting around. A Miami-Dade one-month transit pass costs $112.50. In New York, the OMNY seven-day fare cap of $35 works out to about $140 across four weeks. The passes cost about the same; the networks don't. Miami's rail lines cover a fraction of the city, the Metromover is free but only loops downtown and Brickell, and most households here run a car. Budget the car, not the pass comparison.

Tolls. Driving the full length of SR 836, the Dolphin Expressway, costs about $2.40 with a SunPass, and Toll-by-Plate charges double. Most single toll points run under a dollar. It's a line item, not a budget.

Eating out. A wash. Numbeo's mid-range meal for two sits at about $120 in both cities on its November 2026 figures.

Car insurance: the line that does not save you

People expect Florida car insurance to be the cheap part. It isn't. Bankrate's 2026 figures, built on Quadrant rate data, put average full coverage at $3,884 a year in Florida and $4,090 in New York, about $200 apart across a year. Where you garage the car inside either state moves your own quote more than the state line does. Get the Florida quote before the move, because you need Florida insurance in place before you can title and register the car here anyway. Our moving checklist puts that step in the right week.

The honest net

For the renter case this page is built on, median one-bedroom in each city and a $100,000 salary that moves with you, the two big lines alone come to about $33,600 a year: $25,320 of rent and about $8,301 of income tax. Sales tax adds a few hundred. Car insurance adds roughly nothing. What can shrink it: buying a home with a four or five figure insurance premium, keeping a car you didn't need in New York, and flights back north every month. What can grow it: the Amendment 3 exemption schedule if your residency lands before December 31, 2026, which is the single most time-sensitive line in this whole comparison.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a New Yorker actually save by moving to Miami?
On this page's worked case, a renter at the median one-bedroom keeping a $100,000 salary, about $33,600 a year from rent and income tax alone. Your figure turns on your rent, your salary, and whether you buy a home and take on Florida insurance costs.

Is there really no income tax in Florida?
Right. Florida levies no state income tax and no city income tax. New York State and New York City both do, and the combined bill on a $100,000 single-filer salary is about $8,301 for 2026 by the official tables.

What costs more in Miami than people expect?
Homeowners insurance, first. The regulator's statewide average is $3,815 a year and one major aggregator's figure is $8,292; coastal Miami homes can quote above both. Car insurance runs about the same as New York's, and a purchase resets your property tax assessment to the sale price.

Does Amendment 3 change this comparison?
Yes, for buyers. It raises the non-school homestead exemption from 2027, and being a Florida permanent resident by December 31, 2026 puts you on the faster schedule, worth about $9,400 over four years on the worked example. Renters feel it later, when they buy.

Are these figures what I'll actually pay?
They're medians, averages, and one worked example, each labeled with its source and date. They're the right size of the gaps. Your lease, your quote, and your tax return give you your numbers.
