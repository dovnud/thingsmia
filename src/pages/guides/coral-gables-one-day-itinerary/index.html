Coral Gables in one day: trolley, Miracle Mile and the Biltmore without a car

Last reviewed: November 8, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Coral Gables is the one Miami neighborhood that hands a car-free visitor a complete day: a free trolley that runs from the Metrorail station, a spring-fed pool the size of a small lake, a four-block main street, and a grand hotel at the end of the line. The day has one scheduling law, and it is printed here so it does not ambush you: the trolley runs Monday to Saturday and rests on Sunday, while Venetian Pool opens Tuesday to Sunday and rests on Monday. Tuesday to Saturday is the full route. Sunday is the brunch version and needs a car or rideshare. Monday is a museum and Mile day without the swim. Pick your day first, then follow the clock below.

Arrive: Metrorail to Douglas Road, trolley from the station

Ride the Metrorail to Douglas Road station, where both of the city's free trolley routes begin, or to University station at 5400 Ponce de Leon Boulevard if the university end of the city is your base. The Coral Gables Trolley is free and runs Monday to Saturday, 6:30 a.m. to 10 p.m., with no service on Sundays, July 4, Thanksgiving, or Christmas Day, per the city. The Ponce de Leon route runs north and south on its boulevard between Douglas Road station and Flagler Street, past the university and the older residential streets. The Grand Avenue route runs from the same station through the McFarlane Homestead district. A third route, the Southern Loop toward Red Road and the University of Miami area, ran as a one year pilot from November 2025; its pilot year has just ended and the city still lists it as a pilot, so this itinerary does not lean on it. The two main routes are enough. They were designed for exactly this day.

Morning: Venetian Pool, the swim that justifies the trip

Take the trolley to Venetian Pool, 2701 De Soto Boulevard, and swim first, before the day heats up and the afternoon crowd builds. The city's venue page gives the current pattern: open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed Mondays, with no separate seasonal hours published, so none are promised here. Admission for non-residents is $23 for adults 13 and up and $18 for children 3 to 12; Coral Gables residents pay $7 and $6 with proof of residency, and the city notes fees can change. One rule surprises families at the gate: children must be at least three years old and 38 inches tall to enter, and staff verify age at entry. The pool itself holds more than 820,000 gallons of spring water from an underground aquifer and is refilled daily, which is why it stays cold in August and why a morning hour here beats any hotel pool on this trip. Our Venetian Pool attraction page carries the history of the old coral rock quarry it was carved from.

Midday: Miracle Mile for lunch and the bridal blocks

Trolley back toward the center and get off at Miracle Mile, the city's main street along Coral Way between LeJeune Road and Douglas Road. The four-block strip was renamed in the 1950s and built its name on bridal shops, which still line it, alongside the lunch counters, bakeries, and sit-down rooms that feed the downtown offices. This is the unhurried hour of the day: a full lunch instead of a snack, because dinner is late and the Biltmore's grounds reward an evening appetite. The Miracle Theatre, built in 1948 and now the Actors' Playhouse, anchors the street's evening end. If a show night lines up with your date, the Mile becomes your dinner-and-theatre plan without moving the car you did not bring. Our Coral Gables restaurants guide sorts the street's tables by occasion.

Afternoon option: the museum, with an honesty note

The Coral Gables Museum at 285 Aragon Avenue is the natural indoor hour, a block off the Mile in the old police and fire station. One fact about it is solid enough to plan around: its Gallery Night on the first Friday runs free, 6 to 10 p.m. For general visiting hours and admission, this page prints nothing, on purpose. The museum's own site carried a renovation closure banner and no current hours when this itinerary was checked, and the figures in circulation elsewhere disagree with each other. Check coralgablesmuseum.org on the morning of your visit. If it is open, it fills the hot hour between the pool and the evening perfectly well. If it is closed, the Mile's bookshops and cafes fill the same hour without an apology.

Evening: the Biltmore, and the Sunday problem

End the day at the Biltmore, 1200 Anastasia Avenue, the 1926 tower that still organizes the city's skyline. The hotel's pool is a sight on its own: about 23,000 square feet and 600,000 gallons, among the largest hotel pools in the continental United States, open sunrise to sunset, with nine private cabanas the hotel prices by season (in the October to April season, Friday to Sunday suite cabanas are listed at $725++ for up to eight guests and deluxe cabanas at $475++ for up to four, checked on the hotel's pool page). Non-guests should read those cabana listings as the pool's public door and confirm day access when booking, since policy is the hotel's to set. Stay through the golden hour on the grounds, then trolley or rideshare back to Douglas Road and the train. Our Biltmore hotel guide covers the stay version, bookable through the Biltmore on Expedia.

The Sunday version of this day is a different animal, and it is built around one event: the Signature Sunday Brunch at Fontana, the hotel's courtyard restaurant, reservations recommended, with omelet, caviar, crab, sushi, carving, and fresh pasta stations and bottomless bubbly in the hotel's description. The hotel publishes no brunch price and warns that hours and pricing can change, so no price is printed here, and the listing example runs 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. Because the trolley rests on Sundays, the brunch day runs on rideshares or a rental. Venetian Pool is open Sundays, so the brunch-plus-swim Sunday works well. It just does not work car-free, and a guide that pretends otherwise strands somebody at a trolley stop that is not running.

Staying over: the Gables pair

Two hotels make the Gables a base rather than a day trip. The Biltmore is the resort version, golf and pool and tower views. The Hotel Colonnade, on Miracle Mile itself, is the walker's version, bookable through the Colonnade on Expedia, with the Mile's restaurants as its dining room. The wider airport corridor runs through Miami airport area hotels on Expedia, ten minutes west. Our Coral Gables neighborhood guide maps the rest of the city beyond this one day.

The facts that matter

Coral Gables facts checked November 8, 2026: the city trolley is free, Monday to Saturday 6:30 a.m. to 10 p.m., no Sundays or listed holidays, with Ponce de Leon and Grand Avenue routes starting at Douglas Road Metrorail station (City of Coral Gables). Venetian Pool, 2701 De Soto Boulevard, opens Tuesday to Sunday 10 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed Mondays; non-resident admission is $23 adult and $18 child, resident $7 and $6; entry requires age three and 38 inches tall (city venue page). The Biltmore pool is about 23,000 square feet and 600,000 gallons, open sunrise to sunset; the hotel publishes no Sunday brunch price (Biltmore Hotel). Coral Gables Museum hours were not verifiable from the museum's site at this check and are not printed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best single day for the full route?
Saturday. The trolley runs, the pool is open, the Mile is fully staffed, and the Biltmore grounds carry their weekend crowd. Tuesday to Friday run the same route with thinner crowds.

Can toddlers swim at Venetian Pool?
Only if they clear both gates: at least three years old and at least 38 inches tall, verified at entry. Families with a two year old should build the day on the Mile and the Biltmore instead.

Do we need the car for the Biltmore?
Not Monday to Saturday. The trolley network and a short rideshare at either end cover it. On Sunday, yes, plan wheels, because the trolley does not run.
