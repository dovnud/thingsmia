Miami Packing List: Beach, City, and Rain

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Pack for three trips inside one bag: hot beach days, air conditioned city nights, and a daily chance of a downpour if you travel in rainy season. Light breathable fabrics, real sun protection, a compact rain layer, and a dry pouch for your phone cover nearly everything Miami will ask of you.

What do you pack for the beach?

Swimwear, plural: in Miami humidity a suit takes its time drying, so pack at least two. Add a high SPF sunscreen (consider a reef friendly formula), a wide brim hat or cap, sunglasses with a strap for boat days, a beach towel or quick dry travel towel if your hotel charges for extras, and a mesh bag that sand falls out of instead of traveling home in. One rule shapes the bag itself: glass containers are prohibited on Miami Beach, and alcohol in public is illegal there, so pack a reusable non glass water bottle and leave the glass at the hotel. Smoking is also banned on the beaches, per the City of Miami Beach.

What do you pack for the city?

Miami dresses up at night more than most beach cities. Pack one smart outfit for restaurants and rooftop bars, shoes you can actually walk a neighborhood in, and a light layer for aggressive indoor air conditioning, which is the only cold most visitors meet here. Daytime city exploring, Wynwood's walls, Little Havana, the Art Deco District, calls for the same breathable fabrics as the beach, with a crossbody bag or secure pockets for crowds and transit.

What do you pack for the rain?

If your trip falls in South Florida's rainy season, May 15 to October 15 by the National Weather Service's definition, a compact umbrella or light rain jacket is not optional equipment. Rain usually arrives as a short, heavy afternoon downpour rather than a gray day, so add quick dry shoes or sandals that survive a soaking, and a dry pouch or zip bag for your phone and documents. A small microfiber towel earns its space on boat tours and beach days alike.

What do Miami visitors always forget?

A portable phone charger, because navigation, transit apps, and photos drain batteries fast in the heat. After sun gel, because the first day fools almost everyone. Insect repellent, if your plans include the Everglades or evening time near the water. A light sweater for winter evenings and over cooled restaurants. And a photograph of your documents on your phone, plus the paper copies habit every traveler should keep, which the Trip Emergencies hub explains.

The facts that matter

South Florida rainy season runs May 15 to October 15 and brings about 70 percent of the year's rainfall (National Weather Service Miami). The average August high in Miami is about 91 degrees (1991 to 2020 National Weather Service normals), so breathable fabric is a fact, not a style choice. City of Miami Beach rules: glass containers are prohibited on the beach, consuming alcohol in public is illegal, and smoking is banned on beaches and in parks.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a jacket in Miami?
A light one, yes, even in summer, for air conditioning, boat breezes, and winter evenings. Leave anything heavy at home; Miami cold snaps are handled by layers, not coats.

Can I just buy what I forget when I get there?
Of course; Miami Beach and downtown stock every item on this list. You will simply pay resort area prices for sunscreen and chargers, which is why they headline the pack at home list.

What should cruise passengers pack differently?
A carry on day bag with swimwear and essentials for embarkation day, since checked luggage can reach your cabin hours after you board. Our cruise hub covers the pre and post cruise routine in Miami.
