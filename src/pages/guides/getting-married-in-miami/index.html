Getting Married in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The legal skeleton of a Miami wedding is simpler than the industry makes it look. Florida marriage licenses come from the county Clerk, carry no waiting period for nonresidents, and are valid statewide for 60 days. A ceremony on a Miami Beach public beach needs a city ceremony permit applied for at least 14 days ahead. Everything else, venues, planners, flowers, is commerce; these two pieces of paper are the law, and this page covers them carefully.

The marriage license: Florida's rules

Florida's framework, administered in Miami by the Miami-Dade Clerk of Court's Marriage License Bureau, works like this. Couples apply in person together; the Central Marriage License Bureau sits at 601 NW 1st Court in Miami, and an online pre application exists to complete before the in person visit, which shortens the bureau trip considerably. Florida residents face a three day waiting period before the license can be used, waived if both partners complete a state registered premarital preparation course. Nonresidents of Florida have no waiting period at all, which is why destination weddings here are logistically easy. The license is valid for 60 days from issue, and the completed license must be returned to the Clerk within 10 days after the ceremony.

One number this page will not print: the license fee. Secondary sources quoting the Clerk mostly agree on a figure, but they do not all agree, and the Clerk's own current fee schedule could not be confirmed directly while this guide was checked in October 2026. Confirm the current fee on miamidadeclerk.gov before your visit and budget from the Clerk's number, not anyone's blog post, including ours. The same caution applies to the fee for a Clerk performed civil ceremony at the bureau, the simplest legal wedding in the county.

Beach ceremonies: the city permit

A wedding on Miami Beach's public sand is a permitted event, and the city's instrument for it is the Ceremonies and Team Building Permit, issued for simple wedding ceremonies with up to 150 attendees lasting a maximum of four hours. Under the city's Special Events guidelines, the application, with the exact location, date, times, and attendee count, must be submitted no less than 14 days before the ceremony, accompanied by a certificate of insurance naming the City.

Bigger productions leave this lane: weddings on public property that exceed the simple ceremony scope fall under full Special Event Permits, with lead times in the city's guidelines running to 90 days before load in, professional event requirements, and a different level of planning entirely. Most visiting couples stay in the simple ceremony lane or hand the whole problem to a beachfront hotel or venue, whose private property and in house events teams absorb the permitting into the package. Either way, the permit question is the first question to ask any Miami Beach wedding venue or planner, and the answer should name the instrument, not wave at it.

Planning basics around the paperwork

Season shapes everything. The comfortable wedding months in Miami are November through April, dry and warm; summer weddings are a heat and hurricane season negotiation, beautiful at sunset and demanding at noon, and our hurricane season guide is required reading for a June to November date. Guest logistics are the second budget: a Miami Beach wedding puts guests inside the free trolley network and the Lincoln Road restaurant scene, and a room block in one hotel turns a wedding weekend into a small festival.

For the legal ceremony only path, the Clerk's civil ceremony, paired with a celebration afterward, is the minimalist Miami wedding and a legitimate choice couples make every week. For the full production, the order of operations that works is venue first, then the permit lane confirmed in writing, then the license timed inside its 60 day validity window, never the other way around.

Frequently asked questions

How do we get a marriage license in Miami?
Apply in person together at a Miami-Dade Marriage License Bureau; the central bureau is at 601 NW 1st Court, Miami, and you can complete a pre application online first. Confirm the current fee on the Clerk's site before you go.

Is there a waiting period to marry in Florida?
Florida residents wait three days, waived if both complete a state registered premarital course. Nonresidents of Florida have no waiting period. The license is valid for 60 days.

Can we get married on the beach in Miami Beach?
Yes, with the city's Ceremonies and Team Building Permit for simple ceremonies up to 150 guests and four hours, applied for at least 14 days ahead with a certificate of insurance. Larger productions need a full Special Event Permit.

When is the best time for a Miami wedding?
November through April for dry, warm weather. Summer dates are sunset ceremonies and hurricane season planning; read the season guide before booking June to November.
