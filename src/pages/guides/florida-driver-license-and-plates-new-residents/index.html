Florida driver license and plates: the new resident sequence

Last reviewed: November 6, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Florida gives new residents two clocks, and they run at different speeds. Your vehicle comes first: title and registration within 10 days of establishing residency, and only after Florida insurance is in force. Your license has 30 days. The first visit costs more than movers expect, because Florida charges a $225 initial registration fee the first time a vehicle is registered in your name here. The full stack for a typical car lands around $360 before county and other statutory fees, and this page shows it line by line from the Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles fees page, checked November 6, 2026, so you can total your own visit instead of trusting a single number from a forum.

When the clocks start

You're a Florida resident for these rules when you start a job, a trade, a profession or an occupation in Florida, enroll your children in public school, register to vote, file for homestead exemption, or live in Florida for more than six consecutive months. The license clock is 30 days from that point. The vehicle clock, title and registration, is 10 days from the same point, and it also runs from starting a job or enrolling a child in school. The two clocks starting on different errands is the trap. People finish the license in week four and learn the car was late in week two. Do the car first. It's first in this sequence for a reason.

Step one: Florida insurance, before anything else

You must hold insurance from an agent licensed in Florida before you can title and register a vehicle here. An out of state policy, even from the same national company, doesn't satisfy the step until a Florida licensed agent has written the Florida policy. Do this step from your old home if you can, by phone, so the 10 day clock isn't spent shopping. Step two: title and registration, within 10 days

Bring the out of state title, your identification, proof of Florida insurance, and the vehicle itself for the inspection in step three. The fees, from the FLHSMV fees page: an original used vehicle title is $85.25 for an electronic title, with $2.50 added if a paper title is printed and a $2 lien recording fee where it applies. A new license plate is $28. The initial registration fee is $225, charged the first time a vehicle is registered in Florida in your name, and it's waived when you're transferring an existing Florida plate. On top sits the registration tax by weight for a car: $14.50 at up to 2,499 pounds, $22.50 from 2,500 to 3,499 pounds, and $32.50 at 3,500 pounds and up, with other statutory and county fees added at the counter.

Run the stack for a mid weight car: $225 plus $85.25 plus $28 plus $22.50 is $360.75, before those county and statutory extras and before any use tax question in step four. That's the honest first visit figure for most newcomers with one car. A second car in the same name pays its own title, plate and weight tax, and the $225 applies per vehicle where no Florida plate is being transferred.

Step three: the VIN and odometer check

Florida requires the vehicle identification number and odometer reading of an out of state vehicle to be physically verified on form HSMV 82040 or HSMV 82042. The inspection can be done by a law enforcement officer, a military police officer, a licensed Florida dealer, or a county tax collector employee or Division of Motorist Services Compliance Examiner. One correction to advice you'll read elsewhere: a notary is not on FLHSMV's new resident list for this inspection. Plan on the tax collector office or a dealer doing it, and bring the form with you.

Step four: the use tax question

If you bought the car outside Florida and bring it in within six months, Florida generally charges its 6 percent use tax, plus any applicable county surtax, with credit for a like tax you lawfully paid in the other state. Pay the same or more there and nothing more is due; pay less and you owe the difference. A vehicle you used in another state, the District of Columbia or a US territory for six months or longer before bringing it to Florida, with documentation, owes no Florida use tax. This is the Florida Department of Revenue's rule, set out in its 2026 guidance. If your purchase date sits near the six month line, bring the paperwork that proves the dates and let the tax collector run your case.

Step five: the license, within 30 days

The original Class E license fee is $48, and tax collector offices add a $6.25 service fee. A Class E license is valid eight years if you're under 80, and six years at 80 and over. If you hold a valid out of state US license, it normally waives the knowledge and road tests in exchange; a vision screening always applies. Retests, if you need them, are $10 for the knowledge exam and $20 for the skills test. Don't extend the waiver to a foreign license in your planning. FLHSMV doesn't promise it.

One change catches international arrivals cold. Since February 6, 2026, under FLHSMV's January 30, 2026 announcement, all driver license knowledge and skills exams are given in English only, in every classification, including exams given orally. Translation services are no longer permitted and printed exams in other languages were removed. Some older FLHSMV pages still list other languages. The 2026 announcement governs. If English is a stretch for you, prepare for the exam itself, not for an interpreter who won't be there.

Bring the documents in one trip: one primary identification document, proof of your Social Security number, and two proofs of your Florida residential address. If you're not a US citizen, bring Department of Homeland Security proof of lawful presence, such as a permanent resident card or a passport with visa and I-94. Florida credentials have been REAL ID compliant since January 1, 2010, so the license you receive works as federal identification.

Where to go in Miami-Dade

Driver license and registration services in Miami-Dade are consolidating under the Miami-Dade Tax Collector, and the change is still in progress, with offices transferring in stages. The Tax Collector's main office is at 200 NW 2nd Ave, Miami, FL 33128, phone (305) 375-5448. Because the branch picture is moving, check mdctaxcollector.gov for the current office list and appointment options before you drive across the county. Book the appointment, bring the full document set, and do the car and the license in the same visit if the office handles both.

The facts that matter

License and plate facts checked November 6, 2026 on flhsmv.gov: residency begins with employment, enrolling children in public school, voter registration, a homestead filing, or more than six consecutive months in Florida. Title and registration are due within 10 days, with Florida insurance in force first; the license is due within 30 days. Fees: Class E original $48 plus a $6.25 tax collector service fee; original used title $85.25 electronic; plate $28; initial registration $225 under Fla. Stat. 320.072, waived when transferring an existing Florida plate; car registration tax $14.50, $22.50 or $32.50 by weight, plus other statutory and county fees. VIN and odometer verification runs on form HSMV 82040 or 82042 by an officer, a licensed dealer, or tax collector staff. Exams have been English only since February 6, 2026. The use tax rule is the Florida Department of Revenue's: 6 percent plus surtax inside six months, with credit for tax paid elsewhere, and no tax after six months of documented use outside Florida.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do the license before the car?
You can, and the order doesn't matter to the state. It matters to your clocks. The car's 10 days expire first, so the car is the urgent errand.

Why is my first registration so expensive?
The $225 initial registration fee. It's a one time charge per vehicle when you first register in your name in Florida, and it's the line nobody warns movers about.

My car is leased. What changes?
The title sits with the leasing company, so the title step runs through them. Start that conversation before you move, and keep the 10 day clock in front of the leasing company's paperwork team.

What comes after the paperwork?
The homestead filing, once you own, runs on its own dates. The Amendment 3 guide covers the December 31, 2026 residency line, and the moving checklist holds the full first month order.
