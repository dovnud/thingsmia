Restaurants Near the Convention Center

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Convention eating fails in two ways: the sad desk salad at the venue, and the two hour dinner expedition when you have a 7 a.m. start. This list is the middle way, verified operating restaurants within a short walk of 1901 Convention Center Drive, anchored by the center's own Rum Room on campus and the Lincoln Road strip about half a mile south, plus one famous expedition for the night you have time.

On campus and the first blocks

Rum Room, 2100 Washington Avenue. The convention center's own restaurant sits on the campus itself, a 1920s styled room serving a tapas style menu per the center, and it is the correct answer for the working lunch between sessions and the debrief drink after them: zero travel, zero risk of missing the next keynote.

Cortadito Coffee House, 1656 Meridian Avenue. Just off Lincoln Road, this Cuban coffee house and bakery cafe is the morning institution of the list: a cortadito and a pastelito is the Miami convention breakfast, faster and better than a hotel buffet line. It is also the cheapest cultural experience in this guide.

Lincoln Road: the main strip

The Cheesecake Factory, 600 Lincoln Road. The reliable group answer: a huge menu that absorbs every dietary requirement at a conference table, at the east end of the mall near the beach. The Lobster Shack, 613 Lincoln Road. A seafood shack format a few doors along, lobster rolls and the casual end of a beach day, good for the lunch you did not plan. Salt and Straw, 749 Lincoln Road. The ice cream stop, small batch and inventive, for the walk back to the hotel.

Further west along the mall, Panizza Bistro, 1229 Lincoln Road, is an Argentine and Latin bistro cafe that has been in Miami Beach since 2001 by its own account, Buenos Aires inspired and unhurried, and Pizzette by Leonardo, 1223 Lincoln Road, covers the Italian pizza and wine bar slot next door. Between them, the mall's cafe row handles the long European style lunch that convention schedules occasionally allow.

The expedition: Joe's Stone Crab

Joe's Stone Crab, 11 Washington Avenue. About 1.6 miles south in South of Fifth, Joe's has been serving since 1913 by its own history and is the name out of towners ask for. It is not a walk from the center in conference clothes; it is a short trolley or car ride and a reservation level decision, the celebration dinner at the end of the week rather than the Tuesday lunch. Stone crab season runs through the cooler months, and summer visitors should check the restaurant's own site for its seasonal schedule before building a plan around it.

A note on hours and seasons, because convention guides usually skip it: Miami Beach restaurant hours shift with the season, and every entry on this page is best confirmed on the restaurant's own site on the day. The list itself was verified as operating in October 2026; what each kitchen does at 10 p.m. in August is between you and their website.

Frequently asked questions

What is the closest restaurant to the convention center?
Rum Room, at 2100 Washington Avenue, is on the convention center campus itself. Lincoln Road's restaurant row starts about half a mile south.

Where do groups with mixed diets eat near the center?
The Cheesecake Factory at 600 Lincoln Road is the standard answer for a big mixed group. Panizza Bistro and Pizzette by Leonardo cover the relaxed sit down end of Lincoln Road.

Is Joe's Stone Crab walkable from the convention center?
It is about 1.6 miles south, a short trolley or car ride rather than a conference clothes walk. Treat it as the celebration dinner, and check its seasonal schedule on its own site.

Where do I get a proper Cuban coffee near the center?
Cortadito Coffee House, 1656 Meridian Avenue just off Lincoln Road. A cortadito and a pastelito is the local convention breakfast.
