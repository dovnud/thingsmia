Spring Break in Miami Beach: Rules, Curfews, Logistics

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami Beach no longer runs an open invitation spring break. The whole month of March is a designated high impact period with extra policing, beach checkpoints on the busiest weekends, doubled towing rates, and flat rate garage pricing that starts where normal Miami parking gives up. The measures are set fresh every year, so treat this page as the shape of the rules, check the city's current year release before you travel, and book your logistics around the busiest two weekends, not the calendar average.

What the high impact period actually means

The City of Miami Beach designates all of March as a high impact period, with added traffic management, staffing, and public safety measures across the entertainment district. In the city's most recent published plan, the peak weekends, when the strictest measures apply, fell in the middle of the month: two consecutive long weekends when college breaks overlap. If your trip can move by a week, the first and last weekends of March are noticeably calmer than the peak pair, and the beaches on either side of the city, Surfside and Key Biscayne, barely register spring break at all.

The measures are decided and published fresh each year, usually in February. That matters for how you plan: anything you read about a specific past year, including on this page, is the pattern, not a promise. Before you book nonrefundable anything for March, read the city's current spring break release on miamibeachfl.gov and match your dates against its peak weekend list.

The beach rules, which apply all year and bite hardest in March

Some of the rules that surprise spring breakers are not spring break rules at all. Alcohol and smoking are prohibited on Miami Beach's public beaches at all times of year, and the city enforces both. During peak spring break weekends the beach gets a checkpoint layer on top: entrances along Ocean Drive are limited to a short list of streets (in the most recent plan, 5th, 7th, 8th, 10th, 12th, and 14th Streets), with security screening at those entrances. Coolers, glass containers, inflatable devices, tents, and tables are prohibited items at the checkpoints, and amplified music without a city permit is restricted.

Read that list as a packing list in reverse. Bring a soft bag, not a cooler. Buy drinks at a beachfront cafe, not a package store run to the sand. If your group's plan depends on a speaker, a tent, or a cooler of anything, the plan needs to move to a private venue, a boat, or a hotel pool deck, because the public beach will not host it in March.

Curfews: the honest history

Miami Beach has used curfews in the past, and you will see old headlines quoted as if they were standing rules. In 2022 the city declared a state of emergency with a midnight to 6 a.m. curfew over one late March stretch, with off premises alcohol sales banned after 6 p.m. inside the curfew area. In 2024 a similar midnight curfew ran for one mid March weekend in the entertainment district. In the most recent published plan, no blanket curfew was announced in advance; the city instead said stricter tools remain available to the police chief if conditions require them.

The takeaway for visitors: a curfew is a tool the city reaches for mid month if things go wrong, not a fixture you can plan around either way. If being out past midnight in the entertainment district is the point of your trip, March in Miami Beach is the wrong month and the wrong city district; the mainland neighborhoods, Wynwood and Brickell, run on normal hours and do not inherit these measures.

Parking, towing, and the rental toys ban

Parking is where March gets expensive fastest. In the most recent city plan, the Art Deco District garages (the 7th Street and Collins, 12th Street and Washington, 13th Street and Collins, and 16th Street garages) charged flat fees starting in the $40 to $100 range on Thursdays through Sundays, and on street and surface parking in the core was elevated well above normal rates, while the resident rate stayed at $1 an hour. Package liquor stores in the Art Deco District close strictly at 8 p.m. during the period.

Towing is the other trap. The nonresident towing rate in the Art Deco District is doubled during the period, to $548 plus fees in the most recent plan, and license plate reader details run on the MacArthur and Julia Tuttle causeways late at night on peak weekends, alongside DUI enforcement details on the peak Fridays and Saturdays. Finally, the rentals most associated with spring break marketing, golf carts, slingshots, scooters, and mopeds, have their rental businesses suspended on peak weekends. If a cart or slingshot day is on your itinerary, schedule it outside the peak pair of weekends or expect the shop to be closed.

How to do spring break here without fighting the city

Stay on the island and walk. A hotel inside Miami Beach removes the causeway, the parking, and the towing from your trip entirely, and the free city trolley covers the island's length. Eat early or book: restaurants take reservations seriously in March and the walk in wait at peak hours is the second longest line of your day after the beach checkpoint. Put your beach time in the morning, when entrances are quiet and the sand is at its best, and treat the afternoon as pool, spa, or excursion time.

Groups should read our group trips guide before booking a house: Miami Beach bans short term rentals in all single family homes and many apartment buildings, which catches out spring break groups every year. And if your dates are flexible, the single best spring break decision is choosing the shoulder weekends instead of the peak pair.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a curfew in Miami Beach during spring break?
Not as a standing rule. The city imposed midnight curfews on specific weekends in 2022 and 2024, and reserved the right to use them again, but its most recent published plan announced no blanket curfew in advance. Check the current year release on miamibeachfl.gov before you travel.

Can you drink on the beach in Miami Beach?
No. Alcohol is prohibited on Miami Beach's public beaches all year, and smoking is banned as well. In March the rule is enforced through staffed beach entrance checkpoints on peak weekends.

When are the busiest spring break weekends?
The middle of March, when college breaks overlap. In the city's most recent plan the two peak weekends fell in the middle two weeks of the month, and the strictest measures and highest parking prices attach to those weekends.

How much is parking during spring break?
In the most recent city plan, Art Deco District garages charged flat fees starting at $40 to $100 on Thursdays through Sundays, and core street parking was elevated. Staying on the island and walking is the cheaper pattern.
