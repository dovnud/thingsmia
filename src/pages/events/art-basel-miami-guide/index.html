Art Basel Miami Beach Guide

Last reviewed: November 1, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Art Basel Miami Beach 2026 runs its public days on Friday December 4 to Sunday December 6 at the Miami Beach Convention Center, 1901 Convention Center Drive. The two days before, December 2 and 3, are VIP Preview Days and are invitation only, so a public ticket will not get you in early. Buy tickets only through Art Basel's own online shop at artbasel.com: day tickets were listed from $92, with reduced tickets at $68, when checked on November 1, 2026.

What is Art Basel Miami Beach, exactly?

It is the American edition of the Art Basel fairs, and the commercial center of the art world's calendar. For 2026, Art Basel has announced 272 exhibitors from more than 40 countries and territories, with more than 30 galleries taking part for the first time. The show is the anchor of Miami Art Week, the loose name for the satellite fairs, museum openings, and private collection visits that fill the same week across Miami Beach, Wynwood, and the Design District. The fair's Global Lead Partner is UBS, and the Miami Beach show is directed by Bridget Finn.

One change matters for visitors in 2026: the floor has been substantially reconfigured. The fair occupies about 12 percent less space than the last edition, organized around four hospitality plazas, with the biggest galleries spread across the floor instead of grouped together. Art Basel says the point is longer sightlines and a calmer, more focused visit. In practice it should also mean shorter bottlenecks than the old layout produced.

How do tickets work?

Tickets are sold online through Art Basel's own ticket shop, and the public days are Friday December 4, Saturday December 5, and Sunday December 6, 2026. When prices were checked on November 1, 2026, a day ticket started at $92 and a reduced day ticket at $68, the reduced rate covering students, seniors, veterans, and Miami Beach residents with proof. A two day weekend ticket and a range of Premium passes, which add preview style access and lounge entry, are sold in the same shop and cost considerably more. Hours run from late morning to 6 p.m. on the public days; your ticket carries your exact entry details, so read it before you set out. Two warnings that save real grief: third party resale tickets for this fair are a known scam vector, so buy from artbasel.com only, and the preview days are genuinely invitation only. No public ticket tier opens December 2 or 3 to a walk up buyer.

What are the sectors inside the fair?

The fair is arranged in sectors, and knowing them turns a wander into a plan. For 2026, per Art Basel's announcement:

Galleries is the main sector, with 204 exhibitors showing modern and contemporary work. Inside many Galleries booths, Kabinett adds 26 focused mini exhibitions on one artist or theme. Nova holds 20 galleries showing work made in the last few years by up to three artists each; it is where the newest painting tends to surface. Positions gives 12 emerging artists a solo project each, and it is the best sector for discovery. Survey presents 16 historically focused exhibitions by 18 galleries, older work that deserves a second look. Zero 10 is the fair's platform for art of the digital era: its 2026 presentation, titled Generation, brings 19 projects by artists including Refik Anadol and Jon Rafman, shown by 21 galleries and platforms. The Conversations talks program runs alongside the selling floor and is the part of the fair aimed squarely at listeners rather than buyers.

What is free, and what happens outside the hall?

Two headline projects for 2026 are outdoors and free. The Turrell Art Foundation and Art Basel, supported by Pace Gallery, will unveil a new outdoor body of work by James Turrell. In Collins Park, the park directly across from the convention center, Public Art Fund presents Between Tides, a participatory exhibition of artist designed ping pong tables that visitors are meant to play on. The Art Basel Awards, run with BOSS, reveal their Gold Awardees at an awards night on Thursday December 3. Beyond that, the city's museums time their biggest openings to this week, and the satellite fairs, including Design Miami beside the convention center and Untitled Art in its tent on the sand at Ocean Drive, run their own tickets and dates. Treat the fair as the anchor and Art Week as the festival around it.

How should you plan one day, or three?

One day: buy a day ticket, arrive at opening, and work the sectors in an order that matches your energy, Positions and Nova first while you are fresh, Galleries after, Zero 10 whenever the floor gets dense because its darker rooms reset your eyes. Eat inside the fair or accept a short walk; leaving the island at midday on a fair day costs more time than any lunch saves. Three days: give one day to the main fair, one to the satellites and Collins Park, and one to the mainland, where Wynwood and the Design District galleries and museums run their Art Week programs. Evenings are for openings and dinners, and reservations for both disappear early in the week.

Where you sleep shapes all of it. The walkable base is the Collins Avenue corridor by the convention center: our Loews and Setai guides cover the two closest full service options, and The Betsy is the culture first boutique a short walk south. Getting there is its own subject: read the Art Basel traffic and parking guide before you decide to drive.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a ticket for the preview days?
No. December 2 and 3, 2026 are VIP Preview Days and admission is by invitation, extended to galleries, collectors, institutions, and press. Public tickets cover December 4 to 6 only.

Is one day enough?
For a first visit, yes, if you plan it. The 2026 floor is deliberately smaller, around 12 percent less space than the previous edition, which makes a full, satisfying day realistic instead of a rush.

What should I wear and carry?
The convention center is air conditioned hard against the Miami December sun, so bring a layer. Fair days involve miles of walking on concrete; this is the week to wear the comfortable shoes, not the statement ones.

Is Art Basel the same as Miami Art Week?
No. Art Basel is the single fair at the convention center. Miami Art Week is everything scheduled around it, and our Art Basel hub links the full set of ThingsMIA guides for the week.
