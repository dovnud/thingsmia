Florida Grand Opera Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Florida Grand Opera, FGO, is the resident opera company of the Adrienne Arsht Center, where it performs in the Ziff Ballet Opera House in downtown Miami, and one of the oldest opera companies in the United States. Its 85th season, 2026-27, is an all Italian program of three full productions: Donizetti's Lucia di Lammermoor, November 14 to 17, 2026; Mozart's Don Giovanni, January 30 to February 2, 2027; and Verdi's Nabucco, April 17 to 20, 2027. Everything is sung in Italian with English and Spanish translations projected above the stage, so no, you do not need to speak Italian, and yes, you will follow every word. The company also performs each production at the Broward Center in Fort Lauderdale, stated plainly because the two cities share this company, and closes its season with a one night Viva Verdi concert there on May 1, 2027.

The season, production by production

Lucia di Lammermoor, November 14, 15, and 17, 2026. Donizetti's tragedy of a forced marriage and a famous mad scene opens the season, and it carries a piece of music history: this is the opera in which Luciano Pavarotti made his American debut in South Florida, a fact the Arsht Center itself notes in presenting the season. If you see one opera this year, the combination of the story, the sextet, and that history makes Lucia the connoisseur's pick and a surprisingly gripping first opera. Don Giovanni, January 30, 31, and February 2, 2027 in Miami, then February 18 and 20 at the Broward Center in Fort Lauderdale: Mozart's dark comedy about the seducer who will not repent, the most psychologically modern opera of the classical era. Nabucco, April 17, 18, and 20, 2027: Verdi's biblical epic of exile and liberation, built around the chorus "Va, pensiero," the closest thing opera has to a national anthem, performed here during the Passover and Easter season, a resonance the company notes. All Miami performances are at the Ziff Ballet Opera House; tickets and subscriptions sell through fgo.org and the Arsht box office.

Opera for first timers: it is easier than you think

The barriers are imaginary and worth dismantling one by one. Language: the projected translations in English and Spanish run above the stage for every performance, and following them quickly becomes as natural as subtitles on a film. Length: these productions run roughly two and a half to three hours with intermissions, the shape of a long film with better breaks. Story: read the one page synopsis in the program before the lights go down, and the evening will make complete sense. Dress: Miami opera dress is smart casual with opening night elevation; nobody is checking for black tie. Price psychology: the top tier of the Ziff is a genuinely good opera seat at a fraction of orchestra prices, because opera sound carries in a hall built for it. Start with Lucia for the drama or Nabucco for the chorus, sit where you can see the surtitle screen comfortably, and arrive early enough for the plaza and a drink; the Arsht's rituals are part of the pleasure, and our Arsht Center guide covers the building.

The company and its two homes

Florida Grand Opera's 85 seasons make it a survivor in an art form that has folded companies in larger cities, and its dual home structure is the reason the arithmetic works: Miami's Arsht Center and Fort Lauderdale's Broward Center share each production, one company serving the whole South Florida corridor. The season around the mainstage includes the OperaFest festival of the voice in January (January 8 to 10, 2027, in the company's published plans) and free community singing through its SongFest programs. For a Miami visitor, the practical takeaway is simple: the Miami dates above are your dates, at the Arsht; the Fort Lauderdale performances are the same productions a few weeks later, about a 45 minute drive north, if your trip is based in Broward.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to understand Italian?
No. Every Florida Grand Opera performance projects English and Spanish translations above the stage. Read the program synopsis first and you will follow everything.

Which opera should a beginner choose?
Lucia di Lammermoor for drama and the famous mad scene, or Nabucco for the great choruses. Both are in the 2026-27 season, in November and April respectively.

Where does Florida Grand Opera perform?
In Miami at the Ziff Ballet Opera House of the Adrienne Arsht Center, downtown. Each production also plays the Broward Center in Fort Lauderdale.
