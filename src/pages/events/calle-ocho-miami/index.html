Calle Ocho Festival Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Calle Ocho is the free, one day street festival that closes Carnaval Miami: on Sunday March 14, 2027, from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m., SW 8th Street in Little Havana closes to traffic from SW 12th Avenue to SW 27th Avenue, fifteen blocks of stages, food stands, and dancing in the street. It is produced by the Kiwanis Club of Little Havana, and proceeds support the Kiwanis of Little Havana Foundation's youth programs in the community. There is no ticket and no gate: you walk in from any side street. The paid upgrade is the VIP Passport Tour, a guided way to eat and drink through the festival with a host. Everything else is showing up early, dressing for sun, and letting the day happen.

What the day actually is

Calle Ocho is the marquee event of Carnaval Miami, the Kiwanis Club of Little Havana's season of fundraisers, and it is best understood as Little Havana turning itself inside out for a day. Music stages anchor intersections along the fifteen blocks, playing salsa, merengue, reggaeton, and Latin pop in rotation, and the blocks between them fill with food vendors: lechon, empanadas, croquetas, fresh juices, cafecito, and every Caribbean and Latin American kitchen the neighborhood can field. The regular businesses of Calle Ocho, the cigar rollers, the domino players of Maximo Gomez Park at SW 15th Avenue, the record shops and galleries, all stay open and become part of the festival. The crowd is the show as much as the stages: families who have done this for decades, dancers in the street, and visitors who planned a Miami trip around one Sunday in March.

The wider Carnaval Miami

Calle Ocho is the finale, not the whole season. Carnaval Miami builds through late February and March with events across the community, including Carnaval on the Mile, the art and music weekend in Coral Gables (March 6 to 7, 2027, per the Greater Miami Convention and Visitors Bureau), and the domino tournament tradition that leads into festival weekend (March 13, 2027, per the bureau). If you are in town for the week before, the rhythm is worth catching: the neighborhood warms up for days before the Sunday itself. The Kiwanis' own site, carnavalmiami.com, is the source of record for the official program each year, and the lineup of stage acts typically publishes in the final weeks before the festival.

The VIP Passport Tour, and whether you need it

The one paid layer is the VIP Passport Tour, the festival's hosted upgrade: a guided route through the blocks with scheduled food and drink stops and a host who knows where the lines are not. It suits first timers who want the greatest hits without the guesswork, and groups celebrating something. It is not necessary. Calle Ocho is built to be wandered, the food is sold street by street at street prices, and the stages are free and visible from the road. Spend the upgrade money only if the hosting itself is the point; otherwise your budget is better aimed at the food stands.

Getting there and pacing the day

Fifteen closed blocks means the streets around Little Havana are a parking puzzle with no solution; do not drive in. The workable plans are rideshare or taxi to a drop point a few blocks outside the closure (around SW 27th Avenue or the side streets off SW 12th Avenue), or transit into the area early, accepting a slow ride home. The festival runs 11 a.m. to 7 p.m., and the shape of the day is consistent: late morning is for eating while lines are short, early afternoon is the heat and the peak crowd, and the last two hours bring the biggest stage sets and the best light. Sun is the main hazard, then dehydration; the domino park's shade and the indoor businesses are the reset points. Families tend to come early and leave by mid afternoon; the party deepens as they go. Frequently asked questions

Is Calle Ocho free?
Yes. The festival is free and unticketed; you pay only for food, drink, and any upgrades like the VIP Passport Tour.

Where exactly is it?
On SW 8th Street, Calle Ocho, between SW 12th and SW 27th Avenues in Little Havana, with stages and vendors along all fifteen blocks.

What should I bring?
Sun protection, comfortable shoes, cash friendly payment options for small vendors, and patience for the peak afternoon crowd. Arrive before noon for the easiest version of the day.

Keep planning

March in Miami is crowded with reasons to visit; the festivals guide puts Calle Ocho next to the Miami Open and the run into Music Week, and the events hub holds the full calendar.
