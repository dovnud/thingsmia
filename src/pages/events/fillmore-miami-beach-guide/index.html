Fillmore Miami Beach Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Fillmore Miami Beach at the Jackie Gleason Theater, 1700 Washington Avenue in Miami Beach, is the city's favorite mid size room: 2,713 capacity, a historic Art Deco shell next to the Miami Beach Convention Center, and a calendar that runs from rock and Latin concerts to A list stand up and dance. The building's story began in 1950 as the Miami Beach Municipal Auditorium; Jackie Gleason moved his television show here in 1964, the city renamed the theater for him in 1987, and after a full renovation it reopened in 2007 as a Fillmore, operated by Live Nation. Every seat is close, the chandeliers are original glamour, and the free Miami Beach trolley stops outside.

A short and remarkable history

Few American venues have lived this many lives in one skin. The auditorium opened in 1950 into Miami Beach's golden age, hosting song and dance revues, pageants, and boxing. Television changed it: The Dick Clark Show and The Ed Sullivan Show filmed here, and in 1964 Jackie Gleason brought The Jackie Gleason Show to Miami Beach, declaring its audiences the greatest in the world, and broadcasting the city into American living rooms every week. In the 1970s it became the Miami Beach Theater of the Performing Arts under Morris Lapidus' renovation, a touring Broadway house (Gypsy with Angela Lansbury played here), and in the 1980s a classical stage for Horowitz, Perlman, and Nureyev. The city renamed it for Gleason in 1987, the year of his death. The 2007 transformation into The Fillmore added rock and roll bones, modern sound, and the Fillmore's signature chandeliers and poster art, while keeping the Deco exterior and the grand lobby. The venue's own telling of this story hangs in its history; it is worth arriving early enough to read the walls.

The room and its formats

At 2,713 capacity (the venue's own figure), the Fillmore is the largest room in Miami where a show still feels like an event rather than a logistics exercise. The layout is classic theater: an orchestra level, a mezzanine, and a balcony, in a single steep rake that keeps sightlines honest. Format changes by event. For many concerts the orchestra floor runs as general admission standing, with the mezzanine and balcony seated; that is the high energy configuration, and the front of the mezzanine is the connoisseur's seat, close, elevated, and seated while the floor surges. For comedy, dance, and theatrical shows the room seats fully. Sound is a house strength: the room was tuned in the Fillmore renovation and carries both a whisper set and a full band without strain. Bars ring the lobby and the Gleason Room hosts smaller events and pre shows; the whole building runs on the Live Nation ticketing system, so buy through the venue or its official seller.

What plays here, and the neighborhood around it

The Fillmore's calendar is the most varied in Miami: touring rock and indie, Latin superstars, jam bands, comedians at their theater peak, podcast live shows, and special dance and orchestral nights. It shares its block with the Miami Beach Convention Center, which means Art Basel week and convention nights double the neighborhood's energy; the fair's own shows sometimes spill into the Fillmore itself. Lincoln Road is a five minute walk south, which makes the classic Fillmore night: dinner on Lincoln Road, the show, and a walk back along the promenade for a nightcap. Getting there

The theater stands at 1700 Washington Avenue, at 17th Street, on the west side of the convention center. The free Miami Beach trolley loops past on Washington Avenue and covers the beach end to end; from South Beach hotels it is often the fastest route. The city's 17th Street garages, a block or two away, are the standard drive in answer, and they fill on double header nights when the convention center is also live, so arrive before the dinner crowd, not after. Rideshare pickup after a show clears faster a block off Washington. Staying nearby is easy: the Collins Avenue corridor hotels, covered in our Loews and Betsy guides, are a short walk or one trolley ride away.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the Fillmore Miami Beach?
2,713 capacity, in a three level theater layout of orchestra, mezzanine, and balcony. It is Miami's largest true theater room.

Is the orchestra floor seated or standing?
It depends on the show: general admission standing for many concerts, fully seated for comedy, dance, and theater. Your ticket states the format; check before you buy.

What is the Jackie Gleason connection?
Gleason moved his television show to this auditorium in 1964 and filmed here for years. The city renamed the theater in his honor in 1987, and the Fillmore name was added in 2007.
