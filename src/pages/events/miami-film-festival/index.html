Miami Film Festival Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Miami Film Festival is Miami Dade College's annual celebration of cinema, and its 44th edition runs Thursday April 1 to Sunday April 11, 2027. It is a working international festival with a specialty no other major United States festival matches: it is the leading showcase for Ibero-American cinema in the country, premiering films from Spain, Latin America, and the Latino United States alongside world cinema, documentaries, and Florida made work. Around a hundred or more features and shorts screen across its run (the exact program publishes in early 2027), with filmmakers in attendance for question and answer sessions after most screenings. Its fall sibling, the GEMS festival, runs October 29 to November 5, 2026, with a concentrated program of awards season titles.

What kind of festival is it?

Miami Dade College has run the festival for decades as a public, audience facing event rather than an industry closed shop, and that shapes everything about it. Screenings are ticketed to the public, the galas are big civic nights, and the audience is Miami itself: multilingual, film literate, and loyal. The programming leans into the city's identity. Ibero-American cinema is the historic heart, and the festival's premieres of Spanish language films routinely draw their directors and stars to Miami for the weekend. Around that core sit international festival circuit titles fresh from Sundance, Berlin, and Cannes, a documentary program, Florida filmmaker showcases, and retrospectives. For a visitor, the pleasure is the mix: a Cuban or Argentine premiere with its director answering questions in two languages is a night out that exists almost nowhere else in the United States.

GEMS: the fall festival, on now

The festival's autumn edition, GEMS, runs October 29 to November 5, 2026, which means it is on as this page publishes. GEMS is the concentrated version: a curated slate of the year's most anticipated international and awards season films, screened over about a week in the fall, and it is where Miami's film crowd warms up for the main event in April. If you are in town for it, treat GEMS as the sampler and April as the feast. Both festivals are Miami Dade College productions, and both sell through the festival's own site, miamifilmfestival.com.

Venues and tickets: how attending works

The festival screens at cinemas across Miami; the exact venue list publishes with the full program in early 2027, and in recent editions the screens have been concentrated downtown and in Miami Beach, Coral Gables, and Little Havana, close to the college's Wolfson Campus base at 300 NE 2nd Avenue downtown. Tickets work the festival way: passes and member presales open first, then individual screening tickets, with rush lines at each venue for sold out shows, which release unclaimed seats minutes before the start. A pass pays if you will see a film a day or more; otherwise, buy the three or four titles you care about on the day single tickets open, and use rush lines for the rest. Screenings start on time, question and answer sessions run long in the best way, and the social center of the festival is the sidewalk outside each cinema between shows.

Planning the trip

April is one of Miami's sweet spots: warm, mostly dry, and priced below the winter peak, though the festival overlaps the tail of spring break travel. Stay downtown or in Brickell if the downtown screens dominate the year's venue list, or in Miami Beach if they do not; either way, the festival's venues are spread widely enough that no single base wins every night. Build the trip with unhurried days, beaches and neighborhoods, and two films a night, rather than attempting a critic's schedule. One more habit of regulars: the opening and closing night galas sell first and double as the festival's social peaks, so if you want one dressed up night at the movies during the run, those are the tickets to chase the day sales open, and the gatherings around them are where Miami's film world holds its annual reunion.
