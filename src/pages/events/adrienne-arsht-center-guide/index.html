Adrienne Arsht Center Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Adrienne Arsht Center for the Performing Arts is Miami's cultural front door: two great halls facing each other across Biscayne Boulevard at 1300 Biscayne Boulevard in downtown Miami, joined by the Thomson Plaza for the Arts. The Sanford and Dolores Ziff Ballet Opera House seats 2,400 and carries Broadway tours, ballet, and opera. The John S. and James L. Knight Concert Hall seats 2,200 and carries orchestras, jazz, and concerts. A third space, the Carnival Studio Theater, is a black box of up to 250 seats. The center opened in 2006, was designed by Cesar Pelli, and is home to resident companies Florida Grand Opera and Miami City Ballet. The Metromover station outside is named for the center itself, and the free train is the smart way in.

The campus: two halls and a plaza

The Arsht campus occupies two city blocks in the Arts and Entertainment District, north of downtown proper, and its geography is the first thing to understand. The Ziff Ballet Opera House stands on the east side of Biscayne Boulevard, its entrance on NE 13th Street and through the plaza; the Knight Concert Hall stands on the west side, entered from NE 14th Street. Between them, the Parker and Vann Thomson Plaza for the Arts stretches across the boulevard itself, an outdoor gathering space that hosts free community performances and serves as the center's front yard. On a performance night the plaza fills with both audiences at once, the boulevard's traffic slows to a promenade, and the whole district feels like the city's living room. The center's scale is civic: it is one of the largest performing arts centers in the United States, and it was built, at a cost reported in the hundreds of millions, to give a young city a permanent cultural anchor. It worked.

Ziff Ballet Opera House: the big stage

The Ziff, at 2,400 seats spread over an orchestra level and four tiers, is where the spectacle lives. It is the home stage of Miami City Ballet and Florida Grand Opera, and the room for the Broadway in Miami touring series, which brings five national tours a season (the current season's titles are in our theater guide). The house was built for dance and opera first, with a full fly tower and orchestra pit, and Broadway inherits that machinery. Seating guidance regulars repeat: the tiers are steeply raked and genuinely good for dance, where the full stage picture matters; for musicals, center orchestra and the first tier balance sound and view; the top tier is the value play and better than its price suggests. The Carnival Studio Theater sits inside the Ziff building, a flexible black box of up to 250 seats for plays, cabaret, and comedy, entered from Biscayne Boulevard between 13th and 14th Streets.

Knight Concert Hall: the listening room

Across the plaza, the Knight Concert Hall seats 2,200 over an orchestra level and three tiers, including 200 choral riser seats behind the stage that put audience members inside the sound for big choral and orchestral works. The hall was built as a room within a room for acoustic isolation, and it is the city's reference concert space. Its signature series tell the story: the Jazz Roots series, the classical program that brings the Cleveland Orchestra's Miami residency and soloists like Yo-Yo Ma and Itzhak Perlman (both on the 2026-27 calendar), flamenco and gospel festivals, and Miami performances by the New World Symphony. If you only attend one Arsht night as a visitor, a Knight Hall concert is the purest version of what the building is for.

Planning a visit

Getting in: the Metromover's Adrienne Arsht Center station sits at the campus, and the free loop connects to Government Center and the Metrorail; driving, the center runs valet and nearby lot and garage parking, and Biscayne Boulevard on a two show night is predictably slow. Arrive early enough for the plaza, where pre show drinks and people watching are part of the ticket. The center's box office is the primary seller for everything under its roof, and subscriptions for the Broadway, ballet, opera, and classical series are the discount mechanism; single ticket buyers should watch the on sale dates for the big titles. Dining options ring the district and downtown is five minutes south; our restaurant guides cover both. Dress smart casual and you will match the room, opening nights excepted.

Frequently asked questions

Which hall is which?
The Ziff Ballet Opera House (2,400 seats) hosts Broadway tours, ballet, and opera. The Knight Concert Hall (2,200 seats) hosts orchestral, jazz, and concert programming. They face each other across Biscayne Boulevard, joined by the Thomson Plaza.

How do I get there without driving?
Ride the free Metromover to the Adrienne Arsht Center station, which is named for the center and sits at the campus. It connects to the Metrorail at Government Center.

Where do the ballet and opera perform?
Both resident companies, Miami City Ballet and Florida Grand Opera, perform in the Ziff Ballet Opera House. Their seasons are covered in our company guides, linked from this page.
