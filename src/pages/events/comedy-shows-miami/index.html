Comedy Shows in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami comedy comes in three sizes. The biggest names play theater and arena one nighters at the Fillmore Miami Beach, the Kaseya Center downtown, and Hard Rock Live in Hollywood. The week in, week out stand up scene belongs to the Miami Improv Comedy Club and Dinner Theater at CityPlace Doral, the city's dedicated comedy club, which books national headliners every weekend and local showcases midweek. And underneath both sits a busy Spanish language comedy scene, in a city where a huge share of the audience is bilingual and the biggest Latin comics in the world route through Miami on every tour.

The club: Miami Improv at CityPlace Doral

The Miami Improv Comedy Club and Dinner Theater, at 3450 NW 83rd Avenue, Suite 224, in CityPlace Doral, is the room that carries Miami stand up on its back. It is a classic dinner theater club layout: cabaret tables, a low stage, a full kitchen and bar, and a calendar built on national touring headliners doing multiple shows across a weekend, typically an early and a late show on Fridays and Saturdays. Weeknights run to showcases, open mics, and themed nights, which is where the local scene shows itself. Expect the standard comedy club economics: a ticket plus a two item minimum per person once you are seated. Doral is a drive from the beach, about 25 to 35 minutes depending on traffic, and CityPlace's garages make parking the easy part. The club's site, miamiimprov.com, carries the calendar and sells the tickets; buy there rather than through resellers, because club shows that sell out get marked up hard.

Theater and arena comedy

Above club level, Miami's comedy ladder is the same ladder as its concerts. The Fillmore Miami Beach (2,713 seats) is the sweet spot for major stand up tours and comedy podcasts recording live, and its theater seating means no drink minimum and a proper intermission format; the Fillmore guide covers the room. Hard Rock Live in Hollywood (7,000 seats) books the superstar tier, the comics who can fill a small arena, and the Kaseya Center downtown takes the handful of arena level acts each year. The Arsht Center's Knight Concert Hall also hosts comedy one nighters inside its classical calendar. For any of these, the buying rules from our concerts guide apply exactly: official seller first, beware the resale markup, and know that late shows on a double header night start when the early crowd clears, not at the printed time.

Spanish language comedy: Miami's second circuit

Miami is a capital of Spanish language stand up. Latin American and Spanish comics sell out Miami rooms on tours that skip most other United States cities, the local scene produces its own stars, and the international Spanish language comedy festival Miami es un Chiste ("Miami is a Joke") has staged editions at the Miami Improv, positioning the city as a hub for Latino comedy. If your Spanish is good, these shows are among the most fun nights in town, and they sell on word of mouth inside the community, so they are easy to miss on the English language listings. The same is true in reverse for the city's bilingual local comics, who switch languages mid set in a way that only works in Miami.

Planning a comedy night

Three things regulars know. First, the late show is the looser show: headliners play freer, crowds are drunker, and running times stretch, so pick early for a dinner after or late for the real club feel. Second, seating order inside clubs is usually by arrival for general admission tables, so the line is part of the ticket strategy; arrive at doors if you care where you sit. Third, age policies vary by room and by show, with clubs generally 18 or 21 and over for late shows and theaters admitting younger audiences for appropriate acts, so check the event page before bringing teenagers. Photography rules are strict everywhere now: phones stay away during sets, and ushers enforce it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main comedy club in Miami?
The Miami Improv at CityPlace Doral is the city's dedicated stand up club, booking national headliners every weekend and showcases midweek.

Where do big name comics play in Miami?
The Fillmore Miami Beach for theater tours, Hard Rock Live in Hollywood for the superstar tier, and the Kaseya Center downtown for the arena level acts.

Is there comedy in Spanish in Miami?
Yes, a full parallel circuit. Spanish language comics tour through Miami constantly, and the Miami es un Chiste festival has staged editions at the Miami Improv in Doral.
