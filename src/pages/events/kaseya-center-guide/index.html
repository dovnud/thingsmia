Kaseya Center Visitor Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Kaseya Center is downtown Miami's arena, at 601 Biscayne Boulevard on Biscayne Bay, and the home of the Miami Heat. It seats 19,600 for basketball and up to about 20,000 for center stage concerts, and it is one of the easiest arenas in America to reach without a car: the free Metromover stops a short walk away, and Bayside Marketplace sits next door for dinner before the doors. The building opened at the end of 1999 as the American Airlines Arena; it carried the FTX Arena name briefly, spent a season as the Miami-Dade Arena, and has been the Kaseya Center since April 2023. Heat games and arena concerts are its twin pillars, and this guide covers both.

The arena in brief

The Kaseya Center stands on the downtown waterfront between Bayfront Park and the PortMiami bridge, which gives it the best arrival view of any arena in the league: the bowl faces the bay, and the building's glass and terraces open onto the water. Miami-Dade County owns it, and the Heat's operating company runs it, which shows in the polish of the event operation. Inside, the bowl is steeper and closer than most arenas of its size, wrapped by wide concourses with a strong local food showing for an arena, from Cuban sandwiches to stone crabs in the premium areas. The court carries the name Pat Riley Court at Kaseya Center, a nod to the coach and executive who built the Heat's championship era; the Heat's three title banners (2006, 2012, 2013) hang over the floor. Seating: games versus concerts

For basketball, the 19,600 seats stack in a lower bowl, a club and suite ring, and an upper bowl, and the honest secret of this arena is that the upper bowl's first rows, hanging over the court, are better than the back rows of many lower bowls. Courtside and the club seats along the sidelines are the premium inventory; the American Airlines branded lounges of earlier years have given way to current sponsors, but the layout of clubs on the suite level persists. Concerts reconfigure the room three ways: end stage (the most common, seating roughly three quarters of the bowl), center stage in the round (the full 20,000 plus), and half house theater setups for smaller shows, down to about 5,000. For end stage concerts, the floor is either seated or general admission depending on the artist, and the sections behind the stage stay dark. Check the event's own seating map when you buy; in this building the difference between a good and a poor concert seat is usually about angle, not distance.

Getting there: take the train

The Metromover is the answer. Miami's free downtown circulator stops at Freedom Tower and College North stations, each a short walk from the arena doors, and it connects at Government Center to the Metrorail, which runs to Miami International Airport's station and down the US 1 corridor to the southern suburbs. On event nights the mover runs full and frequent until the crowd clears. Driving is possible and slower: the arena district garages and the Bayside and downtown lots fill early on big nights, and Biscayne Boulevard locks up after the final buzzer or encore. Rideshare has designated pickup areas a block or two from the doors; walking those blocks before ordering is the difference between a ten minute and a forty minute wait. Brightline riders arrive at MiamiCentral, a few blocks west, an easy walk or one Metromover stop.

Before and after

Bayside Marketplace, the open air mall immediately north of the arena, exists for exactly this: bayfront restaurants, bars, and the marina, full of people in jerseys and concert shirts from two hours before doors. Downtown and Brickell widen the options; our restaurant guides cover the neighborhoods. The classic arena night runs dinner at Bayside or Brickell, the event, and a walk along the bay afterward, when the skyline is lit and the causeways glow. Bayfront Park to the south, Ultra's home in March, is the green space on the other side. If your event is part of a bigger trip, the arena anchors a downtown stay better than any other Miami venue; the hotel guides start that planning.

The facts that matter

Kaseya Center, checked October 30, 2026: 601 Biscayne Boulevard, Miami, FL 33132. Home of the Miami Heat (NBA). Capacity: 19,600 for basketball; concerts from about 5,000 (half house) to 20,021 (center stage). Opened at the end of 1999. Former names: American Airlines Arena (1999 to 2021), FTX Arena (2021 to 2023), Miami-Dade Arena (2023); renamed Kaseya Center in April 2023. Owner: Miami-Dade County. Transit: free Metromover to Freedom Tower or College North stations; Metrorail via Government Center.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the Kaseya Center?
At 601 Biscayne Boulevard in downtown Miami, on Biscayne Bay, next to Bayside Marketplace and Bayfront Park.

How many people does it hold?
19,600 for Miami Heat basketball. Concerts range from about 5,000 in a half house setup to just over 20,000 for a center stage show.

What is the best way to get there?
The free Metromover to Freedom Tower or College North station, both a short walk from the doors. Driving works but the downtown garages and Biscayne Boulevard jam on event nights.
