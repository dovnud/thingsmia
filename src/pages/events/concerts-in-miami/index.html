Concerts in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami concerts run on a ladder of rooms, and knowing the ladder tells you almost everything about a show before you buy. At the top indoors is the Kaseya Center on Biscayne Bay downtown, home of the Miami Heat and the city's arena concert room at 19,600 seats for basketball and around 20,000 for concerts. Below it sit Hard Rock Live at the Seminole Hard Rock in Hollywood at 7,000 seats, the Fillmore Miami Beach at 2,713, and the two Adrienne Arsht Center halls downtown at 2,400 and 2,200. Above the arena, the very biggest tours step outside to Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens or loanDepot park in Little Havana. Tickets are sold through the venues and their official sellers, and the golden rule is simple: start from the venue's own event page and buy through the seller it links to.

The rooms, from biggest to most intimate

Stadium shows. A handful of tours each year are simply too big for arenas. Those land at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens, the Dolphins' home, or at loanDepot park, the Marlins' retractable roof ballpark in Little Havana. Expect festival scale logistics: prebooked parking, long walks, and a crowd measured in tens of thousands. Our Miami sports hub covers both stadiums from a visitor's angle.

Kaseya Center, downtown. The default arena stop for major tours, at 601 Biscayne Boulevard on the bay. Basketball configuration is 19,600 seats; concerts run from about 5,000 in a theater setup to just over 20,000 for a center stage show. Our Kaseya Center visitor guide covers seating, arrival, and the Metromover in detail.

Hard Rock Live, Hollywood. A 7,000 seat indoor theater inside the Seminole Hard Rock Hotel and Casino in Hollywood, in Broward County, roughly 30 to 45 minutes north of downtown Miami by car. Its clamshell seating keeps every seat close, which is why so many legacy acts and residencies choose it. Read the Hard Rock Live visitor guide before you plan the drive.

The Fillmore Miami Beach. The historic Jackie Gleason Theater at 1700 Washington Avenue, now a 2,713 capacity concert room run by Live Nation, a short walk from Lincoln Road. This is the sweet spot size for rock, Latin, and comedy tours. See the Fillmore visitor guide.

The Arsht Center halls. Classical, jazz, and acoustic tours play the Knight Concert Hall (2,200 seats) and the Ziff Ballet Opera House (2,400) at the Adrienne Arsht Center, 1300 Biscayne Boulevard. The Arsht Center visitor guide explains both rooms.

How tickets are sold

Most Miami concerts sell through one official primary seller per venue, linked from the venue's own calendar, with the venue box office selling the same inventory in person. Three habits protect you. First, buy from the venue page outward; search ads for big shows are full of resale sites charging well above face value for tickets they may not yet hold. Second, expect presales: artist fan clubs, venue newsletters, and cardholder presales routinely open a day or two before the public on sale, and for arena shows the public sale is where the cheapest seats disappear in minutes. Third, treat prices as dynamic. Primary sellers adjust prices with demand, so a seat can cost more on Friday than it did at the presale, and can also drop in the final week if a show has not sold through. If a show is sold out, use the venue's official resale exchange if one is offered, and never buy a screenshot of a mobile ticket from a stranger: Miami venues run on mobile entry, and a screenshot is not a ticket.

When the big nights cluster

Miami's concert calendar is not evenly spread. It piles up around the event weeks that already fill the hotels. March is the peak: Miami Music Week and Ultra pull arena and club shows into the same days. December stacks concerts around Art Basel and the holidays, and New Year's Eve adds one off arena and ballroom shows; our New Year's Eve guide tracks that night. The practical effect: if your concert falls in a festival week, book the hotel the day you buy the ticket, not the week of the show. The Miami festivals guide maps the crowded weekends across the year.

Getting there, and getting home

Downtown is the easy case. The Metromover, Miami's free downtown circulator, stops a short walk from both the Kaseya Center and the Arsht Center, and the Metrorail feeds it from the airport corridor and the south suburbs. Driving downtown on an arena night means garage queues on Biscayne Boulevard; the train genuinely wins. For the Fillmore, the free Miami Beach trolley and the 17th Street garages cover most arrivals. Hard Rock Live is the car venue: it sits off State Road 7 in Hollywood with large on site garages, and a rideshare back to Miami Beach late at night is a long, surge priced ride, so groups often do better driving or staying nearby. Wherever the show is, plan the exit before the encore: leaving with 19,000 people is the slowest part of any Miami concert night.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest concert venue in Miami?
For stadium tours, Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens. Indoors, the Kaseya Center downtown is the largest room, at about 20,000 for a center stage concert.

Where should I stay for a concert?
Match the hotel to the room: downtown or Brickell for the Kaseya Center and the Arsht Center, Miami Beach for the Fillmore, and Hollywood or Fort Lauderdale, or Miami with a planned drive, for Hard Rock Live. Our Miami hotel guides cover the main areas.

Can I buy tickets at the door?
Venue box offices sell on show day when a concert is not sold out, and that can dodge online fees. For anything popular, assume it will sell out and buy ahead through the official seller.
