The Everglades gear kit: what to wear and carry, by activity

Last reviewed: November 6, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Everglades punishes the wrong kit in three different ways, because an airboat ride, the Shark Valley tram and a Shark Valley bike ride are three different days. The National Park Service puts its whole advice in one line: bring water, insect repellent, sunscreen and proper clothing. The details decide whether that line saves your day. Shark Valley's loop is 15 miles with no shade along the path and no drinking water available along the way. An airboat is loud enough that operators hand out or sell ear protection. Mosquitoes run the wet season, roughly May to November, and bite hardest at dawn, dusk and on overcast days. Build the kit for the activity you booked, from the lists below, and leave the beach bag logic at the hotel.

The base kit, for every Everglades day

Water comes first, in quantity. The Park Service tells visitors to bring water, especially in summer, and at Shark Valley it states plainly that adequate drinking water matters because none may be available along the way. Carry more than you think a two hour outing needs. Heat here doesn't negotiate.

Sun protection is the second base layer: sunscreen, a hat and light colored clothing that covers skin. The park's own safety page pairs that with the insect advice below. Closed shoes belong in the base kit too. Trails, boardwalks and wet grass are not sandal ground, and the one pair of water shoes on Amazon with real tread will outlast three pairs of flip flops out here.

Insect repellent: what the EPA registration means

The park's safety guidance is direct: mosquitoes are plentiful at certain times of year, you protect yourself with long sleeves and pants and repellent, and they're more common on overcast days and at dawn and dusk, especially during summer. The wet season, May to November, is when the pressure builds; the dry season, December to April, is the easier window, which is one reason winter is the park's busy season.

For the bottle itself, buy an EPA registered product. The Environmental Protection Agency lists the skin applied repellent active ingredients it has registered: DEET, picaridin, IR3535, oil of lemon eucalyptus and its component PMD, 2-undecanone, catnip oil and oil of citronella. DEET and picaridin are the two with the deepest product ranges. Registration is the part that matters: it means the EPA has reviewed the product's data, and it's the check to run on any bottle a gift shop hands you. EPA registered repellents on Amazon are a category search away; match the ingredient on the label to the list above and you're done. Clothing does the other half of the work, so add a light long sleeve layer you can ride in: UPF sun and insect clothing on Amazon covers both lists at once.

The airboat kit

An airboat is an open boat behind a very large fan, and the kit starts with your ears. Coopertown Airboats, an authorized National Park Service concessioner since 1947, provides complimentary ear protection for all guests. Everglades Holiday Park makes ear protection available for purchase on request. Translation: ask your operator before you go, and if the answer is purchase on site, bring a few dollars or your own. Don't ride unprotected and call it atmosphere. It's just loud.

Everglades Holiday Park's own what to bring guidance adds sunglasses that protect against UV rays, a wide brimmed hat and light colored clothing. Follow it. Glare off the water is the airboat's second weather. Dress in clothes that can handle wind and a possible damp seat, keep phones and hats secured against the fan's wash, and put the camera in a dry bag from Amazon between stops. The tram and bike kit: Shark Valley

Shark Valley is a 15 mile loop road: 7 miles out to the observation tower and 8 miles back, no private cars, done by guided tram in about two hours or by bicycle in two to four hours. The Park Service states the fact that shapes the whole kit: there's no shade available along the path, so sun protection and water aren't advice, they're the entry fee. Riders should plan on carrying every drop they drink. The tram rider's kit is the base kit plus a hat that stays on in a breeze. The cyclist's kit adds more water again, a repair aware mindset (you're your own support vehicle out there), and an early start. A winter morning is the kind version of this ride. A summer afternoon is the other one. Choose the morning.

What to leave behind

Leave the cooler sized food bag, the beach umbrella and the heavy camera rig you're not insured on. The park rewards the walker who carries water, cover and repellent and punishes the one who packed for a picnic. One small pack, the lists above, and a phone with the tour's meeting point saved offline. That's the kit. It fits in a daypack and it works on all three days, with the airboat and bike additions slotted in where they belong.

The facts that matter

Everglades gear facts checked November 6, 2026: the National Park Service advises bringing water, insect repellent, sunscreen and proper clothing, and says mosquitoes are more common on overcast days and at dawn and dusk, especially in summer, with the wet season running May to November and the dry season December to April (nps.gov). Shark Valley's tram road loop is 15 miles, with no shade along the path and no drinking water available along the way; the bike ride takes two to four hours (nps.gov). Coopertown Airboats provides complimentary ear protection; Everglades Holiday Park sells it on request and advises UV sunglasses, a wide brimmed hat and light colored clothing (operator sites). EPA registered skin applied repellent ingredients include DEET, picaridin, IR3535, oil of lemon eucalyptus and PMD, 2-undecanone, catnip oil and oil of citronella (epa.gov, page updated January 23, 2026).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need repellent in winter?
Less than in summer, and the Park Service still lists it in the base advice. Dry season pressure is lower, not zero, and dawn and dusk are the hours that matter most. Carry a small bottle.

Are airboats safe for kids' ears?
With the ear protection on, that's the operator's system working as designed. Check whether your operator provides it or sells it, and fit it before the fan starts, not after.

Can I do Shark Valley in sandals?
You can, and the Park Service's proper clothing line argues you shouldn't. Fifteen miles of exposed road, wet edges and fire ants in the grass are a closed shoe argument. Our Miami packing list keeps the Everglades day inside the general trip kit.
