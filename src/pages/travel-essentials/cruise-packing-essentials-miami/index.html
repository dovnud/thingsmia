Cruise Packing Essentials for a Miami Sailing

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Pack the cruise in two halves: a carry-on bag you keep with you at PortMiami holding your documents, medications, a change of clothes, swimwear, sunscreen, and chargers, and a checked suitcase for everything else, which can take until the evening to reach your cabin. Sort the documents first, using your cruise line's own requirements, because the one unforgivable packing error is the right passport or ID sitting in the wrong bag, or at home.

What documents do you pack, and where?

Documents travel in your carry-on, on your person or in the bag that never leaves your hand at the terminal, never in luggage you hand over. What you need depends on your citizenship, your itinerary, and your cruise line, so treat your line's documentation page as the authority and check it before you pack, not the night before you sail.

The United States State Department's guidance for cruise passengers, linked in the fact box, is the right baseline. It says your cruise company may require you to have a passport book even when border rules do not, and it strongly recommends that all cruise passengers travel with a passport book, even when one is not required, because a passport book is what you need to fly home internationally in an emergency. It also notes that a passport card can be used to reenter the United States at seaports of entry from Mexico, Canada, Bermuda, and the Caribbean, but cannot be used to fly back. Some closed loop sailings from United States ports accept other document combinations for United States citizens under border rules, but your line can set a stricter requirement than the border does. Check your cruise line's requirements for your exact sailing, and check the State Department's page for your ports.

What goes in your embarkation day bag?

Documents and boarding passes, prescription medications in their original containers, phone and charger, one full change of clothes, swimwear and sunscreen so the first afternoon is usable, a light layer for over air conditioned terminals and dining rooms, and anything you would be genuinely stuck without for six hours. Bags handed over at the terminal are delivered to cabins through embarkation day and can arrive toward the evening, so pack the carry-on as if the suitcase shows up at dinner.

What cabin gear is worth packing?

Packing cubes earn their space in a cabin with limited drawers. Magnetic hooks work on the steel walls many cabins have and add hanging space for hats, lanyards, and wet swimwear. A small night light helps in an inside cabin that is fully dark at noon. Wrinkle release spray does the job of the iron you should not pack. For power, bring a multi port USB charger; if you want a power strip, many lines prohibit surge protected models and some prohibit strips entirely, so check your line's prohibited items list before it goes in the bag.

What do you pack for port days?

Most Miami sailings call in the Bahamas or the Caribbean, so the port day kit overlaps with a beach kit: swimwear, sunscreen you reapply, a waterproof phone pouch, and a small dry bag. Carry ashore the identification your cruise line tells you to carry, which is usually your ship card plus photo ID, and follow your line's guidance on whether your passport comes ashore or stays in the cabin safe. Our beach packing essentials page covers the full kit.

What should you leave out?

Anything on your line's prohibited items list. The categories that most often catch people out are heating appliances such as travel irons and kettles, candles and incense, weapons and lookalike items, and surge protected power strips. Lists differ between lines and change, so the only reliable version is the one your cruise line publishes for your sailing.

The facts that matter

United States State Department, cruise ship passengers guidance (checked October 26, 2026): a cruise company may require a passport book even if Customs and Border Protection or the destination country does not; the Department strongly recommends all cruise passengers travel with a passport book, because one is needed to fly home on an international flight in an emergency; a passport card works for reentry at seaports from Mexico, Canada, Bermuda, and the Caribbean, but not for international flights. Read it in full at travel.state.gov, Cruise Ships, and confirm requirements with your cruise line.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a passport for a Bahamas cruise from Miami?
It depends on your citizenship, your itinerary, and your cruise line, and the cruise line's requirement wins where it is stricter. The State Department strongly recommends a passport book for every cruise passenger regardless, since it is the document that lets you fly home in an emergency. Confirm on your line's documentation page and at travel.state.gov before you sail.

Can you pack a power strip?
Check your line's prohibited items list. Surge protected strips are commonly prohibited, and rules on plain strips and multi plugs vary by line, so this is a verify before you pack item, not a standard inclusion.

When should you arrive in Miami for a sailing?
The day before, as a rule. A same day flight into Miami leaves no slack for delays on embarkation morning, and a night near the port turns the sailing day into a short transfer. See hotels for cruise passengers for where to stay.
