Best Portable Chargers for Travel

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Buy capacity by your day, not your anxiety. A slim 10,000 milliamp hour bank covers a full Miami day of maps, photos and rideshares with a phone top up or two. The 20,000 class covers couples, tablets and long boat days. Everything flies in the cabin only, never checked, and the right cable matters as much as the bank. Capacity math without the fog

A modern phone battery holds roughly 4,000 to 5,000 milliamp hours. Conversion losses mean a 10,000 bank delivers about one and a half to two full phone charges, a 20,000 about three to four. Miami days are charge hungry: navigation in bright sun at full brightness, camera at the murals, video at the beach club. If that is your day, the 10,000 is the minimum, and cruise passengers should note cabins still run short of outlets, which makes the bank a nightstand citizen too.

Flight rules, exactly

Power banks travel in carry on baggage only; checked carriage is prohibited by airline rules worldwide. Capacity limits sit far above consumer banks for standard carriage, with the largest banks sometimes needing airline approval; the 10,000 and 20,000 classes are unproblematic. Keep the bank accessible, because some carriers and airports ask to see ratings printed on the case. A bank with a worn off rating print is a bank that invites questions; buy one with the specs legibly printed.

Fast charging and cables

USB C Power Delivery is the current standard for fast top ups on both major phone platforms; match the bank's output to your phone's fast charge capability and carry the short cable that came with neither in a drawer at home. Multi port banks serve couples from one pocket on a Everglades day or a boat trip. Built in cable models trade a little bulk for never being caught without the cord, a fair trade for beach bags.

Miami specific uses

The charger is beach infrastructure here: it keeps the phone alive for the rideshare home from a remote beach, powers the speaker at a picnic in Bayfront Park, and saves the embarkation day when the cruise app is your boarding pass and the terminal line is long, as our embarkation guide warns. Heat note: never leave the bank baking on a beach towel in direct sun; shade it like you shade yourself.

Heat, sand and charger care

Miami is hard on batteries. Heat accelerates wear, so keep the bank out of direct sun and never charge it inside a closed hot car or under a beach towel in August; charge in shade, ideally in moving air. Sand invades ports: a small pouch keeps the connectors clean, and a blast of air beats a poking key for clearing grit. Battery health habits travel well: top up rather than deep drain, and retire a bank that swells, heats oddly or smells sweet, at a battery recycling point rather than a bin. Treated gently, a good bank outlives several phones' worth of trips.

The facts that matter

Rules stated as of October 2026: spare lithium batteries and power banks are cabin only under IATA aligned airline rules, and individual airlines set their own watt hour approval thresholds above standard sizes. Your airline's dangerous goods page is the authority for your flight. No product names, prices or output claims are endorsed here; match the retail listing's printed specs to your phone.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take a power bank on the plane?
Yes, in your carry on only. Never in checked baggage. Standard sizes need no approval.

How big for a Miami day?
10,000 milliamp hours for a solo phone day, 20,000 for couples, tablets or boat days.

Why does my bank give fewer charges than its number suggests?
Conversion losses of roughly a third are normal. The math in this guide already accounts for them.

Is fast charging worth it?
On travel days, yes: thirty minutes at a cafe can restore half a phone. Match the bank's USB C PD output to your phone.
