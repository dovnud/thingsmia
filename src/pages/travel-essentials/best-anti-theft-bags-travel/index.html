Best Anti Theft Bags for Travel

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami is a normal big city for theft risk: the danger spots are the crowded ones, festival streets, transit and cruise terminals on turnaround day. An anti theft bag buys margin with four features: slash resistant straps and fabric, zippers that lock or hide, an RFID shielded pocket for cards and passport, and a way to anchor the bag to a chair or table. Style decides which you will actually carry: crossbody for city days, backpack for transit and excursions, undercover pouch for the highest crowd days.

The features, ranked by usefulness

Locking or concealed zippers first, because quick unzips in a crowd are the common theft. Slash resistant straps with internal wire second, aimed at the rarer cut and run. RFID shielding third: real but lower risk than the first two, since modern card skimming in the wild is uncommon. Anchoring, a strap that loops a fixed object at a cafe, is the quiet hero at beach restaurants. Water resistance is a Miami bonus feature this whole site keeps recommending, for weather more than thieves.

Styles for Miami days

Crossbody bags carry the Calle Ocho, Wynwood and festival days, worn in front when the crowd thickens. Anti theft backpacks serve the Metrorail, the Brightline and excursion days to the Everglades, with hidden rear pockets against the back. Undercover neck or waist pouches handle the marathon crowd and New Year's Eve on Bayfront, the peak pickpocket stages in our New Year's guide. Choose the style for your biggest crowd day and let it cover the rest.

Cruise port thinking

Port days concentrate every risk factor: crowds, distraction, cash economies and a ship that sails without you. The excursion bag should hold passport copies, cards, the phone and nothing irreplaceable, worn in front in port towns. Our embarkation guide covers the document side; the bag is the physical side of the same discipline. Leave the real jewelry and the second laptop in the cabin safe.

Honest limits

No bag is theft proof against a determined thief with your bag unattended; these features defeat the opportunist, who is the actual threat profile in Miami's tourist districts. Awareness beats equipment: phone out of the back pocket, bag on the lap side away from the street, and the usual city sense that our neighborhood guides assume throughout.

If something is taken: the first hour

Preparation decides how bad a theft gets. Photograph your passport and cards into a secure cloud folder before the trip, and keep a second card and emergency cash separate from the day bag, in the hotel safe. If the bag goes: freeze cards in their apps first, it takes two minutes and stops the spending; report to the local police for the record your insurer and the passport office will want; and use the cruise line or hotel desk for help replacing documents if you are mid itinerary. Travelers who split, copy and freeze lose an afternoon. Travelers who kept everything in one bag lose the trip. The bag features above exist so the afternoon version is the worst case.

Frequently asked questions

Is Miami dangerous for pickpockets?
It is a normal big city: risk concentrates in crowds and transit. Standard awareness plus a secured bag is the proportionate answer.

Is RFID blocking necessary?
Useful, not essential. Locking zips and slash proof straps address the commoner thefts first.

Crossbody or backpack for excursions?
Crossbody for port towns and city days, backpack for transit and gear heavy days. Both in front in the densest crowds.

What should never go in the day bag?
Your only passport when a copy serves, all your cards, and anything irreplaceable. Split money and cards between bag and hotel safe.
