Best Snorkel Gear to Own

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Own the mask, rent the fins. The mask is where fit decides everything, and a mask fitted to your face ends the leak and fog misery that ruins snorkel days. A modern dry top snorkel is a cheap comfort upgrade. Fins are bulky, forgiving to rent, and worth buying only for frequent snorkelers. This guide explains the fit test and the features so your purchase survives more than one trip to the reefs off Miami and the Keys.

The mask: fit is the whole product

Test fit without the strap: place the mask on your face, inhale gently through the nose, and a good mask stays sealed hands free. Tempered glass lenses, a silicone skirt, and a low volume frame that brings glass close to the eyes for a wider view are the quality markers. Prescription lens options exist for glasses wearers and transform the experience for them. Fog is managed by preparation, defog solution or the classic toothpaste treatment on new glass, and by never touching the inside lens wet. Full face masks: the honest note

Full face snorkel masks are easy breathing for calm surface floating, and they are controversial: several tour operators and safety bodies caution against them over carbon dioxide buildup concerns in some designs, and they complicate equalizing and diving down. Traditional mask and snorkel remains the setup guides and boats standardize on, and the one this site recommends for Miami's boat trips. If you already love a full face mask, keep it to gentle surface use in calm water.

Snorkels and fins

A dry top snorkel keeps the chop out on Biscayne Bay's breezy afternoons; a splash guard is the budget version of the same idea. Purge valves are optional and help beginners clear. Fins: short travel fins fit a carry on and serve shore snorkeling; full length fins serve boat trips and currents, and are precisely what rental fleets stock well. Buy fins when you snorkel monthly or when rental fit has failed you twice.

Care and the rest of the kit

Rinse everything in fresh water, dry in shade, store the mask in its box so the skirt keeps its shape. The kit completes with the reef considerate sunscreen from our sunscreen guide, a rash guard that replaces half the sunscreen you would need, and a mesh gear bag that drips dry on the balcony. Pack the mask in your carry on, in the hard case, because a crushed skirt leaks forever.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth buying gear for one trip?
Buy the mask if you will snorkel more than twice in your life. Rent fins per trip until you are a regular.

Why does my mask fog?
New masks carry a factory film; clean it, use defog before each session, and keep fingers off the inner lens.

Are full face masks safe?
They are debated. Operators and safety groups have raised carbon dioxide concerns with some designs; traditional gear is the recommended default here.

Can I snorkel with a mustache or glasses?
Mustaches break the seal under the nose, a dab of petroleum jelly helps. Prescription masks and lens inserts solve glasses honestly.
