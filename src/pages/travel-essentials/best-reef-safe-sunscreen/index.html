Best Reef Safe Sunscreen for Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Reef friendly is a marketing term, not a regulated one, so buy by ingredients instead: mineral filters, zinc oxide and titanium dioxide, ideally non nano, are the filters marine researchers point to as the lower impact choice. In Miami the practical bar is simpler: a broad spectrum SPF 30 to 50 you will actually reapply, water resistant for swim and snorkel days, in a formula your skin tolerates daily. This guide explains the labels so the search below lands on the right tube.

Mineral versus chemical, plainly

Chemical filters such as oxybenzone and octinoxate absorb UV inside the skin; they are the ingredients Hawaii banned from sale over reef concerns, and the ones reef friendly brands exclude. Mineral filters sit on the skin and scatter UV; they can leave a cast, which modern tinted and sheer formulas work to fix. For snorkeling over the reefs off Key Biscayne and in Miami's snorkel spots, mineral is the considerate pick; for a city day under a hat, either compliant formula beats the sunscreen left in the hotel room.

What Florida rules actually say

State law preempts local sunscreen bans in Florida, which is why Key West's voted ban on oxybenzone and octinoxate sales never took effect in 2020. No Miami beach bans your chemical sunscreen. The reef friendly choice here is voluntary and ethical rather than legal, and tour operators on reef trips increasingly ask for it. If your trip continues to Hawaii or certain Caribbean parks and protected areas, their rules do apply, and the mineral tube covers you everywhere.

SPF, water resistance and application math

SPF 30 blocks about 97 percent of UVB in lab conditions; SPF 50 adds a point or so. The number matters less than the dose: two finger lengths for the face, a shot glass for the body, reapplied every two hours and after every swim. Water resistant means 40 or 80 minutes of rated protection in water, not waterproof. For Miami's summer UV, the index routinely runs extreme by midday, so the beach plan in our beaches guides already assumes shade breaks; sunscreen plus shade plus a shirt is the system, not sunscreen alone.

Formats for a Miami trip

Lotion for the base layer, a stick for faces and ears on the move, and a spray only as a top up over lotion, never as the sole coat, because sprays underapply in beach wind. Travel sizes for the carry on, a full tube in the beach bag from our beach bags guide, and lip balm with SPF, the most forgotten burn of the trip. Buy before you fly: resort shop markups are the classic Miami tax.

The facts that matter

Label honesty: no sunscreen is proven reef safe in an absolute sense; reef friendly describes formulas without the specific chemical filters most associated with reef harm in studies, chiefly oxybenzone and octinoxate. Hawaii's sale ban on those filters is in force; Florida preempted its cities from similar bans in 2020. This page states no product names, prices or ratings; ingredient lists on the retailer's page are the check.

Frequently asked questions

Is reef safe sunscreen required in Miami?
No. Florida law prevents local bans. It is the considerate choice for reef snorkeling, and some operators request it.

Does mineral sunscreen work as well?
Yes when applied at the full dose and reapplied on schedule. The best sunscreen remains the one you use properly.

What SPF for Miami in summer?
SPF 30 to 50, water resistant for water days, reapplied every two hours. Add shade and a rash guard for midday.

Can I bring sunscreen in a carry on?
Yes, in containers of 3.4 ounces or less per item in the liquids bag. Full tubes go in checked luggage.
