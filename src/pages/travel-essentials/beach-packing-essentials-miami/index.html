Beach Packing Essentials for Miami

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. For a Miami beach day, pack more sunscreen than feels reasonable, a waterproof pouch for your phone and keys, two swimsuits so one can dry, a hat and a cover up that works for lunch, an insulated bottle, and a small dry bag for the walk back. Add a light rain layer, because Miami beach weather can turn inside an hour. Everything else is optimization.

What sun protection do you actually need?

Bring a full size bottle of broad spectrum sunscreen for the trip, plus a small one that lives in the beach bag for reapplication. Miami sun burns people who tan easily at home, and a single travel bottle does not survive a beach week of reapplications. If you are flying with carry-on only, the TSA liquids rule in the fact box below limits containers to 3.4 ounces, so either pack several minis or buy the big bottle on arrival. Reef friendlier formulas are widely sold and are the kinder choice for the water off Miami; lip balm with SPF and aloe gel for the evening round out the kit.

How do you keep sand and water off your phone and keys?

A waterproof phone pouch with a lanyard is the single highest value item on this page: it keeps sand out of charging ports, survives a waist deep wade, and lets the phone double as your camera. Add a small roll top dry bag for keys, cards, and a shirt, and a plain zip pouch for the damp swimsuit on the way back. Sand brushes off microfiber towels far better than off hotel terry cloth, which is one reason a quick dry travel towel earns its space.

What should you wear and carry?

Two swimsuits, rotated, so you never pull on a wet one in the morning. A cover up that passes as a casual dress or shirt gets you into a beachfront lunch without a trip back to the room. A brimmed hat beats a cap for ears and neck. Sunglasses with a retainer strap survive boat days and paddleboards. For footwear, sandals that rinse clean handle both hot sand and the Miami Beach Beachwalk, and a pair of water shoes helps at the rockier entries around Key Biscayne.

How do you handle Miami heat and sudden rain?

An insulated bottle you refill all day is the difference between a good beach day and a headache; Miami tap water is safe, and most beach hotels have refill stations. Afternoon storms build fast in the warmer months, so a compact umbrella or a packable rain shell in the bag saves the walk back. For evenings near the water or a side trip toward the Everglades, insect repellent earns its place after sunset.

What is worth buying well, and what is fine cheap?

Buy well: the waterproof phone pouch (cheap seams are where pouches fail), sunglasses you will actually wear, and the insulated bottle. Buy cheap: the beach bag itself, sand toys if you are traveling with kids, and a spare pair of flip flops, which are the most lost item on any beach trip. Skip packing: bulky beach towels if your hotel provides them, and your own shade. Check the posted rules at your beach entrance for what shade structures are allowed before buying a canopy to bring.

The facts that matter

The TSA 3-1-1 liquids rule for carry-on bags: liquids, gels, and aerosols go in containers of 3.4 ounces (100 ml) or less, all fitting in one clear quart size bag, one bag per passenger. The limit is on the container size printed on the bottle, not on how much is left inside; a half empty large bottle still goes in checked luggage. Sunscreen counts as a liquid under the rule (TSA guidance, checked October 26, 2026).

Frequently asked questions

What do people forget most often?
A second swimsuit, the phone pouch, and enough sunscreen. All three are cheap at home and noticeably more expensive within a block of the sand.

Can you just buy everything in Miami?
Yes, and plenty of visitors do. The trade off is price and choice near the beach, plus the first morning spent shopping instead of swimming. Pack the personal fit items, buy consumables like sunscreen on arrival if luggage space is tight.

Do you need different gear for a boat or snorkeling day?
Mostly the same bag, plus the dry bag promoted to essential and a strap on the sunglasses. Our snorkeling tours guide covers what operators provide, which usually includes mask and fins.
