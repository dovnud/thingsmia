Miami vs Fort Lauderdale: Where Should You Stay?

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami is the bigger, louder, more international city with the famous beaches, the art and food scenes, and the prices to match. Fort Lauderdale, about 30 miles north, is the calmer, cheaper, boat filled sibling: better value, easier pace, less scene. Neither is a consolation prize. They suit different trips, and this post draws the line honestly.


Miami sells energy. Fort Lauderdale, up the coast, sells the ease Miami had before the world arrived. Both sell the same ocean. Photo: Pexels.

The two cities in one paragraph each

Miami. A global city of about 450,000 in the city proper and over 6 million in the metro, the capital of Latin American business and culture in the US, with Miami Beach's barrier island scene, Wynwood's art, Little Havana, a year round mega event calendar, and hotel rates that spike hard around those events. Everything on this site is the detail.

Fort Lauderdale. Broward County's seat, laced with 165 miles of canals per the city's own tourism materials, which is why it is marketed as the Venice of America and the yachting capital: the Intracoastal, the marinas, the boat show (the Fort Lauderdale International Boat Show is one of the largest in water shows in the world). Its beach is a wide, calm, classic Florida strand along A1A, its downtown runs along Las Olas Boulevard, and its airport (FLL) is a major low cost carrier hub.

Beaches, scene, and food

Beaches: Miami Beach has the architecture, the towers, and the people watching; Fort Lauderdale beach has the same Atlantic with a promenade, volleyball, and a fraction of the crowd and the price. Scene: Miami wins nightlife and events outright; Fort Lauderdale's Las Olas and Wilton Manors (one of the country's best known LGBTQ centers) hold their own at a gentler volume. Food: Miami's Latin and Caribbean depth is unmatched in the US; Fort Lauderdale eats well, especially seafood and waterfront dining, at lower checks. For Miami's case in full, the food culture post and the events hub are the evidence.

Money and logistics, honestly

Fort Lauderdale is the value play: beachfront rooms that would carry Miami Beach prices at half to two thirds in the same week (season and demand move the gap; the direction is consistent), cheaper parking, and FLL's fares often undercut MIA's for domestic trips. Miami counters with the bigger airport for international routes, PortMiami's cruise dominance, and the simple fact that most of the famous list is here. The Brightline train ties the two downtowns in about 35 to 40 minutes, which makes the hybrid trip real: sleep in one, play in the other. Our airport guide covers MIA, and the Bimini ferry page covers the Fort Lauderdale sailing that leaves from Port Everglades.

The verdict, by trip

Choose Miami if

It is your first trip, the events calendar is the draw, food and art matter, or you are sailing from PortMiami. Miami is the headline act.

Choose Fort Lauderdale if

You want the beach week at a calmer tempo and a lower bill, you are boating or boat show bound, or you found the flight deal into FLL. It is a destination, not a suburb.

Do both if

You have five or more days. Brightline makes it one trip: Miami for the scene days, Fort Lauderdale for the slow ones.

Airports, settled

MIA is the international gateway, with the deepest Latin America and Caribbean route map in the country and American's hub banks. FLL is the domestic value airport, heavy with low cost carriers, and often the cheaper ticket for US origins. Flying into FLL for Miami, or MIA for Fort Lauderdale, is rational when the fare gap beats the transfer cost; Brightline's stations in both downtowns, with connections to each airport area, make the cross option cleaner than it used to be.

The facts that matter

Fort Lauderdale sits about 30 miles north of Miami. The city markets 165 miles of inland waterways (Visit Lauderdale and city materials). Brightline connects downtown Miami and Fort Lauderdale in roughly 35 to 40 minutes (Brightline). Frequently asked questions

Can I fly into FLL for a Miami trip?
Yes, and many do. It is about 30 miles; budget the transfer, and the savings often survive it. Brightline from the airport area via the downtown station is the elegant option.

Which beach is nicer?
The sand and water are the same Atlantic. Miami Beach is a scene with a beach attached; Fort Lauderdale beach is a beach with a promenade attached.

Is Fort Lauderdale boring?
No. It is calmer, which is different. Las Olas at night and the water taxi circuit are a real evening, just not a Miami one.
