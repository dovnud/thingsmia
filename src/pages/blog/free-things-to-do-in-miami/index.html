Free Things to Do in Miami

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The strongest free things in Miami are the Metromover trains downtown (fare free, confirmed by Miami-Dade Transit), Bayfront Park and its baywalk (free admission, open 365 days a year), two free trolley networks, Wynwood's street art viewed from public sidewalks, a monthly free street festival in Little Havana, and free admission days at two major museums each month. Every item below was confirmed free with the venue, the city, or the transit agency itself.

How this list was checked

Free is a factual claim, so this page treats it like one. An item is listed only if the operator's own source, a city source, or the transit agency states it is free, in material checked in October 2026. Where a popular claim could not be confirmed from an official source, it is not on the list, and the honest catches are in the fact box further down.

The verified free list

1. Ride the Metromover

Miami-Dade Transit's fares page lists the Metromover fare as free, and a county release from March 2026 states it plainly: "Metromover is a free service." The driverless trains loop through downtown and Brickell and connect to Metrorail, and riding a full loop is a free skyline tour.

2. Bayfront Park and the Baywalk

The Bayfront Park Management Trust, a City of Miami agency, states free admission, open to the public 365 days a year. The park's baywalk runs along the edge of Biscayne Bay through the park and continues toward Bayside Marketplace. Admission is free; the park's own FAQ lists parking at $20, so arrive by Metromover if you can.

3. The Miami Beach Trolley

The City of Miami Beach describes its trolley as a free citywide service, running 8 a.m. to 11 p.m., seven days a week. It is the easiest free way to move up and down Miami Beach without a car.

4. The City of Miami Trolley

The Miami Downtown Development Authority describes the City of Miami's trolley network as thirteen routes that "will take you around the City of Miami for free, seven days a week," reaching neighborhoods the Metromover does not.

5. Wynwood street art from the sidewalk

Wynwood's murals cover public streets well beyond the Wynwood Walls compound, and viewing them from the sidewalk costs nothing. The Walls themselves are ticketed for visitors; see the catches in the fact box before you assume either way.

6. PAMM on a Free Second Saturday

Pérez Art Museum Miami runs Free Second Saturdays: per the museum, "admission is free all day" on the second Saturday of every month. Time a downtown day to one and a major museum costs nothing.

7. Museum of Miami Family Fun Day

The Museum of Miami, formerly HistoryMiami Museum, offers free admission on the second Saturday of each month for its Family Fun Day, per the museum's own ticketing page. It falls on the same Saturday as PAMM's free day, and the two museums sit a short Metromover ride apart.

8. Viernes Culturales in Little Havana

The Greater Miami Convention and Visitors Bureau listing for Viernes Culturales states admission is free, with food, drinks, and art sold along Calle Ocho. The monthly Friday festival has current 2026 editions listed; check the month's date before you go, because the Friday has varied.

9. The Underline

The Underline is the public linear park built under the Metrorail line in Brickell. Its official FAQ says it stays open 24/7, 365 days a year, and it works as a free green corridor for walking and cycling between Brickell and points south.

10. Miami Design District public art walk

The Design District's outdoor installations and murals sit on public streets, and the district's official site runs a Public Art Tour of them; its October 2026 tour listing was priced at zero dollars. Walk it yourself any time, or book the free guided tour ahead.

The honest catches

Wynwood Walls is ticketed. Per the Walls' own October 2026 release, visitor admission is $12 per adult, children under 12 are free, and Miami-Dade County residents now get free admission with a county photo ID and digital membership. Venetian Pool and Frost Science are paid, not free. The City of Coral Gables sells Venetian Pool tickets, and Frost Science's own plan your day page prices general admission, with free entry limited to members, children 3 and under, and first responders and military with ID. Bayfront Park parking is paid even though admission is free ($20 per the park FAQ, checked October 26, 2026).

What about the beach and the boardwalk?

Readers ask why the sand is not item one on a free Miami list. The honest answer: in this check we could not confirm a free access statement for Miami Beach from a City of Miami Beach source, so the beach is not listed as a verified free item here. What city sources do confirm is that the free Miami Beach Trolley will take you there. Budget for parking, chairs, and umbrellas, which are sold all along the beach.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Metromover really free?
Yes. Miami-Dade Transit lists its fare as free, and the county repeated that in a March 2026 service release. There is no ticket and no fare gate; you walk on and ride.

Are any big Miami museums free?
On the right Saturday, yes. PAMM is free all day on second Saturdays, and the Museum of Miami's Family Fun Day is the same day each month. Frost Science has no general free day; outside those two programs, expect to pay for major museums.

Is Wynwood free?
The neighborhood's street art on public streets is free to view, including plenty of murals outside the compound. The Wynwood Walls attraction itself charges visitors, with the resident and child exceptions in the fact box above.
