Best Views in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami's views come in three kinds: the skyline across the bay, the ocean from the barrier island, and the green flat water world of Biscayne Bay to the south. The strongest free view in the city is the MacArthur Causeway corridor at sunset, and the strongest ticketed view is the top of the Cape Florida lighthouse at Key Biscayne. This post ranks the lookouts by what you actually see, and says which ones cost money.

Across the bay: the skyline shots

The MacArthur Causeway and Watson Island. The drive or ride between downtown and South Beach crosses the port and the bay with the full skyline behind you and the cruise ships beside you. The shoulder viewpoints on Watson Island, near the Miami Children's Museum, give the classic downtown across the water photograph without a ticket. Go at golden hour. It pairs with a Biscayne Bay cruise, which is the same view at water level with narration.

Bayfront Park and Museum Park terraces, downtown. The baywalk behind PAMM and Frost Science looks east across the bay to Miami Beach, best in the morning when the sun is behind you and the water is flat. Free, central, and easy to combine with a museum day from our museums guide.

The Rusty Pelican and the Rickenbacker Causeway, Key Biscayne road. The causeway itself is a rolling viewpoint, and the Hobie Island beach pull offs face the Brickell skyline across the water. Sunset here is the locals' pick, with the skyline lighting up as the sky goes orange. Parking is metered and fills on weekend evenings.

From the island: ocean and skyline together

South Pointe Park, Miami Beach. The southern tip of the island gives you three views at once: the ocean, the Government Cut channel with ships sliding past at arm's length, and Fisher Island and the downtown skyline beyond. The pier and the grassy rise are free. This is the single best free viewpoint in Miami Beach, and it sits at the quiet end of the South Beach strip.

The Cape Florida Lighthouse, Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, Key Biscayne. Climb the 1825 lighthouse (the oldest standing structure in Miami Dade County, per the park) for the full sweep: Atlantic, bay, the Stiltsville stilt houses offshore, and the Miami skyline on the horizon. Entry is a state park fee per vehicle; the lighthouse tours run on a schedule, so check the park page before you go. Our beaches hub covers the park's beach, which usually ranks among the best in the country in the annual Dr. Beach list.

The high floors

Rooftop bars and restaurants. The skyline rooftops in Brickell and downtown sell the view with a drink: the pattern is a high floor, a bay facing terrace, and a sunset rush. Sugar at EAST Miami in Brickell is the famous one, on the 40th floor; book ahead, because the sunset tables go first. On the beach side, the rooftops are lower and face the ocean, better at sunrise. Since openings and concepts change, treat any named rooftop as a check the current listing suggestion; the durable advice is Brickell for skyline, the beach for water.

Skyviews Miami, Bayside Marketplace. The observation wheel at Bayside lifts you over the marina for a downtown and bay panorama, about 200 feet up per the operator. It is the easy ticketed view if you are already downtown with an hour to fill, and it combines with a bay cruise from the same dock.

Sunrise versus sunset, honestly

Miami faces east, so the ocean side owns the sunrise and the bay side owns the sunset. Photographers: shoot the skyline from the causeway at sunset, shoot the beach and the lifeguard towers at sunrise when the sand is empty, and shoot the lighthouse mid morning when the water color peaks. Summer afternoons build thunderheads that can deliver the most dramatic skies of the year, with the obvious caveat that the same clouds bring the rain. The facts that matter

Cape Florida Lighthouse dates to 1825 and is described by Florida State Parks as the oldest standing structure in Miami Dade County. Skyviews Miami's height is about 200 feet per the operator. Sugar sits on the 40th floor of EAST Miami, Brickell. Viewpoints listed as free are public parks and causeway pull offs checked October 30, 2026; Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park charges a per vehicle entry fee.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the best free view in Miami?
South Pointe Park for the combination of ocean, ships, and skyline; the Watson Island shoulder for the pure skyline photograph.

Where do you see the whole skyline?
From the water or the causeways. A bay cruise gives the full width of it; the Rickenbacker Causeway gives the Brickell wall.

Is there a skyscraper observation deck in Miami?
Not a dedicated public one like Chicago or New York. The wheel at Bayside and the hotel rooftops fill the role.
