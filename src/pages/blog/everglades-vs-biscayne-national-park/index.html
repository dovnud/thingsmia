Everglades vs Biscayne National Park

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami is the only city in America with two national parks at its edges, and they are opposites. Everglades National Park is the river of grass: alligators, sawgrass, and airboats, best in the dry winter. Biscayne National Park is 95 percent water: reefs, islands, and a lighthouse, best on a calm summer morning. Do both if you have the days; here is how to choose if you have one.


The Everglades is a river you can barely see moving. Biscayne is an ocean you explore by boat. Pick by element: grass or water. Photo: Pexels.

Everglades National Park: the grass

The Everglades is a slow sheet of water flowing south across sawgrass marsh, the largest tropical wilderness in the country, about 1.5 million acres (National Park Service). The visitor Miami uses three doors: Shark Valley, west on the Tamiami Trail, with its 15 mile loop road, tram tours, and bike rentals, and alligators along the canal in the dry season; the Ernest F. Coe entrance near Homestead, for the Anhinga Trail's boardwalk wildlife show; and the tour strip on the Tamiami Trail, where the airboat tours run. Season rules the experience: December to April, the water falls, wildlife concentrates, and the mosquitoes retreat. Summer is hot, wet, buggy, and gloriously empty. There is no single entry fee logic to print here because passes and tour prices vary; the airboat guide handles the current operator picture.

Biscayne National Park: the water

Biscayne protects the northern Florida Reef, mangrove shoreline, and the bay's island chain, 95 percent of it underwater (National Park Service). There is almost nothing to do here without a boat, which is the point: snorkel and dive trips to the reef and the Maritime Heritage Trail of shipwrecks, paddling in the mangroves from the Dante Fascell Visitor Center at Convoy Point, and boat trips to Boca Chita Key, where a 1930s lighthouse (built as a private folly, never an official aid to navigation) watches the channel. The park's concessioner runs the bookable trips; summer's calm, warm water is the prime snorkel season, the mirror image of the Everglades calendar. Stiltsville, the stilt house community in the park's north, is seen from the water on park and private tours.

Head to head

Wildlife

Everglades: alligators guaranteed in the dry season, wading birds in clouds, manatees at the edges. Biscayne: sea turtles, rays, reef fish, dolphins, and manatees in the shallows. Land versus sea, both rich.

Effort and cost

Everglades: drive yourself, walk boardwalks, add an airboat if you like. Biscayne: the boat is mandatory for the best of it, so trips cost more and book ahead on weekends.

Best season

Everglades peaks December to April. Biscayne peaks on calm days from May to September, with winter fronts the spoiler. A winter trip favors the grass; a summer trip favors the reef.

The verdict, by traveler

First timers with one park day in winter: the Everglades, via Shark Valley and an airboat, because the wildlife show is guaranteed and the story is like nowhere else. Swimmers, snorkelers, and boat people: Biscayne, and bring the morning booking. Families with small kids: the Anhinga Trail and Shark Valley tram beat open water. Photographers: Anhinga at dawn for the birds, Boca Chita at golden hour for the lighthouse. And if your trip is long enough for both, do the Everglades first and end on the water; the day trips guide sequences them with the Keys honestly.

Practicalities

Both parks are National Park Service units, so the same interagency passes (including the America the Beautiful pass) cover entry at both, and each park's site carries its current fees and any seasonal closures. Everglades access is road based: your own car and park roads, plus commercial tours. Biscayne access is boat based: the park's authorized concessioner runs the bookable snorkeling, diving, and island trips from the Dante Fascell Visitor Center near Homestead, and private charters fill the rest. Book Biscayne boats ahead on weekends in any season.

The facts that matter

Everglades National Park covers about 1.5 million acres (NPS). Biscayne National Park is 95 percent water (NPS). Shark Valley's loop road is 15 miles (NPS). The Boca Chita lighthouse was a private 1930s structure, not an official lighthouse (NPS). Checked October 30, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Can you visit both parks in one day?
Technically yes, happily no. They sit on opposite sides of the county and each deserves a full day.

Are airboats inside the national park?
The commercial airboat strip operates along the Tamiami Trail at the park's edge under authorization; the park interior is explored by tram, bike, foot, and paddle. Our airboat guide maps who runs where.

Which park is better for kids?
The Everglades, for guaranteed alligators at close, safe range. Biscayne wins for confident swimmers.
