Everglades Airboat Tours Compared

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Airboats do not run inside Everglades National Park. The National Park Service prohibits airboats in most of the park, which is managed as wilderness, and no park entrance offers them. From Miami, airboat tours go to authorized operators outside the park: the three concession services the Park Service lists along US-41 (the Tamiami Trail) west of the city, operators in the Francis S. Taylor Wildlife Management Area, and the Everglades City area on the Gulf side. The ride itself usually lasts 30 to 60 minutes; half day tours with transport from Miami run about 4 to 6 hours in total.

Where do Everglades airboats actually run?

Outside the national park, and the Park Service is explicit about it. Its Ernest F. Coe Visitor Center page states that there are no airboat tours available through that entrance, and that airboats are offered through three concession services located along Route 41: Coopertown Airboats at 22700 SW 8th Street, Gator Park at 24050 SW 8th Street, and Everglades Safari Park at 26700 SW 8th Street, all in Miami. The park's own FAQ explains why: most of the nearly 2,400 square miles of Everglades National Park is managed as wilderness, and airboats are prohibited in those parts of the park, including Florida Bay. Airboat operators in the Everglades City area say the same thing from their side: tours are not permitted within state and federal parks, so their boats run in the waters around the town instead.

Any listing that promises an airboat ride "in Everglades National Park" is describing the region, not the park. That distinction matters because it changes what you should expect: sawgrass marsh, canals, and wildlife areas managed for exactly this use, rather than a route through the park's protected wilderness.

What are the main ways to book from Miami?

Bus tours with hotel pickup. The easiest format if you have no car. Listings on Viator and Expedia checked in October 2026 put half day Everglades trips with round trip transport from Miami at about 4 to 6 hours. A representative Viator listing, "Everglades Airboat, Wildlife Exhibit, and Roundtrip Bus from Miami," ran 5 hours from $50, and Expedia's "Everglades Adventure with Airboat" with the Miami Tour Company ran 5 hours with a 1 hour airboat ride at $61 per adult. Prices move, so treat these as October 2026 reference points, not quotes.

Drive yourself to a US-41 operator. Coopertown, Gator Park, and Everglades Safari Park all sit on the Tamiami Trail west of Miami, east of the Shark Valley entrance. Driving lets you choose your operator, your boat size, and your timing, and pair the ride with Shark Valley on the same day (below). A 60 minute small group airboat tour booked through Viator as a 4 to 5 hour product from Miami was listed from $78 in October 2026.

Everglades City. The Gulf side town near the park's Gulf Coast entrance has its own airboat scene, with small boats: one Everglades City operator's FAQ describes boats running about 55 to 60 minutes on the water with Coast Guard capacity of 1 to 6 passengers. It is the better fit if you are already heading to the Gulf Coast or Sanibel, and the longer trip if you are based in Miami.

Airboat ride or Shark Valley?

They are different products, and many visitors do both. Shark Valley, the park entrance on US-41, has a 15 mile loop road with narrated tram tours of about 2 hours, bicycle rentals, and an observation tower, and no airboats at all. The tram is the quiet, interpretive way to see the same sawgrass ecosystem, with a ranger style narration and a tower view at the halfway point. The airboat is the loud, fast version, run by commercial operators outside the park boundary. Note that the park's three entrances, Ernest F. Coe near Homestead, Shark Valley on US-41, and Gulf Coast near Everglades City, do not connect to each other inside the park, so plan each as its own trip.

The facts that matter

The National Park Service lists three airboat concession services along Route 41: Coopertown Airboats, 22700 SW 8th Street, Miami, FL 33194; Gator Park, 24050 SW 8th Street, Miami, FL 33194; and Everglades Safari Park, 26700 SW 8th Street, Miami, FL 33194. No airboat tours run from any national park entrance, including Ernest F. Coe, Shark Valley, and the Gulf Coast entrance. The Ernest F. Coe Visitor Center, the park's main visitor center, is open 365 days a year, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM from April through mid December and 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM from mid December through March (phone 305-242-7700). Frequently asked questions

Can I ride an airboat inside Everglades National Park?
No. Airboats are prohibited in the park's wilderness waters, which cover most of the park, and no entrance offers airboat tours. Commercial airboats run outside the park with authorized operators along US-41, in the Francis S. Taylor Wildlife Management Area, and around Everglades City.

How long is the airboat ride itself?
Typically 30 to 60 minutes on the water. The 4 to 6 hour figures on Miami tour listings include the drive out, the ride, and usually a wildlife exhibit or show at the operator's base.

Do the tours include transport from Miami?
Many half day products do, with pickup in Miami Beach and downtown. Self drive gives you more choice of operator and boat size. Check what a listing includes before comparing prices, because a ride only ticket and a door to door tour are different products.

What is the difference between the US-41 operators and Everglades City?
Distance and boat size. The US-41 operators are the closest airboat bases to Miami, on the same road as Shark Valley. Everglades City is on the Gulf side of the peninsula and runs notably small boats, which suits visitors already touring the west coast.
