Fort Lauderdale Day Trip From Miami by Brightline

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Fort Lauderdale is the easiest day trip from Miami that feels like a different city, and Brightline is the reason: trains from MiamiCentral reach Fort Lauderdale station in about 33 minutes, running roughly hourly, with no I-95 traffic and no parking hunt at the other end. The station sits in downtown Fort Lauderdale, a short walk from the Las Olas Boulevard dining and shopping strip and a short rideshare from the beach. The day builds itself: train up in the morning, beach or a canal boat late morning, lunch on Las Olas, an easy wander, train home for dinner. Drivers can do the same trip in 45 minutes to well over an hour each way depending on traffic, which is precisely the point of the train.

What to do with the day

The beach. Fort Lauderdale Beach is the broad, palms and promenade kind: a long paved beachwalk, lifeguarded swimming, and cafes across A1A. It is calmer and more local than South Beach, which is either the appeal or the letdown depending on what you came for. The station to sand leg is a few miles; budget a rideshare or the local bus rather than walking it in the heat.

Las Olas Boulevard. The city's main street runs from downtown toward the beach through the canal neighborhoods: boutiques, galleries, patios, and the densest lunch options in the city. It anchors the day for most visitors, and the Riverwalk along the New River connects it back toward the station.

The water. Fort Lauderdale calls itself the Venice of America for its canal grid, and the water taxi and sightseeing boats that work the Intracoastal and the canals are the local equivalent of Miami's bay cruises: waterfront mansions, yachts, and the port. A half day pairs naturally with the beach or follows it.

The train, honestly

Brightline is a comfortable, modern service: reserved seats, a clean station experience, and fares that move with demand, so booking ahead usually beats walking up. The 33 minute timing is station to station; add your MiamiCentral access time and the Fort Lauderdale station to beach leg for the true door to door number, which still beats driving on any weekday. The honest limits: the last trains home set your evening curfew, service disruptions happen as they do on any railway, and a family of five should price the train against a rental car plus parking before assuming the train wins on cost. For couples and solos it usually wins on both time and sanity. The same line continues north to West Palm Beach and Orlando; our Palm Beach day trip guide uses it for the next stop up.

Day trip or guided tour?

This is one of the rare Miami day trips with no real need for a guided product: the train is the logistics, the city is walkable at its core, and packaged tours from Miami to Fort Lauderdale mostly replicate the drive you just avoided. Spend the tour money on the canal boat or a good Las Olas lunch instead. The exceptions are cruise passengers on a tight port clock and travelers who want the Everglades or a beach club built into the same day; for them the packaged versions in the marketplace earn their markup.

The return trip and timing the day

Build the day backwards from your return train. Evening departures thin out compared with the daytime rhythm, so pick your home train before you sit down to lunch, not after. A comfortable shape is a mid morning arrival, the beach or canal boat through the middle of the day, a late lunch on Las Olas when the patios are lively, and a late afternoon train that puts you back in Miami for the evening. If the day is going well and you stay for dinner, know your last realistic train and set the alarm for it; Fort Lauderdale evenings have a way of convincing visitors to miss trains on purpose, and the rideshare home is the expensive souvenir.

The facts that matter

Brightline trains connect MiamiCentral with Fort Lauderdale station in about 33 minutes, running roughly hourly through the day. Fort Lauderdale station is in the downtown core, walking distance to Las Olas Boulevard and the Riverwalk, and a short rideshare from Fort Lauderdale Beach. Fares vary by departure and booking window. The driving alternative on I-95 takes 45 minutes to well over an hour depending on traffic. Frequently asked questions

How long is the train from Miami to Fort Lauderdale?
About 33 minutes station to station, with roughly hourly departures. Book ahead for the best fares and the departure times you want.

Can I walk from Fort Lauderdale station to the beach?
It is a few miles. Las Olas and the Riverwalk are walkable from the station; for the beach itself, take a rideshare or bus and save the walking for the beachwalk.

Is Fort Lauderdale worth a day if I have the Miami beaches?
Yes, for the change of register: calmer beach, canal boats, and a proper main street. It is the lowest effort different city day in South Florida.

Train or drive?
Train for solos, couples, and anyone traveling at rush hour. Drive when the group's train fares exceed a rental plus parking, or when your day needs wheels at the other end.
