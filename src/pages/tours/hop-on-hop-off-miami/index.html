Hop-On Hop-Off Bus in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami's hop-on hop-off service is an open top double decker loop run by Big Bus, sold as 24 hour and 48 hour passes. Rode end to end without getting off, the panoramic loop takes about 2 hours; buses come roughly every 30 minutes in season, and the hub stop, called Central Station, is at Bayside Marketplace (401 Biscayne Boulevard) downtown. The commentary is recorded in several languages. The product works best as a first day orientation and a beach to downtown shuttle in one: ride the full loop once to get the geography, then hop off at the two or three stops you actually care about. It is not efficient transport, and it is not a guided tour with a human; judged as an orientation with wheels, it earns its fare.

What the loop covers

The route stitches together the visitor's Miami: downtown and Bayside, across the causeway to Miami Beach and Ocean Drive, through Wynwood's mural blocks, into Little Havana along Calle Ocho, and back through Coconut Grove and Coral Gables side streets on some routings. Route structures have changed over the years as the company has split, merged, and renamed its lines, so treat any stop list, ours included, as a snapshot and check the operator's live map on the day. The constants are the anchors: Bayside as the hub, South Beach as the beach stop cluster, and Little Havana and Wynwood as the neighborhood stops. From the top deck the causeway crossing alone justifies the format; Miami is a city you understand from bridges, and the bus gives you the bridge views with narration.

Passes, the night tour, and the free alternative

The 24 hour pass is the standard buy; the 48 hour version spreads the loop across two days and suits beach based visitors using it as a shuttle. Bundles add products like the bay cruise or the separate night tour. Note carefully: the night tour is a different product, a single panoramic evening loop that does not stop for hopping off, covered in our Miami night tour guide. The other comparison is the free one: Miami and Miami Beach run free trolley services on fixed routes that locals actually use. The trolleys carry no narration and no top deck, but if your goal is simply moving between the Beach, downtown, and the Grove, they do it for nothing while the bus does it for the price of a nice lunch. Choose based on whether you want the tour or the transport; our Miami city tour guide covers the guided middle ground.

Honest downsides

Traffic is the first: the bus swims in the same congestion as everyone, and in the evening peak or during events the 30 minute headway is an aspiration. The top deck in summer is a sun exposure decision, not a seat choice; the lower deck has the air conditioning and the worse photos. Recorded commentary is competent but generic, and it cannot answer the question a live guide on a small group tour handles easily. And as pure transport the bus loses to everything: trolleys, the Metromover downtown, rideshares. Buy it as a moving orientation on day one, ride the full loop first without hopping off, and it will organize the rest of your trip. Buy it as a taxi and you will be annoyed by noon.

How to ride it well

Two tactics separate a good bus day from a frustrating one. First, ride the full loop once without getting off, ideally on the first bus of your day, and mark the stops worth your time; Miami rewards the overview because its neighborhoods sit far apart and look nothing alike. Second, hop off at no more than two or three stops, because every hop costs you up to a full headway waiting for the next bus, and in traffic that wait stretches. The Beach cluster and Little Havana repay the longest stops; Wynwood works as a shorter photo and coffee break. Sit up top for the causeways, and keep the pass until the last bus if your hotel is near a stop, since the loop doubles as your ride home.

The facts that matter

Miami's hop-on hop-off operator is Big Bus, selling 24 hour and 48 hour passes. The full panoramic loop takes about 2 hours if ridden without stops; buses run about every 30 minutes in season. The hub stop is at Bayside Marketplace, 401 Biscayne Boulevard, downtown. The night tour is a separate, non stop panoramic product. Miami and Miami Beach also operate free trolleys on fixed routes. Sources: operator listings checked October 2026; route structures change, so verify the current map before travel.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 24 or 48 hour pass better?
Twenty four hours if you ride the loop on arrival day and hop off at a few stops. Forty eight if you are based in Miami Beach and want the bus as your downtown shuttle across two days.

Where do I start?
Central Station at Bayside Marketplace downtown is the hub, but you can board at any stop with a valid pass or mobile ticket.

Is the night tour included?
Only if your bundle says so. The night tour is a separate panoramic loop and you cannot hop off during it.

Hop-on hop-off or a guided city tour?
The bus for freedom and the top deck views; a guided city tour for a human guide, hotel pickup, and stops with time inside the neighborhoods. See our city tour guide for the comparison.
