Palm Beach Day Trip From Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Palm Beach, the barrier island town of Gilded Age money about 70 miles north of Miami, makes a polished day trip: Brightline from MiamiCentral to West Palm Beach takes about an hour and a quarter, and from the station a short trolley or rideshare crosses the bridge onto the island. The day there has three acts: Worth Avenue, the most elegant shopping street in Florida, with its vine covered courtyard vias; the Flagler Museum, Henry Flagler's 1902 mansion Whitehall, which explains how all of this got here; and a walk past The Breakers, the great resort hotel his company built, and the beach and Lake Trail behind the mansions. Driving works too, at about an hour and a half in kind traffic, but the train keeps the day effortless.

The Flagler story, in one paragraph

Palm Beach exists because Henry M. Flagler, the Standard Oil partner turned railroad builder, pushed his Florida East Coast Railway down the coast in the 1890s and built resort hotels to fill it. His Royal Poinciana and then The Breakers (opened in the 1890s in its first incarnation; the current grand hotel dates to 1926) made the island America's winter society capital, and Whitehall, the mansion he built as a wedding gift for his wife in 1902, is now the Flagler Museum. Understanding that one man's railway and hotels explain the town turns the day from window shopping into a story, and the museum, a few minutes from Worth Avenue, is the best single hour of the trip.

How the day fits together

Take a morning train to West Palm Beach. Cross to the island and start at the museum while you are fresh, then walk or ride down to Worth Avenue for lunch and the vias: the little courtyards off the main street, Via Parigi and Via Mizner among them, are the real Palm Beach, quieter and prettier than the storefronts. In the afternoon, walk the grounds side of The Breakers from the public beach access or book a drink or meal there to see the interiors legitimately, then stroll a stretch of the Lake Trail, the paved path along Lake Worth Lagoon behind the mansions, before the train home. Add the Norton Museum of Art on the West Palm Beach side if your group is museum strong; skip it if the day is already full, because the island itself is the exhibit.

Honest downsides

Palm Beach is a town that runs on private clubs, private beaches, and private houses, and the day visitor's access has edges: the mansions are viewed from the street and the trail, the beach access points are public but the sand in front of the great houses is a walk, not a lounge, and lunch prices on Worth Avenue reflect the zip code. Season matters too: in the winter social season the island is at full gleam and full price; in summer it is hot, quiet, and some storefronts keep short hours. Finally, the two city geography confuses first timers: West Palm Beach, the real city with the station and the Norton, is across the bridge from Palm Beach, the island town; the day uses both, on purpose. For the simpler version of this coast, our Fort Lauderdale day trip is half the journey.

The West Palm Beach side deserves its footnote for art lovers: the Norton Museum of Art, a few minutes from the station, is one of Florida's strongest museums, and Clematis Street nearby is the working city's dining row, useful for a more locally priced dinner before the train home. It is entirely possible to ride up for the Norton and Clematis alone and treat the island as the bonus, and in summer, when the island slows down, that is arguably the better day.

The facts that matter

Palm Beach is a barrier island town east of West Palm Beach. Brightline runs MiamiCentral to West Palm Beach in about an hour and a quarter; the island is a short trolley or rideshare ride from the station. The Flagler Museum occupies Whitehall, Henry Flagler's mansion completed in 1902. Worth Avenue is the island's main shopping street, known for its courtyard vias. Frequently asked questions

How do I get from West Palm Beach station to Palm Beach island?
A short rideshare or local trolley ride across the bridge, a few minutes. Worth Avenue and the Flagler Museum are both an easy distance once you are on the island.

What is the one must see in Palm Beach?
The Flagler Museum at Whitehall. It is the key that makes Worth Avenue, The Breakers, and the mansions make sense.

Can I use the beach in Palm Beach?
Yes, at public access points, including the municipal beach areas. Facilities are simpler than Miami's big beaches, and much of the island's shoreline is residential.

Is Palm Beach worth it versus Fort Lauderdale?
Different trips: Fort Lauderdale is a beach and boating day; Palm Beach is architecture, history, and the most polished street in Florida. Palm Beach rewards the longer ride if those are your interests.
