Bimini Ferry Day Trip (From Fort Lauderdale, Passport Required)

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first, because it corrects the common mistake. The Bimini ferry does not sail from Miami. Balearia Caribbean's fast ferry to Bimini in the Bahamas departs from Port Everglades in Fort Lauderdale, Terminal 21 at 2021 Eller Drive, about 30 to 45 minutes north of Miami. The crossing takes about 2 hours each way on a high speed catamaran, and a passport is required for every passenger, including children; there is no birth certificate option on this route. With a typical 9:00 AM departure and check in opening 3 hours before, a same day round trip is very doable: beach and island time in Bimini, home the same night. Budget from about $199 per person for the ferry based on prices checked in September 2026, plus what you spend on the island.

The logistics that decide the day

Everything about this trip runs on the check in clock. Balearia Caribbean opens check in 3 hours before departure and the terminal doors close 1 hour before sailing, and the typical Miami area departure is 9:00 AM, which means a Miami based day tripper is leaving the hotel around 5:30 to 6:00 AM to reach Port Everglades, park or drop off, and clear check in and US departure formalities comfortably. Miss the door closing and there is no later boat that saves the day trip. The ferry lands at Alice Town on North Bimini, where the island is small enough to cover by golf cart, taxi, or on foot around the resort and beach areas. The return sailing gets you back to Fort Lauderdale in the evening, with the drive to Miami still ahead of you, so plan the next morning accordingly.

The passport rule, stated plainly

Bimini is the Bahamas, a foreign country, and the ferry operator requires a valid passport for every passenger regardless of age. US passport books and passport cards are accepted for this sea route; birth certificates, driver's licenses alone, and photos of documents are not. Children need their own passports. Travelers of other nationalities should check Bahamian entry rules for their passport, including any visa requirements, well before booking, because the check in desk will turn away anyone the Bahamas will not admit, and ferry tickets bought on a wrong assumption are the classic way this trip goes bad. If your group includes anyone without a passport, this is simply not your day trip; pick Key West or Key Largo instead.

What you do in Bimini

The day writes itself around the water. The big resort on North Bimini sells day access to its beach and pools, and the public beaches on the island's west side give you the famous gin clear shallows for free. Alice Town adds the local layer: conch stands, a small museum and straw market, and the bars that have served fishermen and Hemingway lore for generations. Add ons sold locally and through the ferry include snorkeling trips to the Sapona wreck and reefs, and excursions toward the wild dolphin and stingray grounds the island is known for. Keep spending expectations honest: resort day passes, carts, and excursions are extra, food and drinks are island priced, and the handful of hours ashore passes fast. A one night stay converts the trip from a taster into the real Bimini, and the ferry sells that version too.

Honest downsides

The early start is brutal and non negotiable. The crossing is on open Atlantic water and can be rough; anyone prone to seasickness should medicate before the terminal, not on board. Weather and sea state cancel or delay sailings, and the schedule is thinner than a cruise line's, so a scrubbed day has limited backup. Finally, run the full math before the romance: ferry fare, early transport to Fort Lauderdale, parking, day passes, and food make this the priciest day trip on this site, competing with a night in Key West. It earns it for travelers who specifically want a Bahamas stamp in the passport and that water color. Everyone else should compare the Key West day trip honestly first.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Bimini ferry leave from Miami?
No. It leaves from Port Everglades in Fort Lauderdale, Terminal 21, 2021 Eller Drive, roughly 30 to 45 minutes north of Miami. Build that transfer into your morning.

Do children need passports for the Bimini ferry?
Yes. Every passenger, including infants and children, needs a valid passport. US passport cards are accepted for adults and children on this sea route; birth certificates are not accepted.

How long is the crossing?
About 2 hours each way in normal conditions. Check in opens 3 hours before departure and doors close 1 hour before, so the travel day is longer than the crossing suggests.

Can I really do Bimini as a day trip?
Yes, same day round trips are the standard product: morning ferry out, most of the day on the island, evening ferry back. It is a long day, and a one night stay is the relaxed version.
