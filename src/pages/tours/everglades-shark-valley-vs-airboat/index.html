Everglades in winter: Shark Valley tram, bike or airboat

Last reviewed: November 7, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. These are two different days wearing one name. Shark Valley is the national park at its calmest: a 15 mile paved loop with no private cars, done on a 2 hour guided tram or on a rental bike, in the middle of the river of grass with alligators beside the road. The airboat is speed, noise and spray on the Tamiami Trail corridor, a ride first and a wildlife walk second. Winter is when the choice matters most, because the dry season, December to April per the National Park Service, is the busy season with the widest variety of wading birds and predators on show. If you have one Everglades day in winter, take Shark Valley in the morning and add an airboat only if the ride itself is the point. The facts below, including one permit rule that most pages state wrong, come from nps.gov and the tram operator, checked November 7, 2026.

Shark Valley by tram: the 2 hour version

Shark Valley sits off US-41, the Tamiami Trail, at 36000 SW 8th Street, about 25 miles west of the Turnpike. Private vehicles go no further than the entrance road and parking lot. From there the loop belongs to feet, bicycles and the tram. The tram tour is a guided, narrated 2 hour run around the full 15 mile loop, with a stop at the observation deck halfway, a 45 foot high deck with ramp access and a 20 mile view across the marsh. In high season the park service lists trams running hourly from December 20 to April 5, run by Shark Valley Tram Tours, the park's operator, with site hours of 8:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Two winter warnings from the park itself: book the tram ahead, and in high visitation the parking lot can go one car in, one car out between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m., with the park advising arrival 1.5 to 2 hours before a scheduled tram. A noon arrival in January is a plan to wait. Go early. The marsh rewards it anyway.

Shark Valley by bike: $27 and your own pace

The same operator rents the bikes: $27 per bike per day, rentals from 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. with every bike back by 5:00 p.m. The bikes are single gear with coaster brakes, adjustable seats and baskets, with 20 inch children's bikes and child seat bikes in the fleet, and a driver license or ID is required to rent. Some bikes can be reserved online, and a share is held for first come riders at the desk. The ride itself is 15 flat paved miles and takes most people 2 to 3 hours with stops, and stops are the point: the loop's wildlife sits beside the road, alligators on the banks, herons in the shallows, and you set the distance between you and each one, within the park's rules and common sense. Bring water for the full loop. There's no refill station at the far end, and the second half of the ride is the exposed half.

The airboat, and the permit rule stated right

Now the correction this page exists to make. You'll read on other sites that airboats are banned inside Everglades National Park. As a blanket claim, that's wrong. The park service's own guided tours page lists airboat tours inside the Everglades with exactly three authorized operators, Coopertown, Everglades Safari Park and Gator Park, running on designated routes in the East Everglades addition along the Tamiami Trail, a grandfathered arrangement from before 1989. Everywhere else in the park, Shark Valley and Royal Palm included, airboats are prohibited, private airboats included, and a long strip of other Tamiami Trail operators runs outside the park boundary in Water Conservation Area 3A. So the honest split isn't park versus no park. It's three permitted in-park operators, a set of outside operators, and a park interior where the tram and the bike are the only rides. Our Everglades airboat tours page lists the ride options, and airboat tours on Viator carries the bookable set.

What the airboat gives you is the ride: fast, loud, open, with wildlife sightings that depend on the day and the driver's eye. What it can't give you is the quiet. If your group includes a birder, a photographer, or a kid who wants to watch an alligator for ten minutes, the tram and bike day is the one they'll talk about. If your group wants the wind and the slide of a flat bottom boat in sawgrass, no tram lecture will substitute. Plenty of visitors do both, airboat on the way out in the morning, Shark Valley after, and the Tamiami Trail makes that pairing a straight line.

Entry money: $35 a vehicle, and the $100 line visitors miss

Park entry, from the park's fees page: a private vehicle pass is $35, a motorcycle $30, and entry on foot, by bicycle or by paddlecraft is $20 per person at 16 and over, with 15 and under free. The pass runs 7 consecutive days and covers every park entrance, and the park is cashless at the Homestead, Shark Valley and Gulf Coast entrances, card only. The Everglades annual pass is $70 for US citizens and residents. Then the line that catches international visitors: since 2026, each non-US resident aged 16 and over pays an additional $100 per person unless they're admitted on an annual or America the Beautiful pass, and it applies on concessioner and commercial tours too. A family of four from overseas can add $400 at the gate if nobody warns them. Consider yourself warned, and price the annual pass options before you travel if the surcharge applies to your group.

The third leg: Anhinga Trail, and how the days combine

Shark Valley isn't the park's only winter walk, and for many visitors it isn't the densest one. The Anhinga Trail sits at Royal Palm, 4 miles inside the Homestead entrance past the Ernest F. Coe Visitor Center: 0.8 miles round trip on pavement and boardwalk, flat and wheelchair accessible, through sawgrass marsh where the park says alligators, turtles, anhingas, herons and egrets gather, above all in winter, and calls it one of the park's most popular trails for the amount of wildlife it holds. It's a different entrance, about an hour from Shark Valley, so it's a second stop on a long day or the main event on a short one. The facts that matter

Everglades facts checked November 7, 2026: dry season runs December to April, the busy season for wading birds and predators (nps.gov). Shark Valley is a 15 mile loop with no private cars beyond the parking lot; the tram is a guided 2 hour tour, hourly December 20 to April 5 in high season, with a stop at the 45 foot observation deck (nps.gov and Shark Valley Tram Tours). Bike rental is $27 per bike per day, out from 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., back by 5:00 p.m. (operator). Park entry is $35 per vehicle, $30 motorcycle, $20 per person on foot or bike at 16 plus, for 7 days, cashless at the main entrances, plus a $100 surcharge per non-resident aged 16 and over without an annual pass (nps.gov fees page). Three operators, Coopertown, Everglades Safari Park and Gator Park, are the park's authorized airboat tours (nps.gov guided tours page).

Frequently asked questions

Tram or bike, if I can only pick one?
The tram if anyone in the group can't ride 15 miles or the day is hot; the bike if you want to stop on your own schedule. First timers with kids usually land happier on the tram.

Do I need to book the tram in winter?
Yes. The park recommends reservations, trams fill in high season, and the parking lot itself can meter entry at midday. Book the tram, arrive early, and let the bike riders in the group rent at the desk.

Will I see alligators?
Winter at Shark Valley and on the Anhinga Trail gives you the best odds the park can give, and the park's own pages list alligators among the regular sightings on both. Odds aren't a guarantee. Any page that promises you a sighting is selling, not informing.
