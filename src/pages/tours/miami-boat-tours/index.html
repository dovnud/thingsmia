Best Boat Tours in Miami

Miami is a city you understand from the water. Boat tours here split into four useful groups: narrated sightseeing cruises on Biscayne Bay, sunset and evening sailings, private boats and yachts, and active trips where you paddle, snorkel, or fish. Most sightseeing boats leave from Bayside Marketplace in downtown Miami, and the standard bay cruise runs about 90 minutes. Pick your group below; each guide compares routes, durations, and departure areas before you spend on tickets.

Checked facts

The standard Biscayne Bay sightseeing cruise runs 90 minutes, narrated in English and Spanish, from the marina at Bayside Marketplace, 401 Biscayne Blvd in downtown Miami (operator: Island Queen Cruises).

Sightseeing routes stay inside Biscayne Bay, taking in the downtown skyline, PortMiami, Fisher Island, Miami Beach, and the bay island homes of Millionaire's Row, rather than the open ocean.

Common questions

Where do most Miami sightseeing boats leave from?

Bayside Marketplace in downtown Miami is the main departure point for narrated bay cruises. Private charters and activity boats also leave from marinas in Miami Beach, Coconut Grove, and Key Biscayne, which is why the departure area is worth checking before you book.

Which boat tour should a first time visitor take?

The 90 minute Biscayne Bay sightseeing cruise. It is short, central, and shows you the skyline, the port, and the island homes in one loop. Add a sunset sailing if you have a second evening.

Do boat tours run all year?

Miami boating is year round, but individual schedules vary by season and weather, and bay conditions can cancel activity trips like snorkeling. Check the operator's live schedule for your date rather than assuming a daily departure.

What is the difference between a cruise and a charter?

A cruise is a scheduled sailing with other passengers and a set route. A charter is the whole boat for your group, with the route and stops agreed in advance, priced per boat rather than per person.
