Private Yacht Charters in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. A private yacht charter in Miami means renting the whole boat, with a captain, for your group. The standard formats are a half day of about 4 hours and a full day of about 8, plus shorter sunset charters. Most Miami charters cap the group at about 12 guests, because Coast Guard rules for the uninspected charter vessels that make up most of the fleet limit them to 12 passengers; bigger groups need a larger, inspected vessel and a different price class. The headline rate is rarely the final bill: fuel, crew gratuity, tax, and marina fees may or may not be inside it, so the skill in booking a charter is reading the inclusions before comparing prices.

The formats: half day, full day, sunset

The half day is the Miami staple, long enough for a bay cruise, a swim stop, and an anchor at the sandbar, short enough to leave the evening free. The full day opens the farther options: down the bay toward Key Biscayne and Stiltsville, the community of stilt houses standing in the shallows south of the cape, or a slow mansion route with a long lunch anchor. Sunset charters run 2 to 3 hours and are the best value in the market for couples and small groups who want the yacht experience without a day rate. Overnight and Bahamas charters exist but are a different booking world of provisions, customs, and weather windows; treat any operator who sells them casually with suspicion.

The routes captains actually run

Most Miami charters work some combination of the same greatest hits: out of the marina and past the downtown skyline, around Star Island and the bay islands (the same water as the celebrity homes cruise, at your pace), then to an anchor spot. The famous one is the Haulover Sandbar, north of Bal Harbour, where boats raft up in waist deep water on weekends; the quieter alternatives are the sandbars and anchorages around Key Biscayne, including Nixon Beach off the cape. Swimming stops depend on conditions and the captain's call, and jellyfish season and boat traffic shape the choice. A good pre booking question is simply: where would you anchor for a group like ours, on a day like ours?

What the price includes, and what it does not

Read every quote against the same checklist: captain (almost always included and required), fuel (included for bay routes on some boats, metered or capped on others), crew or steward on larger yachts, ice, water, and soda, water toys such as floats and paddleboards, and docking if you stop at a waterfront restaurant. The common extras are crew gratuity (customarily in the 15 to 20 percent range of the charter rate), sales tax, catering and alcohol, which most charters let you bring aboard yourself, and premium toys like jet skis, which are a separate rental with their own rules. Two quotes that look far apart often converge once the exclusions are added, which is why the cheapest headline rate is a weak way to choose.

Honest downsides and how to book well

Weather rules the day: captains cancel or reroute for lightning and rough seas, and the reschedule terms matter more in summer. The 12 guest cap surprises groups who count heads late; confirm your final number against the boat's legal capacity, crew excluded, before paying a deposit. Deposits and cancellation windows in this market are stricter than hotels, so put the trip insurance question on the table for large bookings. Book through established charter companies or platforms with verified boats and reviews rather than social media listings, confirm the exact boat name and marina in writing, and arrive knowing that the captain's word on safety and weather is final. For most visitors, of course, the shared version of this day, a sunset cruise or a bay sightseeing boat, delivers the same water at a fraction of the price; the charter premium buys privacy, pace, and the swim stop.

The facts that matter

Miami private charters are typically sold as half day (about 4 hours), full day (about 8 hours), and sunset (2 to 3 hours) formats, captain included. Most charter yachts in the local fleet operate as uninspected passenger vessels limited by Coast Guard rules to 12 guests; larger groups require an inspected vessel. Common anchor stops include the Haulover Sandbar and the Key Biscayne anchorages. Quoted rates vary in what they bundle; fuel, gratuity, and tax are the exclusions to check. Frequently asked questions

How many people can a Miami charter yacht take?
Usually up to about 12 guests, set by Coast Guard rules for the uninspected vessels that make up most of the fleet. Groups larger than that need an inspected vessel, which is a bigger and pricier class of boat.

Can we bring our own food and drinks?
On most charters, yes. Bringing your own provisioning is standard in this market; catering can also be arranged. Confirm alcohol and glass bottle policies with the operator.

Is the captain included?
Effectively always for visitors: Miami charters are sold as captained trips, and the captain also decides routes and swim stops based on conditions.

Half day or full day?
Half day covers the bay, the mansions, and a sandbar swim with time to spare. Full day earns its premium if you want Key Biscayne and Stiltsville or a long lunch anchor.
