Biscayne Bay and Millionaire's Row Cruise Guide

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The classic Miami sightseeing cruise is a 90 minute narrated boat ride from Bayside Marketplace in downtown Miami. It takes in the downtown skyline, PortMiami, Fisher Island, and Miami Beach, then slows along Millionaire's Row, the waterfront mansions of the bay islands, while the guide points out homes said to belong to famous owners. The established operator is Island Queen Cruises, whose ticket booth sits inside Bayside Marketplace at 401 Biscayne Blvd. From prices varied by seller when checked in October 2026, from $18 on Viator to $35 through Big Bus Tours for the same style of cruise.

What do you actually see on the cruise?

Two things: the city from its best angle, and the houses. The route out of Bayside gives you the full downtown skyline, the cruise terminals of PortMiami, and the Brickell shoreline. The boat then works around the bay islands, passing Star Island, Palm Island, and Hibiscus Island, and runs along Fisher Island and the Miami Beach waterfront. The Millionaire's Row portion is the slow pass in front of the big waterfront homes, with the guide naming the celebrity owners the houses are associated with.

Read that last part the right way. Guides point out homes said to belong to famous owners, and the names are part of the fun. But waterfront mansions change hands, and no guide can verify current ownership from the water. Treat the names as entertainment layered on a genuinely good sightseeing route, not as a property record, and the cruise is exactly what it should be.

Where do the boats leave from, and how does boarding work?

Island Queen Cruises runs its ticket booth inside Bayside Marketplace, the open air mall at 401 Biscayne Blvd in downtown Miami, and its boats leave from the marina there through the day. Boarding is first come, first served, with no assigned seating, so the practical advice from sellers is to arrive well before your sailing, 30 to 45 minutes, exchange or buy your ticket at the booth, and queue for the upper deck if views matter to you. The signature boat is a modern yacht with an air conditioned lower deck and an open air upper deck, and the narration runs in English and Spanish. The same operator also runs the Bayside Blaster, an open air adventure boat on weekends, listed on the operator's own site at $28 for adults ($23 online) when checked in October 2026; it is a different, faster product than the sightseeing cruise.

How long is it, and what should it cost?

The signature sightseeing cruise runs 90 minutes. Ticket prices depend heavily on where you buy: Viator listed a "Miami Millionaires Sightseeing Cruise" of 1 hour 30 minutes from $18, Big Bus Tours listed the signature cruise from $35 per adult and $25 per child, and the operator's own site prices its boats in the high twenties and up, all checked in October 2026. The spread is normal for Miami boat tours. Compare the departure point, the boat, and the duration rather than the headline price, and buy from the channel that shows all three clearly.

Frequently asked questions

Are the celebrity homes really owned by those celebrities?
The guide will say so, and some of the associations are long standing. Ownership changes without announcement, though, and there is no way to verify it from a boat. Enjoy the stories; do not quote them as fact.

Is seating assigned?
No. Boarding is first come, first served. Arrive early for a rail spot on the open upper deck, which is where the sightseeing value is, and use the air conditioned lower deck if the heat gets to you.

Does the cruise go into the open ocean?
No. The route stays in Biscayne Bay, working around the islands and the Miami Beach waterfront. That keeps the water sheltered compared with the open Atlantic, which matters if anyone in your group is nervous on boats.

Day cruise or sunset cruise?
The daytime sailing is the better sightseeing product: you can actually see the houses. A sunset departure trades house spotting for skyline light and a cooler deck. Our sunset cruises guide compares the evening options.
