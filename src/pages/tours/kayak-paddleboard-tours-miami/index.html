Kayak and Paddleboard Tours in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami paddling splits into two worlds. The sheltered world is the mangroves: above all Oleta River State Park in North Miami, the largest urban park in Florida's state park system, where guided kayak tours thread calm creeks under a mangrove canopy. The open world is Biscayne Bay: Key Biscayne and the Virginia Key shoreline, where paddleboards and clear kayaks work the shallow, clear water with skyline views. Guided tours run about 2 hours, gear and instruction included, and suit beginners. Confident paddlers can simply rent by the hour at the same launches and skip the tour entirely.

The mangrove tours: Oleta River and the creeks

Oleta River State Park is the center of gravity for guided paddling in Miami. The park's outfitter rents kayaks, canoes, and paddleboards at the river's edge, and guided tours push off into mangrove tunnels where the air drops a few degrees, the water goes glassy, and the city noise disappears. Herons, egrets, ibis, and jumping mullet are the regular cast; manatees drift through in the cooler months. The paddling is easy, current free creek work, which is why this is the right first paddle for families and nervous beginners. Farther afield, outfitters run similar mangrove and river routes at parks up and down the coast, and the format travels well: if you enjoyed Oleta, the creeks of the upper Keys are your next step.

The bay paddling: Key Biscayne and the skyline views

Open water paddling trades the canopy for the postcard: launch from Key Biscayne or the marinas along the Rickenbacker Causeway and the downtown skyline fills the horizon across clear, shallow flats. Clear kayak and LED lit night tours have become a Miami specialty in recent years, working the calm nearshore water after dark with lights under the hull. Sunset tours do the same trick at golden hour and are the easiest sell in this whole category. The tradeoff for the views is exposure: wind and boat chop matter out here in a way they never do in the creeks, and a breezy day turns a beginner paddleboard outing into work. Check the wind forecast, not just the rain forecast, before booking an open water paddle.

Wildlife, honestly, and logistics

Guides know where the animals tend to be, and the hit rate on birds and fish is high. Manatees are the headline everyone hopes for; they are most present in the cooler months, roughly late fall through early spring, when they seek out warmer water, and even in season they are a maybe, not a schedule. Florida law protects them: look, do not approach, chase, or touch. Logistics are simple: tours provide boats, paddles, life jackets, and a dry bag talk; you bring water, reef safe sunscreen, a hat, and clothes that can get wet. Most operators set minimum ages for kids in tandem boats and require an adult per child; confirm when booking. Morning departures have the calmest water and the best wildlife odds year round; in summer they also dodge the afternoon storms.

Tour or just rent?

Rent if you can already paddle, read a simple route, and mind boat channels: hourly rentals at Oleta and the Key Biscayne launches are the cheapest good paddle in Miami. Take the tour if you are new, if you want the wildlife interpretation, or if you are doing a specialty product like the night or full moon paddles, where the operator's route knowledge and lights are the point. Either way, this is the most peaceful two hours available in Miami tourism, and the only one powered entirely by you.

The facts that matter

Oleta River State Park in North Miami is the largest urban park in Florida's state park system and the main base for guided mangrove kayak tours; its outfitter rents kayaks, canoes, and paddleboards and runs guided trips of about 2 hours. Open water paddling centers on Key Biscayne and the Rickenbacker Causeway shoreline. Manatees are protected under Florida law and most likely to be seen in the cooler months. Frequently asked questions

Do I need experience to take a kayak tour?
No. The mangrove tours at Oleta are beginner water: calm, shallow, and guided, with instruction before launch. Tandem kayaks let a stronger paddler carry the work.

Kayak or paddleboard for a beginner?
Kayak. A paddleboard adds balance to the learning curve and wind affects it more. Save the board for a calm morning or a second outing.

Will we see manatees?
Maybe, most likely in the cooler months. They are wild and protected; guides know the favored spots but no sighting is promised, and approaching them is against the law.

What should I bring?
Water, sunscreen, a hat, and a change of clothes for after. You will get at least splashed. Phones go in a dry bag or stay on shore.
