Jet Ski Tours in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami jet ski products are guided tours, not free range rentals: you follow a guide on a set route around Biscayne Bay, typically for about an hour on the water, past the skyline and the bay islands, with the throttle open in the permitted zones and idling through the no wake areas. Florida law shapes who can drive: you must be at least 14 years old to operate a personal watercraft and at least 18 to rent one, and anyone born on or after January 1, 1988 needs a Florida Boating Safety Education ID Card, or the 90 day temporary certificate, plus photo ID, to operate a motorized vessel of 10 horsepower or more, jet skis included. Operators handle the certificate paperwork at check in, but read the rules below before you book a teenager's birthday around one.

How a jet ski tour actually runs

You check in at a marina, most of them on the bay side of Miami Beach or the downtown waterfront, sign the waivers, show ID, and get the safety briefing and the rules of the ride: follow the guide, keep the spacing, respect the no wake zones near shore, marinas, and anchored boats, and understand the kill switch lanyard on your wrist. Then the group files out in single file and the tour proper begins: a loop of the bay with the skyline as backdrop, speed sections in the open water the guide chooses, and slower sightseeing stretches past the islands and waterfront homes. Machines carry two riders, driver and passenger, and pairs often swap halfway if the operator allows it and both riders qualify to drive. Life jackets are provided and mandatory. The whole product, dock to dock, runs about 1.5 hours with roughly an hour on the water; longer 2 hour tours exist for the committed.

The Florida rules, in full

Three rules decide most bookings. Age to operate: 14 is Florida's minimum to drive a personal watercraft, and rental companies require the renter, the adult signing the contract, to be 18 or older; many Miami operators set their own higher driving minimums, commonly 16 or 18, so a 14 year old's legal right to operate does not guarantee a given company will hand them the keys. Boater education: drivers born on or after January 1, 1988 must carry the Florida Boating Safety Education ID Card or a valid temporary certificate, earned from a short online test, together with photo ID; operators usually steer you to the temporary certificate as part of booking, for a small fee, so build the extra minutes into your arrival. Hours: personal watercraft cannot operate from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise in Florida, so there is no such thing as a night jet ski tour here, whatever a listing implies; the last tours of the day are late afternoon rides that end well before the cutoff.

Honest downsides

The guided format is the first: riders who imagined renting a ski and roaming the bay will find the follow the leader structure confining, and Florida's rental rules and Miami's crowded bay are exactly why the tour format dominates. Price is the second: an hour on a jet ski costs what a half day costs on many boats, and per person math improves only when two riders share a machine. Weather is the third: wind chop turns the bay ride from thrilling to pounding, and operators cancel for lightning and rough water. The final honesty is about speed: in the marked zones and near shore you will idle, and the fast water is rationed by the route, so the tour is a highlight reel, not an hour at full throttle. Riders who want the water slower and cheaper should look at kayak and paddleboard tours; riders who want the bay with a drink in hand should look at the sunset cruises.

Frequently asked questions

Can my 15 year old drive a jet ski in Miami?
Florida law allows operation from age 14, but many Miami operators set higher driving minimums and all require an adult renter of 18 or more on the contract. Check the specific operator's age policy before booking.

Do I need a boating license?
If you were born on or after January 1, 1988, yes: the Florida Boating Safety Education ID Card or a temporary certificate, plus photo ID. Operators usually arrange the temporary certificate at booking or check in.

Can two people share one jet ski?
Yes, machines take a driver and a passenger, and sharing is the standard way to bring the per person cost down. Swapping drivers is allowed only where the operator permits and both riders meet the rules.

Are there sunset jet ski tours?
Late afternoon tours, yes; true sunset riding, no. Florida prohibits personal watercraft from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise, so tours end before dark.
