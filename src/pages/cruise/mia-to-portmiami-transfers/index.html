MIA to PortMiami: Every Transfer Option Compared

Last reviewed: October 26, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami International Airport puts PortMiami at about 9 miles away, and the drive is typically 15 to 25 minutes. For most cruisers the choice is a taxi or rideshare from the arrival level, or their cruise line's pre arranged bus from the airport's bus stations. The budget route is real too: the Metrorail Orange Line runs from the airport station to Government Center for $2.25, with the free Metromover and the free Coral Way trolley covering the downtown legs.

Should I take my cruise line's transfer?

It is the option with the least thinking. Miami-Dade County's port directions page notes that most cruise lines offer shuttle service from Miami International Airport, and the airport's own ground transportation page explains where they board: pre arranged cruise line transportation uses the North bus station at Concourse D, Level 1, Door 1, or the South bus station at Concourse J, Level 1, Door 33. You book it with the cruise, your name is on a list, and the line knows you are coming, which matters if your flight runs late. The trade offs are price per person, which adds up for families, and a bus that leaves when it fills rather than when you land.

How do taxis and rideshares work at MIA?

Both pick up where you would expect. The airport's ground transportation page places taxi and ride app pickup zones on the arrival, or ground, level, outside baggage claim, so you walk out with your bags and join the line or order the car. Taxis are metered and ride app fares are quoted in the app before you confirm; this page does not print a number for either, because both move with traffic and demand, and a fare quoted on a calm Tuesday is wrong on a five ship Saturday. For a group with full cruise luggage, one larger vehicle usually beats two standard cars, and either beats a per person shuttle price once you are three or more travelers.

What is the cheapest way to reach the port?

Transit, and it is cheaper than most visitors expect. From the terminal, the free MIA Mover, which leaves from the third level between the Dolphin and Flamingo garages, takes you to the Rental Car Center and Miami Intermodal Center, where the airport's Metrorail station sits. Ride the Orange Line to Government Center station in downtown: Miami-Dade Transit prices a one way Metrorail trip at $2.25 and a one day pass at $5.65 (county transit release, March 2026). From Government Center, the Metromover is free for the downtown and Brickell loops, and the transfer from Metrorail to Metromover costs nothing. The final hop to the port is where the airport itself points transit riders to the City of Miami trolley: its ground transportation page names the Coral Way route, among others, for reaching PortMiami from the Intermodal Center area, and City of Miami trolleys are free to ride. It is the slowest option here and the least forgiving with big suitcases, but it is a genuine airport to port route for the price of a coffee.

Does renting a car ever make sense?

Only when the car earns its keep beyond the transfer. The airport's Rental Car Center is reached by the same MIA Mover to the Intermodal Center, and a rental makes sense if you are touring Miami for a day or two before sailing, or continuing to the Keys or Fort Lauderdale after. For a straight airport to port run it adds rental formalities, port parking charges if you keep it, and a return trip to the rental center on the way home. Park and cruise math lives in our PortMiami parking guide.

Which option should I choose?

Match the transfer to the trip. Flying in the day before with a downtown hotel, take a taxi or rideshare and be done. Sailing the same day your flight lands, the cruise line bus is the resilient choice, because the line tracks the booking. Traveling light and cheap, ride the Orange Line and the trolley. And whatever you choose, respect the port's rhythm: on multi ship mornings every one of these options slows down, which is why the pre cruise guide argues for arriving the day before.

The facts that matter

Distance and time: about 9 miles from MIA to PortMiami (Miami International Airport), typically 15 to 25 minutes by road. Cruise line buses board at the airport's North bus station (Concourse D, Level 1, Door 1) or South bus station (Concourse J, Level 1, Door 33). Taxi and ride app pickups are on the arrival level outside baggage claim. The MIA Mover connects the terminal, from the third level between the Dolphin and Flamingo garages, to the Rental Car Center and Miami Intermodal Center. Metrorail and Metrobus fares are $2.25 one way, with a $5.65 one day pass (Miami-Dade Transit, March 2026). The Metromover is free, the Metrorail to Metromover transfer is free, and City of Miami trolleys are free to ride.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I allow between landing and sailing?
Hours, not minutes. The drive itself is typically 15 to 25 minutes, but baggage claim, the transfer itself, and port check in all sit between you and the ship. Same day arrivals should land in the morning; the relaxed pattern is to arrive the day before.

Is there a free shuttle from MIA to PortMiami?
No free door to door shuttle is published by the airport or the county. The free pieces are the MIA Mover inside the airport complex, the Metromover downtown, and the City of Miami trolleys; the Metrorail leg between them costs $2.25.

Where exactly do cruise line buses pick up?
At the airport's two bus stations, per its ground transportation page: the North bus station at Concourse D, Level 1, Door 1, and the South bus station at Concourse J, Level 1, Door 33. Your cruise line will tell you which one and what time window.
