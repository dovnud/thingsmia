Brightline and Port Everglades From Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Not every South Florida cruise sails from PortMiami. Port Everglades, in Fort Lauderdale about 30 miles north, is one of the busiest cruise ports in the world, and Brightline is the civilized way to reach it from Miami without the I-95 drive. Ride from MiamiCentral station downtown to the Fort Lauderdale station, which sits a short taxi or rideshare hop from the port gates, roughly three miles. The train does not stop at the port itself, so plan the last leg as deliberately as the first.

The route: MiamiCentral to Fort Lauderdale

Brightline runs up the Florida East Coast corridor from MiamiCentral, its downtown Miami station at 600 NW 1st Avenue, through Aventura and Fort Lauderdale, then on to Boca Raton, West Palm Beach and Orlando. The Miami to Fort Lauderdale leg is the short hop, roughly half an hour of run time, on trains that leave through the day. For a cruise morning it beats the highway in the one way that matters: predictability. I-95 between Miami and Fort Lauderdale is fine until the day it is not, and embarkation day is the wrong day to learn which. Book the train to land you at the port with the same slack you would give a drive, and the last leg below stays a formality.

The last three miles: station to ship

Fort Lauderdale's Brightline station is downtown, and Port Everglades lies about three miles southeast, past the airport's southern edge. Taxis and rideshares cover it in around ten minutes in normal traffic, and the port's own taxi queues run the reverse on disembarkation days. Brightline has marketed cruise connections and shuttle style add ons around sailings at various times; what is offered changes, so check the booking flow for your date and treat anything not confirmed in your reservation as not existing. With luggage, do not attempt to improvise this leg on foot or on city buses with cruise day timing.

Port Everglades in brief

Port Everglades is Broward County's port, home to a deep roster of lines including Royal Caribbean, Celebrity, Princess, Holland America and Carnival sailings among others, with terminals spread along the port's Eller Drive and numbered berths. It is a working port first, so the experience is more industrial than PortMiami's island, and terminal assignments matter even more: the port is large, and the walk between terminals is not a thing. Your line's documents name your terminal; the taxi driver needs it. Parking at Port Everglades is priced by Broward County on its own pages, entirely separate from PortMiami's rates, so do not carry Miami numbers north with you.

Brightline back to Miami, and onward

The same route serves the return: ship to station by taxi, train to MiamiCentral, and you are downtown with the Metrorail and Metromover at the same complex for the airport or the rest of the city. Northbound, the line continues to West Palm Beach and Orlando, which makes a Fort Lauderdale cruise pair naturally with an Orlando or Palm Beach leg for travelers building a longer Florida trip. Book return trains with disembarkation slack; ships clear customs on the port's schedule, not the railway's, and a missed train is a rebooking fee rather than a catastrophe only if you leave the margin.

Who should drive instead

Honesty about the alternative: for a family of four with a week of luggage, a car, whether your own or a one way rental, can price under four train fares plus two taxi legs, and it keeps the schedule entirely yours. The train wins for couples and solo travelers, for anyone whose alternative is I-95 at rush hour, and for travelers already staying downtown near MiamiCentral. Price both for your party size; Brightline fares vary by departure and class and are shown live at booking, which is why this page quotes none.

The facts that matter

Brightline stations relevant here: MiamiCentral (600 NW 1st Avenue, downtown Miami), Aventura, Fort Lauderdale, then Boca Raton, West Palm Beach and Orlando. The Fort Lauderdale station is about three miles from Port Everglades; there is no rail stop inside the port. Port Everglades is Broward County's port, roughly 30 miles north of Miami, with its own parking rates and terminal assignments, separate from PortMiami in every operational detail. Brightline's own site blocked our direct check on October 30, 2026; fares and add on services are deliberately not quoted and should be confirmed in the booking flow.

Frequently asked questions

Does Brightline go directly to Port Everglades?
No. The Fort Lauderdale station is about three miles from the port. A taxi or rideshare covers the final leg in around ten minutes in normal traffic.

How long is the train from Miami to Fort Lauderdale?
Roughly half an hour of run time between MiamiCentral and the Fort Lauderdale station. Check the timetable for your date, since frequencies vary.

Is Port Everglades the same as PortMiami?
No. They are different ports in different counties, about 30 miles apart, with different terminals, parking and rates. Confirm which port your sailing uses before you travel.

Can I ride Brightline to a Miami cruise?
PortMiami has no station. From MiamiCentral the port is a short taxi or rideshare across downtown; our transfer guide covers the options.
