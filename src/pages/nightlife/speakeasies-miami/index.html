Best Speakeasies in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami's speakeasy scene skips the fake passwords and keeps the parts that matter: small rooms, low light, serious spirits and an entrance you have to know about. Swizzle Rum Bar and Drinkery, hidden in the Stiles Hotel on Collins, leads the list for rum depth. Medium Cool, in the Gale Hotel's basement, does the candlelit lounge version. The ScapeGoat, a tiny South of Fifth room, is the locals' pour. Go on a weeknight, go in a small group, and let the bartender drive.

Swizzle Rum Bar and Drinkery: the rum library

Swizzle hides inside the Stiles Hotel on Collins Avenue in South Beach, down a level from the lobby and into a wood dark room built around rum: hundreds of bottles deep by most counts, tiki classics and serious stirred drinks sharing one menu, and bartenders who treat the category as a library. It is the connoisseur's pick of this list and the best first speakeasy for a visitor, because the room rewards curiosity at every price point. Arrive early on weekends; the room is small and the word is out.

Medium Cool and The ScapeGoat: the quiet rooms

Medium Cool, in the basement of the Gale South Beach, is the date night entry: low ceilings, candles, a compact cocktail list and a room that stays at conversation volume. The ScapeGoat, down on South of Fifth's Meridian Avenue side, is smaller still, a standing room pour of rare spirits and natural wine for the neighborhood that knows. Neither performs the hidden door theatre hard; both keep the speakeasy virtues of scale and focus. If the mega clubs are Miami at full volume, these rooms are the city at a murmur, and many locals prefer the murmur.

The listening bar detour: Dante's HiFi

Wynwood's Dante's HiFi stretches the definition, a Japanese style listening bar where the hidden treasure is a wall of vinyl and a tuned sound system rather than a secret door. It belongs on this page because the experience is the speakeasy experience translated: small, hushed, reverent about its subject. Reservations pace the room. Our Wynwood bars guide places it in its neighborhood circuit.

Speakeasy etiquette and strategy

The rules that make these rooms work: groups of four or fewer, voices at room level, phones mostly away, and trust in the bartender's questions over a photographed menu from last year. Menus here rotate with seasons and whims, which is why this page names rooms and strengths rather than specific drinks. Weekend waits are real at the famous two; weeknights are the insider move across this whole list. For the louder craft end of Miami drinking, the cocktail bars guide is the companion read.

Why these rooms stay small, and why that is the point

Speakeasies are an economic argument as much as an aesthetic one. A forty seat room cannot chase volume, so it chases margin and loyalty instead: better spirits, longer conversations, regulars who return monthly rather than crowds who visit once. That is why the drinks are serious and the rooms stay hushed; the model depends on it. It is also why these bars are fragile, and why Miami has lost good ones to rent and redevelopment. Visiting on a weeknight, ordering the bartender's own suggestion and treating the room gently is not just etiquette here; it is how the category survives.

Frequently asked questions

Do Miami speakeasies need passwords or reservations?
No passwords. Reservations or early arrival handle the small rooms, above all on weekends.

Which speakeasy is best for a date?
Medium Cool, for the candlelight and the conversation volume. Swizzle for a date who loves spirits and a story.

Are these bars actually hidden?
Lightly. Unmarked or inside hotels, findable once you know, which is the modern speakeasy bargain everywhere.

What should I order?
Tell the bartender a spirit and a direction, sweet, bitter, bright, and let the room show off. That is what these bars are for.
