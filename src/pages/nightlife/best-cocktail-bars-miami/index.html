Best Cocktail Bars in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami's cocktail scene has a flagship: Cafe La Trova in Little Havana, where the cantinero tradition and live Cuban music make it the city's essential bar. Around it, the map sorts cleanly: Sweet Liberty for joyful craft in South Beach, the Broken Shaker for the tropical patio classic, the Regent Cocktail Club for the hushed hotel version, and Swizzle for rum depth. This is an editorial ranking, ordered by the rooms we would send a drinker to first, and every one of them earns its place on consistency rather than novelty.

Cafe La Trova: the essential

Julio Cabrera built La Trova on the cantinero code, the white jacketed Cuban bartending tradition of precision, memory and hospitality, and the room delivers it with live classic Cuban music steps from the bar. The drinks are the classics executed at the highest level, daiquiris above all, in a room on Calle Ocho that feels like a celebration that happens to serve perfect cocktails. It is the rare bar that tops both the cocktail lists and the music lists, including ours: it anchors the Latin music guide too. Go early evening, eat, and let the band arrive around you.

Sweet Liberty and Broken Shaker: the joyful craft

Sweet Liberty, tucked behind South Beach's Collins Park side, runs on its founding motto of pursuing happiness: a serious drinks program in a room that never gets solemn about it, with a food menu that respects the hour. The Broken Shaker, in the Freehand's courtyard, is the tropical patio that launched a thousand imitators: garden seating, punch and tiki inflections, and the most forgiving dress code on this list. Between them they cover the beach's moods, craft with a grin and craft with sand nearby. Both fill on weekends; both reward the early arrival.

The Regent and Swizzle: the hushed register

The Regent Cocktail Club, in the Gale Hotel on South Beach, is the classic cocktail bar as a quiet art: low light, leather, a short menu of perfectly built standards and bartenders who remember regulars' orders across years. Swizzle, its near neighbor inside the Stiles Hotel, takes the same seriousness to rum specifically and earns its own deep dive in our speakeasies guide. Choose Regent for the archetype, Swizzle for the library.

How to drink well in Miami: strategy

Three rules. Start at the flagship of whichever neighborhood you are in rather than bar hopping across causeways; Miami distances tax a night. Eat where you drink when the room allows it; Trova and Sweet Liberty both feed you properly, which is part of their greatness. And let the classics lead: in the city that runs on cafecito and rum, the daiquiri, the mojito done right and the Cuban coffee cocktails are the local canon, best judged where they were perfected. The wider map, rooftops to dives, is in the nightlife hub.

The bench: hotel bars and the next rooms

Behind the flagship five sits a deep bench. The hotel bars of the big resorts run serious programs for their guests and the walk in drinker alike, and the Regent's style, hushed, classic, exact, repeats in variations at the Setai and Faena bars where the room is half the recipe. Wynwood and downtown add the newer school: smaller, chef adjacent rooms where the menu changes with the market. The scene's health is that the flagship and the bench push each other; Trova sets a standard the new rooms chase, and the new rooms keep the flagship honest. Drink at both ends of that argument on any long Miami weekend.

Frequently asked questions

What is the one cocktail bar I must not miss?
Cafe La Trova. It is the city's flagship for drinks and music together, and nothing else in Miami does both at its level.

Do these bars take reservations?
Some do for parts of the room; most run first come at the bar. Early arrival is the universal strategy.

Are cocktail bars expensive in Miami?
Expect big city craft prices, highest at the hotel rooms. The value pick of this list is the Broken Shaker's patio.

Can I eat at these bars?
At the best of them, yes: La Trova and Sweet Liberty run real kitchens, which is part of why they top the list.
