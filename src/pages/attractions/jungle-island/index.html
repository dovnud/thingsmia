Jungle Island Miami Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Jungle Island is an eco adventure park on Watson Island, between downtown Miami and Miami Beach, at 1111 Parrot Jungle Trail. It is not a zoo, and the park says so itself: the visit is built around animal encounters you book and feed, from capybaras and lemurs to sloths and flamingos, plus play areas, a splash zone, treehouses, and shaded trails under a jungle canopy. It suits families and animal fans who want interaction. It will disappoint anyone expecting a large, full scale zoo, and this guide is honest about that trade.

From Parrot Jungle to an encounter park

The park began in 1936 as Parrot Jungle, a roadside attraction in Pinecrest where birds flew freely around visitors, and it became one of classic Florida's best known names. It moved to Watson Island in 2003 and took the Jungle Island name, reopening as a larger park on the causeway between downtown and the beach. Hurricane damage in 2017 closed it for a long rebuild, and the park that emerged from that period is a different product from the old bird show park: smaller in spirit, centered on close up encounters, play structures, and events rather than on big habitat exhibits. Knowing that history sets the right expectation. This is a hands on, half day park, not a rival to Zoo Miami's scale, and it is priced and planned accordingly.

What the official site currently sells

General admission covers entry to the grounds, the animal exhibits and trails, the petting zoo where you can feed goats, the playgrounds, Treewalk Village with its interconnected treehouses, and Jungle Splash, the water play zone that matters a great deal on a hot day with small children. Shows and keeper talks run through the day. On top of admission, the park sells the encounters that most visitors come for, and its own FAQ lists them: baby and adult capybaras, lemurs, sloths, wallabies, flamingos, and giant tortoises, plus budgie feeding and a bunny encounter aimed at younger children. Encounters include food to feed the animals, run at set times, and the VIP encounters require online reservations in advance.

Treetop Trekking, the ropes and zipline course in the canopy, is sold as an added experience rather than included in standard admission, again per the park's own information. Practical translation: the headline admission price is the floor of your spend, not the total. A family that adds two encounters and the trekking course will spend several times the gate price, so choose your one or two must do encounters before you arrive, book them online, and treat the rest of the park as the relaxed filler between timed slots. The park's site also promotes offers that change over time; at the time of review it was advertising free online admission for children ages 9 and under with a paid adult ticket under a promo code, the kind of deal worth checking on the official site before you buy, since it materially changes the family math.

Hours, location, and planning the visit

The park is open daily for general admission from 9:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., with last admission at 4 p.m., per the official site checked on October 30, 2026. Animal encounters run daily from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., and VIP encounters are by online reservation. Tickets are sold at the park and online. This page deliberately prints no general admission price: the park sells through its own online store with changing offers and bundles, and any fixed figure printed here would age badly. Check the official store for the current price of admission and of each encounter before you budget the day.

Who it suits, and who should choose the zoo instead

Jungle Island suits families with children roughly ages 3 to 10, animal lovers whose dream list includes feeding a capybara or holding a photo moment with a lemur, cruise and downtown visitors who want an animal stop without the drive to the south county zoo, and anyone who values shade and a compact site over scale. The encounter format creates the kind of close contact memories that a barrier separated zoo visit rarely does, and the splash zone and treehouses give children unstructured play between the booked moments. For a birthday or a small celebration, the park sells party packages built on the same admission plus food formula.

Frequently asked questions

Is Jungle Island a zoo?
The park itself says no. It is an eco adventure park built around encounters, play areas, and trails. If you want a large zoological park, see our Zoo Miami guide and choose based on the day you want.

Which encounters should we book first?
Capybaras and lemurs are the most talked about, and sloth encounters sell out early. Book online before your visit, especially on weekends, and build the rest of the day around those timed slots.

How long do we need?
Three to four hours covers the grounds, a show or two, the splash zone, and one or two encounters. It is a half day park for most families.
