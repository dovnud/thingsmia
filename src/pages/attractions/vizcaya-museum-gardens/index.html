Vizcaya Museum and Gardens Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Vizcaya is a historic waterfront estate turned museum at 3251 South Miami Avenue, in north Coconut Grove on Biscayne Bay. You come for two things in equal measure: a richly decorated main house built as the winter home of James Deering, and 10 acres of formal gardens between the house and the bay. Give it a full morning. It rewards slowness and punishes rushing.

What is Vizcaya, in plain terms?

Vizcaya was built between 1914 and 1922 as the winter estate of James Deering, a farming machinery manufacturer, and it opened as a public museum in 1953. The official site calls it a National Historic Landmark and one of the most intact remaining examples of the era when wealthy American industrialists built European inspired estates. Inside the main house you move through furnished historic rooms filled with a decorative art collection gathered in Europe. Outside, the formal gardens use fountains, grottoes, sculpture and long sight lines down to Biscayne Bay, with a rockland hammock, a mangrove shore and a historic village area completing the property. It is a house museum and a garden in one ticket, and both halves deserve time.

Tickets, hours and getting there

Vizcaya is open Wednesday through Monday from 9:30 am to 4:30 pm, and closed on Tuesdays and major holidays, per the official planning page checked October 30, 2026. Admission listed there is $25 for adults age 13 and up, $10 for children age 6 to 12, and free for children age 5 and under, with free admission for veterans year round and for visitors identifying as disabled plus one caregiver. The official page also lists a $2 online saving with a promo code, so buy online rather than at the gate. Parking is free in two lots. By transit, the Metrorail Vizcaya station is about a ten minute walk away, which makes this one of the easier big Miami attractions to reach without a car.

How to split your time inside

Start in the gardens if you arrive at opening, while the light and temperature are kind, then move into the main house as the day heats up. In the gardens, follow the main axis from the house toward the bay, detour into the side grotto and fountain areas, and do not skip the stone barge breakwater on the water, which is the estate's signature view. In the house, move slowly: the value here is in the details, from painted ceilings to the mix of European antiques placed for a subtropical waterfront house. Note that the official site currently advises that guided tours of the main house are temporarily unavailable due to preservation work, while self guided visits and seasonal garden tours continue, so plan on exploring at your own pace and using the free self guided audio material the site describes.

Who Vizcaya suits, and who it does not

Vizcaya suits travelers who like historic houses, gardens, photography and a calm half day away from the beach scene. It is a strong choice for couples and for anyone building a Coconut Grove day. It suits young children less well: much of the house is look, do not touch, garden paths include uneven historic surfaces, and the official accessibility notes flag those surfaces even though ramps and elevators are available. In summer, treat it as a morning attraction. Afternoon heat and sudden rain can shorten a garden visit, and there is no way to move the gardens indoors.

Practical tips that actually help

Buy tickets online before you go, arrive near the 9:30 am opening, and carry water even though this is a museum. Wear flat shoes for the garden paths. Photography is a large part of why people come, so give the bay side of the gardens proper time rather than treating it as the exit route. If you are combining sights, Vizcaya pairs naturally with a Coconut Grove lunch and, on a separate half day, with the Coral Gables garden day at Fairchild. It does not pair well in a single rushed afternoon with Zoo Miami or the Museum Park museums; the distances and the heat work against you.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Vizcaya take?
Two to three hours for most visitors, split between the main house and the gardens. Garden lovers and photographers regularly take longer.

Is Vizcaya closed on Tuesdays?
Yes. The official planning page lists opening Wednesday through Monday only, closed Tuesdays and major holidays. Plan your Coconut Grove day around that.

Can I get to Vizcaya without a car?
Yes. The Metrorail Vizcaya station is about a ten minute walk from the entrance, per the official site, and parking is free if you do drive.
