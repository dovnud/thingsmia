Superblue Miami Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Superblue Miami is a 50,000 square foot immersive art venue in Allapattah, across from the Rubell Museum, where the work of teamLab, Es Devlin, James Turrell, and Rafael Lozano-Hemmer fills whole rooms and responds to your body, your movement, and even your heartbeat. There is nothing to read on walls and nothing behind glass: you walk inside the art. Tickets are timed, cheaper on weekdays, and cheaper online, and most visits run 90 minutes to two hours. It is one of the strongest rainy day and Art Week stops in Miami.

What Superblue is, and where it sits

Superblue opened in a converted industrial building at 1101 NW 23rd Street, Miami, FL 33127, in the Allapattah neighborhood, directly across from the Rubell Museum and a short drive west of Wynwood. The concept is simple to state and hard to overstate in person: a venue built only for large scale immersive and interactive installation art, the kind of work that needs a warehouse rather than a gallery. The 50,000 square foot interior holds a sequence of full room environments, and the visit flows from one to the next, with staff guiding capacity in the most popular rooms so they never feel as crowded as the ticket line outside might suggest.

The works you walk through

The anchor names have stayed consistent since opening. Es Devlin's Forest of Us centers on a mirrored labyrinth that multiplies you into the architecture, a room that is equal parts beautiful and briefly disorienting, and the most photographed threshold in the building. teamLab contributes digital environments where flowers, water, and light respond to where you stand and move, so no two minutes in the room are identical. James Turrell's light work strips the experience down to pure color and perception, asking you to sit and let your eyes adjust while the field of light shifts almost below the threshold of noticing. These are slow rooms inside a fast moving attraction, and they reward the visitors who stop scrolling their phones and stand still.

Rafael Lozano-Hemmer's Pulse Topology turns the building's social energy into the artwork: thousands of light bulbs, 3,000 in the usual count, pulse to the heartbeats of participants who hold the sensors, so the room literally flickers with the people inside it. teamLab's Massless Clouds, where visitors move among cloud like sculptural forms, is sold as an additional experience on top of general admission, priced at $12 on the official site at the time of review, and it is the add on most worth considering if your group likes the tactile, playful end of the program. Works rotate and new pieces are added over time, so the exact room list on your visit may differ from this page; the artists above are the venue's stated core, checked against superblue.com on October 30, 2026.

Tickets, prices, and hours, checked

Superblue sells timed entry, and its own Miami page urges buying online, where prices are lower. General admission for adults was listed at $34 Monday to Thursday and $42 Friday to Sunday, checked on October 30, 2026. Florida residents, students, seniors 65 and up, military, and frontline healthcare workers were listed at $29 on weekdays and $36 on weekends, with the Florida resident rate sold online only and ID required for the discount categories. Child tickets for ages 3 to 12 were $19 on weekdays and $21 on weekends, children 2 and under enter free, and the Massless Clouds add on was $12, with a general admission ticket required. The venue's FAQ notes that adult tickets begin at $29 across its categories, which matches the resident and discount weekday floor. Prices and the add on menu are the venue's to change, so treat these figures as the checked baseline and confirm in the booking flow.

How to get the most from a visit

Book a weekday if your schedule allows: lower prices, thinner crowds, and more time alone in the mirrored and digital rooms, which changes the experience more than any other single decision. Inside, slow down. The Turrell work reads as almost nothing in the first minute and as the strongest room in the building by the fifth. Photography is a natural part of the visit and the rooms are built to photograph well, but the visitors who put the phone away for at least one full room consistently rate the place higher, because peripheral vision and sound are doing half the work.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Superblue take?
Plan 90 to 120 minutes. Rushing defeats the purpose, especially in the James Turrell light work, which needs several quiet minutes to register.

Is Superblue good for kids?
Yes, for most ages. Children 2 and under are free, child tickets cover ages 3 to 12, and the interactive rooms delight children. Keep small children close in the dark mirrored labyrinth.

Superblue or Wynwood Walls?
Different pleasures. Wynwood is outdoor street art you can partly see for free from the street; Superblue is a ticketed indoor immersion. They are minutes apart, and our Wynwood Walls guide helps you do both in one art day.

Can I buy tickets at the door?
The venue sells online and emphasizes online pricing, and popular slots sell out. Buy online in advance, and consider the flexible entry ticket if your arrival time is uncertain, because sales are final.
