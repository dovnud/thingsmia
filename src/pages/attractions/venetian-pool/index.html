Venetian Pool Coral Gables Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Venetian Pool is a historic public swimming pool in Coral Gables, carved from a coral rock quarry in 1923, opened in 1924, and filled with 820,000 gallons of spring water from an underground aquifer. It is a swim, not a museum stop: waterfalls, cave like grottos, two lookout towers, and a palm shaded lagoon in a residential neighborhood. Entry is capacity controlled, hours change by season, and children under 3 are not admitted at all, so read the rules below before you promise anyone a visit.

How a quarry became the most famous pool in Florida

Coral Gables founder George Merrick built his planned city with Mediterranean features, grand entrances, plazas, and limestone taken from local quarries. The Venetian Pool began as one of those quarries. With artist Denman Fink and architect Phineas Paist, the city turned the pit into a Venetian style lagoon, opened in 1924 as the Venetian Casino. Much of the original coral rock was kept and used to border and shape the pool, and limestone from the quarry went into some of the earliest neighboring buildings. The lookout towers, loggias, porticos, signature bridge, and waterfalls are all part of that original design language, softened by a century of palms and planting.

What swimming here is actually like

The water is the headline. The pool holds 820,000 gallons of spring water fed from an underground aquifer, and in the spring and summer seasons the city fills and drains it daily, which is why the water feels cooler and cleaner than a typical chlorinated pool on a hot Miami afternoon. The basin is large and irregular, edged with coral rock, with two waterfalls, cave like grottos to swim into, a small sandy beach area, and the bridge and island that appear in every photograph of the place. It is a leisurely swim and float pool, not a lap pool, and the depth and rock edges reward taking your time rather than treating it as a workout.

Hours, admission, and the rules that catch people out

Hours are seasonal and change through the year, so treat any printed schedule, including this one, as a guide and confirm on coralgables.com before you set out. In broad terms, the summer pattern runs weekdays from late morning into the early evening, with weekends opening earlier and closing earlier in the afternoon, and the cooler months run shorter days, generally Tuesday through Sunday. The pool closes on a list of holidays that includes New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Day, Presidents Day, Thanksgiving, and Christmas Day, and it can close for maintenance and private rentals at other times.

Admission splits sharply between Coral Gables residents and everyone else, with proof of residency required for the resident rate. On the city ticket information checked October 30, 2026, nonresident admission was $23 for adults ages 13 and up and $18 for children ages 3 to 12, while Coral Gables residents paid $7 for adults and $6 for children. Prices are set by the city and change over time, so confirm the current figures when you buy. The hard rule is age: children under 3 are not admitted, and the city requires proof of age for toddlers, so bring documentation for a small 3 year old. Children must also meet the city's minimum height requirement for entry. No amount of charm at the gate changes these rules, and families with babies should plan a different pool day.

Who it suits, and how to pair it

The Venetian Pool suits swimmers, architecture fans, and anyone who wants one unmistakably Miami experience that is not a beach. It is an excellent family stop for children ages 3 and up who swim confidently, and a romantic, photogenic swim for couples on a weekday. It does not suit families with children under 3, lap swimmers, or anyone who needs guaranteed entry at a precise hour on a peak weekend, because capacity closures are real. Shade is limited over the water itself, so bring sun protection, and expect the spring water to feel brisk at first entry even in summer.

Frequently asked questions

Can babies and toddlers enter the Venetian Pool?
No. Children under 3 are not admitted, and the city asks for proof of age for young children. Children must also meet a minimum height requirement. Families with a child under 3 should choose a beach or hotel pool instead.

Is the Venetian Pool a hotel pool?
No. It is a historic public pool run by the City of Coral Gables. Anyone can buy admission, with Coral Gables residents paying a much lower rate on proof of residency.

What if the pool is full when I arrive?
Entry closes when the pool reaches capacity, which can happen early on hot weekends. Arrive near opening, buy through the city's official channels where available, and check the city page that morning for notices.
