Zoo Miami Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Zoo Miami is a large, open air, cage free style zoo at 12400 SW 152nd Street in southwest Miami Dade, far from the beaches and worth the drive for families. Animals live in open exhibits separated by moats, grouped by region. It is a full day, high step count visit. Arrive at opening, rent help for the walking if you need it, and work the loop before the afternoon heat builds.

What kind of zoo is this?

Zoo Miami spreads across a very large site, commonly described at about 750 acres, with the developed zoo covering a large part of that land. The design point matters for your day: exhibits are open and landscaped, animals are grouped largely by geography, and distances between areas are real. Major zones include Florida: Mission Everglades, Amazon and Beyond, Asia, Africa and Australia, plus a large aviary. You will see elephants, lions, giraffes, rhinos, orangutans, flamingos and a long list of species that need space, which is exactly what this inland site provides and a compact city zoo could not. Keeper talks, feedings and animal encounters run on a daily schedule that changes, so check the board or the official site when you arrive rather than planning around a time you read months earlier.

Tickets, hours and getting there

Zoo Miami is at 12400 SW 152nd Street, Miami, FL 33177. The official FAQ page checked October 30, 2026 lists the zoo open daily from 10 am to 5 pm, with the last ticket sold at 4 pm, and notes that some animals begin going in for the night from about 4:30 pm. General admission there is $25.95 plus tax for adults and $21.95 plus tax for children age 3 to 12, with children age 2 and under free. Discounts exist for groups including military members, seniors and teachers, detailed on the official tickets page. Most visitors drive; the zoo sits near the Florida Turnpike in the Country Walk and Richmond Heights area, roughly a half hour from downtown Miami in normal traffic, longer at peak times. Build that drive into the day honestly: this is not a sight you squeeze in next to a beach morning.

How to walk the zoo without wrecking your day

Start at opening and pick a direction for the main loop, then keep moving in that direction instead of zigzagging back for animals you missed. Prioritize the big, active morning exhibits first, because many animals rest out of sight in the midday heat, which in Miami is a large part of the year. Use the zoo's paid extras strategically: safari style tram tours and rented safari cycles, where available, are not indulgences here, they are how smaller legs and older visitors finish the loop in good shape. Plan a proper indoor or shaded lunch break in the middle of the day rather than snacking on the move, and treat the water play areas for children, in season, as a scheduled stop with a change of clothes in your bag.

Heat, rain and the honest downsides

The downsides are weather and distance, and pretending otherwise sets visitors up to fail. Shade exists but the zoo is fundamentally an outdoor, walking attraction in South Florida: hats, sunscreen, water and a morning start are not optional advice in summer. Afternoon thunderstorms can pause activities and send animals under cover, so a forecast with heavy afternoon rain argues for the earliest possible start, not a cancellation, since mornings are often clear. If your group cannot manage a long walking day, Frost Science gives you animals, in aquarium form, plus science, entirely indoors, and it is the better choice. Zoo Miami rewards the visitors who plan it as the day's main event.

Who Zoo Miami suits best

It suits families with children old enough to enjoy the walking, animal lovers who want a major zoo rather than a small animal park, and anyone staying in Kendall, Coral Gables or the southern suburbs, for whom the drive is short. It suits a short Miami Beach weekend less well, simply because the return drive eats the day. Members and Miami Dade residents who can come back often get the best value of all, splitting the zoo into two half days by region. First time visitors with one day should do the full loop once, slowly, rather than racing for a species checklist.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Zoo Miami take?
A full day for the complete loop at family pace. Half a day covers roughly half the zoo well, so pick your regions in advance if you are short on time.

Is Zoo Miami near Miami Beach?
No. It is in southwest Miami Dade at 12400 SW 152nd Street, roughly a half hour drive from downtown Miami in normal traffic and longer from the beach. Plan it as a dedicated trip.

What time should I arrive?
At the 10 am opening. Animals are more active, temperatures are lower, and you finish the loop before the late afternoon wind down, when some animals go in from about 4:30 pm.
