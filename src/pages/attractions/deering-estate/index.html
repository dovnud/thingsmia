Deering Estate Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Deering Estate is a historic house museum inside a 444 acre environmental preserve on Biscayne Bay in Palmetto Bay, south of central Miami. Go for the guided tour of the Richmond Cottage, the bayfront lawn, and nature trails through pine rockland and tropical hardwood hammock that feel nothing like the rest of Miami. It is a half day trip, not a quick stop, and the Stone House interior is currently closed for renovation, which this guide flags honestly below.

Where is the Deering Estate, and whose house was it?

The estate stands at 16701 SW 72nd Avenue, Miami, FL 33157, in the Cutler area of Palmetto Bay, east of Old Cutler Road. It was the winter home, and later the permanent home, of Charles Deering, the Chicago industrialist who was the first chairman of International Harvester and a serious art collector and conservationist. Deering began buying land here in 1913 and lived on the property from 1922 until his death in 1927. Charles was the brother of James Deering, who built Vizcaya on the bay to the north, and the two estates make a natural pair for anyone interested in how wealthy northerners wintered in early Miami.

What do you actually see on a visit?

Two houses anchor the visit. The Richmond Cottage is a three story wooden house built at the end of the 19th century, expanded into a 22 room inn around 1900, and bought by Deering in 1916. It is one of the oldest wooden buildings in Miami-Dade County and the last survivor of the pioneer town of Cutler. The Stone House, completed in 1922 to a design by Phineas Paist, is a Mediterranean style mansion built with thick poured concrete walls to protect Deering's art collection, with a wine cellar fitted out during Prohibition. The boat turning basin, carriage house, pump house, and power house complete the working estate picture, and the main lawn runs down to the bay with the kind of open water view that has mostly been built over elsewhere in Miami.

As of April 28, 2025, the Stone House is closed to the public while it undergoes renovation and restoration, and the estate's own site says so plainly. The daily guided museum tour currently covers the exterior of the Stone House and the interior of the Richmond Cottage. The grounds, trails, and bayfront remain open, so a visit is still worthwhile, but if your main goal is walking through the Stone House rooms, check the estate site before you drive down, because reopening dates are the estate's to announce, not ours to guess.

Tours, trails, and the natural areas

The guided museum tour runs daily at 11:30 a.m. and is included with general admission, meeting on the front porch of the Richmond Cottage. Space is limited and filled on a first come, first served basis, and all guests, including members, sign up at the main gate, so arrive with time to spare if the tour is your priority. In season, from October to May, naturalist led tours of the natural areas are also offered, and guided bird walks run on the second Saturday of the month in season. Tour routes can change without notice, and the historic houses can close during private events, which the estate also states openly.

Hours, admission, and who it suits

The estate is open every day except Thanksgiving and Christmas, from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., with no admission after 4 p.m. when the gates close. General admission, checked on the estate's own site on October 30, 2026, is $18 for adults ages 15 and up and $10 for children ages 4 to 14, with foundation members admitted free. The guided museum tour carries no extra charge after admission. Special programs, from kayak trips to evening events, are priced separately and listed on the estate calendar.

Deering suits history readers, garden and nature walkers, birders, and families who want a calm, educational half day away from the beach crowds. It suits photographers for the bayfront and the cottage porches. It does not suit anyone looking for air conditioned, high energy entertainment, and it is a real drive from Miami Beach and Brickell, roughly 15 miles south of downtown depending on traffic. Pair it with a Coral Gables or Coconut Grove stop on the way back, or with Fairchild Tropical Botanic Garden for a full south Miami garden day, rather than trying to stack it with Wynwood or South Beach in the same afternoon.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Stone House open?
Not at the time of review. The estate closed the Stone House to the public on April 28, 2025 for renovation and restoration. The rest of the estate is open, and the daily guided tour covers the Stone House exterior and the Richmond Cottage interior. Check deeringestate.org for the latest status before you go.

How long does a visit take?
Plan two to three hours for the guided tour plus a walk on the grounds and trails. Nature lovers who add a natural areas tour or bird walk should allow a half day.

Is Deering Estate the same as Vizcaya?
No. They were built by two brothers, Charles Deering here in Palmetto Bay and James Deering at Vizcaya in Coconut Grove. The houses, gardens, and settings are distinct, and both are worth seeing. Our Vizcaya guide covers the other estate.
