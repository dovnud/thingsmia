Fairchild Tropical Botanic Garden Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Fairchild Tropical Botanic Garden is an 83 acre botanic garden in Coral Gables at 10901 Old Cutler Road, built around rare tropical plants: palms, cycads, flowering trees, orchids and a rainforest, plus a butterfly conservatory. It is the calmest major attraction in Miami. Go in the morning, take the tram for an overview, then walk the collections at garden pace.

What is Fairchild, and why does it matter?

Fairchild describes itself as an 83 acre botanic garden with extensive collections of rare tropical plants, including palms, cycads, flowering trees and vines, set beside Matheson Hammock Park in Coral Gables. It is also a museum, laboratory and conservation research center, not only a pretty park: the garden runs plant science and education programs, including its Million Orchid Project work returning native orchids to South Florida. For a visitor, that seriousness shows up as depth. The Montgomery Palmetum is one of the signature collections, the rainforest recreates a habitat that is rare in the continental United States, and the plant labels and layout reward anyone who actually wants to learn the names of what grows in South Florida gardens, including, possibly, the one at your hotel.

Tickets, hours and the online saving

Fairchild is at 10901 Old Cutler Road, Coral Gables, FL 33156. The official hours and admission page checked October 30, 2026 lists the garden open daily from 10 am to 5 pm, with free parking for all visitors. General admission pricing there depends on the day and how you buy: weekday online tickets are listed from $24.95 for adults, $11.95 for children age 3 to 11 and $17.95 for seniors, with weekend online adult tickets from $27.95, and in person tickets costing more, since the garden encourages online or kiosk purchase with a $5 per ticket saving. Children age 2 and under and Fairchild members enter free. Because prices vary by day and channel, treat those figures as checked October 30, 2026 and confirm your date on fairchildgarden.org before you set out. Special festival days can carry their own pricing.

What to see, in a sensible order

Take the tram tour early if one is running when you arrive; the garden's own visitor materials have long offered a narrated tram loop, and it turns an 83 acre site from a map into a mental picture in about forty minutes. Then walk the highlights in depth: the Wings of the Tropics butterfly conservatory, where butterflies fly free in a screened house and releases happen at set times; the rainforest, best in the morning light; the palmetum and the Bailey Palm Glade for the long, narrow view across the lowlands; and the lakes and overlook in the garden's lower half, where the landscape opens up. Families should build in the children's garden, and everyone should build in the cafe: this is a garden that improves when you sit down in it rather than marching.

When to go, and when not to

Season changes Fairchild more than most Miami attractions. Winter and spring bring cooler walking weather and a run of festivals and plant focused events, including orchid and chocolate themed weekends the garden is known for; summer brings the fullest tropical growth, flowering trees at their peak, and serious heat and humidity with afternoon storms. Whatever the season, mornings win. Arrive at the 10 am opening, walk the exposed lowlands first, and save the shaded rainforest and the butterfly house for later. The garden closes on December 25 per long standing listings, and event days can change hours, so a quick check of the official calendar is part of planning, not a formality.

Pairing Fairchild with the rest of Coral Gables

Fairchild sits on Old Cutler Road, one of the prettiest drives in Miami Dade, shaded by banyans and old houses, and it pairs naturally with Matheson Hammock Park next door for a picnic or a waterfront stop, and with a Coral Gables day built around the Venetian Pool and Miracle Mile dining. It also pairs well with Vizcaya, a short drive north in Coconut Grove, if your trip wants a full historic house and gardens day, though doing both justice in one day means an early start and a decision about which gets the morning. Visitors staying in Miami Beach should count the drive honestly: Fairchild is a destination trip south, and it deserves to be the headline of its day.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Fairchild take?
Two to three hours for the highlights with a tram overview. Gardeners and photographers can happily spend most of a day across 83 acres.

Is parking free at Fairchild?
Yes. The official hours and admission page states parking is free for all visitors, and buying tickets online saves $5 per ticket compared with in person prices as listed on October 30, 2026.

What is the one thing not to miss?
The Wings of the Tropics butterfly conservatory for most visitors, and the rainforest for plant lovers. Take the tram first so you know where everything sits.
