Cape Florida Lighthouse and Bill Baggs Park Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Cape Florida Lighthouse stands at the southern tip of Key Biscayne inside Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, at 1200 S. Crandon Boulevard. First built in 1825, it is the oldest standing structure in Miami-Dade County, and you can climb its 109 spiral steps on free ranger and volunteer led tours, Thursday through Monday, with park entry your only cost. Around it, the park protects over a mile of Atlantic beach, bike paths, fishing, and a harbor, making this the best lighthouse plus beach day in the Miami area.

The lighthouse and its rough history

A lighthouse was first completed at Cape Florida in 1825, guiding ships around the Florida reef at the entrance to Biscayne Bay. Its early history was violent: during the Second Seminole War in 1836 the tower was attacked and burned, and it stood as a damaged shell for about a decade before being rebuilt and returned to service in the 1840s. It was later raised to its present height of about 95 feet. The light was discontinued in 1878 when the offshore Fowey Rocks lighthouse took over the work, and the tower spent a century as a landmark without a job, until it was restored, transferred into the state park, and relit in the modern era as a historic beacon. The white tower you climb today is that layered survivor, brick under its bright coat, standing over a restored keeper's cottage that serves as a small museum.

Climbing the tower: tours, times, and the rules

The lighthouse tower, keeper's cottage, and grounds are open only during tour times, which is the planning fact most visitors miss. Florida State Parks lists tower tours five days a week, Thursday through Monday, with the tower open from 10 a.m. to 12 p.m. and closed on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, and tours are free with park entry. Other park materials describe guided tours gathering at 10 a.m. and 1 p.m. on tour days, so treat the morning as your reliable window, arrive for the first tour, and confirm the day's schedule at the ranger station when you enter, because volunteer staffing sets the final timetable. There is no separate lighthouse ticket and no booking system; you pay park entry, walk or cycle to the southern tip, and join the tour.

The climb is 109 iron spiral steps, with narrow passages and real height at the top, and the park sets clear conditions: guests must be able to climb the steps and handle narrow spaces and heights, children must be at least 42 inches tall and able to climb independently, and pets are not permitted in the tower. There is no elevator and no carrying small children up. Anyone unsteady with heights or tight stairwells should enjoy the cottage and grounds instead, which are worthwhile on their own. Wear shoes that grip, travel light, and bring water, because the walk from the parking areas to the lighthouse in summer sun is part of the effort, not just the stairs.

The park around it: beach, harbor, and food

Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park covers the southern third of Key Biscayne, about 400 acres of coastal strand, mangrove wetland, and beach. The Atlantic beach runs for over a mile along the park's east side, with calm, clear water and a natural shoreline, and it is a very different experience from South Beach: no skyline of hotels behind you, no club music, just dunes, sea grape, and the lighthouse at the end of the sand. Our dedicated beach guide covers the swimming and facilities in detail. The park rents bikes, kayaks, and beach gear through its concession, maintains paved paths that make the park one of the best easy cycling stops near the city, and allows fishing along the seawall on the bay side, a local ritual at sunset.

Fees, getting there, and how to plan the day

Florida State Parks lists entry at $8 per vehicle carrying two to eight people, $4 for a single occupant vehicle or motorcycle, and $2 per pedestrian, bicyclist, or extra passenger, checked against floridastateparks.org on October 30, 2026. The lighthouse tour itself is free with that entry, which makes this one of the best value historic visits in the county. The only road in is the Rickenbacker Causeway from Brickell, which charges its own toll for cars, and the drive across, with water on both sides and the skyline behind you, is part of the experience. Parking inside the park is included in entry, divided among lots near the beach and harbor.

Frequently asked questions

Can I climb the lighthouse any day?
No. The tower opens only on tour days, Thursday through Monday, with the tower open from 10 a.m. to 12 p.m., and it is closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays. Arrive for a morning tour and confirm the schedule at the ranger station, since tours are staff and volunteer led.

How much does the lighthouse cost?
Nothing beyond park entry. Entry is $8 per vehicle for two to eight people, with lower rates for single occupants, pedestrians, and cyclists, and the tour is free with entry.

Is the beach part of the same visit?
Yes. The park's Atlantic beach is a short walk from the lighthouse, and our Bill Baggs and Cape Florida beach guide covers swimming, rentals, and facilities for the same trip.
