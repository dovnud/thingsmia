Fontainebleau Miami Beach Guide

Last reviewed: October 28, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Fontainebleau is the big one: a 1954 Morris Lapidus landmark at 4441 Collins Avenue in Mid Beach, with about 1,500 rooms and suites, a sprawling poolscape, a two level spa, headline restaurants, and a nightclub in the building. Book it if you want a self contained resort where the hotel is the destination. Skip it if you want quiet, small, or cheap, because it is none of those things.

What is the Fontainebleau, and where exactly is it?

The hotel opened in 1954 to a design by Morris Lapidus, whose curving Chateau tower and theatrical interiors defined the Miami Beach resort style the world now copies. It stands at 4441 Collins Avenue, oceanfront in Mid Beach on the stretch once called Millionaire's Row, with the beach and the Miami Beach Beachwalk directly in front. Mid Beach is the calmer middle of the island: South Beach's Ocean Drive strip is roughly ten minutes south by car or trolley, Lincoln Road is closer, and the Miami Beach Convention Center is a short drive south, which is why the hotel fills with Art Basel week guests who want a resort attached to their fair trip.

What are the rooms like?

Rooms and suites spread across four towers, the original Chateau and Versailles buildings and the later Sorrento and Tresor towers, and the tower you land in changes the stay. The older towers carry the historic bones and generally the lower prices; the all suite towers carry more space, kitchens in some layouts, and the bigger balconies. Views split between the ocean, the bay and skyline, and partial angles of both, and the spread between a base room and a good suite here is wider than at most Miami Beach hotels. Read the exact room name before you pay: at this scale, "ocean view" and "oceanfront" are different products.

What is there to do without leaving?

The poolscape is the center of gravity: a series of interlinked pools, including the bowtie shaped pool that quotes Lapidus's original design, ringed by cabanas and daybeds that are sold as a premium layer on top of your room. The Lapis spa occupies two levels. Dining runs deep for a single address, with Hakkasan for Cantonese, Scarpetta for Italian, and StripSteak by Michael Mina among the signatures, plus oceanfront and casual options below that tier. LIV, one of the most famous nightclub rooms in the country, operates inside the hotel, which tells you the evening energy level. The beach out front is served with chairs and umbrellas, and the Beachwalk puts a morning run or a bike ride to South Beach on your doorstep.

How does the beach work here?

The hotel's stretch of sand is its backyard: cross the pool deck and you are on the beach, with the hotel's chair and umbrella service set up on it. The Miami Beach Beachwalk, the paved oceanfront path, runs along the back of the sand in front of the hotel, which makes the location better than it first looks for getting around: walk or cycle south on the Beachwalk and you reach the convention center district and then South Beach without touching traffic, and the path continues north toward Surfside and Bal Harbour for a morning run. For a resort this size, that path is the quiet escape hatch from the pool deck crowds, and the sunrises over the water from this stretch are the Mid Beach standard.

Who does the Fontainebleau suit, and who should skip it?

It suits groups celebrating something, families who want the resort to entertain the kids, conference and gala travelers, and anyone whose Miami plan is mostly pool, beach, dinner, and a big night without ever calling a car. It does not suit light sleepers near the club floors on weekends, couples hunting for a hushed boutique, or budget travelers: the room rate is only the start, with a daily resort fee, valet parking, cabanas, and on site dining priced at flagship levels. The sheer scale also means real walks, from tower to pool to beach, and check in lines at peak arrival hours.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Fontainebleau in South Beach?
No. It is in Mid Beach, the quieter central stretch of Miami Beach, about ten minutes north of the Ocean Drive strip. That is a plus for sleep and a minus for walking to South Beach nightlife.

Is the Fontainebleau good for Art Basel?
Yes, as a resort base: the convention center is a short drive south, and the hotel runs a full social program during fair week. If you want to walk to the fair, look at our Loews and Setai guides instead.

Which tower should I book?
For space and newer interiors, the suite towers (Sorrento and Tresor). For the classic building and lower rates, Chateau and Versailles. Confirm the tower in the room name before paying.
