The Biltmore Hotel Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Biltmore is Miami's grand historic resort, inland in Coral Gables rather than on the beach: a National Historic Landmark opened in 1926 at 1200 Anastasia Avenue, spread over 150 acres with a Donald Ross golf course, a very large pool, and a tower modeled on the Giralda in Seville. Book it for history, golf, gardens, and a resort day that does not depend on the ocean. Skip it if a beach outside the door is the whole point of your Miami trip.

What is The Biltmore, and where exactly is it?

The hotel stands at 1200 Anastasia Avenue, Coral Gables, FL 33134, in a residential part of Coral Gables, the planned city of Mediterranean Revival streets, plazas, and banyan trees southwest of downtown Miami. The Biltmore's own site describes a National Historic Landmark built in 1926, with classic Italian, Moorish, and Spanish influences, hand painted frescoes on barrel vaulted ceilings, travertine floors, marble columns, and carved mahogany, all under the copper topped Giralda Tower. The hotel says it has been a favorite of world leaders and notables since its opening in 1926, and the building carries the layered past to match, including decades in the middle of the last century when it served other uses before returning as a hotel. The practical geography matters: this is not a beach hotel. Miami Beach and downtown Miami are drives away, and the hotel positions itself about 15 minutes from Miami International Airport, which makes it a strong first or last night of a Florida trip as well as a destination in its own right.

What are the rooms like?

The Biltmore's home page counts 271 spacious guest rooms, including suites, done in a palette the hotel describes as plums, silver sage, and regal gold, with open air balconies on many rooms looking over the golf course, the pool, or the Coral Gables treetops. Named categories on the hotel's own site include Superior rooms, Tower Suites, Anastasia Suites, Golf Suites, and larger one bedroom suites, so the spread runs from a classic hotel room to multi room setups for families and longer stays. The feel is traditional grand hotel rather than boutique minimalism: period architecture outside, recently renovated interiors inside, per the hotel. If you want the landmark experience, ask for a room with a tower or golf course view when you book, because outlook varies a lot across a property this size, and the view is much of what you are paying for here.

What is there to eat, drink, and do on site?

Start with the two signatures. The golf course is a restored Donald Ross layout: the hotel describes an 18 hole, par 71 championship course originally designed in 1925 and updated for the modern game, running right through the resort grounds. The pool is the other landmark: the hotel describes it as one of the largest hotel pools in the country, and listings for the property put it at about 23,000 square feet, ringed by private cabanas and gardens, with the poolside Cascade restaurant at its edge. Dining centers on Fontana, the hotel's Italian restaurant set around the courtyard fountain, known for its Sunday brunch; the hotel also lists Fairways at the golf course, afternoon tea, and a Culinary Academy running hands on cooking classes. A European style spa, a fitness center, and tennis courts round out the stay in place list, and the grounds themselves, 150 acres of tropical landscaping, are an activity: this is a resort you can fill two or three days at without starting a car.

Who does The Biltmore suit, and who should skip it?

It suits golfers, history lovers, couples after a romantic and slightly formal resort, families who want space and a huge pool without a beach scene, and travelers doing business or events in Coral Gables or at the nearby University of Miami. It is also a classic wedding and conference address, and the hotel lists more than 75,000 square feet of event space, which cuts both ways: on a big event weekend the public spaces belong to the event as much as to leisure guests, so ask what is on when you book. Skip it if you want to walk to the sand, if you want South Beach nightlife at the door, or if you prefer a small, quiet boutique; the Biltmore is a large, busy, full service resort with prices and extras to match, and beach days from here mean a drive and a parking plan.

The facts that matter

The Biltmore Hotel, checked against biltmorehotel.com on October 30, 2026: 1200 Anastasia Avenue, Coral Gables, FL 33134; a National Historic Landmark built in 1926, set on 150 acres; 271 guest rooms including suites, per the hotel's home page; Mediterranean architecture with Italian, Moorish, and Spanish influences and a Giralda Tower; a Donald Ross 18 hole, par 71 championship golf course originally designed in 1925; a pool the hotel describes as one of the largest hotel pools in the country, listed at about 23,000 square feet, with private cabanas and the poolside Cascade; Fontana Italian restaurant around the courtyard fountain; a European style spa and fitness center on site. Rates, golf, spa, and cabana charges are extra to the room in most cases; check the all in total before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

Is The Biltmore on the beach?
No. It is an inland resort in Coral Gables. Miami Beach is a drive away, and the resort day here is built around the pool, golf course, spa, and gardens instead.

When did The Biltmore open, and is it a historic landmark?
The hotel dates to 1926 and is designated a National Historic Landmark, per the hotel's own site. Its tower was modeled on the Giralda Tower in Seville.

Can I play golf at The Biltmore?
Yes. The resort's course is an 18 hole, par 71 championship layout originally designed by Donald Ross in 1925 and since restored and updated, per the hotel. Book tee times ahead for weekends.
