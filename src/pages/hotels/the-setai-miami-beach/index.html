The Setai, Miami Beach Guide

Last reviewed: October 28, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The Setai is the calmest luxury address in South Beach: an Asian inspired retreat at 2001 Collins Avenue, built around a serene courtyard, three temperature controlled pools, and service that stays nearly invisible. Unusually for Miami Beach, the hotel does not charge a separate resort fee; it is folded into the rate. Book it for privacy and quiet. Skip it if you came to Miami to be seen.

What is The Setai, and where exactly is it?

The Setai occupies two buildings at 2001 Collins Avenue: a restored 1930s Art Deco building, the former Dempsey-Vanderbilt Hotel, and a 40 story glass tower behind it. Interiors by Jaya Ibrahim run to dark teak, stone, and still water, a deliberate counterweight to the neon a few blocks south. The position is quietly strategic: 20th Street puts the Miami Beach Convention Center a few blocks away, Lincoln Road a short walk south, and the Bass Museum and Collins Park across the street, which is why collectors quietly book here for Art Basel while the louder hotels take the parties.

What are the rooms like?

The split between the buildings is the booking decision. Rooms in the Art Deco building are smaller, lower, and face the courtyard or the city; they suit travelers who spend their days out. The tower holds the Ocean Suites, one to four bedroom residences with kitchens and full ocean views, which suit families and longer stays who want hotel service wrapped around apartment space. Throughout, the palette is hushed and the detailing is the luxury: this is a hotel that spends its money on materials and staff ratios rather than spectacle.

What is there to do without leaving?

The three pools are the signature, each held at its own temperature: a lap pool at 75 degrees, a central lounge pool at 85, and a family pool at 90, arranged through the courtyard gardens with daybeds on a first come basis for guests. Dining is three restaurants deep: Jaya, the South and Southeast Asian dining room in the courtyard, famous for its Sunday jazz brunch; Japon, a contemporary Japanese room for dinner; and Ocean Grill, Mediterranean Italian cooking in the oceanfront setting. The spa is by Valmont. On the sand in front, attendants run a quiet beach service that matches the house tone. There is no nightclub, no pool party, and no scene, and that absence is the product.

What does a day at The Setai actually look like?

Breakfast in the courtyard at Jaya, where the room is all still water and stone. A morning on a daybed between the pools, which are held at three different temperatures precisely so that lap swimmers, loungers, and families never have to negotiate. Lunch at Ocean Grill facing the sand, an afternoon treatment at the Valmont spa or an hour on the beach under the attendants' umbrellas, and dinner at Japon or back at Jaya before the courtyard bar. Nothing on that schedule requires a reservation war, a wristband, or a queue, and that frictionlessness is what the rate is actually buying. Guests who need a concierge to arrange a boat, a gallery visit, or a Wynwood dinner will find the staff ratio up to it; the hotel simply declines to perform luxury loudly.

Who does The Setai suit, and who should skip it?

It suits couples on a deliberate splurge, privacy first travelers, repeat Miami visitors who have done the strip, and Art Basel guests who want the fair close and the hotel silent. Practical points from the hotel's own FAQ: check in begins at 4 p.m. and check out is a civilized noon, parking is valet only at an extra daily charge, and there is no separate resort fee to surprise you at checkout. Skip it if your group wants energy at the pool, if teenagers need entertaining, or if the budget has any flex left in it at all: rates are at the top of the South Beach market, and the restaurants price to match.

Frequently asked questions

Does The Setai charge a resort fee?
No. The hotel's own FAQ states it does not charge a traditional resort fee and that it is included in the stay, which is rare enough in Miami Beach to be a real booking argument. Our resort fees guide explains how the rest of the market handles it.

Which building should I book, Art Deco or tower?
The Art Deco building for character and lower rates, accepting smaller, courtyard facing rooms. The tower's Ocean Suites for space, kitchens, and the full ocean view.

Is The Setai good for Art Basel?
It is one of the closest luxury hotels to the convention center and deliberately quiet during the city's loudest week. See the Art Basel hub for the week as a whole.
