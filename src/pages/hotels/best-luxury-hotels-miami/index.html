Best Luxury Hotels in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami luxury splits three ways: a Miami Beach resort, an enclave in Bal Harbour or Sunny Isles, or a city base in Brickell or Coral Gables. All were verified on official sites in October 2026. The Ritz-Carlton Bal Harbour is excluded: it is closed until 2027.

The luxury hotels to look at first

Faena Hotel Miami Beach

The theatrical flagship of Mid Beach at 3201 Collins Avenue, wrapped in red, gold and animal print, with interiors shaped with filmmaker Baz Luhrmann and designer Catherine Martin. The Tierra Santa Healing House spa fills the third floor, Los Fuegos cooks over open fire in the Argentine tradition, Pao serves Asian dining, and the Faena Theater stages cabaret on site. Rooms are saturated, maximal and deliberately dramatic, which is either the whole appeal or a reason to look elsewhere. Book it for a splurge where the hotel is the event, and for Art Week in December, when the Faena Forum across the street is a social center. Skip it for understatement, minimalism or value. See our Faena guide.

The Setai, Miami Beach

The calm counterweight at 2001 Collins Avenue, built around a restored 1930s Art Deco building with an adjoining tower and an Asian inspired design language of dark wood, stone and still water. The courtyard pools are hushed, service is formal without being stiff, and Jaya, Ocean Grill and Japon keep most guests happily on property each night. It sits in the Collins Park end of South Beach, walkable to Lincoln Road and the convention center yet screened from Ocean Drive noise. Choose it for a quiet luxury splurge, a couples stay where privacy is the point, and travelers who find the big resorts exhausting. The trade off is scene: there is deliberately little here. See our Setai guide.

1 Hotel South Beach

A large, plant filled oceanfront resort at 2341 Collins Avenue, built around reclaimed materials, natural textures and a sustainability program the hotel puts at the center of its identity. Rooms run large by South Beach standards, there are several pools, and the Bamford Wellness Spa anchors the wellness offer. The position at 23rd Street puts the convention center and Lincoln Road within walking distance while the immediate streets stay calmer than the Ocean Drive strip. It suits travelers who want resort scale without the party register, wellness minded guests, and families who need space. Expect a big property, and pricing firmly at the luxury end despite the relaxed styling. Our 1 Hotel guide breaks down pools and rooms.

Fontainebleau Miami Beach

The big icon at 4441 Collins Avenue in Mid Beach, open since 1954 and designed by Morris Lapidus, whose curved Chateau building still defines this stretch of Collins Avenue. Scale is the product: a large poolscape, many restaurants and bars, the Lapis spa, a major nightclub scene and convention sized spaces. That makes it the easiest luxury pick for groups and celebrations, and a genuine option for families who will actually use the pools. The honest trade offs are crowds, queues at peak times and a resort machine feel that boutique travelers dislike. For Art Week, a wedding weekend or a first big Miami Beach stay, it earns its place. Read our Fontainebleau guide before picking a tower.

Acqualina Resort and Residences, Sunny Isles Beach

A Mediterranean inspired beach resort at 17875 Collins Avenue in Sunny Isles Beach, set on 4.5 beachfront acres with 400 feet of coastline, three oceanfront pools and a spa, with dining that includes Il Mulino New York. With 98 hotel rooms, it reads as a grand private estate rather than a resort machine, and service is what repeat guests cite. It suits couples and families who plan to stay on property, and anyone who wants Miami luxury without Miami Beach nightlife at the door. The trade off is distance: Sunny Isles sits north of South Beach, so Ocean Drive evenings become a drive, not a stroll. Choose it when the resort is the destination. See our Acqualina guide.

The St. Regis Bal Harbour Resort

A 27 story oceanfront tower at 9703 Collins Avenue in Bal Harbour, directly across from Bal Harbour Shops, pairing a quiet residential enclave with classic formal service in the St. Regis tradition, including its butler service. Rooms are large with wide balconies, the pools and beach service run at a measured pace, and the spa and dining keep evenings self contained. It suits shoppers who want the luxury mall across the street, couples after polished calm, and travelers who find South Beach too loud but still want a Collins Avenue beach. Bal Harbour is a drive from the South Beach strip, and the tone is formal and grown up throughout. Our St. Regis guide covers rooms and pools.

Four Seasons Hotel Miami

The urban pick at 1435 Brickell Avenue, a glass tower in the Brickell financial district with views over Biscayne Bay and a pool terrace of nearly two acres, with hammocks over the water and the EDGE Steak and Bar below. There is no beach at the door, and the page says so plainly: the sand is a causeway drive away in Miami Beach. What you get instead is the strongest downtown base here, walkable to Brickell City Centre and Mary Brickell Village, with Four Seasons service levels throughout. It suits business travelers, cruise passengers adding downtown nights, and anyone whose Miami is restaurants and Wynwood rather than a beach chair. Our Four Seasons guide covers the pool terrace.

The Biltmore Hotel, Coral Gables

A National Historic Landmark from 1926 at 1200 Anastasia Avenue in Coral Gables, spread over 150 acres of grounds with a restored Donald Ross 18 hole golf course, one of the largest hotel pools in the country by the hotel tradition, a spa and the Fontana restaurant in the courtyard. The tower, modeled on the Giralda in Seville, makes it the most romantic historic stay on this list. It suits golfers, history lovers, weddings and travelers who want old Florida grandeur with room to breathe. It is not a beach hotel: Coral Gables is inland, Miami Beach is a drive, and most guests pair it with a beach day elsewhere. Our Biltmore guide covers golf and the pool.

Nobu Hotel Miami Beach

A hotel within a hotel at 4525 Collins Avenue in Mid Beach, occupying part of the historic Eden Roc building and run in a Japanese beach house style that contrasts with the bigger resort around it. The official description counts 206 rooms and suites, with its own adults only pool beside shared Eden Roc pools, the Esencia Wellness spa and the Nobu Miami restaurant, which many guests book the hotel to eat at. It suits food led travelers, design minimalists and couples who want Mid Beach energy with a quieter room to return to. Know the arrangement first: lobbies and some facilities are shared with Eden Roc. Our Nobu guide explains what is Nobu and what is shared.

How to choose

Mid Beach (Faena, Fontainebleau, Nobu) is a resort strip near South Beach. Bal Harbour and Sunny Isles trade nightlife for calm. Brickell and Coral Gables suit city and golf trips. Check the all in total.

Frequently asked questions

Which luxury hotel is best for a first Miami Beach trip?
Faena for design, The Setai for calm, Fontainebleau for scale. All sit on the Collins Avenue beach.

Which luxury hotels on this list are not on the beach?
Four Seasons in Brickell and The Biltmore in Coral Gables. The beach is a drive.

Why is The Ritz-Carlton Bal Harbour not on the list?
Its official site states it is closed until February 2027.
