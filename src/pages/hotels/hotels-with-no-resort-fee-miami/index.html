Miami Hotels With No Resort Fee

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. The honest list is short. On October 30, 2026 we checked the official sites of Miami and Miami Beach hotels one by one, and only two properties state in their own words that they charge no resort fee, no destination fee and no amenity fee: The Vagabond Hotel Miami and Nassau Suite Hotel. Several famous names that travelers often assume are fee free are not; their own sites confirm a nightly fee, and we name four of them below. Fees change, so confirm the all in total, with taxes, before you pay for any hotel on this page.

How do Miami resort fees work?

A resort fee, also sold as a destination fee, hotel fee or amenity fee, is a mandatory nightly charge added on top of the room rate. It is not optional and it is not waived for skipping the amenities. On the official pages we checked for this article, confirmed fees at Miami area hotels ran from $23 to $60 per night before tax, and the inclusions followed a pattern: wireless internet, pool and fitness center access, beach chairs and towels, sometimes a bike hour, bottled water, or a food, drink or spa credit that has to be used daily or lost. The fee is charged per room per night, taxed, and collected at the hotel even when a booking site showed a lower headline rate. The only reliable comparison is the all in total for your dates, which is why every entry below is about what the hotel's own site says, not what a listing promises.

Verified no resort fee (checked October 30, 2026)

The Vagabond Hotel Miami

A 42 room boutique hotel at 7301 Biscayne Boulevard in the MiMo District, a restored 1953 mid century property listed on the National Register of Historic Places, built around a landscaped courtyard and a heated pool with a restored mermaid mosaic. The verification is unusually direct: the hotel's own FAQ answers the question "Does the hotel charge resort fees?" with "No. The Vagabond Hotel Miami does not charge resort fees," and its amenities page runs a section headed "No Resort Fees. No Nonsense," adding that self parking, the gym, high speed Wi-Fi and all day coffee and tea are included in the room rate. It suits travelers who want Wynwood, the Design District and Midtown on the doorstep and Miami Beach a drive away, and who would rather have free parking than a beach club. Confirm the all in total at booking anyway; policies can change. The Vagabond has no ThingsMIA guide yet, so book through the hotel's official site.

Nassau Suite Hotel

An all suite Art Deco hotel at 1414 Collins Avenue in South Beach, a block from Ocean Drive and the beach, with suites built for longer stays, a rooftop pool, jacuzzi and gym terrace, and the Blue Lavender cafe on site. The verification sits on the hotel's own Hotel Policies page, which closes its Included Room Amenities section with the line "There are no resort fees!" and lists what the rate does include: Wi-Fi, rooftop gym, pool, jacuzzi and terrace access, with beach chairs, towels and umbrellas available at the front desk. The hotel's contact page repeats the same promise for direct bookings. It suits South Beach travelers who want a suite, a kitchen friendly layout and a Collins Avenue base without the nightly fee most of its neighbors charge. As with every property here, recheck the total on the final booking screen, because a policy page is a snapshot, not a contract. Nassau Suite has no ThingsMIA guide yet; the official site is the source of record.

Checked and excluded, because a fee applies

Four well known properties failed this test on their own official pages, and they stand for dozens more. EAST Miami states a destination fee of $54 plus tax per night on its official Hotel Policies page. InterContinental Miami answers its own FAQ with a nightly resort fee of $43 plus tax per room. The Biltmore in Coral Gables prints a $23 resort fee per room per day in the terms of its official offers, covering the fitness center, internet and reading app. Four Seasons Hotel Miami offers groups a waived resort fee valued at $60 per night on its official site, which confirms the fee exists for everyone else. Freehand Miami, Kimpton EPIC, Hotel Colonnade Coral Gables and Generator Miami were also checked and excluded: their official booking terms or property supplied fee listings show a resort or destination fee. If a hotel is not in the verified list above, assume a fee until its own site proves otherwise.

How can you protect yourself from fee surprises?

Compare all in totals only, never headline rates. On the final booking screen, look for line items named resort fee, destination fee, hotel fee, amenity fee or facility fee, and check whether the total includes the tax charged on the fee itself. Book direct when a hotel markets a no fee policy, because the promise lives on the hotel's own pages and third party listings go stale. If a total jumps at checkout, screenshot it and ask the hotel to honor the published policy. And remember what no resort fee does not mean: parking, breakfast, pet charges and incidental holds can still apply, as both verified hotels state openly on their policy pages.

Frequently asked questions

How many Miami hotels truly charge no resort fee?
Far fewer than listicles suggest. Checking official sites on October 30, 2026, we could verify only two: The Vagabond Hotel Miami and Nassau Suite Hotel. Other hotels may not charge one, but if the hotel's own site does not say so, we do not list it here.

Is a destination fee different from a resort fee?
No, in practice. Destination fee, hotel fee, amenity fee and facility fee are all names for the same mandatory nightly charge. EAST Miami calls its $54 charge a destination fee; the effect on your bill is identical to a resort fee.

Can a hotel add a resort fee after I book?
Fees and policies change, which is why this page carries a check date and tells you to confirm the all in total before paying. If the final screen shows a fee the hotel's own policy page denies, contact the hotel directly with the policy page open; direct bookers are in the strongest position.
