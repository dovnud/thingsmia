Best Art Deco Hotels in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Every hotel below is a real historic building in or beside the Art Deco Historic District, verified as operating on its official site in October 2026. Two ground rules: historic rooms run small, and renovation levels vary room by room, so the category you book matters more here than anywhere else in Miami Beach.

Which historic Art Deco hotels can you actually stay in?

The Betsy, South Beach

The culture hotel at 1440 Ocean Drive, and the honest starting point is that it is two hotels joined together: the Georgian Revival original building, built as the Betsy Ross Hotel, and an Art Deco wing, the former Carlton Hotel, linked at the top by The Orb, the egg shaped skybridge that has become an Ocean Drive landmark. The wings feel different: rooms in the original building run smaller and more classic, and the Deco wing holds the lighter, larger suites, so your category decides the stay. Around that sit a rooftop pool, live jazz and a writer in residence program. It suits readers, art travelers and couples wanting Ocean Drive at its calm northern end. Our full Betsy guide explains the two wings room by room.

The Colony Hotel

The blue neon postcard at 736 Ocean Drive, an Art Deco hotel dating to 1935 whose sign is one of the most photographed on the strip. Behind the facade it is a compact, mid priced hotel: rooms are modest, the beach and Lummus Park are directly across the street, and Montana's restaurant runs the ground floor. Its own site lists a daily resort fee covering items such as beach towels and Wi-Fi, so factor it in. The Colony suits travelers who want the classic Ocean Drive picture at a fair price and will be out most of the day. It does not suit light sleepers: Ocean Drive at 7th Street is party central, and front rooms face it. Ask for a rear room if sleep matters.

Clevelander South Beach

The party landmark at 1020 Ocean Drive, on Ocean Drive since 1938, built around its pool patio with pool bars, a rooftop terrace, a sundeck and a sports bar, where the entertainment is the hotel. Rooms are modern in style, but nobody books the Clevelander for the room: you book it to sit in the middle of the Ocean Drive scene from check in to last call, with the beach across the street. That cuts both ways. If you want DJs and people watching at your door, nothing else here competes. If you want sleep before midnight, a family base or a quiet stay, book any other hotel on this page: the music is the point and it runs late.

The Marlin Hotel

A small, polished Art Deco boutique at 1200 Collins Avenue, a 1939 landmark two blocks from the beach near Espanola Way, fully renovated behind its historic facade. Its own site puts rooms between 240 and 500 square feet, the useful honesty for this list: even renovated deco boutiques are compact, and the Marlin answers with strong, spa style bathrooms, rainfall showers and a calm, grown up finish rather than resort facilities. There is no big pool scene, and that is the appeal. It suits couples and design travelers who want a quiet, high quality base in the middle of South Beach, walkable to Lincoln Road and Ocean Drive but sleeping on Collins Avenue, calmer at night than the Ocean Drive front row.

The National Hotel

The elegant tower at 1677 Collins Avenue, opened at the turn of the 1940s and fully restored in 2014, recognized by Historic Hotels of America for its architectural authenticity. It is an adults only oceanfront resort, organized around a long infinity edge pool, with 116 rooms in the Historic Tower and 36 cabanas and suites by the pool, Mareva1939 for Spanish food and Bar1939 in the lobby. The position, beside Collins Park and a short walk from Lincoln Road, is one of the best here. It suits couples who want Art Deco scale with resort calm. Tower rooms vary in size and view, and the premium sits on direct ocean views, so read the room details before paying for the word historic.

Eurostars Winter Haven

The quiet corner at 1400 Ocean Drive, a 1939 Art Deco facade at the calmer northern end of the strip, across from Lummus Park. Note the name change, because older guides will mislead you: the hotel left the Marriott Autograph Collection in September 2024 and now operates as Eurostars Winter Haven, verified on the Eurostars site, while the property also keeps its own Winter Haven site with the same address and phone. Inside are about 70 rooms, a rooftop terrace and the Il Bolognese restaurant. It suits travelers who want Ocean Drive without its loudest blocks, in a calmer house style. Rooms in a 1939 building vary, and Ocean Drive facing rooms trade view for street sound, so choose your exposure carefully.

The Plymouth South Beach

The Streamline outlier at 336 21st Street in Collins Park, a four story Art Moderne building a few blocks west of the beach, now part of Small Luxury Hotels of the World, with interiors mixing metro tiles, vintage inspired furnishings and murals by Roman Chatov. The Plymouth suits travelers who prefer the cultural district: the Bass Museum is at the corner, the convention center is a short walk, and the hotel runs guests to its beach area on 21st Street. On site are a courtyard pool and Blue Ribbon Sushi Bar and Grill, an unlikely and excellent pairing. It suits Art Week visitors, museum goers and couples. It is not on the sand, and standard rooms in a historic low rise run compact.

Leslie Hotel

A small Ocean Drive original at 1244 Ocean Drive, a 1937 Art Deco building with 35 rooms, directly across from the beach. Its own description makes a value point worth repeating: breakfast, beach chairs and towels are included in its rates, which is rare on a strip that usually unbundles everything into a resort fee. Facilities are boutique scale: a restaurant, a bar, a pool and a terrace rather than a resort deck. It suits travelers who want a genuine small deco hotel at the center of Ocean Drive, with Espanola Way and Lincoln Road walkable. The warnings apply: 35 rooms means categories sell out and vary, historic rooms are not large, and this stretch of Ocean Drive is lively at night, so rear rooms sleep better.

What should you know before booking a deco hotel?

Treat the building as the amenity: you pay for a 1930s facade and a place in the district, and the room will be smaller than a new tower at the same price. Front rooms come with street noise; rear rooms are the quiet play. Ask about the specific room before paying. For space, pick the National or The Betsy's Deco wing.

The facts that matter

Checked on official sites on October 30, 2026: The Betsy, 1440 Ocean Drive (Georgian Revival and Art Deco wings); Colony, 736 Ocean Drive, from 1935; Clevelander, 1020 Ocean Drive, since 1938; Marlin, 1200 Collins Avenue, 1939, rooms 240 to 500 square feet; National, 1677 Collins Avenue, adults only, restored 2014; Eurostars Winter Haven, 1400 Ocean Drive, renamed September 2024; Plymouth, 336 21st Street, not on the beach; Leslie, 1244 Ocean Drive, 1937, 35 rooms.

Frequently asked questions

Are Art Deco hotel rooms really that small?
Often, yes. The Marlin lists rooms from 240 square feet, and standard Colony and Leslie rooms are compact. Suites are the fix.

Which deco hotel is quietest?
The Plymouth, in Collins Park, and the Eurostars Winter Haven at Ocean Drive's calmer northern end. The Clevelander is the loudest stay here by design.

Why are some famous deco hotels not listed?
Because they are not bookable under their famous names. The Delano has been closed and in transition, and Winter Haven appears only under its current Eurostars name.
