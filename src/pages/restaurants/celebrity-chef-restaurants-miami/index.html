Celebrity Chef Restaurants in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. For a celebrity chef room with a real sense of place, Red Rooster Overtown from Marcus Samuelsson. For television famous cooking, Gordon Ramsay Hell's Kitchen downtown. For Miami's own stars, Jeremy Ford at Stubborn Seed and Michael Beltran at Ariete. Every attribution below comes from the restaurant's own site or the chef's official materials, checked on October 30, 2026, with closures handled honestly below.

Television names with Miami rooms

Red Rooster Overtown, Marcus Samuelsson

Red Rooster Overtown, at 920 NW 2nd Avenue in Overtown, is Marcus Samuelsson's Miami restaurant, with the chef named on the restaurant's own team pages. The kitchen cooks the chef's Harlem meets the South format, comfort food with global edges, in a historic Overtown room. Order the fried chicken and the cornbread the house is known for, and check its site for live music nights. It suits diners who want a celebrity name attached to a neighborhood with its own food history. Price level: splurge, listed as $$$ on the restaurant's own site.

Gordon Ramsay Hell's Kitchen, Downtown

Hell's Kitchen Miami, at 333 Biscayne Boulevard Way downtown, is Gordon Ramsay's restaurant, listed on the chef's own restaurant group site with its Miami menu. Order the beef Wellington, the dish the television show made famous, plus the lobster risotto and sticky toffee pudding. It suits fans of the show, celebration dinners, and downtown hotel guests. Expect the television format translated into a working dining room. Book ahead on weekends, when the name draws its biggest crowds. Price level: splurge.

Bazaar Meat by Jose Andres, Mid-Beach

Bazaar Meat, at 4041 Collins Avenue inside the Andaz Miami Beach, is Jose Andres's steakhouse concept, and it opened on October 22, 2026, per launch coverage in Miami food press and the brand's own location page. Order with the meat first format the name promises, and share across the table. It suits Andres fans and Mid-Beach hotel guests. One caution matters: this is a new room in a new hotel, not the original Bazaar at the SLS South Beach, which closed in March 2023. Older guides pointing you to the SLS address are out of date. Price level: splurge.

Miami's own chef stars

Stubborn Seed, Jeremy Ford, South Beach

Stubborn Seed, at 101 Washington Avenue in South Beach, is chef Jeremy Ford's restaurant, and its own materials note his Top Chef win. The format is a chef's counter style tasting approach with a short, changing menu. Order the tasting format and let the kitchen drive, rather than hunting for a signature dish that may rotate off. It suits serious food travelers and special occasion diners who want Miami's own television chef rather than an imported brand. The room is small, so reservations matter more here than anywhere else on this page. Price level: splurge.

Ariete, Michael Beltran, Coconut Grove

Ariete, at 3540 Main Highway in Coconut Grove, is chef and owner Michael Beltran's restaurant, per its own site, cooking what it describes as New American food through a Miami Cuban American lens. Order the duck press experience if your table is celebrating, or the frita burger at the bar end of the menu. It suits Grove diners, locals marking an occasion, and visitors who want a chef's room outside the beach circuit. Beltran is a Miami raised chef cooking in his own city, a different weight than the imported names. Price level: splurge.

Carbone Miami, Mario Carbone, South Beach

Carbone Miami, at 49 Collins Avenue in South Beach, is the Miami room of Major Food Group, whose own site names its founders as Mario Carbone, Jeff Zalaznick, and Rich Torrisi. Order the spicy rigatoni vodka and the tableside Caesar, the two dishes the room is booked for. It suits scene first diners, celebrations, and anyone who wants the New York Italian American format in a South Beach room. It is one of the hardest reservations on this page, so plan weeks, not days, ahead. Price level: splurge.

L'Atelier de Joel Robuchon, Design District

L'Atelier de Joel Robuchon, at 151 NE 41st Street in the Design District, carries the name of the late French chef Joel Robuchon, and its own site is careful in a way this page copies: it describes culinary director Christophe Bellanca as a longtime Robuchon protege carrying on the legacy. Robuchon himself is not in this kitchen, and any guide implying otherwise is misleading you. Order the tasting format around the counter for the full legacy experience. It suits French fine dining devotees and Design District splurges. Price level: splurge.

How to read a celebrity chef name

A famous name on a door can mean three different things: the chef owns and cooks the concept, as with Beltran and Ford here; the chef's group runs the room to its format, as with Ramsay and Samuelsson; or the name is a legacy, as with Robuchon, who died in 2018. This page labels each case as the restaurant's own site states it. None of it guarantees the named chef is on the line the night you visit.

Frequently asked questions

Which celebrity chef restaurant is hardest to book?
Carbone Miami, where weekend tables go weeks out, and Stubborn Seed, where the room is small. Book either as soon as your trip dates are fixed.

Is The Bazaar by Jose Andres still open?
The original Bazaar at the SLS South Beach closed in March 2023. Jose Andres is back in a new form: Bazaar Meat opened October 22, 2026, at the Andaz Miami Beach on Collins Avenue, and that is the room listed above.

Which of these chefs are actually from Miami?
Michael Beltran of Ariete is Miami raised, and Jeremy Ford's Stubborn Seed is his own Miami room after his Top Chef win. Michael Schwartz at Michael's Genuine, on our brunch page, is the other longtime local name to know.

Are celebrity chef rooms worth the price?
At splurge level, judge them as special occasion dinners. The rooms above earn their place on consistency and a distinct point of view, not on the name alone.
