Best Rooftop Restaurants in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. For a Brickell skyline dinner, choose Sugar at EAST Miami. For an ocean facing splurge, Watr at the 1 Rooftop in South Beach. For a full dinner rather than drinks and snacks, MILA on Lincoln Road. For something local and low key, Terras above Little Havana. Every rooftop below was checked as operating on October 30, 2026, and each entry says what to order and who it suits, because Miami rooftops split sharply between restaurants that happen to be high up and bars that happen to serve food.

High rise rooftops in Brickell and Wynwood

Sugar at EAST Miami, Brickell

Sugar sits on the 40th floor of EAST Miami at 788 Brickell Plaza, and its own site describes an Asian inspired rooftop bar and garden serving share plates and cocktails above Brickell. Order small plates built for a group, such as sushi rolls and skewers, and treat it as drinks with serious food rather than a quiet dinner. It suits after work groups, birthdays, and visitors staying in Brickell who want the skyline without crossing to the beach. Expect a scene at peak hours, and book ahead on weekends. Price level: splurge leaning.

Astra, Wynwood

Astra, at 2121 NW 2nd Avenue in Wynwood, is a rooftop restaurant serving Mediterranean and Greek leaning food, with its own site taking reservations for the rooftop. Order grilled seafood, dips and pita to start, and a whole fish if your table shares. It suits diners who want a full sit down meal outdoors above the Wynwood art district, and it pairs well with an afternoon of gallery and mural walking before dinner. Sunset is the seat everyone wants, so reserve for the early evening slot. Price level: mid range to splurge.

South Beach rooftops

Watr at the 1 Rooftop, South Beach

Watr sits 18 stories up at 1 Hotel South Beach, 2341 Collins Avenue, and the hotel describes it as a rooftop restaurant with Japanese influenced food and ocean views. Order sushi, crudo, and grilled items, and go for lunch or golden hour if you want the view in daylight. It suits a splurge beach day that ends upstairs, couples, and anyone whose hotel is in Mid-Beach who wants dinner without leaving the sand behind. The setting is the draw, so ask for a perimeter table when you book. Price level: splurge.

MILA, Lincoln Road, South Beach

MILA, at 1636 Meridian Avenue above Lincoln Road, serves what it calls MediterrAsian food, a mix of Mediterranean and Japanese cooking, in a large rooftop space. Order from both sides of the menu, grilled meats and maki, rather than picking one cuisine. It suits groups, celebrations, and diners who want a full restaurant dinner on a rooftop, not a lounge with snacks. It is one of the few Miami rooftops where the kitchen is the headline as much as the view. Book well ahead for weekend nights. Price level: splurge.

Serena Rooftop at Moxy South Beach

Serena, at the Moxy South Beach, 915 Washington Avenue, is an open air rooftop serving contemporary Mexican food, per the hotel and the restaurant site. Order tacos, ceviche, and shareable plates, and come for the casual end of the rooftop spectrum. It suits younger groups, pre dinner drinks that turn into dinner, and visitors who want a rooftop without a splurge dress code. Because it is open air, check the weather before you commit to a special occasion here. Price level: mid range.

A neighborhood rooftop in Little Havana

Terras, Little Havana

Terras, at 528 SW 9th Avenue atop the Life House hotel in Little Havana, serves Latin American street food style plates on a rooftop facing downtown. Order the shareable Latin plates and a rum based drink, and time your visit for the skyline lighting up. It suits travelers exploring Little Havana who want to end the day above the neighborhood rather than leaving it, and it makes a natural finale after our Little Havana food tour route. Price level: mid range.

Rooftop weather and booking notes

Most Miami rooftops are open air or partly covered, and summer rain can close a terrace on short notice. Hotels usually post weather changes on their own social channels first. Reserve for Friday and Saturday nights at every rooftop on this page, ask for a covered or perimeter table by name when you book, and keep a same neighborhood indoor backup in mind during the June to October wet season.

Frequently asked questions

Which Miami rooftop is best for a full dinner?
MILA in South Beach and Astra in Wynwood run full restaurant menus on their rooftops. Sugar and Watr lean toward share plates and sushi alongside drinks, so order with that format in mind.

Do Miami rooftops take reservations?
The hotel rooftops on this page take bookings through their own sites, and weekend evenings fill first. Serena and Terras are more casual, but a reservation still protects you on Friday and Saturday nights.

What happens if it rains?
Open air terraces may pause or close service during heavy rain, which is common on summer afternoons. Check the forecast, book the earliest dinner slot in the wet season, and treat a hotel rooftop with an indoor lounge as the safer special occasion pick.

Is Juvia still open?
No. Listings checked for this page in October 2026 show Juvia on Lincoln Road as permanently closed, so it is not included above. Always confirm a rooftop on its own site before a special trip, because rooftop rooms churn fast in Miami.
