Best Restaurants in Wynwood

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Wynwood eats in two gears. For a proper dinner, book KYU for wood fired Asian cooking, Uchi Miami for Japanese tastings and sushi, or Doya for Aegean plates built for sharing, with Bakan covering the lively Mexican table. For the casual hours around the murals, Coyo Taco, Zak the Baker, and The Salty Donut keep things fast and affordable. Every place on this page was checked against its own website or a current listing on October 30, 2026, and the closed rooms we left off are named in the fact box below.

The dinner anchors

KYU

At 251 NW 25th Street, KYU is Wynwood's wood fired, Asian inspired flagship, back open after a long closure for storm repairs. The kitchen works over open fire, so order with that in mind: the Korean fried chicken, the roasted cauliflower, and something from the grill to share. It is a splurge by Wynwood standards, and it suits dates and small groups who want the neighborhood's most polished dinner. Book ahead on weekends, when the room fills early.

Uchi Miami

Uchi Miami, at 252 NW 25th Street, is the Miami outpost of the Austin born Japanese restaurant founded by chef Tyson Cole. The menu mixes signature tastings, sushi, and seasonal specials rather than sticking to a traditional format, which makes it a good pick for diners who like to order a little of everything. It reads as a splurge and a date night room: calm, precise, and a step removed from the noise of NW 2nd Avenue outside.

Doya

Doya, at 347 NW 24th Street, cooks modern Aegean food, meaning the meze traditions of Greece and Turkey served as plates for the table. Start with cold and hot meze, add grilled octopus, and let the table keep ordering in rounds. The format suits groups better than almost anywhere else in Wynwood, because nobody has to commit to a single main course. Prices sit in the mid range to splurge band, depending on how long the table keeps going.

Bakan

Bakan, at 2801 NW 2nd Avenue, is the big, cactus lined Mexican dining room on Wynwood's main corner. Tortillas are made in house, and the order that covers the table is queso fundido, tacos built on those tortillas, and a whole fish or grilled meat for sharing. It is mid range to splurge, loud in a good way, and one of the easier Wynwood rooms for a group celebration. Its own site lists late hours late in the week, which is why it also appears on our late night page.

Casual stops and daytime fuel

Coyo Taco

Coyo Taco, at 2300 NW 2nd Avenue, is the neighborhood's taqueria workhorse. The menu on its site runs from al pastor and carne asada to beef birria and duck carnitas, with guacamole made to order and tortillas pressed in house. It is budget friendly, fast even when the line looks long, and the natural lunch between mural blocks. Vegetarians are covered too, with cauliflower and plantain tacos on the same menu.

Zak the Baker

Zak the Baker, at 295 NW 26th Street, is a kosher bakery and cafe and the daytime anchor of Wynwood eating. Come for the breads, babka, and pastries, and stay for sandwiches, salads, and soups at the cafe tables. It is budget friendly to mid range, and it suits anyone starting a Wynwood day early, because the neighborhood's dinner rooms are still dark when the first loaves come out. Lines at the counter move faster than they look.

The Salty Donut

The Salty Donut, at 50 NW 24th Street, covers the sweet stop. The Wynwood shop pairs a serious coffee program with donuts that rotate from the traditional glazed to the guava and cheese and the brown butter and salt. It is budget friendly, quick, and an easy win with kids in tow. If you are building a Wynwood food crawl, this is the pause between tacos and dinner, not an afterthought.

Checked before we listed them

Every restaurant above was confirmed as operating on October 30, 2026, from its own website or a current primary listing. Two well known Wynwood rooms are not on this list because they have closed: Beaker and Gray shut in 2024, and Wynwood Kitchen and Bar is also closed. KYU, sometimes still described as closed in older guides, has reopened and was taking reservations when we checked.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need reservations for Wynwood restaurants?
For KYU, Uchi Miami, and Doya on a Friday or Saturday, yes, book ahead. Bakan takes reservations and is easier on weeknights. The casual stops on this page, Coyo Taco, Zak the Baker, and The Salty Donut, are walk in.

What is the cheapest way to eat well in Wynwood?
Build the day around Coyo Taco for lunch and Zak the Baker or The Salty Donut for a daytime stop, then save the budget for one dinner. Wynwood's casual end is genuinely good, not a compromise.

Which Wynwood restaurant suits a group?
Doya, because the meze format is designed for sharing, and Bakan, because the room and menu both handle a crowd. KYU works for a smaller group that wants one shared, fire driven meal.

Can you eat in Wynwood before the galleries and walls get busy?
Yes. Zak the Baker opens the day, and the blocks around NW 2nd Avenue and NW 25th Street put most of this list within a short walk of each other.
