Best Haitian Restaurants in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Miami's Haitian food heart is Little Haiti, and the name to start with is Chef Creole on NW 54th Street. For a small, beloved neighborhood spot, Chez Le Bebe, two blocks east on the same street. For garden seating and a community hub, Naomi's Garden. For a modern Caribbean kitchen with Haitian roots, Manjay in Little River. Every kitchen below was checked as operating on October 30, 2026, and this page keeps the list short on purpose, because Haitian restaurants here are mostly small family operations rather than chains.

Little Haiti kitchens

Chef Creole Seasoned Restaurant, Little Haiti

Chef Creole, at 200 NW 54th Street in Little Haiti, is the neighborhood's flagship Haitian restaurant, serving Haitian and Caribbean seafood and plates from its own menu online. Order griot, the fried pork the cuisine is known for, with pikliz on the side, or the fried snapper and conch fritters if you came for seafood. It suits first time Haitian diners who want a full menu in one stop, families, and anyone building a Little Haiti day. Takeout does a brisk business here too, so the counter stays busy even between the main meal rushes. The NW 54th Street corridor here is the center of Haitian Miami, so pair the meal with a walk past the Caribbean Marketplace nearby. Price level: budget friendly to mid range.

Chez Le Bebe, Little Haiti

Chez Le Bebe, at 114 NE 54th Street in Little Haiti, is a small Haitian restaurant whose own site walks through the classics, griot, fish, oxtail, and Soup Joumou, the squash soup tied to Haitian independence celebrations. Order griot or oxtail with rice and beans, and ask about Soup Joumou when it is on, traditionally around the New Year period. It suits diners who want the home cooking end of the cuisine and a room that serves its own neighborhood first. Portions run generous, so order to share if you want to taste widely. Price level: budget friendly.

Naomi's Garden Restaurant and Lounge, Little Haiti

Naomi's Garden, at 650 NW 71st Street in Little Haiti, is a Haitian and Caribbean restaurant with garden seating that doubles as a community gathering place, per its own site, which also lists a newer South Beach location on Alton Road. Order the Haitian plates and grilled items, and eat outside in the garden if the weather allows. It suits groups, a slow lunch, and visitors who want to understand Little Haiti as a living neighborhood rather than a photo stop. Live events and a lounge format make evenings different from lunchtimes here, so check its site for what is on. Call ahead for large groups, because the garden tables are the first to go on weekends. Price level: budget friendly to mid range.

A modern Caribbean table

Manjay, Little River

Manjay, at 8300 NE 2nd Avenue in Little River, inside The Citadel food hall area, is a modern Caribbean restaurant founded by Haitian chefs, per its own site and local coverage, and it also lists a Doral location. Order the Caribbean plates run through a modern kitchen, and expect Haitian flavors presented in a food hall format rather than a traditional dining room. It suits diners new to the cuisine who want an easy entry point, and groups where everyone orders differently. Calling it modern Caribbean is deliberate on this page: Manjay draws on Haitian roots but cooks across the Caribbean, and it is listed here with that framing rather than as a traditional Haitian kitchen. Price level: mid range.

What to order, in one box

Four dishes explain Haitian cooking in Miami. Griot is pork shoulder marinated in citrus and Scotch bonnet, boiled, then fried, and it is the plate every kitchen here is judged on. Pikliz is the spicy pickled cabbage slaw that comes with it and cuts the richness. Diri ak djon djon is rice cooked with black mushrooms, a special occasion dish. Soup Joumou, a beef and squash soup, is eaten on January 1 to mark Haitian independence in 1804. Order griot and pikliz first, then explore.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the center of Haitian food in Miami?
Little Haiti, along and around NW 54th Street. Chef Creole and Chez Le Bebe sit on that corridor a few blocks apart, and Naomi's Garden is a short drive north on NW 71st Street.

What is the one dish to try first?
Griot with pikliz and rice. It is the plate Haitian kitchens are known for, and every restaurant on this page serves a version of it.

Is Tap Tap still open in South Beach?
No. Tap Tap, the longtime Haitian restaurant on 5th Street, closed in 2020, per local reporting referenced in listings checked for this page, so it is not listed above. For Haitian food, plan on Little Haiti instead of South Beach.

Are these restaurants expensive?
Mostly no. The Little Haiti kitchens on this page sit at budget friendly to mid range levels, with generous portions. Manjay, in a food hall format, is the mid range entry.
