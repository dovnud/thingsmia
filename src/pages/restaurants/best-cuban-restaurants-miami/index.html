Best Cuban Restaurants in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Start on Calle Ocho in Little Havana with Versailles and La Carreta for the classic sit down experience, then branch out: Islas Canarias for croquetas, Sergio's on Coral Way, Havana Harry's in Coral Gables, and Enriqueta's, El Rey de las Fritas, Sanguich, and Caja Caliente for counters. Every place below was checked as operating October 30, 2026.

The classic Calle Ocho rooms

Versailles Restaurant, Little Havana

Versailles, at 3555 SW 8th Street, has operated since 1971 and describes itself on its own site as serving Cuban cuisine in the heart of Miami's Cuban community. Order ropa vieja or a Cuban sandwich, and finish at La Ventanita, its walk up coffee window, for a cortadito or a colada. It suits first time visitors and groups who want a large, full service room. Our dedicated Versailles guide covers the visit in detail. Price level: mid range.

La Carreta, Little Havana

La Carreta sits at 3632 SW 8th Street, a short walk from Versailles, and serves the same Calle Ocho corridor with a broad, family style Cuban menu. Order ropa vieja, vaca frita, or lechon asado, and add a cafecito from its coffee window. It suits families, larger groups, and anyone who wants the classic menu without a special occasion frame. Many visitors try one for lunch and the other for coffee later in the day. Price level: mid range.

Neighborhood favorites beyond Calle Ocho

Islas Canarias, Tamiami area, southwest Miami

Islas Canarias, at 13695 SW 26th Street, is a family restaurant established in 1977, per its own site, serving Cuban, Spanish, and international dishes from family recipes. Order the croquetas first, they are the dish regulars talk about, then vaca frita or pan con bistec. It suits a deliberate trip west for a local favorite rather than a tourist stop. Price level: mid range.

Sergio's, Coral Way

Sergio's lists its Coral Way location at 3252 SW 22nd Street, one of several Miami locations for a business that says it has served Cuban cuisine in South Florida since 1975. Order a Cuban sandwich, croquetas, or pan con bistec. It suits families and everyday lunches, the kind of dependable Cuban restaurant Miami residents use week after week. Price level: mid range, with the chain listing a $$ range on its locations page.

Havana Harry's, Coral Gables

Havana Harry's, at 4612 South Le Jeune Road in Coral Gables, was founded in 1995 and, after a long closure, reopened its doors on October 19, 2026, according to its own site. Order ropa vieja, arroz con pollo, or a Cuban sandwich, and save room for dessert. It suits Coral Gables lunches and dinners in a refreshed, sit down room. Because it has only just reopened, check its site before you go. Price level: mid range.

Counters, sandwiches, and Cuban tacos

Enriqueta's Sandwich Shop, Edgewater

Enriqueta's, at 186 NE 29th Street near Wynwood and Edgewater, is a small Cuban sandwich shop and cafe. Order pan con bistec or a classic Cuban sandwich, plus a colada if you are sharing. It is a breakfast and lunch stop, and it suits anyone touring Wynwood or Midtown who wants a local lunch counter. Expect a fast moving queue at peak lunch hours. Price level: budget friendly.

El Rey de las Fritas, Little Havana

El Rey de las Fritas, at 1821 SW 8th Street, is built around the frita, the seasoned Cuban style burger topped with crisp shoestring potatoes on a Cuban roll. Order the original frita first, then the Frita Suprema for the loaded version. It suits a quick, inexpensive Calle Ocho stop, and pairs naturally with a walk through Little Havana. Price level: budget friendly.

Sanguich, Little Havana

Sanguich, at 2057 SW 8th Street, makes its Cuban sandwiches in house from the bread and mustard to the lechon, per its own site. Order El Cubano, or pan con lechon, and a batido (a fruit milkshake). It suits sandwich purists and anyone doing a Calle Ocho tasting walk who wants one carefully made sandwich rather than a full plate. The room is small, so takeout may be the practical choice at lunch. Price level: mid range.

Caja Caliente, Coral Gables

Caja Caliente, listed at 808 Ponce de Leon Boulevard in Coral Gables, is known for its original Cuban tacos, Cuban fillings served in taco format. Order lechon or ropa vieja tacos and pan con lechon to share. It suits anyone who wants Cuban flavors in a relaxed setting rather than a formal dining room. Price level: mid range.

How to order a first Cuban meal

Start with croquetas, choose one main plate (ropa vieja, vaca frita, or lechon asado), add rice, black beans, and maduros (sweet plantains), and finish with a cortadito. That structure works at almost every restaurant on this page.

Frequently asked questions

Where should a first time visitor eat Cuban food in Miami?
On Calle Ocho in Little Havana. Versailles and La Carreta give you the classic rooms, and El Rey de las Fritas and Sanguich give you the counter version. See our Little Havana food tour guide for how those walks work.

What is a ventanita?
A ventanita is a walk up coffee window. You order standing on the sidewalk, drink a small, strong coffee such as a cortadito or share a colada, and move on. Versailles calls its window La Ventanita and treats it as a centerpiece of the restaurant.

What is the difference between ropa vieja and vaca frita?
Ropa vieja is shredded beef simmered in a tomato based sauce until tender. Vaca frita is shredded beef crisped on the grill or flat top, usually with onions and lime. Ordering one of each for the table is the fastest way to learn which style you prefer.

Do I need a reservation at these restaurants?
Most places on this page are walk in, counter, or casual family rooms. For a weekend dinner, check the restaurant's own site on the day, because policies change.
