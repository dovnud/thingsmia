Best Restaurants in Little Havana

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Little Havana eating runs along Calle Ocho, which is SW 8th Street, and the winning move is to mix one landmark with the everyday counters. Versailles is the big dining room, Sanguich and El Rey de las Fritas cover the sandwich and frita end, Old's Havana and El Cristo are the sit down Cuban staples on the walking core, Cafe La Trova is the night out, and Azucar is the sweet finish. Start with our Little Havana neighborhood guide for the walking route, and if you would rather eat with a guide, read the Little Havana food tour guide before you book anything.

The landmark and the night out

Versailles Restaurant

Versailles, at 3555 SW 8th Street, is Little Havana's landmark Cuban restaurant, a big mirrored dining room west of the Domino Park core on the same street. Order ropa vieja or the Cuban sandwich, add croquetas, and finish at the walk up coffee window, the ventanita, for a cafecito. It is budget friendly to mid range, works for families and first timers alike, and keeps the latest hours in the neighborhood: its own site lists closing at midnight Monday to Thursday and Sunday, and at 1 a.m. on Friday and Saturday.

Cafe La Trova

Cafe La Trova, at 971 SW 8th Street, pairs a 1950s style Cuban bar with a real kitchen. The food program is led by chef Michelle Bernstein, and the menu on the restaurant's site runs to Cuban sandwich empanadas, jamon serrano croquetas, lechon, and skirt steak ropa vieja, while the cantineros behind the bar work in the classic Cuban style and live trova musicians play the room. It is a splurge by Calle Ocho standards and suits a night out more than a quick lunch. Book ahead.

The Calle Ocho staples

Old's Havana Cuban Bar and Cocina

Old's Havana, at 1442 SW 8th Street, sits right beside Domino Park and leans into old Havana atmosphere, with live music, a patio, and a menu of Cuban standards. The Cubano sandwich and the lechon asado are the orders, with a mojito if the afternoon allows. It is mid range, easy with a mixed group, and the most convenient sit down lunch in the middle of the walking core, because you can watch the street from the table.

Sanguich

Sanguich, at 2057 SW 8th Street, treats the Cuban sandwich as a craft product: the shop makes its own ham, pork, mustard, and pickles in house. Order the El Cubano, the pan con lechon, or the croqueta preparada, and expect a line at lunch that moves steadily. It is budget friendly, small, and best for sandwich purists who want to taste what the fuss is about. This is a lunch stop, not a lingering dinner room, and it is worth the short walk west of the core.

El Rey de las Fritas

El Rey de las Fritas, at 1821 SW 8th Street, is the home of the frita cubana, the Cuban style burger of seasoned beef topped with crisp julienne potatoes on a Cuban roll. The original frita is the order, and the suprema loads it further. It is budget friendly, quick, and a piece of Miami food history you eat at a counter. Families and anyone building a Calle Ocho tasting walk should put it between the coffee stops.

El Cristo Restaurant

El Cristo, at 1543 SW 8th Street, is the everyday Cuban restaurant in the middle of the core, serving breakfast, lunch, and dinner plates with Cuban and American touches. It suits travelers who want a sit down meal without a reservation, a big menu, and prices that stay budget friendly. When the landmark rooms have a wait, El Cristo is the local answer a few blocks away, and its position makes it easy to fold into a walking day.

Azucar Ice Cream Company

Azucar, at 1503 SW 8th Street, is the dessert stop of the Calle Ocho walk, scooping Cuban inspired ice cream a few steps from Domino Park. The signature flavor is Abuela Maria, guava with cream cheese and Maria cookies, and the cafe con leche flavor covers the coffee drinkers. It is budget friendly, fast, and the right way to end a Little Havana afternoon before the evening music starts on the street.

Frequently asked questions

Where should a first timer eat in Little Havana?
Versailles for the landmark meal, then walk the core and add Sanguich or El Rey de las Fritas for the sandwich traditions. The two experiences together explain the neighborhood better than either one alone.

Is Versailles on the main walking stretch?
It is on the same street, SW 8th Street, but west of the Domino Park core. Most visitors treat it as a short drive or rideshare hop from the central blocks rather than part of the walk itself.

Do you need to speak Spanish to eat in Little Havana?
No. Restaurants on Calle Ocho serve visitors all day and English is fine, though a few words of Spanish are always appreciated at the counter.

Which Little Havana restaurant is best at night?
Cafe La Trova, for the combination of a full dinner menu, classic Cuban cocktails, and live music. Versailles is the later kitchen, open to midnight or 1 a.m. depending on the day, per its site.
