Versailles Restaurant Little Havana Guide

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Versailles Restaurant is at 3555 SW 8th Street on Calle Ocho, at the western edge of Little Havana. It has operated since 1971, per its own site. Go for a full Cuban meal in the mirrored dining room, order ropa vieja or a Cuban sandwich, and do not leave without a coffee at La Ventanita, its walk up window. It is a walk in, everyday restaurant, not a special occasion reservation exercise.

Why Versailles matters

Versailles' own history page says the restaurant opened in 1971 and soon became a gathering place for Miami's Cuban exile community, a role it describes as an unofficial town square. That history is still the experience: politicians, journalists, families celebrating, and neighbors debating all pass through the same room and the same coffee window. For a visitor, that makes Versailles different from a themed restaurant. The food is the reason to sit down, but the room and the window are the reason this specific address, rather than any good Cuban kitchen, became a landmark. Plan it as both a meal and a piece of Miami civic life, ideally on a Calle Ocho walk that also takes in Domino Park and the rest of the street.

The room, the bakery, and La Ventanita

The main dining room is large, bright, and lined with mirrors and etched glass, with a menu long enough to cover breakfast, lunch, and dinner in one sitting. Next door, the Versailles bakery handles the pastry side of a Cuban morning: pastelitos, croquetas to go, and cakes for the neighborhood. The element to build your visit around is La Ventanita. Versailles' own page on the window describes it as serving the restaurant's Cuban cafecito, including cortaditos and coladas, and credits founder Felipe Valls Sr. with developing the walk up window concept. Order standing up, drink the small coffee the way the regulars around you do, and treat it as a short ceremony rather than a takeaway errand. A colada is meant for sharing, served in a larger cup with small cups alongside, while a cortadito is a single, milk softened shot.

What to order

For a first meal, order croquetas to start, then ropa vieja with rice, black beans, and maduros, or the Cuban sandwich if you want the pressed classic. The menu also runs through staples such as vaca frita, lechon asado, and arroz con pollo, so a group can order across the canon and share. Add a mamey or guava shake if it is offered that day, and finish with flan or a bakery pastry and a cortadito at the window on your way out. Portions are generous and the price level is mid range, which is part of the restaurant's everyday role: this is where Miami eats Cuban food routinely, not where it performs it at splurge prices. Versailles at a glance

Address: 3555 SW 8th Street, Miami, FL 33135, per the official site. Operating since: 1971, per the official history page. Known for: full service Cuban dining room, an adjacent bakery, and La Ventanita, the walk up window serving cortaditos and coladas. Neighborhood: Calle Ocho, Little Havana.

How to visit well

Come hungry and come in the daytime at least once, because Calle Ocho reads best as a street, not just a dinner destination. Walk the blocks around Domino Park first, then eat, then take coffee at the window and watch the street do its work. Weekends and news making days bring crowds, and the room can be busy at peak lunch hours, so build in a little patience rather than a tight schedule. Dress is everyday casual. Frequently asked questions

Where is Versailles Restaurant in Miami?
At 3555 SW 8th Street, Miami, FL 33135, on Calle Ocho at the western side of Little Havana. The address is published on the restaurant's official site.

What is La Ventanita?
La Ventanita is Versailles' walk up coffee window. Its own page describes it as serving Cuban cafecito, including the cortadito and coladas, and credits the restaurant's founder with developing the ventanita concept.

What should I order at Versailles the first time?
Croquetas to start, ropa vieja or a Cuban sandwich as the main, rice and black beans with maduros on the side, and a cortadito at La Ventanita to finish. That order covers the dishes the restaurant is most associated with.

Is Versailles only for tourists?
No. Its own history page describes it as a gathering place for Miami's Cuban exile community since shortly after it opened in 1971, and the coffee window crowd on any weekday is mostly local. Visitors are welcome, but the restaurant's role is a neighborhood institution.
