Best Bakeries in Miami

Short answer first. For artisan bread, go to Zak the Baker in Wynwood. For Cuban pastelitos and cafecito in one stop, go to Vicky Bakery or CAO Bakery and Cafe. For a neighborhood Cuban bakery with many locations, look at Pinecrest Bakery. For pastry chef level viennoiserie, go to Bachour in Coral Gables. And for a small, quality focused bakery cafe, try Madruga Bakery, also in Coral Gables. Miami bakeries split into Cuban bakery counters, which are a daily ritual, and artisan bread and pastry shops, which are a destination, and a good Miami morning uses both.

Two bakery cultures

The Cuban bakery is built around pastelitos, flaky pastries filled with guava, cheese, or seasoned meat, plus croquetas, Cuban toast, and coffee at the counter. It is budget friendly, fast, and woven into workday life across the city. The artisan bakery is built around slow fermented bread, laminated croissants, and a pastry case that changes with a chef hand. Prices run higher and the visit is the point. Neither replaces the other. Start a Little Havana morning at a Cuban bakery counter, and save Zak the Baker or Bachour for a morning when the bakery itself is the plan. For the coffee half of the ritual, see our best Cuban coffee in Miami guide.

The bakeries

Zak the Baker, Wynwood

Zak the Baker, at 295 NW 26th Street in Wynwood, is a kosher bakery and cafe known for naturally leavened breads, babka, and a cafe menu built on its own baking. The bread is the headline, so buy a loaf to take away even if you sit down for toast and eggs. Note the kosher rhythm when planning, and check the bakery site for current days and hours before a Saturday visit. Best for bread lovers and a Wynwood breakfast. Price level: mid range.

Vicky Bakery, multiple locations

Vicky Bakery states on its own site that Antonio and Gelasia Cao opened the first Vicky Bakery in Hialeah in 1972, and the group now serves pastelitos, croquetas, flan, and cafe con leche across South Florida, including Flagler Street locations in Miami. This is the classic Cuban bakery counter, order at the case, coffee in hand, box of pastelitos to go. Best for an authentic, budget friendly bakery run and for party trays of pastries. Price level: budget friendly.

CAO Bakery and Cafe, multiple locations

CAO Bakery and Cafe carries the same Cuban bakery tradition in a cafe format, with locations including North Miami Beach and Miami Beach listed in current directories and its own site at caobakerycafe.com. The order is the same ritual, pastelitos, croquetas, Cuban sandwiches, and cafecito, with more room to sit than a pure counter. Best for a casual bakery breakfast or lunch that can stretch a little longer. Price level: budget friendly.

Pinecrest Bakery, multiple locations

Pinecrest Bakery, with its site at pinecrestbakery.com and locations across Miami Dade, is another Cuban bakery staple, known for pastelitos, cakes, and coffee served from early morning. It suits the south end of the city, Pinecrest, Kendall, and the neighborhoods between, where a Vicky or CAO run would mean a drive. Best for south Miami residents and anyone ordering a celebration cake from a Cuban bakery. Price level: budget friendly.

Bachour, Coral Gables

Bachour, at 2020 Salzedo Street in Coral Gables, is chef Antonio Bachour flagship bakery and restaurant, described on its own site as a Michelin Guide Bib Gourmand bakery with signature pastries, brunch, lunch, and dinner. The pastry case, croissants, pistachio and pecan viennoiserie, petits gateaux, and macarons, is the reason to go. Best for a pastry splurge, a Coral Gables brunch, and gifts that need to impress. Price level: splurge leaning for a bakery, mid range for brunch.

Madruga Bakery, Coral Gables

Madruga Bakery, in Coral Gables with its site at madrugabakery.com, is the smaller, bread focused counterpoint to Bachour in the same neighborhood, built around fresh breads, pastries, and a cafe menu. It suits a quieter morning and a loaf to take home. Best for Coral Gables locals and anyone doing a two stop Gables bakery morning, bread at Madruga, pastry at Bachour. Price level: mid range.

Pastelito basics

A pastelito is a flaky, puff pastry turnover. Guava, guava and cheese, and picadillo style meat fillings are the classics at Cuban bakeries. They are best warm and fresh from the case in the morning, and a mixed dozen is the standard take home order for a group.

Frequently asked questions

What should I order at a Cuban bakery first?
A guava pastelito, a ham croqueta, and a cafecito or cafe con leche. That trio is the baseline order, and every Cuban bakery on this list does all three.

Where is the best bread in Miami?
Zak the Baker in Wynwood is the artisan bread destination on this list, with Madruga Bakery in Coral Gables the smaller alternative. Both bake on site and sell loaves to take away.

Are these bakeries good for cakes?
The Cuban bakeries, Vicky, CAO, and Pinecrest, are the celebration cake tradition in Miami, especially for family parties. Order ahead for a custom cake rather than relying on the case.

When should I go?
Morning, for all of them. Cuban bakery cases are fullest early, artisan bread sells out at Zak the Baker, and Bachour pastry case is at its best before the brunch rush thins it.
