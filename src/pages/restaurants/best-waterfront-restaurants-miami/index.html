Best Waterfront Restaurants in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Pick your water first. For the skyline at sunset, Rusty Pelican on Key Biscayne. For the working Miami River, Garcia's and Casablanca for seafood and Seaspice for a dressed up night. For Biscayne Bay in Edgewater, Amara at Paraiso. For a casual marina afternoon, Monty's Raw Bar in Coconut Grove. For a state park harbor lunch, Boater's Grill on Key Biscayne. For ships passing at South Pointe, Smith and Wollensky in Miami Beach. Every place below was checked as operating on October 30, 2026.

Bay and skyline settings

Rusty Pelican, Key Biscayne

Rusty Pelican, at 3201 Rickenbacker Causeway on Key Biscayne, describes itself as offering contemporary American cuisine and fresh local seafood with a view of the Miami skyline across Biscayne Bay. Order seafood first, such as a whole local snapper or a seafood tower. It suits sunset reservations and celebrations. Book ahead for the sunset window and allow time for causeway traffic. Price level: splurge.

Amara at Paraiso, Edgewater

Amara at Paraiso, at 3101 NE 7th Avenue in Edgewater, presents itself as waterfront dining by chef Michael Schwartz, with bold, coastal Latin American cuisine on Biscayne Bay. Order yuca cheese puffs and a seafood plate to share, or a tiradito to start. It suits dates and small groups who want bay views without a causeway trip. Indoor and outdoor seating both face the water. Price level: splurge.

The Miami River

Seaspice, Miami River

Seaspice, at 412 NW North River Drive, describes itself on its own site as a waterfront brasserie and lounge on the banks of the Miami River, set in a converted warehouse, where guests can arrive by land or by sea. Order oysters, lobster, or a whole fish, and expect a scene as much as a meal, with yachts at the dock. It suits a dressed up night out. Reservations through its own site are the sensible route for weekend evenings. Price level: splurge.

Garcia's Seafood Grille and Fish Market, Miami River

Garcia's, at 398 NW North River Drive, is the informal counterweight to Seaspice on the same river: a family owned seafood grille and fish market serving for over 50 years, per its own site, with waterfront tables overlooking passing boats. Order the whole yellowtail snapper or the catch from its own market. It suits diners who want the river as a working place rather than a backdrop, and families who want seafood without a dress code. Price level: mid range.

Casablanca Seafood, Miami River

Casablanca, at 400 NW North River Drive beside Garcia's, pairs its restaurant with a fish market dating to 1994 in its own materials, and dining rooms and terraces facing the river and downtown. Order ceviche, a whole fried fish, or a shared seafood stew. It suits groups who want a step up in formality from a fish market lunch while keeping the boats in view. Price level: splurge leaning, so check the current menu when you sit down.

Casual water: marina, park, and South Pointe

Monty's Raw Bar, Coconut Grove

Monty's, at 2550 South Bayshore Drive in Coconut Grove, opened in 1969, per its own history, and sits in a tiki style setting by the marina on Biscayne Bay. Order raw bar items, stone crab in season, and key lime pie, and settle in for live music and a long afternoon. It suits families, boaters, and anyone who wants waterfront to mean sandals rather than reservations. Price level: mid range.

Boater's Grill, Key Biscayne

Boater's Grill, at 1200 Crandon Boulevard inside Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park, sits on No Name Harbor at the southern tip of Key Biscayne, per its own site, and can be reached by land or by sea. Order grilled fish, ceviche, and flan in a setting that is part park outing, part harbor restaurant. It suits a beach and lighthouse day at the state park. Remember that you are entering a state park, so plan for park entry. Price level: mid range.

Smith and Wollensky, South Pointe, Miami Beach

Smith and Wollensky, at 1 Washington Avenue in South Pointe Park, Miami Beach, describes its location as oceanview dining with outdoor spaces and skyline views, watching over Government Cut. Order a dry aged steak and seafood starters such as oysters or stone crab in season. It suits a splurge dinner after a South Pointe walk, with ship watching alongside. Price level: splurge.

A closure to know about

La Mar, the Peruvian restaurant at the Mandarin Oriental on Brickell Key, closed when the hotel closed on May 31, 2025. It is not included above, and any guide still sending you to Brickell Key for La Mar is out of date. Facts checked October 30, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Which waterfront restaurant is best for sunset?
Rusty Pelican on Key Biscayne, for the skyline across the bay as the light changes. Book the sunset window ahead and allow causeway time. Amara at Paraiso is the mainland alternative with bay views in Edgewater.

Can I arrive at a Miami waterfront restaurant by boat?
Some river and harbor restaurants are set up for it. Seaspice says guests can arrive by land or sea, and Boater's Grill sits on No Name Harbor inside Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park. Confirm docking arrangements with the restaurant before you set out.

Are waterfront restaurants in Miami always expensive?
No. Garcia's and Monty's Raw Bar are mid range, and Boater's Grill is a park harbor lunch rather than a splurge. The splurge end is Rusty Pelican, Seaspice, Amara at Paraiso, and Smith and Wollensky. Price levels on this page are described in words and were checked against each restaurant's own positioning in October 2026.

What should I wear to a waterfront dinner in Miami?
Most Miami waterfront rooms are smart casual, and the casual end, Monty's and Boater's Grill, is genuinely casual. For Seaspice, Amara, and Smith and Wollensky in the evening, dress as for a nice city dinner and check the restaurant's own site if you are unsure.
