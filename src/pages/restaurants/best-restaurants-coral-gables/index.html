Best Restaurants in Coral Gables

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Coral Gables eats like a small city of its own: Spanish tapas at Bulla Gastrobar, Cuban comfort food at the newly reopened Havana Harry's, the Miracle Mile reliability of Hillstone, Asian small plates at Zitz Sum, island minded cooking at Mamey, old school Italian at Caffe Abbracci, Argentine grills at Graziano's, and pastry worth a detour at Bachour. The list splits between the Miracle Mile and Giralda dining core and the US 1 corridor near the University of Miami. Every place was confirmed as operating on October 30, 2026.

Around Miracle Mile and Giralda Plaza

Bulla Gastrobar

Bulla Gastrobar, at 2500 Ponce de Leon Boulevard, is a Spanish gastrobar inspired by the tapas bars of Spain, per its own site, with the bar at the center of the room. Order patatas bravas, gambas al ajillo, and ham croquettes, then a paella for the table if the group is big enough. It is mid range, lively, and the easiest Coral Gables pick for a group that wants to share everything. The terrace fills first on mild evenings, so book it specifically.

Hillstone Coral Gables

Hillstone, at 201 Miracle Mile, is the polished American restaurant on the Mile's corner with Ponce de Leon. Its own site describes a modern, warm room, and the menu is the group's familiar lineup: spinach and artichoke dip, the French dip sandwich, grilled fish, steaks, and pressed sushi. It is mid range to splurge and suits almost any party, which is exactly why locals use it for business lunches and family dinners alike. Expect a wait at peak times even with a booking.

Zitz Sum

Zitz Sum, at 396 Alhambra Circle, is the Gables' small plates outlier, cooking Asian inspired dishes in a dim sum spirit, built for ordering in waves. Dumplings and small plates come out as they are ready, so the table eats communally whether it planned to or not. It is mid range, suits adventurous couples and small groups, and gives a Coral Gables evening an option that is neither tapas nor Italian. Book ahead; the room is not large.

Caffe Abbracci

Caffe Abbracci, at 318 Aragon Avenue, is old school Italian fine dining that has anchored its block for decades. Its own site says the name means hugs, and the room runs on that family logic: black lobster ravioli, veal dishes, and the Napoleon for dessert. It is a splurge, quieter than the Giralda scene, and suits dates and family occasions where the grandparents set the standard. Lunch runs on weekdays, and dinner is the main event.

Graziano's Restaurant

Graziano's, at 394 Giralda Avenue, is the Argentine entry on the pedestrian plaza, from the group behind the Graziano's markets. Expect empanadas, grilled meats, and milanesa, with a glass of Malbec, in a room that handles families and groups easily. It is mid range and suits diners who want the Giralda Plaza setting with a menu everyone can read. The market side of the family business explains the depth of the meat and wine offering.

The US 1 side: hotels, campus, and pastry

Havana Harry's

Havana Harry's, at 4612 South Le Jeune Road, is Coral Gables' Cuban comfort food landmark, reopened on October 19, 2026, after more than a year closed, with the same recipes it has served since 1995. Ropa vieja, arroz con pollo, croquetas, and Cuban sandwiches come in the generous portions the room is known for. It is mid range, deeply family friendly, and the Gables answer when the meal needs to please three generations at once. Lunch and dinner run daily to start.

Mamey at THesis Hotel

Mamey, at 1350 South Dixie Highway inside the THesis Hotel, describes itself as island minded, and its menu mixes Caribbean and Asian flavors a short walk from the University of Miami campus. The truffle and brie grilled cheese and the jerk margarita give a sense of the kitchen's range. It is mid range to splurge, suits hotel guests and campus visitors, and works as a dinner before events on the Gables side of US 1. The hotel setting keeps it calm even on busy nights.

Bachour

Bachour, at 2020 Salzedo Street, is chef Antonio Bachour's bakery and contemporary restaurant, and the pastry case is the headline: croissants, petits gateaux, and macarons made to a standard few American bakeries attempt. Come for brunch or lunch and treat the desserts as the main course. It is mid range, suits pastry lovers and anyone hosting a visitor who thinks they have seen it all, and it is the Gables stop most likely to send you home with a box for later.

Frequently asked questions

What part of Coral Gables is best for a dinner out?
The Miracle Mile and Giralda Plaza core, where Bulla, Hillstone, Zitz Sum, Caffe Abbracci, and Graziano's sit within a short walk of each other. The US 1 side adds Mamey and Havana Harry's for bigger, more local feeling rooms.

Which Coral Gables restaurant suits a big family dinner?
Havana Harry's. The portions, the menu of Cuban standards, and the room are all built for multi generation tables, and it takes the pressure off ordering for a crowd.

Where should pastry lovers go in Coral Gables?
Bachour, without a contest. Plan it as a brunch or lunch so the full pastry case is still stocked, and expect to take a box home.

Do you need reservations in Coral Gables?
For dinner at Hillstone, Zitz Sum, and Caffe Abbracci, yes, particularly on weekends. Bulla and Graziano's are easier, and lunch is calmer everywhere on this list.
