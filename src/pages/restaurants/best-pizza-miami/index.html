Best Pizza in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. For thin, inventive Italian pies, go to Mister O1. For a Brooklyn style brick oven pie in Sunset Harbour, go to Lucali. For a New York slice that runs late, go to Steve's Pizza in North Miami. For old Miami square cut pizza, go to Frankie's on Bird Road. And for a small Brickell room doing Neapolitan style pies, try Stanzione 87. Miami pizza is a style argument, so this list is organized by the pie you actually want, not by a single ranking.

Styles on this list

Neapolitan and Italian thin crust pies are soft centered, blistered at the edge, and built for one person per pie. New York style means large, foldable slices with a crisp bottom, sold by the slice as well as by the pie. Square cut at Frankie's is its own Miami tradition, a pan baked pie cut into squares rather than wedges. Brick oven pies at Lucali sit between traditions, thin and charred, served whole in a sit down room. Decide the format first, because the best version of each style comes from a different kitchen on this list, and comparing across styles is how pizza arguments never end.

The pizzerias

Mister O1 Extraordinary Pizza, Miami Beach and Wynwood

Mister O1 describes its pies as traditional and extraordinary Italian thin crust, made with ingredients mostly imported from Italy or locally sourced. The signature is the star shaped pizza, with points folded around ricotta, and the Star Luca is the order most tables start with, alongside burrata starters. Locations include Miami Beach at 1680 Michigan Avenue and Wynwood on North Miami Avenue, among others listed on its site. Best for a fun, inventive pie and a casual date. Price level: mid range.

Lucali, Sunset Harbour, Miami Beach

Lucali, at 1930 Bay Road in Sunset Harbour, lists a price range of $$ on its own site and keeps the menu deliberately focused, plain and specialty pies, calzones, and a short list of starters. The room is rustic and sit down, the pies are whole rather than by the slice, and the plain pie is the test of the place, so order it before the loaded versions. Best for a proper pizza dinner in Miami Beach, and for Brooklyn transplants checking the Miami outpost against home. Price level: mid range.

Steve's Pizza, North Miami

Steve's, at 12101 Biscayne Boulevard in North Miami, is the New York slice shop on this list, the place for a large foldable slice, a Sicilian slice, garlic rolls, and stromboli, with late hours that make it the post movie and post beach stop for the north end of the city. The Steve's Special is the loaded order. Best for families, takeout nights, and slice purists who judge a shop by its plain slice first. Price level: budget friendly to mid range.

Frankie's Pizza, Bird Road

Frankie's, at 9118 SW 40th Street, which is Bird Road, is a family owned Miami institution built on square cut pizza, a format locals have strong feelings about. The model is takeout first, with half baked pies you can finish at home a long standing part of the appeal, plus garlic rolls. We have not printed a founding year here because we verify year claims on a restaurant own site before publishing them, and the charm of Frankie's does not need a date to recommend it. Best for old Miami nostalgia and a pie format you will not find at the chains. Price level: budget friendly.

Stanzione 87, Brickell

Stanzione 87, in Brickell, is the small room Neapolitan option, wood fired pies with leopard spotted crusts, a short menu, and a neighborhood feel in the middle of the Brickell towers. It suits a weeknight dinner or a date that wants good pizza without a production around it. Order a margherita to judge the dough, then a specialty pie for the table. Best for Brickell residents and downtown workers staying local. Price level: mid range.

Slice or whole pie

Only some Miami pizzerias sell by the slice, and Steve's is the reliable slice stop on this list. Lucali, Mister O1, and Stanzione 87 are whole pie formats, so they suit a table of two or more better than a solo snack run. Frankie's square cut travels well, which is why takeout and half baked pies are central to its appeal.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most Miami pizza on this list?
Frankie's square cut pie on Bird Road. It is a local format with a local following, and it tastes different from both New York and Neapolitan pies, which is exactly why it belongs here.

Where can I get a single slice?
Steve's Pizza in North Miami is the slice shop pick, with New York style and Sicilian slices plus garlic rolls. Most other spots on this list sell whole pies only.

Which place suits a date?
Lucali for a sit down room in Sunset Harbour, Mister O1 for something playful, and Stanzione 87 for a quiet Brickell evening. All three are mid range rather than splurge.

Do these places take reservations?
Lucali notes on its own site that it does not take reservation requests in the usual way, so expect a wait at peak hours. The others are largely walk in and takeout driven, with waits that peak on Friday and Saturday nights.
