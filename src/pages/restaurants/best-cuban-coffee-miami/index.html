Best Cuban Coffee in Miami (Cafecito Guide)

Short answer first. Order a cafecito if you want one strong, sweet shot, a colada if you are sharing, a cortadito if you want that shot softened with a little steamed milk, and a cafe con leche if you want a full milky cup with breakfast. Then drink it standing at a ventanita, the walk up window that is the real institution. Start in Little Havana, where Versailles and La Carreta face each other on Calle Ocho, and use the bakery counters at Vicky Bakery and CAO Bakery when you want coffee with a pastelito in the same stop.

The four drinks, explained

A cafecito, also called cafe cubano, is a small shot of espresso sweetened during brewing, so the sugar is part of the drink rather than an afterthought. A colada is the same coffee served as a larger shareable portion, usually poured into small cups so a group can split it at work, at a counter, or on a sidewalk. A cortadito is a cafecito cut with steamed milk, stronger and smaller than a latte,. A cafe con leche is the breakfast cup, mostly hot milk with a shot of espresso, often paired with buttered Cuban toast for dipping.

What a ventanita is

A ventanita is a walk up window, literally a little window, cut into the side of a restaurant or cafeteria. You order standing up, drink standing at a counter ledge, and leave within minutes. The format suits Miami heat and work breaks, and visitors get a very local coffee experience for a budget friendly price. Versailles credits its founder with developing the walk up window concept, and its La Ventanita page describes the window serving cafecito, cortaditos, and coladas at 3555 SW 8th Street.

Where to drink it

Versailles Restaurant, Little Havana

The Calle Ocho landmark at 3555 SW 8th Street is the first stop most visitors should make. Use the ventanita on the side of the building rather than waiting for a dining room table if coffee is the mission. Order a colada if you are with friends, a cortadito if you are solo, and a pastelito from the bakery side to go with it. Best for first timers and anyone building a Little Havana day around our Little Havana food tour guide. Price level: budget friendly at the window.

La Carreta, Little Havana

Directly in the same Calle Ocho orbit at 3632 SW 8th Street, La Carreta is the other classic window, with The coffee works the same way, strong and sweet, and the room suits a sit down breakfast of cafe con leche and tostada if you want more than a standing shot. Best for travelers who want the ventanita experience in one stop. Price level: budget friendly to mid range.

Sergio's, Coral Way and beyond

Sergio's, with its Coral Gables area location at 3252 SW 22nd Street and other Miami locations listed on its own site, presents itself as Cuban roots with a broader modern menu. For coffee it is a reliable sit down option, with cortadito and cafe con leche alongside a full breakfast. Best for a slower morning rather than a quick window stop. Price level: mid range.

Enriqueta's Sandwich Shop, Edgewater

At 186 NE 29th Street, near Wynwood and Edgewater, Enriqueta's is an old school sandwich shop and cafeteria where the coffee is part of a working lunch culture. Order a cortadito at the counter with a pressed sandwich or croquetas, and expect a local crowd rather than a tour group. Best for visitors exploring Wynwood who want a genuine neighborhood cafeteria a short hop away. Price level: budget friendly.

Islas Canarias, Westchester and Tamiami area

Islas Canarias, listed at 13695 SW 26th Street, is famous in Miami croqueta conversations, and the coffee follows the same cafeteria logic: a cafecito or cafe con leche with breakfast or with a box of croquetas to take away. It is a drive for beach based visitors, and that is the point. Best for road trippers and croqueta completists. Price level: budget friendly.

Vicky Bakery and CAO Bakery and Cafe

Cuban bakeries double as coffee stops across Miami. Vicky Bakery, founded in Hialeah in 1972 according to its own site, serves cafecito and cafe con leche with pastelitos and croquetas at multiple locations, including its Flagler Street shops. CAO Bakery and Cafe, run by the same bakery family tradition, does the same pairing in a cafe format. Best for a coffee and pastry combination, morning or afternoon. Price level: budget friendly.

How to order without overthinking it

Say the drink name. Coladas are for sharing, so do not order one alone unless you plan to be awake for a while. Sweetness is built in, so ask before you add sugar. And drink a cafecito standing up at least once, that is the format it was made for.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a cafecito and a cortadito?
A cafecito is straight sweetened espresso in a small cup. A cortadito is the same shot cut with a little steamed milk, which softens the intensity without turning it into a milky breakfast drink.

How many people does a colada serve?
A colada is a shareable portion poured into several small cups. It is designed for a group at a window or an office, not as a single large drink for one person.

Is Cuban coffee very strong?
It is concentrated and sweet, served in small amounts. If you are sensitive to caffeine, start with a cortadito or a cafe con leche rather than a full cafecito, and avoid a colada on your own.

Where should a first timer start?
Start at the Versailles ventanita in Little Havana, then compare it with La Carreta across the street. Two windows, one block, and you will understand the culture quickly.
