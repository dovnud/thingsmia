Cheap Eats in Miami That Are Actually Good

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. Eat Cuban. Miami's best low cost food is its everyday Cuban cooking: a frita at El Rey de las Fritas, pan con minuta at La Camaronera, a sandwich at Enriqueta's or Sanguich, croquetas at Islas Canarias, and tacos at Coyo Taco in Wynwood. Add a food hall for groups. Every place below was checked as operating on October 30, 2026, and price levels are described in words, because exact prices change too often to publish honestly.

Cuban counters that anchor a cheap eats day

El Rey de las Fritas, Little Havana

At 1821 SW 8th Street, El Rey de las Fritas serves the frita, a seasoned Cuban style burger topped with shoestring potatoes on a Cuban roll. Order the original first. It suits a Calle Ocho walk in Little Havana, a quick lunch, and anyone who wants a Miami original rather than a generic burger. Price level: budget friendly.

La Camaronera, Flagler Street

La Camaronera, at 1952 West Flagler Street, is a seafood joint and fish market serving since 1973, per its own materials. Order pan con minuta, its fried snapper sandwich, or fried shrimp at the counter. It suits seafood lovers on a budget and shows how Cuban Miami cooks fish: fried, fast, and with the market in the same room. Price level: budget friendly to mid range, depending on whether you order a sandwich or a whole fish.

Enriqueta's Sandwich Shop, Edgewater

Enriqueta's, at 186 NE 29th Street, is a small Cuban cafe serving breakfast and lunch. Order pan con bistec or a Cuban sandwich and a colada to share. It suits a Wynwood or Midtown morning, workers on lunch, and anyone who measures a cheap eat by how many regulars are in line. Go earlier rather than later, because the format is a daytime one. Price level: budget friendly.

Sandwiches and croquetas worth the trip

Sanguich, Little Havana

Sanguich, at 2057 SW 8th Street, makes its sandwich components in house, from bread and mustard to lechon, per its own site. Order El Cubano or pan con lechon and a batido. It costs more than the plainest counter on this page, so treat it as the splurge of a cheap eats day: one carefully made sandwich that still lands well below a sit down dinner. It suits a Calle Ocho tasting walk and pairs with a coffee window stop. Price level: mid range, included here for value rather than the lowest price.

Islas Canarias, southwest Miami

Islas Canarias, at 13695 SW 26th Street, is a family restaurant established in 1977 serving Cuban and Spanish dishes. The cheap eats move here is the croqueta order and a cafe con leche, or a full plate if your group is sharing. It suits drivers exploring beyond the tourist map and families who want a sit down meal at counter style value. The bakery and cafe side makes breakfast an especially good value play. Price level: mid range, with small orders that keep a visit budget friendly.

Beyond Cuban, still affordable

Coyo Taco, Wynwood

Coyo Taco lists its Wynwood flagship at 2320 NW 2nd Avenue (some listings show 2300 NW 2nd Avenue for the same corner). It serves Mexican street food, with tacos such as al pastor and cochinita pibil, fresh guacamole, and tortillas made on site, per its own description. Order a few tacos and esquites and share guacamole. It suits Wynwood gallery days, groups with mixed appetites, and a late, casual bite after the Walls. Price level: mid range, with a taco by taco order keeping the total in cheap eats territory.

Food halls, for divided groups

When your group cannot agree, a food hall keeps everyone at one table and lets each person control their own spend. Our best food halls in Miami guide covers the current options, from Julia and Henry's downtown to 1-800-Lucky in Wynwood. The tactic is simple: set a per person budget before you walk in, order at the counter, and skip the extra round that turns a cheap eat into a regular dinner bill. Price level: budget friendly to mid range, depending on the stall you choose.

Three rules for eating cheaply in Miami

One, order at counters and coffee windows where Miami locals eat daily. Two, make lunch the main meal and keep dinner light, because the same kitchens charge their friendliest prices in the daytime. Three, share the add ons: a colada, a plate of croquetas, and a batido are all built for a table, not a solo diner.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to eat well in Miami?
Build the day around Cuban counters: a sandwich or frita in Little Havana, a coffee at a ventanita, and a fish sandwich at La Camaronera. Those formats are everyday Miami eating, priced for regulars rather than visitors.

Are cheap eats in Miami only Cuban food?
No, but Cuban food is the backbone. Coyo Taco in Wynwood covers Mexican street food at taco by taco prices, and food halls add Asian, Peruvian, and other options under one roof. The Cuban picks dominate because they combine low prices with places locals actually use.

How do I keep a food hall visit cheap?
Decide your budget before you enter, order one main item per person, share a side, and drink water with the meal. Food halls feel casual, which is exactly why small extras add up there faster than at a counter.

What is a frita and a pan con minuta?
A frita is a seasoned Cuban style burger topped with crisp shoestring potatoes on a Cuban roll, the signature of El Rey de las Fritas. Pan con minuta is a fried snapper sandwich, the signature of La Camaronera. Both are single items that make a full, inexpensive lunch.
