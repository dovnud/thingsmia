Best Sushi in Miami

Short answer first. For creative Japanese cooking in a lively room, choose Uchi Miami in Wynwood. For a polished shopping day splurge, choose Makoto in Bal Harbour. For a classic big night out, choose Nobu Miami in the Eden Roc or Zuma downtown. For fun, shareable fusion rolls, choose Pubbelly Sushi. And for a quieter Wynwood counter experience, look at Hiyakawa. Miami sushi splits cleanly into omakase splurges, chef driven dining rooms, and casual roll spots, so pick by occasion first and by fish second.

How Miami sushi breaks down

Omakase means leaving the selection to the chef, course by course, and it is the splurge format at the top end. A la carte sushi rooms let you build your own order of nigiri, sashimi, and rolls, which suits groups with mixed tastes. Fusion spots add Latin and tropical touches that are very Miami, crispy rice, citrus, and heat. None of these formats is better in the abstract. A birthday group will be happier sharing rolls at Pubbelly, while a serious sushi fan celebrating one big night should book the chef driven rooms and let the kitchen lead.

The sushi rooms

Uchi Miami, Wynwood

Uchi, at 252 NW 25th Street in Wynwood, describes its cooking as non traditional Japanese cuisine with signature tastings, sushi, and a seasonal omakase, founded by chef Tyson Cole. Order the hama chili and a run of nigiri, or commit to the omakase if it is a special night. The room is stylish without being stiff, and lunch service makes it more flexible than most splurge sushi. Best for dates, birthdays, and sushi fans who like invention. Price level: splurge.

Makoto, Bal Harbour

Makoto, at 9700 Collins Avenue inside Bal Harbour Shops, is built around Edomae style sushi reshaped for a modern audience, in the chef own description. Crispy rice preparations and hamachi dishes are the signatures to start with, then nigiri. It pairs naturally with a Bal Harbour shopping day and suits a polished lunch as well as dinner. Best for a refined, quiet splurge in the north beach area. Price level: splurge.

Nobu Miami, Miami Beach

Nobu Miami, at 4525 Collins Avenue in the Eden Roc and Nobu Hotel Miami Beach, serves the Japanese Peruvian cooking that made the name global. Black cod with miso and rock shrimp tempura are the famous orders alongside the sushi and sashimi, and yellowtail with jalapeno is the dish most first timers are told to try. Best for a big occasion, a hotel based stay, and diners who want the full signature menu, not only sushi. Price level: splurge.

Zuma Miami, Downtown

Zuma, in downtown Miami on Biscayne Bay, is a large, energetic izakaya style room where sushi is one part of a broader sharing menu of robata grill dishes and small plates. It suits groups who want a scene with their sushi, business dinners that need energy, and late evenings that start at the bar. Order a spread across the menu rather than only rolls, the kitchen is designed for sharing. Price level: splurge.

Pubbelly Sushi, Miami Beach and Brickell

Pubbelly Sushi, with Miami Beach and Brickell City Centre locations listed on its own site, is the relaxed fusion option, known for the Butter Krab Roll and bigeye tuna on crispy rice. The cooking blends Japanese formats with Latin flavors, the rooms are casual, and the bill lands well below the omakase tier. Best for groups, casual sushi nights, and anyone whose table wants cooked and raw dishes mixed together. Price level: mid range.

Hiyakawa, Wynwood

Hiyakawa, in Wynwood, is the quieter, more intimate counter style choice on this list, a Japanese restaurant focused on seasonal cooking and a calmer room than its Wynwood neighbors. It suits a focused dinner for two, or a small group that wants conversation with the meal. Book ahead, small rooms fill first, and let the seasonal menu guide the order. Price level: splurge.

Booking advice that saves the night

Omakase and counter seats are the first to go on weekends. Book the splurge rooms several days ahead for Friday and Saturday, choose an early seating for a quieter room, and tell the restaurant about fish allergies when you reserve, not when you sit down.

Frequently asked questions

What is omakase and is it worth it?
Omakase is a chef selected progression, usually built around the best fish of the day. It is worth it for a special occasion or a serious sushi fan. If you have strong preferences or a fixed budget, ordering a la carte gives you more control.

Where should a group go?
Pubbelly Sushi and Zuma handle groups best, with shareable menus and lively rooms. Uchi also works well for a celebratory table that still cares about the food.

Is there good sushi away from Miami Beach?
Yes. Wynwood has Uchi and Hiyakawa, Brickell has Pubbelly, and downtown has Zuma, so mainland diners do not need to cross the causeway for a serious sushi night.

What should a first timer order?
Start with a signature cooked dish, then a mixed nigiri selection, then one house roll. That sequence shows you the kitchen range without over ordering, and you can add more nigiri if you are still hungry.
