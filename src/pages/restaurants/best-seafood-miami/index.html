Best Seafood Restaurants in Miami

Last reviewed: October 30, 2026. Sources for this page are listed in its fact sheet.

Short answer first. For stone crab and the classic experience, plan around Joe's Stone Crab in South Beach and read the season rules first. For a local, working river setting, go to Garcia's or Casablanca on the Miami River. For a raw bar in Brickell, choose The River Oyster Bar. For a casual marina afternoon, Monty's Raw Bar in Coconut Grove, and for a fried fish sandwich at a counter, La Camaronera near Little Havana. Stone crab and the classics

Joe's Stone Crab, South Beach

Joe's, at 11 Washington Avenue in Miami Beach, was established in 1913, per its own site, and is the name most visitors associate with stone crab claws served chilled with mustard sauce. Order stone crab claws in season, and add the sides the house is known for, such as hash browns, creamed spinach, and key lime pie to finish. It suits a splurge dinner and a special occasion. Read our full Joe's Stone Crab guide before you go, because season, reservations, and the Take Away next door change how you should plan. Price level: splurge.

The Miami River working waterfront

Garcia's Seafood Grille and Fish Market, Miami River

Garcia's, at 398 NW North River Drive, is family owned and says it has served the community for over 50 years from its Miami River site, with waterfront dining and a fish market attached. Order the whole yellowtail snapper or the fresh catch the market is pushing that day, and stone crab claws when they are in season. It suits diners who want seafood that feels connected to boats and a working river rather than a polished dining room, and the upstairs deck is the seat to ask about. Price level: mid range.

Casablanca Seafood, Miami River

Casablanca, at 400 NW North River Drive, sits beside Garcia's on the same river bend, pairing a seafood restaurant with a fish market that its own materials date to 1994. Order ceviche, a whole fried fish, or the seafood zarzuela style stew if your group wants to share. It suits a slightly more dressed up river dinner than its neighbor, with downtown views across the water, and it works well for visitors staying downtown or in Brickell who do not want to cross to the beach. Price level: splurge leaning, described by listings as pricier than the river average, so check the current menu before you order.

Raw bar, marina, and counter picks

The River Oyster Bar, Brickell

The River Oyster Bar, at 33 SE 7th Street, Suite 100 in Brickell, presents itself as a seafood restaurant built around a raw bar, with oysters as the headline and a contemporary take on classic seafood dishes. Order oysters to start, then a crudo or the fish of the day. It suits Brickell business dinners, dates, and anyone whose hotel is downtown and who wants a serious raw bar without a beach trip. The room is a longtime Brickell fixture that moved a short distance to its current address, so use the address above rather than an old listing. Price level: splurge.

Monty's Raw Bar, Coconut Grove

Monty's, at 2550 South Bayshore Drive in Coconut Grove, opened in 1969, per its own history page, and occupies a waterfront tiki style setting by the marina. Order raw bar items, stone crab in season, conch fritters, and key lime pie. It suits a relaxed afternoon that turns into an early dinner, families, and groups who care as much about sitting outside by the water as about the menu. Live music and a casual dress code are part of the format here. Price level: mid range.

La Camaronera, Flagler Street near Little Havana

La Camaronera, at 1952 West Flagler Street, is a seafood joint and fish market that says it has served fresh seafood in South Florida since 1973. Order pan con minuta, the fried snapper sandwich the counter is known for, or fried shrimp. It suits a fast, budget conscious lunch and anyone exploring Little Havana who wants seafood in its Cuban Miami form rather than a raw bar form. Seating is counter and casual tables, and the market side lets you see what is fresh before you order. Price level: budget friendly to mid range.

Stone crab season, in one box

The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission sets the stone crab harvest season in state waters as October 15 to May 1, closed on May 2. Only claws may be harvested, which is why menus sell claws rather than whole crabs. Joe's own menu notes the same October 15 to May 1 season and says stone crabs are available year round, so ask whether claws are fresh in season or frozen if that distinction matters to you.

Frequently asked questions

When is stone crab season in Miami?
The Florida harvest season runs October 15 to May 1, per the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission. That is the window for fresh claws. Outside it, restaurants may serve frozen product, so ask before you order if you came for the season.

Where can I eat seafood without going to South Beach?
On the Miami River at Garcia's and Casablanca, in Brickell at The River Oyster Bar, and in Coconut Grove at Monty's Raw Bar. All four are on the mainland and reachable without crossing a causeway.

What should I order if I do not eat raw seafood?
A whole grilled or fried snapper at Garcia's or Casablanca, pan con minuta at La Camaronera, or cooked fish and shrimp dishes at Monty's. Every kitchen on this page cooks as much as it serves raw.

Is Stiltsville Fish Bar still open?
No. Listings checked for this page in October 2026 show Stiltsville Fish Bar in Sunset Harbour as permanently closed, so it is not included above. Check a restaurant's own site before any trip across town, because Miami seafood rooms turn over quickly.
