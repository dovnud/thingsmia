How to order oysters in Miami

Miami raw bars typically list oysters by harvest area, often a mix of East Coast, Gulf, and West Coast beds, with different salt levels and sizes. Briny and lean usually points north and east, creamier and milder often points to the Gulf. If you are new to raw oysters, ask for a mixed half dozen and taste them plain first, then with lemon, then with any house sauce. Cooked preparations, roasted or Rockefeller style, are a good second round for anyone unsure about raw. Menus at these restaurants carry the standard raw seafood advisory, and anyone with a health concern about raw shellfish should read that advisory and choose cooked dishes instead, which every kitchen here also does well.

The raw bars

The River Oyster Bar, Brickell

At 33 SE 7th Street, Suite 100, in Brickell, The River Oyster Bar is the downtown reference point, a seafood restaurant built around a raw bar with oysters as the headline. Its own menu lists a raw, chilled, and marinated section, which is where to start, before moving to cooked seafood mains. The room suits business dinners, dates, and Brickell evenings when you want the polished version of a raw bar. On happy hour, check the restaurant site on the day, as listed on the restaurant site, checked October 2026, rather than trusting older roundups. Price level: splurge leaning, mid range at lunch.

Monty's Raw Bar, Coconut Grove

Monty's, at 2550 S Bayshore Drive in Coconut Grove, has operated since 1969 according to its own history page, in a waterfront tiki setting on the marina. The raw bar is the draw, with oysters and stone crab when in season, plus the kind of frozen drinks and live music that turn a dozen oysters into an afternoon. Best for groups, boat watching, and anyone who wants their raw bar outdoors. Price level: mid range.

Garcia's Seafood Grille and Fish Market, Miami River

Garcia's, at 398 NW North River Drive, describes itself as family owned and operated for over 50 years, with waterfront dining on the Miami River and fish coming from its own boats to the table. The format is grille plus fish market, so the oyster order sits inside a broader fresh catch menu, and the upstairs and patio seating look over working river traffic. Best for old Miami atmosphere and a casual, local crowd. Price level: mid range.

Casablanca Seafood, Miami River

Casablanca, at 400 NW North River Drive, is Garcia's near neighbor and the slightly more dressed up riverfront option, a seafood restaurant and market listing oysters among its offerings alongside stone crab, ceviche, and paella style dishes. The river view is the setting, sunset is the prime slot, and the raw bar works as a starter before a cooked seafood dinner. Best for a riverfront dinner that feels like an occasion without leaving casual Miami behind. Price level: mid range to splurge.

Frequently asked questions

When are oysters at their best in Miami?
Oysters are served year round in Miami because raw bars source from multiple coasts. Many diners prefer the cooler months for Gulf oysters, but a good raw bar will tell you what is tasting best that week, so ask what arrived most recently.

How many oysters should I order?
Start with a half dozen per person as a starter, or a dozen per person if oysters are the meal. You can always order a second round by origin once you know which style you like.

Are cooked oysters a good option?
Yes. Roasted oysters and Oysters Rockefeller style preparations are staples at Miami raw bars, and they suit anyone who wants the flavor without eating shellfish raw.

Do I need a reservation?
For The River Oyster Bar at prime dinner hours, booking ahead is sensible. Monty's and the riverfront spots are more walk in friendly, though waterfront tables at sunset go first.
